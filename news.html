2:50 PM ET

A federal judge has invalidated Donald Trump's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, determining it violates federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the ruling in response to a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general challenging the fee Trump announced in September. The decision came in a 42-page ruling that vacated the sweeping increase, which represented a 20-to-50 fold jump from existing H-1B visa rates.

The H-1B visa program allows U.S. companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring technical or theoretical expertise. The program has been widely used by technology firms, healthcare organizations, and other sectors facing shortages of specialized workers.

The judge determined that the administration lacked the legal authority to impose such a substantial fee increase through executive action. Business groups and immigration advocates had challenged the fee structure as both economically damaging and beyond the scope of executive authority. Critics argued the dramatic cost increase would effectively price out many legitimate employers from using the program to access global talent pools.

The decision applies immediately, preventing the fee from taking effect while legal challenges continue. The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the ruling or attempt to implement a modified fee structure that might withstand judicial scrutiny.

The case reflects broader tensions over immigration policy and the H-1B program itself. Supporters argue the visa program helps American companies remain competitive by accessing skilled international workers, particularly in technology and specialized fields. Critics contend the program can displace domestic workers or suppress wages in certain industries.

This ruling represents a significant setback for the administration's efforts to reshape immigration policy through regulatory changes. The fee had been a centerpiece of Trump's approach to restricting immigration pathways, particularly for employment-based visas that bring foreign workers to the United States.

The decision also comes amid broader legal challenges to various Trump administration policies. Other recent court rulings have blocked different aspects of the administration's immigration agenda, with judges frequently finding that executive actions exceeded constitutional or statutory authority.

Business organizations across multiple sectors that rely on foreign talent for positions requiring advanced degrees and specialized skills have largely supported legal challenges to the fee. Technology companies in particular depend heavily on the H-1B program to fill technical positions.

The outcome of any appeal could significantly impact how many foreign workers enter the United States through the H-1B program in coming years. Until the appeals process concludes, employers can proceed with H-1B visa applications at the previous fee levels.

United States airlines spent $6.5 billion on fuel in April, marking a dramatic increase in operating costs that has prompted the International Air Transport Association to slash its global airline profit forecast nearly in half. The surge reflects broader economic pressures hitting the services sector as companies struggle with elevated energy prices tied to Middle East tensions.

The spike in jet fuel expenses has created severe margin pressure across the airline industry. Fuel represents one of the largest operating costs for carriers, and the recent escalation has significantly eroded the financial cushion many airlines built during periods of lower energy prices. Industry analysts estimate the global increase in jet fuel costs has reached $100 billion, fundamentally reshaping the financial landscape for air travel.

Rather than absorbing these costs, airlines are increasingly turning to fuel surcharges to maintain profitability. IAG, the conglomerate owning British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus and Vueling, announced it would make "pricing adjustments to reflect these higher fuel costs." Virgin Atlantic has gone further, adding a charge of £360 to business class tickets and £50 to economy fares. Virgin Atlantic's chief executive acknowledged the challenge, stating it would still be "hard to make a profit this year" despite these measures.

A survey by S&P Global found that nearly six in ten companies in the services sector reported rising average costs in April, with fuel and higher wages driving most of the increases. This widespread price pressure across the economy is now the fastest pace of increase in more than three years. S&P Global's economics director noted that "a number of firms also noted that they had brought in fuel surcharges for their customers, which led to a spike in prices-charged inflation."

The situation presents a dilemma for policymakers. While the Bank of England recently held interest rates steady, widespread price increases are likely to pressure the central bank toward further rate hikes. The Bank's governor warned that continued disruption to energy supplies would complicate economic management.

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, has experienced volatile swings as geopolitical uncertainty persists. While prices briefly fell below $100 a barrel on hopes for reopening critical shipping routes, analysts caution that energy costs remain dependent on how Middle East tensions evolve.

Despite the cost pressures, firms in the services sector reported slightly better business activity than expected in April. However, economists cautioned this improvement could prove short-lived, with new business remaining subdued compared with earlier in the year and firms hesitant to make investment decisions amid ongoing uncertainty about energy prices and regional stability.

Apple announced a major overhaul of its voice assistant at its annual Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday. The redesigned tool, now called Siri AI, represents a significant shift in how the assistant works. Rather than functioning as a web-based question-and-answer tool, the new Siri will operate more like AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini, integrating directly with Apple Intelligence across the company's ecosystem.

The announcement addresses years of user frustration with Siri's limitations and multiple delays in delivering promised improvements. Apple first announced an updated Siri in 2024 but postponed its release multiple times. The company even settled a 250 million dollar class-action lawsuit in May after consumers alleged that Apple had falsely advertised Siri's AI capabilities. Some affected customers received payouts of up to 95 dollars.

The new Siri will be powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a billion-dollar partnership between the two companies. According to Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice president of Siri engineering, the assistant has been fundamentally rebuilt with generative AI at its core. The tool will have its own dedicated app and will be more conversational, pulling from Apple's native app ecosystem on both mobile and laptop devices to help with daily planning, information retrieval, and navigation.

In a demonstration, Rockwell showed how the new Siri can handle complex tasks within a single interface. He displayed a sunset beach photo and asked Siri to identify the location. Once identified, he requested his friend's address, then had Siri provide directions to Santa Cruz, California with a stop at his friend's house, all completed without switching between multiple apps.

The assistant will initially launch in English only this fall. It will be capable of proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events, and using the camera to retrieve information like nutrition data by pointing at food. Apple is also integrating AI features into Safari, messaging, the Home app, camera tools, and photo editing capabilities, all developed in collaboration with Google.

The event marked CEO Tim Cook's final WWDC with the company. Cook, 65, is stepping down from his position in the fall after leading Apple for 15 years and transforming it into one of the world's most profitable publicly traded companies.

Apple executives acknowledged that the company had not invested as heavily or quickly in AI development compared to competitors. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, noted that some companies appear to be pursuing AI without clear regard for the people it ultimately serves.

The announcement comes as Apple faces regulatory pressure in the European Union. The company stated it is delaying Siri AI's release for iPhone users in the EU, citing regulators' unwillingness to engage with the company.

President Trump formally nominated Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche to the Senate on Monday, seeking to permanently install his former personal criminal defense attorney as head of the Department of Justice. Blanche has served in the acting role for two months following Trump's decision to fire former Attorney General Pam Bondi in April.

Trump had signaled the nomination earlier in the week during a podcast interview, describing Blanche as "a very talented guy." The position now moves to the Senate, where confirmation will require approval from the chamber.

Before his appointment as acting attorney general, Blanche held the position of deputy attorney general, the second-ranking official at the Justice Department. His background as Trump's personal criminal defense lawyer represents an unconventional path to leading the nation's top law enforcement agency.

The attorney general position carries significant authority over federal law enforcement, including oversight of criminal prosecutions, civil rights enforcement, and antitrust actions. The attorney general also supervises the FBI and other federal law enforcement agencies.

During his two months as acting attorney general, Blanche has overseen several controversial actions at the Justice Department. These include criminal charges against James Comey, the former FBI director, as part of an escalating investigation into ex-CIA director John Brennan. The department also removed press releases regarding prosecutions of individuals involved in the January 6 Capitol attack.

Blanche played a key role in developing a secretive 1.8 billion dollar fund designed to compensate Trump's allies. However, he abruptly announced the fund's cancellation on Tuesday following widespread public condemnation. Even Republican senators had pushed back against the proposed initiative.

The Senate will now review Blanche's qualifications and conduct confirmation hearings. Senators from both parties will have the opportunity to question him about his priorities for the department, his views on Justice Department independence, and his plans for managing the agency's broad responsibilities. The timeline for the confirmation process depends on Senate scheduling and the level of scrutiny the nomination receives.

Blanche's nomination marks the culmination of his alignment with Trump, having joined the president's legal team in 2023. Throughout his tenure as acting attorney general, he has demonstrated himself as what observers describe as a devoted Trump enforcer. His willingness to pursue the administration's priorities suggests he would continue carrying out the president's wishes if confirmed to the permanent position.

The Senate confirmation process will test whether lawmakers believe Blanche can maintain the independence traditionally expected of the attorney general role, or whether concerns about his loyalty to Trump will affect the vote.

The company even settled a 250 million dollar class-action lawsuit in May after consumers alleged that Apple had falsely advertised Siri's AI capabilities. Some affected customers received payouts of up to 95 dollars.

The new Siri will be powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a billion-dollar partnership between the two companies. According to Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice president of Siri engineering, the assistant has been fundamentally rebuilt with generative AI at its core. The tool will have its own dedicated app and will be more conversational, pulling from Apple's native app ecosystem on both mobile and laptop devices to help with daily planning, information retrieval, and navigation.

In a demonstration, Rockwell showed how the new Siri can handle complex tasks within a single interface. He displayed a sunset beach photo and asked Siri to identify the location. Once identified, he requested his friend's address, then had Siri provide directions to Santa Cruz, California with a stop at his friend's house, all completed without switching between multiple apps.

The assistant will initially launch in English only this fall. It will be capable of proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events, and using the camera to retrieve information like nutrition data by pointing at food. Apple is also integrating AI features into Safari, messaging, the Home app, camera tools, and photo editing capabilities, all developed in collaboration with Google.

The event marked CEO Tim Cook's final WWDC with the company. Cook, 65, is stepping down from his position in the fall after leading Apple for 15 years and transforming it into one of the world's most profitable publicly traded companies.

Apple executives acknowledged that the company had not invested as heavily or quickly in AI development compared to competitors. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, noted that some companies appear to be pursuing AI without clear regard for the people it ultimately serves.

The announcement comes as Apple faces regulatory pressure in the European Union. The company stated it is delaying Siri AI's release for iPhone users in the EU, citing regulators' unwillingness to engage with the company.
