Oil prices jumped and stock markets across Asia fell sharply after Iran fired missiles at Israel, threatening a fragile ceasefire in the Middle East. South Korea's Kospi index plunged 7 percent, while U.S. stock futures also declined in early trading.

Iran said the attacks mark the first strikes since an April ceasefire took effect. Oil prices rose more than $2 per barrel as traders anticipated potential disruptions to energy supplies from the region. The strikes raised immediate concerns about the stability of the ceasefire agreement and complicated ongoing mediation efforts.

The market reaction reflected investor concerns about escalating tensions in a region that remains central to global energy supplies. Asian markets bore the brunt of the initial selloff, with the steep decline in South Korea's benchmark index signaling broader anxiety about geopolitical risk. U.S. futures markets showed weakness as traders assessed the potential impact on American equities.

The oil price increase adds to existing pressure on global energy markets and could translate into higher costs for airlines and other fuel-dependent industries. The strikes also threaten a two-week ceasefire agreement between the U.S. and Iran that had included provisions for temporarily reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial shipping route where approximately 1,000 to 2,000 ships have been trapped since the conflict began.

Shipping analysts cautioned that despite the ceasefire agreement, the situation remains complicated. Richard Meade, editor-in-chief at Lloyd's List Intelligence, noted that Iran maintains control over the strait and ships still require permission to transit. "It still requires ships to essentially seek permission, and that's the key. That means that nothing has changed," Meade said.

The market turbulence comes as investors had been hoping for sustained stability in the Middle East. The renewed strikes represent a significant test of the ceasefire agreement and raise questions about whether energy markets will face prolonged volatility in the coming weeks.

Beyond the immediate market impact, the International Monetary Fund warned that the economic consequences of conflict extend far beyond the fighting itself. According to IMF research based on conflicts since 1946, the economic scars from war typically exceed those from financial crises or natural disasters, with recovery taking more than a decade. The fund noted that persistent political and economic uncertainty can continue to depress investment returns and constrain both investment and labor supply even after fighting stops.

For governments considering increased defense spending, the IMF highlighted difficult choices ahead. Nations must decide between higher borrowing, tax increases, or cuts to social protection, health and education programs. The fund also cautioned that boosting defense spending does not automatically translate into economic growth, particularly when countries must import most of their military equipment from abroad.

7:49 PM ET

A powerful magnitude 7.8 earthquake shook the southern Philippines early Monday, triggering tsunami warnings across several regional coastlines and causing significant damage to buildings and infrastructure. The tremor struck near General Santos City on the island of Mindanao, according to the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology.

The earthquake prompted authorities to issue warnings for waves potentially reaching up to three metres in some parts of the Philippines, with smaller waves possible along the coasts of Indonesia and Malaysia. The US tsunami warning system initially alerted coastal communities within 1,000 kilometers of the epicenter about possible hazardous tsunami waves, though the threat was later downgraded.

At least one person died in the aftermath of the quake. "Many buildings were affected, but I cannot enumerate them now because we are busy with ongoing rescues," Master Sergeant Robert Dagon of the General Santos City police told international media.

Tsunami waves were recorded at multiple locations across the region. Indonesia's meteorology agency documented waves at five sites, with the highest reaching 0.75 metres in North Minahasa in North Sulawesi province. Japan's meteorological agency noted that only slight sea level changes might occur along Japanese coasts, with no tsunami damage expected. The Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology and Australia's Bureau of Meteorology confirmed there was no tsunami threat to their respective territories.

The earthquake was initially recorded at magnitude 7.8 by the US Geological Survey. Regional monitoring agencies documented 11 aftershocks following the main quake, the largest measuring magnitude 5.5.

Authorities urged residents in coastal areas to remain vigilant and avoid returning to beaches until they received official confirmation that it was safe to do so. Initial assessments indicated minor to moderate damage to buildings in affected areas, though full damage assessments were still underway.

The southern Philippines sits along the Pacific Ring of Fire, an arc of volcanoes and fault lines where frequent seismic activity occurs. This region of the Molucca Sea has experienced significant earthquake activity historically. Over the past 50 years, nine other earthquakes with magnitudes exceeding seven have occurred within 250 kilometers of Monday's earthquake, though many caused limited damage due to their offshore locations.

Indonesia and the Philippines lie in one of the world's most seismically active zones. In 2004, a massive Indian Ocean earthquake triggered a tsunami that killed more than 230,000 people across multiple countries, with Indonesia's Aceh province suffering the heaviest toll. More recently, a 2018 earthquake and tsunami in Sulawesi killed over 4,300 people.

Residents in affected regions experienced strong shaking lasting 10 to 20 seconds, with many rushing outdoors as a precautionary measure. Recovery and damage assessment efforts continued in the hours following the earthquake.

The RTX Spark combines microprocessor and graphics processing capabilities and was develope...

3:39 PM ET

Nvidia has launched the RTX Spark, a new processor designed to bring artificial intelligence capabilities directly to laptops and desktop computers. The move marks the company's entry into the consumer PC chip market, putting it in direct competition with Intel, Qualcomm, AMD and Apple.

The RTX Spark combines microprocessor and graphics processing capabilities and was developed through a three-year collaboration between Nvidia and Microsoft. The chip is designed to run AI agents locally on devices rather than relying on cloud computing. According to Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, this technology will allow AI agents to navigate PCs autonomously, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interactions.

The chip incorporates design elements from Taiwan's MediaTek, with die analysis showing it features modified Cortex-X925 cores and attributes taken from MediaTek's Dimensity 9400 and Dimensity 9500 processors. These mobile-based designs have been adapted specifically for PC workloads while maintaining power efficiency. Nvidia has described the RTX Spark as "the most efficient PC chip ever built."

The RTX Spark will launch this year and will be used by major computer manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP, paired with Microsoft's Windows software. Microsoft has already showcased the Surface Laptop Ultra featuring the new processor. Despite the chip's power, Nvidia says computers using it will remain thin and light.

Huang described this development as reinventing the PC "for the first time in 40 years," positioning it as essential for the AI era. The company is also developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents and being adopted by early customers including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Analysts view the RTX Spark as potentially transformative. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the moment to the introduction of the iPhone, ChatGPT and DeepSeek, suggesting the chip could transform traditional app-focused computers into "agentic AI personal computers" that will become commonplace in homes.

The move represents Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in datacentre and graphics processing into consumer computing. While strategically significant, some analysts note this venture may take time to generate substantial earnings, as Nvidia's current financial performance remains heavily dependent on AI infrastructure and datacentre demand.

Nvidia's entry into the PC market comes as competitors intensify their AI chip efforts. Intel plans to ship its own AI chip later this year, featuring cheaper memory and cooling technology. The company has also announced the Xe3P graphics processing unit, designed for the emerging generation of AI agents.

When asked about concerns that AI will eliminate jobs, Huang dismissed the notion as "complete nonsense," arguing instead that the technology will increase hiring of software engineers by making workers more productive.

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family.

Head's daughters Emily and Daisy released a statement saying, "It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many."

Head played Giles, the librarian and Watcher mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's vampire slayer, throughout the seven-season run of Buffy from 1997 to 2003. The character became one of the most beloved in the series, providing guidance and fatherly support to Buffy and her friends while managing supernatural threats facing Sunnydale.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the BBC crime drama Maigret during the 1960s. His brother was also an actor known for his lead role in the 1971 film Sunday Bloody Sunday.

Head began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s, he took on various roles including parts in the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and the 1987 film A Prayer for the Dying alongside Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. He also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album.

Before achieving global fame with Buffy, Head became well known in the UK for starring in Nescafe's Gold Blend television advertisements. On stage, he took the leading role as Freddie Trumper in the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess in 1988 and performed in productions of A Patriot for Me and Lady Windermere's Fan. He also starred as Dr Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show.

While filming Buffy in Los Angeles, Head lived separately from his partner Sarah and their two daughters in England. He told The Guardian that the production team worked around his schedule, allowing him to return home every three or four weeks.

After Buffy ended in 2003, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain and appeared in the BBC production of Merlin and the film The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played former football club owner Rupert Mannion in the Apple TV+ comedy Ted Lasso, where he worked alongside co-creator Jason Sudeikis and earned praise for their on-screen chemistry.

Sarah Michelle Gellar mourned her former co-star on Instagram, opening with a quote from her Buffy character and continuing, "I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Head's daughters noted that his legacy will live on through the shows he was part of and the audiences who love them.
