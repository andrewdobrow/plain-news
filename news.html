However, Antonelli's superior pace allowed him to close the gap steadily. By lap seven, he was within three-tenths of a second, and on lap nine he swept past Hamilton to take the lead.

The Italian driver then extended his advantage ruthlessly, crossing the line with a two-and-a-half-second lead to secure his first Formula 1 sprint victory. Hamilton held on for second place in a hard-fought effort, despite being unable to match the speed of his teammate's Mercedes.

Lando Norris claimed third for McLaren after a frenetic battle involving George Russell and Max Verstappen. Russell, who started fifth, moved up to fourth after exploiting Mercedes' power advantage to pass Verstappen on lap ten. Norris had shown strong pace during the sprint, briefly taking Antonelli's position before the Italian reclaimed it on the same lap.

The result extends Antonelli's advantage in the championship standings, leading Russell by 43 points and Hamilton by 47 points. This marks another impressive performance from the second-year driver, who had been narrowly beaten to pole by Hamilton but demonstrated great determination over the sprint distance.

Hamilton will find some encouragement in Ferrari's competitive showing throughout the weekend. Despite the disappointment of losing the sprint to his teammate, the seven-time world champion fought hard to secure second place in front of his home crowd. His resilience and tactical driving earned him credit despite the power deficit that ultimately proved decisive.

The sprint format, which awards points to top finishers and determines part of the starting grid for Sunday's main race, has set up an intriguing dynamic within the Mercedes team. Both drivers will start near the front of the grid for the feature event, with the sprint performance confirming that Mercedes possesses strong pace at their home circuit.

Historical stock market data shows that the 34 years featuring a Republican president and a divided Congress produced an average annual return of 7.33% for the S&P 500, according to an analysis of nearly a century of market performance. A divided government occurs when one or both houses of Congress are controlled by Democrats while a Republican holds the White House.

While the data indicates more modest returns under this configuration compared to other political arrangements, the average annual return remains decisively positive. German startup Isar Aerospace becomes first private company to launch commercial rocket into orbit from Europe

Isar Aerospace successfully launched its Spectrum rocket into orbit Saturday from a spaceport in northern Norway, becoming the first fully commercial launch vehicle in Europe to reach orbit. The 92-foot-tall rocket lifted off at 4:12 pm EST from Andøya Spaceport inside the Arctic Circle and reached orbit seven minutes later, carrying five small satellites and a technology exper...

7:55 AM ET

Isar Aerospace successfully launched its Spectrum rocket into orbit Saturday from a spaceport in northern Norway, becoming the first fully commercial launch vehicle in Europe to reach orbit. The 92-foot-tall rocket lifted off at 4:12 pm EST from Andøya Spaceport inside the Arctic Circle and reached orbit seven minutes later, carrying five small satellites and a technology experiment.

Founded in 2018 by three students at the Technical University of Munich, Isar raised roughly $1 billion through private financing over eight years and now employs nearly 500 people. The company's CEO, Daniel Metzler, said the launch opens sovereign access to space for Europe and provides an alternative to relying on foreign launch providers. The success marks the largest German rocket in history and the first built predominantly in Germany to reach orbit.

The launch holds geopolitical significance as European governments seek strategic autonomy in space amid difficult relations with the United States. Europe currently must either pay a premium to launch satellites on European rockets or turn to SpaceX, which is politically unpalatable for some officials. The Spectrum rocket can place up to one metric ton of payload into low-Earth orbit, filling a gap between smaller launchers and Europe's heavier Vega-C rocket.

Saturday's flight followed a failed first test in March 2025, when the rocket spun out of control and crashed less than 20 seconds after liftoff due to a vent valve malfunction. The European Space Agency awarded Isar $230 million last month through its European Launcher Challenge program, which aims to foster commercial competition in Europe's launch market. Several other European startups are targeting orbital launch attempts by the end of the year, but Isar now leads the field after demonstrating its capability.

Study links Mars's red color to water-formed iron mineral

Ferrihydrite, which typically forms in cool liquid water, appears to dominate the planet's dust.

12:30 AM ET

A 2025 study published in Nature Communications identifies ferrihydrite as the likely dominant source of Mars's red color. The poorly crystalline iron mineral contains water in its structure and commonly forms rapidly in cool liquid water on Earth. Researchers matched laboratory spectra of ferrihydrite-basalt mixtures to observations from multiple Mars orbiters and rovers.

The findings suggest Mars acquired much of its characteristic color through chemical weathering while water still flowed across its ancient surface, not solely through dry oxidation after becoming a desert. Ferrihydrite appears stable under current Martian conditions, allowing material formed billions of years ago to persist and spread globally as dust.

Carnegie Mellon researchers demonstrate in-plane Hall effect, breaking 147-year assumption about magnetic field orientation

Scientists show the Hall effect can work with magnetic fields lying within a material's plane, not just perpendicular to it.

1:06 AM ET

Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University have demonstrated an in-plane version of the Hall effect, in which a magnetic field lying within a material's plane produces a measurable electrical response. The discovery, published in Nature Materials, challenges the conventional understanding of the phenomenon Edwin Hall identified in 1879, which required a perpendicular magnetic field to deflect moving charges and create voltage across a material.

The team layered tantalum iridium telluride with chromium germanium telluride to create an atomically thin device with the symmetry needed to produce the unconventional signal. The researchers said the approach could enable single sensors capable of detecting magnetic fields along multiple directions, with potential applications in electronics, transportation, and medical imaging.

Scientists estimate 5.5 million wild bees nesting at New York cemetery

Researchers documented one of the largest known aggregations of solitary mining bees using emergence traps across 1.5 acres.

8:00 AM ET

Scientists estimate between three million and eight million wild solitary mining bees are nesting underground at a New York cemetery, with 5.5 million as the midpoint, according to research published in the journal Apidologie. Researchers used mesh emergence traps across the 1.5-acre site during spring 2023, capturing 3,251 individual insects and scaling density measurements to calculate the population.

Lead author Steve Hoge and co-authors describe the aggregation as among the largest such concentrations documented in scientific literature. The traps, which ran from late March to mid-May, caught 16 species of bees, flies, and beetles, with mining bees dominating.
