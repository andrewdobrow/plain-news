Trump Faces Continued Political Challenges as Approval Ratings Decline

President Trump is scheduled for an appearance on NBC's Meet the Press as his administration confronts a series of political difficulties. The interview comes during a period marked by declining public approval and visible disagreements within Republican ranks.

Trump's approval rating has fallen to 36% according to Gallup, the polling organization that has tracked presidential popularity for nearly nine decades. This figure represents a significant drop from his second-term starting approval of 47% and places him among the lowest-rated presidents in the company's historical data.

The declining numbers reflect mounting economic pressures facing the administration as midterm elections approach. Disputes with members of his own party have become increasingly public, complicating efforts to advance the legislative agenda.

Trump has responded aggressively to unfavorable polling data. In January, after the New York Times released a poll showing his approval rating at 40%, down three percentage points since September 2025, the president threatened to expand an existing defamation lawsuit against the newspaper. In a post on Truth Social, Trump wrote that the Times Siena Poll "will be added to my lawsuit against The Failing New York Times," characterizing the survey as "always tremendously negative" to him.

The New York Times defended its polling methodology, stating that whether results favor or disfavor the president has no bearing on its research standards. Spokesperson Charlie Stadtlander said: "President Trump likes polls that appear favorable to him and dislikes polls that do not. But whether a poll is good or bad for the president has no bearing on our methodology."

The administration's challenges extend beyond polling disputes. Trump has used his pardon power to excuse financial crimes committed by Republicans, recently pardoning Stephen Buyer, a former Republican congressman from Indiana. Buyer served nearly two years in prison after being convicted of making illegal stock trades based on inside information while working as a consultant and lobbyist. He was sentenced to 22 months in prison in 2023 and ordered to forfeit more than $350,000 in illegal gains.

These actions come as the administration promotes what it characterizes as a crackdown on fraud in states governed by Democrats, creating a contrast between the stated anti-fraud messaging and the use of presidential pardon authority to benefit Republicans convicted of financial crimes.

The combination of declining approval ratings, party tensions, and controversial pardons reflects the political headwinds the administration faces. Trump's continued focus on polling data and media criticism, coupled with the broader legislative challenges ahead, suggests an administration under considerable pressure as it moves forward.

The severity of this week's downturn has drawn comparisons to the FTX crash, which triggered a broader crisis of confidence in cryptocurrency markets. That collapse in late 2022 sent shockwaves through the industry and led to a prolonged bear market for digital assets. The current sell-off has brought Bitcoin's price back near its 2026 low, erasing months of gains in a matter of days.

The parallel moves in both Bitcoin and Ether suggest broad-based selling pressure across the cryptocurrency market rather than issues specific to individual tokens. The synchronized decline across major digital assets indicates a shift in overall market sentiment, with investors pulling back from crypto positions after the post-election rally proved unsustainable.

While market analysts point to multiple potential factors contributing to the decline, specific catalysts for this week's downturn remain unclear. The magnitude of the weekly drop has raised questions about the sustainability of cryptocurrency valuations and the strength of institutional support for digital assets. Bitcoin had been trading significantly higher earlier in the year, making the retracement to the $60,000 level particularly notable for investors who entered positions at elevated prices.

The timing of the decline comes amid broader economic uncertainty. Recent geopolitical tensions, including conflict in the Middle East, have created headwinds across financial markets. Traditional equity markets have also experienced volatility, with technology stocks particularly hard hit. The Nasdaq fell 4 percent in a single day this week, marking its largest daily decline in over a year, while the S&P 500 and Dow Jones also declined sharply.

These macroeconomic pressures may have contributed to the crypto market's sharp reversal.

The current downturn serves as a reminder of cryptocurrency's volatility and susceptibility to shifts in market sentiment. The erosion of gains made since the election suggests that enthusiasm for digital assets among institutional and retail investors may be more fragile than the post-election rally had indicated.

OpenAI Delays Adult Mode for ChatGPT as Security and Performance Take Priority

OpenAI has postponed the launch of "adult mode" for ChatGPT, shifting focus toward what the company considers more urgent development priorities. The decision comes after Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive, had announced last year that the platform would permit adult content alongside age verification systems.

The company stated: "We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive." With over 900 million ChatGPT users, OpenAI believes these enhancements warrant immediate attention.

Since Altman declared a "code red" last October to enhance the chatbot's capabilities, the company has intensified efforts to compete with rivals including Google and Anthropic. This acceleration has redirected resources away from the adult content feature, despite OpenAI's stated belief in treating adults like adults.

Rather than launching adult mode, OpenAI is rolling out age prediction tools that identify users under 18. When younger users are detected, the system activates enhanced safety settings that limit exposure to graphic violence and sexual role-play content. In the UK, the company must comply with the Online Safety Act by using age verification to shield pornographic images from minors.

The postponement reflects broader strategic choices at OpenAI as it navigates competitive pressures and regulatory requirements. The company continues developing safety measures alongside performance improvements, recognizing that responsible deployment requires careful implementation.

In separate developments, OpenAI has faced internal challenges regarding its recent Pentagon partnership. Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over concerns about the defense contract. She expressed worry about mass surveillance of US citizens and autonomous AI-guided weapons systems operating without human oversight.

Kalinowski wrote that while AI plays an important role in national security, "surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got." She emphasized her concern centered on governance, stating the announcement was rushed without proper guardrails.

OpenAI acknowledged the Pentagon deal appeared "opportunistic and sloppy" and has committed to amending the contract to explicitly exclude domestic surveillance applications and autonomous weapons systems. An OpenAI spokesperson emphasized the agreement creates "a workable path for responsible national security uses of AI while making clear our red lines."

The company indicated it would continue engaging with employees, government agencies, civil society groups, and global communities regarding these significant policy questions. The resignation and subsequent contract modifications underscore the tensions between commercial growth, national security interests, and internal employee values at major AI companies.
