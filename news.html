A federal judge has ruled that the Trump administration unlawfully blocked applicants from 39 travel-ban countries from receiving decisions on asylum applications, work permits, green cards and citizenship applications.

The ruling came on Friday, the same day the US Senate voted to pass legislation funding Trump's immigration enforcement initiatives. The decision represents a significant legal challenge to the administration's immigration restrictions.

According to the judge's decision, the policies violated existing law by preventing individuals from these designated countries from advancing their immigration cases. The ruling requires federal officials to resume processing applications and making decisions on pending cases from the affected nations.

The decision addresses a backlog of applications that had accumulated while the restrictions were in place. Thousands of individuals from the targeted countries have been unable to move forward with their immigration proceedings, leaving many in legal limbo and separated from family members in the United States.

The 39 countries subject to the travel restrictions span multiple regions globally. Immigration advocates and civil rights groups challenged the policies in court, arguing that the administration exceeded its legal authority and caused significant harm to applicants seeking to immigrate or seek asylum.

The judge found that preventing applicants from receiving decisions on their cases was an unlawful application of immigration policy. The ruling takes effect immediately, requiring immigration officials to begin accepting and reviewing applications from individuals in the previously restricted countries.

This legal setback adds to a series of judicial challenges to Trump administration immigration policies. Federal courts have repeatedly examined the scope of executive power to restrict immigration based on national origin or security concerns. The ruling joins other recent decisions that have limited certain immigration enforcement actions.

Government attorneys must now decide whether to appeal the decision or comply with the order to resume processing. The administration retains the option to seek review from higher courts if officials choose to challenge the ruling.

The decision comes amid broader tensions over immigration enforcement. Recent controversies have included highly publicized confrontations between federal immigration agents and protesters in cities including Los Angeles and Minneapolis. A separate ruling this week also limited ICE arrests at immigration courthouses in Manhattan, with a federal judge finding that arrests at court locations undermined the integrity of immigration proceedings.

The Senate's passage of $70 billion in immigration enforcement funding on the same day as the judge's ruling demonstrates the competing pressures surrounding immigration policy. The legislation funds Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Department of Homeland Security for three years, ending a partial government shutdown that had persisted since February.

The funding bill passed with a 52-47 vote along party lines, with no Democratic support. The House still must vote on the legislation.

Major stock indexes declined Friday as investors reassessed monetary policy following employment data that surpassed expectations, increasing the likelihood of Federal Reserve interest rate increases. The Nasdaq experienced a steep drop of more than 2 percent, marking its worst performance in nearly eight months, while technology and semiconductor stocks led the broader sell-off. The S&P 500 ended a nine-week winning streak as traders recalculated the odds of Fed policy tightening.

The U.S. economy added 172,000 jobs in May, the third consecutive month in which employment growth exceeded forecasts. The unemployment rate remained steady at 4.3 percent as companies continued hiring across multiple sectors. Prior economists had predicted only around 80,000 new jobs, making the actual figures significantly stronger. Additionally, job figures for March and April were revised upward by a combined 93,000 positions. This robust labor market performance prompted traders to increase their bets that the Federal Reserve would raise interest rates to combat persistent inflation pressures.

Technology and semiconductor stocks drove much of the market decline as investors rotated away from growth-oriented shares that had led recent gains. The Dow Jones Industrial Average moved in the opposite direction of the broader market, rising 875 points to reach a record high as financial stocks rallied. Treasury yields and oil prices both declined during the trading session.

The market reaction reflected uncertainty about monetary policy direction under Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who recently took control of the central bank. Market participants had anticipated a period of stable rates, but the persistent strength in employment data has shifted expectations. Stock and bond prices both declined as investors adjusted their portfolios based on changing rate outlook expectations.

The divergence between the Dow's record performance and the Nasdaq's sharp decline highlighted a rotation among investors away from technology stocks toward financial and traditional sectors. This shift indicates markets are positioning for an environment where interest rate increases make growth stocks less attractive compared to value-oriented investments.

The market turbulence comes as Bank of England deputy governor Sarah Breeden warned that record-high global stock markets do not fully reflect economic risks. She expressed concerns about overvalued artificial intelligence stocks, stretched private credit valuations, and the potential for market corrections. Breeden noted that while risks are substantial, asset prices remain at record levels, suggesting "there will be an adjustment at some point."

As traders navigate this uncertain environment, they face continued volatility while weighing economic data against Federal Reserve policy signals in coming weeks. The combination of strong employment growth, inflation concerns, and warnings from financial stability officials suggests markets will remain sensitive to any new economic information or policy announcements from the Fed.

A few minutes ago

NASA ordered five astronauts aboard the International Space Station to shelter in their docked Dragon spacecraft and prepare for potential evacuation while Russian specialists worked to address air leaks discovered in the station's Russian segment.

The four crew members of NASA's SpaceX Crew-12 mission—American astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, French astronaut Sophie Adenot of the European Space Agency, and Russian cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev—were instructed to enter the spacecraft. A fifth American astronaut, Chris Williams, who had been on the station for 190 days, also prepared for evacuation alongside Russian cosmonauts Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergey Mikayev.

The air leaks were located in the transfer tunnel of the Zvezda Service Module, a 43-foot-long facility that provides living quarters, life support systems, communications equipment, electrical power distribution, and propulsion systems. Russian specialists detected two air leaks while pressurizing the transfer chamber. They quickly sealed one leak site using Germetal-1, a two-component sealant. The second potential leak site was located on the conical portion of the transfer chamber.

Roscosmos, the Russian space agency, stated there was no threat to crew safety or station systems. After approximately two hours of sheltering in their spacecraft, NASA instructed the crew to return to the space station and resume normal operations.

"Roscosmos has paused Friday's structural repair efforts inside the Zvezda service module transfer tunnel, known as PrK, as more measurements and data is assessed," NASA spokesperson Bethany Stevens said in a statement. "Given this development, NASA has instructed the crew members inside the Dragon spacecraft to end the safe haven procedures and return to planned operations aboard the International Space Station. We look forward to working with Roscosmos on a collaborative approach to address the leaks."

The incident reflects ongoing concerns about the Russian segment's structural integrity. Roscosmos has dealt with cracks and air leaks in its station compartments for more than five years, with NASA's Office of Inspector General identifying them as "a top safety risk." In June, a chartered spaceflight carrying astronauts from India, Poland, and Hungary was postponed due to leak concerns.

The safe haven protocol represents standard emergency procedure for the station. When potential risks arise, crew members can quickly board their assigned spacecraft if evacuation becomes necessary, though this situation did not escalate to that level.

The International Space Station, operated by NASA, the Canadian Space Agency, the European Space Agency, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, and Roscosmos, has maintained continuous human presence for 25 years. The orbital laboratory orbits approximately 250 miles above Earth and serves as a research facility where international crews conduct scientific experiments in microgravity.

Arnaldi's withdrawal proved particularly cruel given the difficulties he has faced this year. He won a Challenger event in Italy and squeezed into the French Open main draw as world number 104. By reaching the semifinals, he had spent 19 hours and 42 minutes on court across the tournament.

Following the announcement of Arnaldi's withdrawal, the two Davis Cup teammates held an unusual press conference on the interview podium, sitting far apart from one another. Arnaldi explained the severity of his condition, stating that he had vomited multiple times on Thursday night and experienced dizziness. Anthony Head, the British actor best known for portraying Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at 72. He passed away peacefully from complications related to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

Head achieved international recognition through his role as Giles, the mentor and Watcher to Sarah Michelle Gellar's Buffy Summers in the supernatural drama that ran from 1997 to 2003. His character provided guidance and fatherly wisdom to the teenage vampire slayer while bringing British sophistication to the show's blend of horror and coming-of-age storytelling.

Born in Camden, London, Head grew up in an artistic household. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, and his mother was an actress known for playing Madame Maigret in the 1960s BBC crime series Maigret. His brother was also an actor. This creative environment shaped Head's early passion for performance. He told the Guardian in 2016 that his mother, a skilled seamstress, would craft elaborate dressing-up outfits for him as Christmas gifts, so immersive that playgroup supervisors had to ask his parents to let him be himself occasionally.

Head launched his professional career on the stage, appearing in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell alongside Su Pollard. Throughout the 1980s, he took on diverse roles, including appearances in the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and the 1987 film A Prayer for the Dying with Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. He also provided backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album The Circle & the Square.

On the West End, Head starred as Freddie Trumper in the original 1988 production of the ABBA musical Chess and played Dr. Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 production of The Rocky Horror Show. He also appeared in productions of A Patriot for Me and Lady Windermere's Fan.

Beyond Buffy, Head built a diverse television career. He appeared in the BBC fantasy series Merlin and took a recurring role in the sketch comedy show Little Britain, where he played a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant. More recently, he joined Ted Lasso, the Apple TV+ comedy about an American football coach managing a British soccer team, where he played former football club owner Rupert Mannion.

His daughters emphasized that their father "loved his job very much and always considered himself incredibly lucky to have worked alongside such exceptionally talented people in wonderful productions across a career that spanned several decades."

Head is survived by his longtime partner Sarah and his two daughters.
