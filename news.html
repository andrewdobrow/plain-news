Chief executive Sam Altman had previously announced that OpenAI would permit adult content alongside age verification systems, but the rollout has now been shelved in favor of other improvements.

"We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive," OpenAI said in a statement. The company, which serves over 900 million ChatGPT users, acknowledged the delay while reaffirming its commitment to the broader principle. "We still believe in the principle of treating adults like adults, but getting the experience right will take more time."

The postponement comes as Altman has declared a "code red" to improve ChatGPT's capabilities, responding to intensifying competition from Google and Anthropic. Since Altman's original announcement last October about relaxing content restrictions, the competitive landscape has shifted dramatically, making feature development and performance enhancements the company's focus.

In the interim, OpenAI is rolling out age prediction tools designed to identify users under 18. When such users are detected, stricter safety settings activate, limiting their exposure to graphic violence and sexual roleplay content. The UK's Online Safety Act adds another layer of compliance pressure, requiring OpenAI to block pornographic images from minors through age verification measures.

The company is also facing internal dissent over its Pentagon partnership. Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned citing concerns about the military contract. She expressed worries about mass surveillance of American citizens and AI systems conducting autonomous killing without human authorization.

"This wasn't an easy call," Kalinowski wrote on social media. "AI has an important role in national security. But surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got." While praising Altman and the team, she emphasized that her primary concern was the rushed execution of the agreement rather than the partnership itself.

"To be clear, my issue is that the announcement was rushed without the guardrails defined. It's a governance concern first and foremost. These are too important for deals or announcements to be rushed," she added.

OpenAI announced it would amend its Pentagon contract to explicitly exclude domestic surveillance applications, following Altman's acknowledgment that the deal appeared "opportunistic and sloppy." An OpenAI spokesperson stated that the agreement creates "a workable path for responsible national security uses of AI" while maintaining clear boundaries against domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons development.
