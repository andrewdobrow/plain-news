A few minutes ago

The House narrowly advanced a budget reconciliation package on Tuesday that would provide nearly $70 billion in funding for Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Border Patrol operations. The chamber approved a procedural rule by a vote of 213 to 211, clearing the way for debate and a final vote on the Republican-backed measure.

The rule vote briefly appeared in jeopardy as Rep. Chip Roy of Texas, who serves as policy chair for House Republicans, and other members raised concerns. No Democrats voted in favor of the rule. Speaker Mike Johnson of Louisiana presided over the proceedings as the bill advanced through its procedural hurdles.

The reconciliation package represents one of the largest proposed investments in immigration enforcement operations. Budget reconciliation is a legislative process that allows certain spending and tax bills to pass the Senate with a simple majority, bypassing the typical 60-vote threshold required to overcome a filibuster. Republicans are using this tool to advance their immigration enforcement priorities.

The funding would flow to ICE and Border Patrol, the two main agencies responsible for interior enforcement and border security. The bill comes as congressional Republicans have made immigration enforcement a central legislative priority. The narrow margin on the procedural vote suggests the final passage vote could also be tight, with little room for Republican defections.

The measure now moves to floor debate, where amendments may be offered before a final vote. The House action represents the latest step in a broader Republican effort to reshape immigration policy through the budget reconciliation process.

President Trump announced that Iranian forces shot down a US Apache helicopter over the Strait of Hormuz and declared the United States "must, of necessity, respond to this attack." The two crew members aboard the aircraft were rescued by an American sea drone following the crash near the coast of Oman in the early hours of Tuesday.

The incident represents a significant escalation in tensions between the United States and Iran. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula that serves as a critical shipping route for global oil supplies. Military encounters in the strait have historically increased the risk of broader conflict across the region.

Trump did not specify what form retaliation would take or when it might occur. However, he told reporters at JFK airport that a peace deal with Iran is in its "final throes" and suggested the strait could reopen within "two or three days" if an agreement is reached. He stated that any deal would include a ban on Iranian nuclear weapons.

According to US Central Command, the Army AH-64 Apache helicopter went down near Omani waters, with rescue operations beginning at 7:33 p.m. EST Monday. The two crew members were recovered in approximately two hours and remain in stable condition. The rescue operation marked one of the first known instances of military personnel being recovered by an autonomous maritime drone system during active hostilities.

However, US Central Command stated that the cause of the helicopter crash remains under investigation, stopping short of confirming Trump's assertion that Iranian forces were responsible for shooting it down.

Iran's Foreign Minister Seyed Araghchi responded to the incident with a warning statement, saying "foreign forces in proximity to our territory are at constant risk on account of their own human errors, plain accidents, or potentially being caught in crossfire." He added that "to reduce risk, best solution is for them to leave" while noting that Iran prefers "language of diplomacy but speak other languages too."

Earlier, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator, posted on social media: "We prefer the language of diplomacy, but we speak other languages far more fluently. Break your commitments, and we'll switch to what we speak best."

The Apache helicopter is a twin-engine attack aircraft used by the US military for ground support and reconnaissance operations. The downing of the helicopter and Trump's vow of retaliation signal potential expansion of military activities in the Persian Gulf region.

The incident comes amid broader tensions in the Middle East, with UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres expressing deep alarm over renewed escalation in violence across the region.

Social Security Trust Fund Projected to Run Dry by 2032

A few minutes ago

The trust fund that supports Social Security retirement benefits is projected to be depleted by 2032, according to a new report from the program's trustees. Without congressional action, the roughly 68 million Americans who rely on Social Security could see their benefits cut by an average of 22 percent once the fund runs out.

The accelerated timeline represents a worsening outlook for the program, which faces financial strain as the ratio of workers paying into the system declines relative to retirees drawing benefits. The trust fund's reserves have been gradually depleting as Social Security pays out more in benefits than it collects in payroll taxes. Once the fund is exhausted, the program would only be able to pay benefits from incoming tax revenue, which would cover approximately 78 percent of scheduled payments.

Social Security provides retirement income to tens of millions of Americans, many of whom depend on it as their primary or sole source of support in old age. The program is funded through payroll taxes paid by current workers, with those revenues used to pay benefits to current retirees. For decades, Social Security collected more in taxes than it paid out in benefits, building up a reserve in the trust fund. That surplus is now being drawn down as the large baby boomer generation moves into retirement while birth rates have declined, reducing the number of workers supporting each beneficiary.

Congress has several options to address the shortfall, including raising payroll taxes, reducing benefits, increasing the retirement age, or some combination of changes. Each approach faces political challenges, as tax increases and benefit cuts are broadly unpopular. Lawmakers have been aware of Social Security's long-term funding challenges for years, but have not passed legislation to address them.

The 2032 projection gives Congress six years to act before automatic benefit cuts would take effect. Previous trustee reports had projected slightly later depletion dates, but the timeline has moved up as economic and demographic trends have shifted. Without intervention, millions of retirees and disabled Americans who depend on Social Security payments for housing, food, and medical care would face significant reductions in their monthly checks.

NASA names astronaut crew for Artemis III mission, targets 2027 launch

The crew consists of Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, and Andre Douglas from NASA, along with Luca Parmitano from the European Space Agency....

A few minutes ago

NASA announced the four-person crew selected for the Artemis III mission during an event at Johnson Space Center in Houston on Tuesday. The announcement included details about the next phase of the agency's effort to return humans to the Moon.

The crew consists of Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, and Andre Douglas from NASA, along with Luca Parmitano from the European Space Agency. Bresnik will serve as mission commander. The all-male crew is scheduled to launch in 2027 for a two-week test flight in lower Earth orbit.

Unlike previous Artemis missions, the Artemis III crew will not approach or land on the Moon. Instead, they will remain in Earth orbit while testing critical systems and procedures. The mission will focus on docking the Orion capsule with two lunar landers being developed by private companies Blue Origin and SpaceX. These tests will validate technology needed for the Artemis IV mission, which is scheduled for 2028 and will include the first crewed lunar landing since 1972.

Bresnik is a Marine colonel and former commander of the International Space Station with more than 7,000 hours of flying experience. Rubio, an Army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the record for the longest single spaceflight duration by a NASA astronaut at 371 days. Parmitano is a fighter pilot in the Italian Air Force who previously served on the International Space Station. Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer, is the only crew member without previous spaceflight experience.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman framed the Artemis program as the creation of "Earth's first starfleet," describing a future of regular crewed spaceflight beyond low Earth orbit. He highlighted the complexity of the missions ahead, noting that multiple spacecraft from different nations and companies will operate simultaneously in orbit.

Isaacman acknowledged challenges facing the program, particularly Blue Origin's recent launch pad explosion in Florida that destroyed the New Glenn rocket in May. However, he expressed confidence that the company and SpaceX would be ready to support the 2027 timeline. Jeremy Parsons, NASA's acting deputy associate administrator for the Moon to Mars program, stated that the agency is working closely with all contractors to overcome current obstacles and meet its commitments.

The Artemis program builds on lessons from the Apollo era while incorporating modern technologies designed for extended spaceflight. The progression of missions from Artemis III through Artemis IV represents an acceleration of previous plans and reflects NASA's confidence in the development progress of its spacecraft and crew training programs.

Bob Hines will serve as the support crew member for the mission.

The 44-year-old former champion emphasized that her return is motivated by enjoyment rather than competitive achievement. "I don't need to win. I've won more than most people have in their whole lives, so for me it's not important," Williams said. She stressed that the experience is about sharing her love of tennis with her children, particularly her eight-year-old daughter Olympia and two-year-old daughter Adira.

Williams selected Mboko, currently ranked ninth in singles, as her doubles partner after being impressed by the teenager's performance at the Canadian Open. "She reminded me a lot of myself," Williams explained, noting that she appreciated Mboko's ability to maintain momentum after victories. For Mboko, the partnership holds special significance. The 19-year-old was only 10 years old when Williams won her final Grand Slam title. "Growing up, Serena has always been my idol," Mboko said. "Seeing someone on TV versus actually talking to them in person, of course, it's very different. I think she's really nice and very personable."

When asked about returning to singles competition, Williams remained noncommittal. "I can't say yeah, I can't say no. Right now, no," she responded. She noted that if she did pursue singles play, she would need additional training. Williams has committed to playing doubles in Berlin following her Queen's appearance, though her plans beyond that remain uncertain.

The comeback has generated widespread interest across professional tennis. Emma Raducanu, the British number one who faced Williams in her final competitive match in 2022, praised her return. "It's really great to see her back. I think it's an amazing inspiration," Raducanu said, describing Williams as "the greatest female tennis player of all time."

Williams emphasized that she has nothing to prove at this stage of her career. "I don't have anything to lose and everything here is just a gain," she said. "This whole journey is like I'm putting no pressure on myself." She also highlighted the timing of her return, noting that summer provided the ideal opportunity for her children to experience her competing at the professional level while they were not in school.

The victory against third-seeded opponents Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe demonstrated that Williams' competitive abilities remain sharp despite her extended absence. Her return adds significant star power to Queen's Club and raises questions about the potential scope of her comeback as the grass-court season progresses.

On Thursday, CBS News editor-in-chief Bari Weiss and president Tom Cibrowski removed the show's executive producer and executive editor, along with correspondents Sharyn Alfonsi and Cecilia Vega. Both correspondents released statements claiming the show's editorial independence had been compromised for political reasons.

The dismissals triggered immediate pushback from the journalism community. More than 50 CBS News veterans, including many former 60 Minutes employees, signed a letter to Ellison urging him to publicly commit to the program's editorial independence. The letter emphasized that the show's success came from operating under "an implicit and sacred obligation to the public."

"Institutional trust is not transferred through ownership," the signatories wrote. "60 Minutes prospered and had impact because it operated under this principle. Modernizing the show for new audiences is important, but not at the cost of editorial integrity."

The letter's organizers included legendary producer Lowell Bergman, who created the show's influential tobacco industry investigation in the 1990s. Other signers included former CBS Evening News anchor Dan Rather, documentary director Alex Gibney, and actor Glenn Close.

Veteran correspondent Scott Pelley, who was not affected by the cuts, also strongly criticized the network's actions. According to sources, Pelley accused Weiss of "murdering" the show and suggested she was brought in to destroy it rather than improve it.

The signatories encouraged Ellison to "send a clear message to your staff, your viewers, and the broader public that you respect and value editorial independence and press freedom."

The turmoil at 60 Minutes reflects ongoing tensions between journalistic integrity and corporate interests at major news divisions. The program has been a CBS fixture since 1968 and maintains significant cultural influence in American journalism. Ellison took over as CEO following Paramount's recent corporate restructuring.

The letter signers warned that without a public pledge to maintain the show's values and standards, "the legacy of 60 Minutes is in jeopardy." Ellison's commitment to editorial independence, conveyed through his conversation with Stahl, represents an attempt to stabilize the program and address concerns raised by the journalism community about whether the show can continue operating free from corporate interference.
