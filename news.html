The US military struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday in retaliation for attacks on American warships, escalating a six-month conflict that has disrupted global energy supplies. US Central Command said it permanently disabled two tankers, including one near Kharg Island, and completely destroyed a third in the Gulf of Oman. The attacks came after Centcom said it evaded multiple attacks from Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps earlier in the day targeting a US aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer. No American troops were harmed.

Iran's state media confirmed the strikes and said Tehran launched retaliatory attacks on six vessels in the region, including three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz traveling what Iran called an unauthorized route. Iranian state outlet Irib reported no casualties in the attack near Kharg Island, which hosts Iran's main oil export terminal and handles roughly 90 percent of the country's crude oil. Crews from the two tankers struck in the Gulf of Oman reached shore in lifeboats, according to Irib.

Centcom claimed the three Iranian tankers were part of a multi-billion-dollar shadow network funding the IRGC and its regional proxies. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth wrote on social media hours before Iran's retaliation that if Iran shoots at US ships, the military will destroy their oil tankers. The strikes marked the latest escalation in fighting that resumed last week after a period of relative calm.

The conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz since fighting began in February, squeezing a waterway that previously carried about 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas supplies. The disruption has raised energy prices globally, with the average US diesel price reaching $5.85 per gallon on Friday compared to $3.71 a year earlier. A 60-day ceasefire between the US and Iran expired last month with little sign of a diplomatic settlement ahead of US midterm elections in November.

The 40-year-old added the vacant WBC belt to her WBA, WBO, and IBF light-welterweight titles, winning by scores of 110-90, 110-90, and 98-91.

Taylor, an Olympic champion at London 2012 and a professional since 2016, dominated the overmatched Frenchwoman with the speed and pressure fighting that marked her career. Pili, 29, arrived unbeaten in 12 fights but had never faced an opponent of Taylor's caliber. The crowd represented the sixth-largest post-war attendance for a boxing event anywhere in the world, with approximately 80 percent of attendees never having previously watched a live boxing match.

The event doubled as a celebration of Taylor's legacy as a pioneer of women's professional boxing. Chart-topping group Westlife performed before the fight, and fireworks lit the Dublin sky as Taylor walked to the ring. Taylor retires with a professional record of 26 wins and one defeat, having previously been a two-weight world champion.

After the final bell, Taylor embraced her mother, trainer, and manager in the ring as the crowd sang along to the song "Celebration." Speaking afterward, she asked for a moment to take in the reception from the packed stadium.

US diesel prices reach all-time high as Iran conflict disrupts fuel supply

Diesel prices across the United States have climbed to unprecedented levels, exceeding the previous record reached in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The price surge reflects broader increases in wholesale oil markets sin...

12:47 PM ET

Diesel prices across the United States have climbed to unprecedented levels, exceeding the previous record reached in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The national average for diesel now stands at $5.85 per gallon, driven by supply disruptions stemming from the conflict that began in late February.

The price surge reflects broader increases in wholesale oil markets since the Iran conflict started. The war has interrupted global fuel supply chains, particularly affecting petroleum shipments from the Middle East. The combination of Iran's significance to global energy markets and ongoing constraints from the Ukraine situation has created mounting pressure on diesel availability worldwide.

The economic ripple effects extend far beyond gas stations. Diesel fuels the majority of commercial trucks, freight trains, farm equipment, and construction machinery throughout the country. Transportation and logistics companies facing higher fuel costs typically transfer these expenses to consumers through increased prices for goods and services.

Farmers have been hit especially hard. Diesel is essential for operating tractors, harvesters, and irrigation systems, making fuel cost increases directly threaten agricultural profitability. Both American and British farmers have reported experiencing what observers describe as astronomical fuel price increases, caught between dual pressures from conflicts in the Middle East and Eastern Europe.

The price increases add to inflationary concerns throughout the broader economy. As policymakers consider interest rate decisions, the fuel cost surge affects production and distribution across multiple sectors. Food production, retail delivery, and countless other industries depend on transportation, meaning diesel prices significantly influence the final costs consumers pay for everyday goods.

The global oil market dynamics underscore the far-reaching consequences of regional conflicts. Supply disruptions from the strait of Hormuz have removed substantial quantities of oil from international markets, creating cascading price effects in fuel markets worldwide. While some disruptions have been partially offset through alternative routes and emergency reserves, the underlying supply constraints continue to pressure prices upward.

The situation highlights how regional instability can quickly translate into economic consequences for households and businesses thousands of miles away. For consumers, the diesel spike represents higher costs for transported goods and services. For industries dependent on fuel-intensive operations, it threatens margins and competitiveness. For farmers managing tight profit margins, surging diesel costs can determine whether operations remain viable.

As the Iran conflict continues, market participants are closely monitoring developments that could either ease or intensify the current supply crisis. The resolution of regional tensions remains a crucial factor in determining whether diesel prices will stabilize at current elevated levels or climb further, with significant implications for inflation and economic growth in the months ahead.

11:17 AM ET

Tesla's Cybercab, a two-seat vehicle without a steering wheel or brake pedals, started picking up members of the public in Texas and Florida on Saturday as the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation into whether the vehicle meets federal safety standards. The probe began hours after Tesla welcomed hundreds of riders to downtown Austin for trips in the driverless Cybercabs, which the automaker is deploying on its robotaxi network operating in several cities across the two states.

The investigation highlights a regulatory gap as NHTSA works to update vehicle standards for autonomous cars. The agency is revising eight rules, including requirements for brake pedals, windshield wipers, and rearview mirrors that driverless vehicles do not use. Those updates remain incomplete, leaving decades-old standards in place.

Other companies operating unconventionally designed robotaxis have been required to apply for exemptions from federal rules before carrying passengers. Amazon subsidiary Zoox, whose vehicles also lack steering wheels and manual controls, received the first such exemption this summer, limiting its operations to 2,500 vehicles this year.

Tesla has stated it does not need an exemption because the Cybercab complies with existing safety standards. The company is now operating its robotaxi service publicly while federal regulators examine that claim.

Robert Pattinson's portrayal of "To Catch a Predator" host Chris Hansen in the thriller "Primetime" earned a seven-minute standing ovation at its Saturday premiere at the Venice Film Festival. The film, directed by Lance Oppenheim in his feature debut, follows the production of the "Dateline" series in which Hansen confronted child sexual predators in sting operations. Pattinson's partner Suki Waterhouse kissed him during the ovation as the audience cheered the performance.

The real Chris Hansen has publicly criticized the film, saying he was not involved in the creative process and has not seen the movie. Hansen called Pattinson's portrayal "overly dramatic" and questioned the studio's motives in dramatizing the reality series. He stressed that the film is not a literal account of his career and that he was not consulted during production. "He's saying things I never said and doing things I never did, and that's not who I am," Hansen said, adding that people should remember the film is fiction only based on real events.

US military strikes three Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for attacks on American warships

The US military struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday in retaliation for attacks on American warships, escalating a six-month conflict that has disrupted global energy supplies. US Central Command said it permanen...

6:00 PM ET

The US military struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday in retaliation for attacks on American warships, escalating a six-month conflict that has disrupted global energy supplies. US Central Command said it permanently disabled two tankers, including one near Kharg Island, and completely destroyed a third in the Gulf of Oman. The attacks came after Centcom said it evaded multiple attacks from Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps earlier in the day targeting a US aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer. No American troops were harmed.

Iran's state media confirmed the strikes and said Tehran launched retaliatory attacks on six vessels in the region, including three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz traveling what Iran called an unauthorized route. Iranian state outlet Irib reported no casualties in the attack near Kharg Island, which hosts Iran's main oil export terminal and handles roughly 90 percent of the country's crude oil. Crews from the two tankers struck in the Gulf of Oman reached shore in lifeboats, according to Irib.

Centcom claimed the three Iranian tankers were part of a multi-billion-dollar shadow network funding the IRGC and its regional proxies. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth wrote on social media hours before Iran's retaliation that if Iran shoots at US ships, the military will destroy their oil tankers. The strikes marked the latest escalation in fighting that resumed last week after a period of relative calm.

The conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz since fighting began in February, squeezing a waterway that previously carried about 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas supplies. The disruption has raised energy prices globally, with the average US diesel price reaching $5.85 per gallon on Friday compared to $3.71 a year earlier. A 60-day ceasefire between the US and Iran expired last month with little sign of a diplomatic settlement ahead of US midterm elections in November.

Katie Taylor retires as undisputed champion after final fight at Croke Park

Katie Taylor ended her boxing career as a three-time undisputed world champion Saturday night, defeating unbeaten Flora Pili by unanimous decision before 80,000 fans at Croke Park in Dublin. The 40-year-old added the vac...

6:00 PM ET

Katie Taylor ended her boxing career as a three-time undisputed world champion Saturday night, defeating unbeaten Flora Pili by unanimous decision before 80,000 fans at Croke Park in Dublin. The 40-year-old added the vacant WBC belt to her WBA, WBO, and IBF light-welterweight titles, winning by scores of 110-90, 110-90, and 98-91.

Taylor, an Olympic champion at London 2012 and a professional since 2016, dominated the overmatched Frenchwoman with the speed and pressure fighting that marked her career. Pili, 29, arrived unbeaten in 12 fights but had never faced an opponent of Taylor's caliber. The crowd represented the sixth-largest post-war attendance for a boxing event anywhere in the world, with approximately 80 percent of attendees never having previously watched a live boxing match.

The event doubled as a celebration of Taylor's legacy as a pioneer of women's professional boxing. Chart-topping group Westlife performed before the fight, and fireworks lit the Dublin sky as Taylor walked to the ring. Taylor retires with a professional record of 26 wins and one defeat, having previously been a two-weight world champion.

After the final bell, Taylor embraced her mother, trainer, and manager in the ring as the crowd sang along to the song "Celebration." Speaking afterward, she asked for a moment to take in the reception from the packed stadium.
