Yesterday, 6:55 PM ET

Microsoft's artificial intelligence leadership announced the company is pursuing an independent path in AI development, signaling a major shift away from its previous reliance on OpenAI. The statement came during Microsoft's Build 2026 developer conference, where the company also revealed new AI models aimed at reducing dependence on OpenAI and cutting costs for developers.

This marks a significant turning point in the partnership between the two companies. Microsoft had previously invested billions into OpenAI and woven its technology throughout products including Bing and Office applications. The pivot indicates Microsoft is now building proprietary AI systems rather than primarily relying on OpenAI's offerings.

At Build 2026, Microsoft showcased new AI capabilities and developer tools for Windows applications. The company emphasized its vision for Windows as a dependable platform for development work, while also announcing improvements to workplace technology and productivity features.

The shift reflects Microsoft's broader strategy to gain greater control over its AI infrastructure and decrease reliance on external technology partners. By developing its own models, Microsoft expects to reduce operational expenses while gaining more flexibility in how it deploys artificial intelligence across its product lineup. The approach also gives the company direct control over its AI research and development direction.

The changing relationship between Microsoft and OpenAI comes against the backdrop of significant legal developments. A jury recently ruled in favor of OpenAI's leadership in a high-profile lawsuit brought by Elon Musk. The jury found Sam Altman, OpenAI, its president Greg Brockman, and Microsoft not liable for Musk's claims of unjust enrichment and breach of contract. The case centered on OpenAI's transition from a nonprofit to a for-profit structure. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed with the jury's decision and dismissed Musk's claims, noting substantial evidence supported their findings. The verdict was delivered after less than two hours of deliberation.

The jury determined that Musk's 2024 lawsuit fell outside the statute of limitations, as OpenAI argued that Musk was aware of plans for a for-profit structure as early as 2017. William Savitt, OpenAI's lead attorney, characterized the case as a "hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor." Musk stated he would appeal the verdict.

The evolution between Microsoft and OpenAI reflects a broader industry trend where major technology companies increasingly view AI capabilities as fundamental strategic assets. Rather than outsourcing these critical functions, firms are building internal expertise and infrastructure. For Microsoft, this represents a calculated move to establish independence in what many consider the defining technology of the coming decade. The company's shift toward proprietary development provides greater autonomy over its AI future while reducing its vulnerability to external partners.

The United States military struck Iranian drone and radar positions after Iran launched attacks targeting American forces in Kuwait and Bahrain, marking the latest escalation in a confrontation that threatens a fragile regional ceasefire.

US Central Command reported intercepting Iranian drones near the Strait of Hormuz, shooting down at least four unmanned aircraft. American forces then conducted strikes against Iranian radar installations and drone launch sites. Tehran responded by firing missiles and drones at US military bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, significantly widening the scope of the conflict.

The Iranian attacks extended beyond military installations to civilian and energy infrastructure across the Gulf. Qatar's defense ministry reported that drones struck energy facilities in the industrial city of Ras Laffan, prompting Doha to shut down its liquefied natural gas production. Oman reported an attack on an oil tanker off Muscat. The UAE, which withdrew its ambassador from Tehran in protest, reported detecting 174 ballistic missiles, 689 drones, and eight cruise missiles. The country estimates the cost of intercepting these weapons at approximately two billion dollars, since each interception costs five times more than launching the weapons.

According to Ali Larijani, secretary of Iran's supreme national security council, Tehran justified its actions as targeting US military bases. However, Arab leaders expressed alarm at the scale and scope of the attacks, which they characterized as disproportionate and reckless.

The strikes have shifted sentiment among Gulf states regarding Iran. Qatar's former prime minister Hamad bin Jassim bin Jaber al-Thani warned that Iran had "lost through this action the Gulf sympathy that was pushing with every possible effort towards de-escalation." Many Gulf nations had invested significant diplomatic effort in maintaining neutral relations with Iran while hosting American military bases.

Eight Gulf states and Iraq issued a joint statement warning that "targeting civilians and countries not engaged in hostilities is reckless and destabilising." The attacks even appeared to suspend tensions between rival Gulf monarchies Saudi Arabia and the UAE, which had previously divided over conflicts in Sudan and Yemen.

Some Arab leaders expressed frustration with US priorities. A Saudi official complained that "the United States abandoned the Gulf states and redirected its air defence to protect Israel," leaving Gulf nations hosting American bases vulnerable to Iranian strikes.

The escalation comes as ongoing tensions in Lebanon persist despite ceasefire efforts. Iranian officials have warned of a wider war if attacks continue. The exchange represents one of the most serious direct military confrontations between US and Iranian forces in recent months, raising serious questions about whether the fragile regional ceasefire can survive further escalation.

The New York Knicks continued their remarkable playoff run by defeating the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 to take a commanding 2-0 series lead in the NBA Finals. The Knicks are now just two wins away from claiming their first championship since 1973.

Playing on the road in San Antonio, the Knicks showed resilience and clutch execution down the stretch. Jalen Brunson, the All-NBA guard who has been instrumental throughout the playoffs, sank the go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining after Spurs star Victor Wembanyama committed a costly turnover. Wembanyama's final attempt from the elbow at the buzzer bounced off the back rim, sealing the victory for New York.

This marks the second consecutive road win for the Knicks in the Finals. In Game 1, they defeated the Spurs 105-95 after mounting a fourth-quarter comeback from a 14-point deficit. Brunson finished that game with 30 points, including 13 in the final quarter. OG Anunoby contributed 12 of his 17 points in the fourth quarter of Game 1 as well.

The Knicks became only the third team to win the first two games of an NBA Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets. The team's hot streak entering the Finals included 11 consecutive playoff victories before Game 1 against San Antonio.

The Spurs, led by Wembanyama, have faced an uphill battle in this series. In Game 1, Wembanyama shot just 6-of-21 from the field while recording 26 points, 12 rebounds and three blocks. After Game 2, he acknowledged his performance issues, saying "I was bad tonight, it's not more complicated than that." Wembanyama denied that the occasion affected the Spurs, stating that nerves were not a factor in their performance.

San Antonio came into these Finals after eliminating the defending champion Oklahoma City Thunder in the previous round. However, the team struggled with execution in crucial moments, particularly in the fourth quarter of both games. In Game 1, the Spurs committed five turnovers in the fourth quarter while shooting just 28.6 percent.

The Knicks return home for the next games at Madison Square Garden with momentum on their side. The franchise and its fans have waited over 50 years for another championship. Meanwhile, the Spurs face a steep challenge to recover from their 2-0 deficit, needing to win four consecutive games to claim the title.

The series resumes Monday night in New York, where the Knicks will have strong home court support as they look to close out the Finals.

Jalen Brunson delivered the decisive moment when it mattered most, sinking a go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining after Victor Wembanyama committed a costly turnover. Moments later, Wembanyama's clean look from the elbow at the buzzer rimmed out, sealing the heartbreaking loss for San Antonio.

The win extended the Knicks' postseason winning streak to 13 consecutive games, matching one of the longest single-season playoff winning streaks in NBA history. New York also became only the third team to win the first two games of an NBA Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets.

Brunson finished with 20 points on 7-for-25 shooting despite battling a sore knee and ankle. He provided the composure the Knicks needed in crucial moments, much as he did in Game 1's furious fourth-quarter comeback. Karl-Anthony Towns led the team with 21 points while adding 13 rebounds.

Wembanyama responded strongly after a frustrating Finals debut, scoring 29 points on 11-for-21 shooting in 40 minutes. However, his late-game mistakes ultimately defined the outcome. His wayward pass off the back of teammate Stephon Castle while attempting to initiate the final possession proved pivotal, as did his missed jumper at the buzzer.

The Spurs mounted a remarkable comeback from a 14-point deficit midway through the fourth quarter. San Antonio engineered a 14-0 run behind Wembanyama and De'Aaron Fox to tie the game at 97-97 with three minutes remaining, then took their first lead of the half with less than a minute to play.

Brunson quickly answered with a driving basket to tie the game at 104-104. After Wembanyama came up empty on the Spurs' next possession, the Knicks called timeout. San Antonio forced a miss but Wembanyama threw the ball away on the ensuing possession, allowing Brunson to draw a foul and convert the winning free throw.

"Of course I liked the shot," Wembanyama said afterward. "In moments like this, results matter more than process. We just needed to score. I just needed to score. That's the whole point."

The series now shifts to New York for Games 3 and 4. San Antonio faces an uphill battle, as no team in NBA history has won the championship after losing the first two games at home. Game 3 is scheduled for Monday night at Madison Square Garden.

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications related to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, his daughters Emily and Daisy announced.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said in a statement.

Head's portrayal of the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character defined much of his career and introduced him to international audiences during the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Beyond Buffy, Head's career was remarkably diverse. He appeared in the BBC sketch comedy show Little Britain, playing a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant. He also took on the role of Uther Pendragon in the BBC's Merlin and more recently played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in Ted Lasso, where he received praise for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Head was born in Camden, London, to artistic parents. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actor who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor, known for his lead role in 1971's Sunday Bloody Sunday.

His professional career began on stage, with a role in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell alongside Su Pollard. Throughout the 1980s, he appeared in various productions and films, including the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and 1987's A Prayer for the Dying with Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. He sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and was featured on their 1986 debut album. Head also starred as Freddie Trumper in the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess in 1988 and played Dr Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show.

During Buffy's production, Head moved to Los Angeles for five years while his family remained in England. He told the Guardian that he would travel home every three or four weeks whenever the production schedule allowed, saying the experience taught him not to get caught up in celebrity competition.

His daughters said that Head loved his work and considered himself fortunate to have collaborated with exceptionally talented people throughout his decades-spanning career. "Our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of."

Justice Department ends Trump anti-weaponization compensation fund

The compensation fund was designed to provide payments to individuals who claimed th...

Yesterday, 9:57 PM ET

The Trump administration's nearly $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund will not continue, the Justice Department informed a federal court on Friday. Two DOJ attorneys filed the notification in the Eastern District of Virginia while asking the court to reject a legal challenge to the program.

The compensation fund was designed to provide payments to individuals who claimed they were targeted by federal investigations for political reasons. The program was announced last month as part of a settlement in the president's lawsuit against the IRS over the leak of his tax returns.

The fund drew immediate and intense scrutiny. A federal judge in Virginia temporarily blocked the administration from transferring money after Democracy Forward filed a lawsuit to dissolve it. Multiple Republican senators expressed concern about the program's lack of oversight and potential payouts to participants in the January 6 Capitol riot.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune told reporters that "the best way to handle it is if the administration decides to shut the fund down themselves." Mike Pence, Trump's former vice president, called the possibility of compensating rioters "deeply offensive" and said the view was widely shared among Republicans and Americans.

Senate Democrats vowed to block the fund through legislative action. Minority Leader Chuck Schumer described it as "corruption in broad daylight" and said Democrats would force Republicans to vote on a measure preventing payments. He pledged that Democrats would pursue the issue through multiple avenues, including amendments during budget negotiations and Senate floor votes.

The fund faced legal challenges over its structure. Critics questioned whether it had proper congressional authorization and whether it violated established procedures for federal compensation programs. The terms of the fund did not require disclosing how much was paid to whom, raising concerns about transparency and accountability.

Several state governments also mobilized against the program. California Governor Gavin Newsom proposed a 100 percent income tax on any fund distribution to a California resident. Similar proposals emerged from Illinois, New York, and Connecticut.

The Justice Department's decision to discontinue the fund came less than two weeks after its announcement, marking a significant reversal for what the administration had promoted as a remedy for what it characterized as politically motivated prosecutions during previous administrations.

Questions remain about payments already distributed and whether recipients will be required to return money. The DOJ has not publicly detailed how it will handle existing claims or what criteria were used to determine eligibility before the program ended.

The fund's termination follows mounting political pressure from both Democrats and prominent Republicans who questioned its legality and ethics.
