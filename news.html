Gas and electricity bills in the United Kingdom are forecast to jump by £443 per year starting in January as energy costs continue to push up the country's inflation rate. The annual price cap on gas and electricity is predicted to rise by 26% in the new year, according to researchers at consultancy Cornwall Insight. Energy regulator Ofgem will announce the new cap on November 25.

The sharp increase in energy costs means inflation is at risk of continuing to rise. Official data revealed prices rose 3.6% in the year to October, up from 3.5% in September, mainly because of higher petrol and diesel prices. Inflation is at its highest rate in a year as energy prices have accelerated largely due to the U.S.-Israel war with Iran.

In response to surging prices, the Bank of England raised interest rates for the first time in three years earlier this month in a bid to slow inflation to its target of 2%. Interest rates increased from 3.75% to 4%, and the Bank signaled a further rise could be likely unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved. Higher interest rates lift the cost of borrowing for some mortgages, loans, and credit cards but can also lead to a better return on savings.

The government is providing some help for households with energy costs. Value-added tax has been cut on electricity bills to zero until next July, and the government will hand £100 to 20 million homes in January. But with annual household energy bills forecast to reach £2,166, Cornwall Insight retail markets principal Craig Lowrey said that ultimately this support can only chip away at the edges of a bill that continues to be pushed up by events in the Middle East.

2:06 AM ET

NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft reached a distance of one light-day from Earth early Wednesday, more than 16 billion miles away, marking the farthest any human artifact has ventured from the planet. Launched in 1977 to study Jupiter and Saturn, the probe sailed past the milestone traveling at nearly 38,000 miles per hour. Radio signals from the spacecraft now take 24 hours to reach flight controllers at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, meaning the 13-member team had to wait until Thursday to confirm the achievement.

Voyager 1 and its sister ship Voyager 2, which followed a different path through the outer planets before entering interstellar space in 2018, continue to transmit data about magnetic fields, charged particles, and plasma waves beyond the solar system. Both spacecraft are powered by radioisotope thermoelectric generators using decaying plutonium 238. Voyager 1 now operates on just over 210 watts, down from 470 watts at launch, enough to run only two of its 10 instruments. Voyager 2 has slightly more power at about 230 watts, allowing three instruments to remain active.

Mission manager Kareem Badaruddin, one of three full-time team members maintaining the aging spacecraft, described the mission as one of the greatest human achievements. The team carefully manages remaining power and heat to prevent fuel lines from freezing and to keep the spacecraft antennas pointed toward Earth. With commands taking two full days for a round-trip response, troubleshooting problems has become a slow process.

Engineers hope to keep data flowing for several more years as power steadily declines, though it remains uncertain how long the spacecraft will function. After power is exhausted, both Voyagers will continue drifting through space, expected to remain intact for a billion years or more. Each carries a gold phonograph record encoded with greetings in 55 languages, 115 photographs, natural sounds, and music from multiple cultures as a message for any civilization that might encounter them in the distant future.

England defeated Australia by 97 runs in the third one-day international in Hobart on Wednesday, completing a series sweep with a statement performance ahead of next year's World Cup. Ben Duckett scored 133 runs from 115 balls as England posted 328 before being bowled out in the 48th over, then bowlers Sonny Baker and Gus Atkinson dismantled Australia's chase to limit the hosts to 231 in 46.5 overs.

Duckett's century was his first for England on Australian soil after averaging just 18.33 across 12 previous innings in the country. Will Jacks added a 48-ball 59 to support Duckett's effort, compensating for middle-order wickets that fell to Australia's part-time spinners. Joe Root was bowled for 12, captain Harry Brook hit out for 17, and Jordan Cox was stumped as England's innings slowed through the middle overs before Jacks helped secure a competitive total.

England's new-ball pair proved decisive in the chase. Atkinson dismissed Travis Head in the first over for the third consecutive match in the series and also hit Cameron Green with a bouncer that forced the all-rounder to retire hurt. Baker claimed 4-45, including Australia captain Mitchell Marsh caught at second slip in the second over and Josh Inglis soon after. Liam Dawson later took three wickets with his left-arm spin, bowling Cooper Connolly and dismissing Jack Edwards and Matt Renshaw.

The series win represents a significant turnaround for England's 50-over team after they were dismissed for 131 in the opening match. The recovery follows a difficult period that included a failed World Cup title defense in 2023 and poor results at last year's Champions Trophy. England next face South Africa in a three-match ODI series in January, playing in conditions they will encounter during the World Cup.

Yesterday, 11:18 PM ET

Former Democratic Representative Mary Peltola defeated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska's Senate race, becoming the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Alaska since 2008 and the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate, the Associated Press projects. The victory extends a series of Democratic wins in states President Donald Trump carried in 2024, including Texas, Ohio, Iowa, and South Carolina. Trump won Alaska by 13 points in 2024.

The race drew scrutiny after The New York Times reported allegations that Democratic operatives recruited another candidate named Dan Sullivan to draw votes from the incumbent senator. Peltola's campaign denied involvement. Alaska Governor Mike Dunleavy ordered a state investigation into whether laws were broken, and the Justice Department launched a criminal investigation and issued subpoenas to people connected to the alleged effort. No one involved in the race has been charged with a crime.

Another candidate named Dan Sullivan, a retired teacher from Petersburg listed on the ballot as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., became a factor in the race. The Trump administration sued Alaska on October 22 seeking to delay certification if Senator Sullivan lost and votes for the other Dan Sullivan could have changed the outcome. A federal judge dismissed the lawsuit on November 1, ruling that the administration lacked standing and that its proposed remedy was unworkable. The Justice Department appealed the decision.

Peltola campaigned on affordability, protecting Alaska's fisheries, and putting state interests ahead of party politics. Sullivan, a former Alaska attorney general and Marine Corps veteran who has represented Alaska in the Senate since 2015, campaigned on his record on energy development and national security. Trump traveled to Alaska in October to rally support for Sullivan. Peltola made history in a 2022 special election as the first Alaska Native elected to Congress and won a full term that year before losing her House seat to Republican Nick Begich in 2024.

Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth, the farthest any human-made object has traveled

NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft reached a distance of one light-day from Earth early Wednesday, more than 16 billion miles away, marking the farthest any human artifact has ventured from the planet. Launched in 1977 to study...

8:00 AM ET

NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft reached a distance of one light-day from Earth early Wednesday, more than 16 billion miles away, marking the farthest any human artifact has ventured from the planet. Launched in 1977 to study Jupiter and Saturn, the probe sailed past the milestone traveling at nearly 38,000 miles per hour. Radio signals from the spacecraft now take 24 hours to reach flight controllers at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, meaning the 13-member team had to wait until Thursday to confirm the achievement.

Voyager 1 and its sister ship Voyager 2, which followed a different path through the outer planets before entering interstellar space in 2018, continue to transmit data about magnetic fields, charged particles, and plasma waves beyond the solar system. Both spacecraft are powered by radioisotope thermoelectric generators using decaying plutonium 238. Voyager 1 now operates on just over 210 watts, down from 470 watts at launch, enough to run only two of its 10 instruments. Voyager 2 has slightly more power at about 230 watts, allowing three instruments to remain active.

Mission manager Kareem Badaruddin, one of three full-time team members maintaining the aging spacecraft, described the mission as one of the greatest human achievements. The team carefully manages remaining power and heat to prevent fuel lines from freezing and to keep the spacecraft antennas pointed toward Earth. With commands taking two full days for a round-trip response, troubleshooting problems has become a slow process.

Engineers hope to keep data flowing for several more years as power steadily declines, though it remains uncertain how long the spacecraft will function. After power is exhausted, both Voyagers will continue drifting through space, expected to remain intact for a billion years or more. Each carries a gold phonograph record encoded with greetings in 55 languages, 115 photographs, natural sounds, and music from multiple cultures as a message for any civilization that might encounter them in the distant future.
