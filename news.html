5:26 PM ET

The House of Representatives approved a $70 billion funding measure on Tuesday that will support immigration enforcement agencies through the remainder of President Trump's term. The Secure America Act passed 214-212, largely along party lines, and now awaits the president's signature.

The bill allocates $38 billion to Immigration and Customs Enforcement, $26 billion to Customs and Border Protection, and $5 billion to the Department of Homeland Security through September 2029. The Senate had already approved the legislation last week.

The vote represents a significant political victory for House Republicans, who have maintained a historically narrow majority. Kevin Kiley, an independent aligned with Republicans, joined all Democrats in voting against the measure.

"With today's vote, House and Senate Republicans have officially ended the third Democrat government shutdown of this Congress," House Speaker Mike Johnson said after the passage. Johnson credited Republicans with preventing future shutdowns by securing long-term funding for these agencies.

The bill ends a months-long standoff between the two parties. Democrats had blocked funding for ICE and CBP beginning in January following federal agents' killing of two U.S. citizens in Minneapolis during immigration enforcement operations. That standoff triggered a 75-day Department of Homeland Security shutdown beginning in mid-February. The department reopened in late April after Democrats agreed to fund all DHS operations except ICE and CBP, while Republicans pursued separate, extended funding for those agencies.

Democrats unanimously opposed the final bill, arguing it provides insufficient oversight. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said the legislation would "waste $70 billion in taxpayer money to give a blank check to ICE without any guardrails, any oversight, any accountability."

Republicans countered that the measure represents essential law enforcement funding. House Majority Leader Steve Scalise told supporters they were "voting to fund law enforcement" and "voting to secure America's borders." He framed opposition as an effort to "defund the police" and allow undocumented immigrants to enter the country.

The bill's passage resolved several controversies that had complicated its progress. Senate Republicans removed a proposed $1 billion allocation for security improvements at a Trump White House ballroom after the chamber's parliamentarian ruled it could not be included under budget reconciliation procedures.

The measure also became entangled with a separate Republican proposal for an approximately $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund. While some GOP lawmakers sought to bar this fund through amendment language, those efforts failed. The acting attorney general has stated the proposal is dead, though Trump has refused to rule out its creation in recent interviews.

The legislation ensures that immigration enforcement agencies will have stable funding through the 2028 presidential election without requiring annual congressional appropriations battles.

The strikes began at 10pm UK time on Tuesday, according to U.S. Central Command, which described the operation as a "proportional response to unjustified Iranian aggression."

U.S. military officials said the helicopter went down off the coast of Oman earlier that day during a patrol operation. Trump stated on his Truth Social platform that Iran was responsible for the crash and that Washington "must, of necessity, respond to this attack."

According to U.S. media citing White House officials, the military targeted radar and air defense facilities along the Strait of Hormuz. However, U.S. officials also signaled they were not seeking a return to full-scale war with Iran.

The helicopter's two crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat called a Corsair, a 24-foot vessel operated remotely by a human pilot. Hawkins called it the first known drone rescue at sea conducted by the U.S. military.

The incident threatens a shaky ceasefire between the United States and Iran that was announced in April. It comes amid broader regional tensions following exchanges of fire between Iran and Israel on Monday. Iranian state television reported that Israeli attacks killed at least two members of the country's air defense units.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical waterway for global commerce, with roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum passing through this narrow passage between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula. Apache helicopters have been key assets for enforcing a U.S. blockade on Iranian crude oil shipments.

Before the helicopter incident, Trump had expressed renewed optimism about negotiations with Iran, saying there was "a good chance" of signing a deal within days. He said the U.S. could easily conduct more extensive bombing but warned it would harm negotiations and kill many people.

Tehran has not yet indicated whether it plans its own retaliation. Military officials said the cause of the helicopter crash was still under investigation.

A few minutes ago

The Social Security retirement trust fund will be depleted by 2032 unless Congress takes action, according to new projections that move the insolvency date one year earlier than previously expected. The accelerated timeline puts retirement benefits at risk for millions of Americans who depend on the program.

If the trust fund runs dry without congressional intervention, Social Security will only be able to pay out what it collects in payroll taxes. This would result in automatic benefit cuts of approximately 22 percent for retirees. The program currently provides monthly payments to more than 67 million Americans, including retirees, disabled workers, and survivors of deceased workers.

The shortfall stems from demographic shifts as the large baby boomer generation continues to retire while birth rates remain low. This creates an imbalance where fewer workers pay into the system while more beneficiaries draw from it. Rising life expectancy also means retirees collect benefits for longer periods than when the program was designed.

Congress has several options to address the funding gap, including raising the payroll tax rate, increasing the cap on taxable wages, raising the retirement age, or reducing benefits. Each approach faces political resistance. Raising taxes meets opposition from those concerned about economic impact, while benefit cuts are unpopular among current and future retirees who have paid into the system throughout their working lives.

The 2032 deadline gives lawmakers six years to craft a solution, though past efforts at Social Security reform have stalled amid partisan disagreement. Without action, the automatic cuts would affect everyone receiving retirement benefits, not just new applicants. The situation has become more urgent as the insolvency date continues to move closer with each annual report.

A few minutes ago

NASA announced the crew for its Artemis III mission during an event at Johnson Space Center in Houston on Tuesday. The four astronauts will participate in a two-week test flight scheduled for 2027, marking a crucial step toward returning humans to the moon.

The crew consists of three NASA astronauts and one from the European Space Agency. Randy Bresnik, a Marine colonel and former International Space Station commander, will lead the mission as commander. Luca Parmitano of the European Space Agency will serve as pilot. Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas, both NASA mission specialists, complete the crew. Bob Hines will serve as a support crew member.

Bresnik has logged more than 7,000 hours of flying time and is one of only two astronauts to have a child born while in space. Parmitano, a fighter pilot in the Italian air force, previously experienced a frightening incident during a 2013 spacewalk when his helmet filled with liquid. Rubio holds the record for the longest single spaceflight by a NASA astronaut, having spent 371 days in space. Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer, has no previous spaceflight experience but trained with Artemis II astronauts as part of their backup crew.

Unlike previous lunar missions, Artemis III will not involve a moon landing. Instead, the crew will remain in low Earth orbit while testing docking procedures and life support systems. They will practice connecting their Orion capsule with two competing lunar landers from private companies Blue Origin and SpaceX. These tests will pave the way for Artemis IV, scheduled for 2028, which will attempt the first crewed lunar landing since 1972.

NASA administrator Jared Isaacman described the mission as part of building "Earth's first starfleet." He emphasized the international scope of the effort, noting that multiple spacecraft from different nations, including Russia and China, would eventually operate together in orbit. "This seems like the beginning of the future that we imagined as children," Isaacman said.

The announcement comes two months after Artemis II successfully looped around the moon in April, surpassing the maximum distance achieved during the Apollo 13 mission. Artemis III will test systems critical for longer-duration spaceflight and lunar surface operations.

The mission represents NASA's commitment to establishing sustainable human presence on the moon while laying groundwork for eventual Mars exploration. The effort involves collaboration between NASA, international space agencies, and private contractors. The 2027 target date requires coordination across multiple partners and reflects the ambitious scope of the broader Artemis program.

Serena Williams returned to competitive tennis after 1,375 days away from the sport, securing a victory in doubles at Queen's Club in London. The 44-year-old partnered with Canadian teenager Victoria Mboko to defeat third seeds Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe in the opening round.

The unseeded pair won in straight sets, taking the first set in a tiebreak 7-6 (7-2) before dominating the second set 6-2. Williams' comeback marks her first competitive match since stepping away from professional tennis in 2022.

Williams has described her return as being driven by personal rather than competitive motivations. She emphasized that winning is not important to her, having already achieved 23 Grand Slam singles titles during her career. "I don't need to win. I've won more than most people have in their whole lives, so for me it's not important," she said. "This whole journey is like I'm putting no pressure on myself."

A key motivation for her comeback involves her children. Williams gave birth to daughters in 2017 and 2023, and she expressed that the opportunity for them to see her play was meaningful. "It's really about my kids getting to see me play," she explained.

Williams selected Mboko as her partner after being impressed by the teenager's attitude and drive during previous tournaments. Mboko, the world number nine in singles, was only 10 years old when Williams won her last Grand Slam title. The Canadian expressed her excitement about the partnership, saying she has always admired Williams and found her "really nice and very relatable."

When asked about her performance during the match, Williams was self-critical, grading herself as a "C-minus." Despite her modest assessment, she demonstrated the skill and competitive instinct needed to secure the win.

Williams' future in tennis remains uncertain beyond her current commitments. She has committed to playing doubles in Berlin following Queen's, but she has not confirmed plans to return to singles competition. When asked about potentially playing singles at Wimbledon, she said: "I can't say yeah, I can't say no. Right now, no." She suggested she would need more training if she were to pursue singles play.

The grass court season at Queen's provides a traditional warm-up for Wimbledon, and Williams' decision to return at this prestigious tournament has drawn significant attention from fans and fellow players. Her comeback represents a notable moment in professional tennis, with players including Emma Raducanu welcoming her return to the sport.

Williams and Mboko will face opponents in the second round of the doubles tournament.
