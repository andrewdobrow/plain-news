A few minutes ago

Federal agents conducted a raid on the Cleveland office of the Ohio Organizing Collaborative, a grassroots organization focused on voter registration, prompting concerns about potential enforcement actions against voting rights groups ahead of the midterm elections.

According to Prentiss Haney, a board member of the group, FBI agents seized computers and phones during Thursday's operation. Agents also visited homes of people affiliated with the organization throughout Ohio and conducted interviews with them.

The investigation appears to focus on accusations related to the 2024 election, though specific details remain unclear. The FBI's Cleveland office did not respond to requests for comment about the raid or its underlying investigation.

Haney characterized the operation as part of a broader campaign. "This raid is a full-on coordinated assault weaponizing the justice department and DHS against people who are fighting for working-class voters and Black voters to make sure they have access to the ballot," he said.

The timing of the raid came approximately one month after Todd Blanche, a top official in the acting attorney general's office, instructed prosecutors to prioritize voter fraud cases. Additionally, Frank LaRose, Ohio's Republican secretary of state, referred over 1,000 individuals he identified as non-citizens who appeared to have registered in the state to the justice department last fall. Federal investigators have also gathered voter records from at least six Ohio counties.

The operation drew immediate criticism from Democratic elected officials and civil rights organizations. Shontel Brown, an Ohio congressional representative whose district includes Cleveland, called it "an unprecedented attack on democracy" and stated the raids must end. She characterized the action as part of a systematic effort to attack elections and promote voter fraud myths.

Sherrod Brown, a former senator now running for office, described reports of the raid as "deeply disturbing." Cleveland Mayor Justin Bibb called for transparency, saying the FBI should disclose the basis for its actions or face questions about whether it was unfairly targeting civic participation.

Michael Waldman, president of the Brennan Center for Justice, described the raid as "an outrageous fishing expedition" designed to intimidate people working for democratic participation. He characterized it as an abuse of law enforcement for political purposes that fits a pattern of federal inquiries targeting voting infrastructure before elections.

Ohio remains a significant swing state in national elections. The state delivered 17 electoral votes to Donald Trump in 2024, and Republican Bernie Moreno defeated longtime Democratic Senator Sherrod Brown in a competitive race that helped Republicans gain control of the Senate.

Trump has repeatedly claimed widespread voter fraud without providing evidence, most recently focusing on California's vote-counting procedures.

Danni Wyatt-Hodge hit an unbeaten 105 to guide England to an 87-run victory over Sri Lanka in the Women's T20 World Cup opener at Edgbaston on Friday evening. The triumph represented a dominant display from the hosts, who set a tournament record with their batting performance.

England accumulated 219 for the loss of just one wicket, the highest total in Women's T20 World Cup history. Sri Lanka's reply fell well short, reaching only 132 all out as they struggled against English bowling and fielding.

Wyatt-Hodge and Amy Jones, who opened the innings together, established a powerful foundation with a 135-run partnership. Jones eventually fell in the 14th over attempting to clear the infield, caught by Sri Lanka captain Chamari Athapaththu. Nat Sciver-Brunt then took over, striking an unbeaten 46 from just 22 balls with apparent ease.

The match came down to a tense finale as Sciver-Brunt dominated the final over, hitting back-to-back boundaries. This left Wyatt-Hodge stranded on 97 with few opportunities to reach three figures. Sri Lanka's fielding came to England's aid when they cut off Sciver-Brunt's boundary attempt, allowing Wyatt-Hodge back on strike. She swept the next delivery into the gap behind square for four runs to bring up her century with a single ball remaining.

Wyatt-Hodge's celebration proved particularly poignant. Having given birth to her daughter Daisy just three weeks earlier, she cradled her bat like a baby in tribute to the crowd's applause. "I've been after that third T20 hundred for a few years now, so I'm chuffed to bits to get it," she said afterward. "I've had a big life change the last few weeks, so it was pretty emotional. A few of the girls said they had a few tears."

Later in the match, Wyatt-Hodge took a crucial catch racing back from square leg to dismiss Athapaththu, though she admitted the windy conditions made the catch difficult. "I thought I was going to run into the umpire," she explained. "It was really windy and blustery. I lost the ball for a couple of seconds and then managed to see it, put my hands out and grabbed it."

Sri Lanka's innings never gained momentum after losing their captain. The side slipped to 67 for five as batters attempted aggressive strokes without success. Freya Kemp proved particularly effective with the ball, taking a career-best four for 22 through consistent line-and-length bowling.

Sri Lanka's coach Jamie Siddons acknowledged his team's poor execution. "The bowlers didn't bowl the ball in the right areas," he said. "The ball was flying around everywhere. We're a better cricket team than that. We planned well, we just didn't execute anything that we wanted to do."

Gene Shalit, the television personality known for his distinctive mustache and bow ties, died Friday at age 100. His family confirmed to NBC News that he "passed away peacefully today after 100 years of an amazing life."

Shalit became a recognizable fixture on NBC's Today Show, where he spent four decades reviewing films and books for morning television audiences. He appeared on the program from 1970 until his retirement in 2010, making him one of the longest-serving contributors in the show's history.

His signature appearance and playful approach to criticism helped bring film and book reviews to a mass audience at a time when such coverage was primarily found in newspapers and specialty publications. Shalit's accessible style and use of wordplay distinguished him from more serious critics of his era, and his reviews became known for their lighthearted tone and clever language.

During his 40-year tenure on Today, Shalit reviewed thousands of films and books, offering his perspectives on the latest cultural releases to millions of viewers each morning. His presence on the program made him one of the most recognizable television personalities of his generation. The combination of his distinctive look and his entertaining reviewing style earned him a dedicated following among the show's audience.

When Shalit retired in 2010 at age 84, his departure marked the end of a significant era in television criticism. For decades, his voice had been one of the most influential in shaping how American audiences thought about movies and literature. His ability to reach such a wide audience with his cultural commentary reflected a time when morning television could be a primary source of entertainment criticism for many viewers.

Shalit's career in broadcasting began in the early 1970s when he joined Today as a book and film reviewer. He gradually became a permanent fixture on the program, appearing regularly to share his thoughts on new releases. His approach to criticism emphasized accessibility and humor rather than academic analysis, making his reviews appealing to the general public.

His death marks the end of an important chapter in American television history. Shalit represented a generation of television personalities who could command large audiences and influence cultural conversations through their presence on morning news programs. His distinctive style and long-running role on Today helped establish the format for entertainment criticism on television, setting a template that would influence how movies and books were discussed on the medium for years to come.

NBC News confirmed his passing, with tributes from across the television industry noting his remarkable career and the lasting impact of his unique presence on American television.
