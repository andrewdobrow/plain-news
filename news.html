8:00 AM ET

US crude oil inventories have dropped to their lowest level in more than two decades as the conflict with Iran continues to disrupt global energy markets. The stockpiles have declined for six consecutive weeks, raising concerns about the nation's ability to manage supply disruptions as tensions in the Middle East escalate.

The sharp drawdown in oil reserves comes as the ongoing conflict has affected trade routes and energy flows in the region. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments, has experienced closures that have forced the US to rely more heavily on domestic reserves. The strategic petroleum reserve and commercial inventories have both been affected by the sustained pressure on supply chains.

Despite the headline about perilously low inventories, energy analysts note that the situation is more nuanced than the numbers alone suggest. The US remains a major oil producer, and domestic production capacity provides some buffer against supply shocks. However, the combination of reduced stockpiles and geopolitical uncertainty has created volatility in energy markets and raised questions about preparedness for extended disruptions.

The depletion of oil reserves represents a significant economic concern as the nation navigates both military engagement and energy security challenges. Lower inventories typically translate to reduced flexibility in managing price spikes and supply interruptions. Energy traders and policymakers are closely monitoring the situation as the conflict shows no immediate signs of resolution.

The last time US crude inventories reached comparable levels was in 2004, a period that preceded significant energy price increases. While current production technology and market dynamics differ substantially from that era, the historical parallel has heightened attention to the strategic implications of the drawdown. The administration faces decisions about whether to further tap emergency reserves or implement measures to conserve supplies as the conflict continues.

7:50 AM ET

Texas authorities have confirmed a second case of New World screwworm in cattle, escalating concerns about the parasitic outbreak that has disrupted livestock trade across North America. The infections were discovered about five miles apart in Zavala County, southwest of San Antonio, marking the first detections of the disease in the United States since the 1960s.

The first case was identified in a three-week-old calf with an umbilical lesion. Days later, the U.S. Department of Agriculture confirmed a second infection after testing suspect cases in the area. The New World screwworm is a parasitic fly whose larvae burrow through living flesh, causing a painful condition called myiasis that can be fatal if untreated.

Governor Greg Abbott issued a statewide disaster declaration on Friday and warned that the region faces "an extraordinarily challenging summer" as warmer months increase the risk of disease spread. State and federal agencies are implementing aggressive containment measures, including the release of millions of sterile male screwworm flies in the affected area. These sterile insects prevent wild females from reproducing, a proven eradication strategy that helped eliminate the parasite from the United States decades ago.

The Texas Animal Health Commission has established an infested zone and implemented animal movement restrictions to prevent further spread. Officials are urging ranchers and animal owners to remain vigilant, inspecting livestock for larvae in wounds and reporting any suspected cases immediately. The USDA has assured the public that the food supply remains safe, as the screwworm does not infest meat, fruits, vegetables, or other food products.

The outbreak's economic impact extends beyond Texas borders. Canada's food inspection agency announced a temporary ban on livestock from Texas, prohibiting cattle and horses that were in the state within 21 days of crossing into Canada. However, Canadian officials noted the disease is unlikely to become established there due to the colder climate.

The screwworm's reappearance has compounded existing challenges for the U.S. cattle industry. The U.S.-Mexico border has been closed to live cattle imports for more than a year as the disease moved northward from Central America. This closure has severely impacted the beef industry, which contracts to a 75-year low, as the United States formerly imported more than one million cattle annually from Mexico, representing about 4 to 5 percent of all cattle sold for American beef production.

Texas Animal Health Commission Director Dr. Bud Dinges said the agency has spent over two years preparing for a potential screwworm resurgence and is now implementing those preparations. The combination of sterile fly releases, enhanced surveillance, movement restrictions, prompt wound treatment, and public education represents the established approach to controlling and eradicating the pest.

1:05 AM ET

Astronomers have detected wind flowing from the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way, solving a mystery that has persisted for 50 years. The discovery reveals that even relatively quiet black holes generate outflows of material, a finding that changes how scientists understand these cosmic objects.

The black hole, known as Sagittarius A, sits roughly 26,000 light-years from Earth at the galactic center. Researchers found evidence of wind pointing directly at the massive object, confirming a phenomenon that scientists have sought to observe for decades. The detection marks the first time such an outflow has been confirmed from the Milky Way's central black hole.

Black holes at the centers of galaxies can produce powerful winds and jets of material, but Sagittarius A has been considered relatively inactive compared to black holes in other galaxies. The new observations show that even during periods of low activity, black holes can generate significant outflows. This challenges previous assumptions that only actively feeding black holes produce such winds.

The discovery has implications for understanding how black holes interact with their surrounding galaxies. Winds from supermassive black holes can influence star formation and the distribution of gas throughout a galaxy. Scientists have long theorized that these outflows play a role in galactic evolution, but direct observations from our own galaxy's black hole provide concrete evidence of this process.

The findings come from observations that detected the wind's signature by measuring how it affects surrounding material. Researchers used multiple sources of data to confirm the presence of the outflow and determine its properties. The wind appears to be less powerful than those seen around more active black holes, but its presence even during quiet periods suggests that black hole winds may be more common throughout the universe than previously thought.

The game came down to the final seconds. With 9.5 seconds remaining, Jalen Brunson drew a foul and made the go-ahead free throw after Victor Wembanyama turned the ball over. Wembanyama's attempt at the buzzer from near the elbow bounced off the back rim, sealing the Knicks' second road victory of the Finals. New York became only the third team in NBA history to win the first two games of the Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets.

Brunson finished with 20 points on 7-for-25 shooting despite battling injuries. Karl-Anthony Towns led the Knicks with 21 points and added 13 rebounds, highlighting the team's balanced offensive approach. "We've gotten leads and we've held the leads," Towns said after the game. "Today, the Spurs fought. They got themselves back into the lead and put themselves in a position to win, but we found a way to get it done."

The Spurs mounted a furious fourth-quarter comeback. After trailing by 14 points midway through the final quarter, San Antonio used a 14-0 run to tie the game at 97-97 with three minutes remaining. Wembanyama, looking to bounce back from shooting 6-for-21 in Game 1, finished with 29 points on 11-for-21 shooting while playing 40 minutes. However, his crucial turnover in the final moments proved costly.

"Of course I liked the shot," Wembanyama said after the game. "In moments like this, results matter more than process. We just needed to score. I just needed to score."

The Spurs face a historical mountain to climb. No team in NBA history has won the championship after losing the first two games of the Finals at home. San Antonio must now travel to Madison Square Garden, where the Knicks have the opportunity to take a commanding 3-0 series lead in Game 3 on Monday night.

The return to New York has generated intense excitement throughout the city. Secondary-market ticket prices for Game 3 have approached $9,000 for some seats, reflecting the historic nature of the moment. The Knicks' Finals appearance has captured the imagination of New Yorkers waiting more than five decades for a championship.

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. His daughters Emily and Daisy Head announced that he "passed away peacefully of complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family."

Head gained widespread recognition for his role as Giles, the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character throughout the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. The character became a fan favorite for his dry wit and fatherly relationship with Buffy. More recently, Head appeared in Apple TV's Ted Lasso, where he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, and was praised for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actor. Head began his professional career on stage, starring in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in numerous stage productions including Chess, where he played lead role Freddie Trumper, and The Rocky Horror Show as Dr. Frank-N-Furter.

Before becoming famous for Buffy, Head gained recognition in Britain for his role in Nescafé Gold Blend television advertisements. He also appeared in films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie, and had a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain. Additionally, Head sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and was featured on their 1986 debut album.

During Buffy's production, Head commuted between Los Angeles and England, where his partner Sarah and two young daughters lived. "I'd try to go home to them every three or four weeks," he told the Guardian, explaining that the production team worked around his schedule.

Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute to Head on Instagram, opening with a quote from her character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

His daughters stated that Head "loved his job very much and he always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people, in such wonderful productions, across a career that spanned several decades."

The actors further expressed that "our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of and in the audiences that love them."

Head's partner, Sarah Fisher, died in December 2025 at age 61.

The findings come from observations that detected the wind's signature by measuring how it affects surrounding material.
