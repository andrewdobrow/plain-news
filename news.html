Yesterday, 11:03 PM ET

Hurricane Fausto has intensified to a Category 2 storm in the Pacific Ocean and is expected to approach the Hawaiian Islands in the coming days. Hawaii officials are encouraging residents to begin preparations now, though forecasters predict the hurricane will weaken before it reaches the islands. The storm system is also creating dangerous surf conditions that could affect Southern California.

Fausto gained hurricane status on Monday night with sustained winds of 75 mph, located approximately 745 miles southwest of the southern tip of the Baja California peninsula. The storm was moving west-northwest at 9 mph at the time of the advisory. While no coastal watches or warnings were in effect for Mexico's Baja California coast, the National Hurricane Center warned that ocean swells generated by Fausto could create life-threatening surf and rip tide conditions in that region.

In a separate development, Tropical Storm Genevieve has formed in the Pacific Ocean. Forecasters are monitoring both weather systems as they move across the ocean, with Genevieve potentially tracking behind Fausto.

The timing and trajectory of Hurricane Fausto means residents across Hawaii should take precautions despite expectations that the storm will lose strength during its approach. Officials emphasized that early preparation remains important even as the hurricane weakens. The dangerous surf conditions generated by the storm extend beyond Hawaii, with Southern California expected to experience significant wave activity from the weather system.

The formation of both Fausto and Genevieve demonstrates the active conditions in the Pacific basin. Forecasters continue to track both storms closely as they develop and move across the ocean. The public is being advised to monitor official weather updates from the National Hurricane Center for the most current information about both storms' movements and potential impacts.

Residents in affected areas should review their emergency plans, gather necessary supplies, and stay informed about any watches or warnings that may be issued. While Hurricane Fausto is expected to weaken before reaching Hawaii, the potential for damaging winds, heavy rain, and significant surf conditions means preparation remains prudent. Similarly, those in Southern California should be aware of the hazardous surf that could develop from the distant hurricane.

The convergence of two tropical systems in the Pacific reflects broader weather patterns and ocean conditions that forecasters will continue to monitor throughout the week. Communities across the Pacific region are advised to remain vigilant and prepared for potential impacts from these developing weather systems.

A few minutes ago

More than 200,000 people have been forced to evacuate or shelter indoors as massive wildfires sweep across Spain and France, with authorities declaring a national emergency in Madrid and ordering total evacuations in some of Europe's most popular tourist destinations.

French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez reported that 167,000 people have been evacuated in France, while Spain's interior ministry put its evacuation total at 45,000. The blazes have proven particularly devastating in France, which recorded its highest-ever area of land burned by wildfires in a single year. According to the EU's European Forest Fire Information System, 69,473 hectares have burned in France since the start of the year, surpassing the previous record of 66,337 hectares set in 2022. The fires continue to rage in the south-western Gironde and Landes regions, prompting authorities to order evacuations of multiple areas west of Bordeaux, including parts of three suburbs and the city's airport.

In Spain, the situation has grown increasingly dire. As of late July, 32 large wildfires have been recorded this year compared with seven during the same period last year. From January to mid-July, 131,113 hectares of land burned in wildfires, dwarfing the 24,133 hectares burned during the equivalent period in 2023. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez revealed that 130,000 hectares have burned since January, exceeding the annual average of 100,000 hectares over the past decade.

The scale and intensity of this year's fires have prompted urgent calls for action on climate change. Sánchez described the situation as "complex" while visiting affected areas, noting uncertainty about wind conditions despite dropping temperatures. He called for a "national pact against the climate emergency," emphasizing that the fires are "exceeding scientific predictions" and that climate change is causing increasingly severe blazes.

Both countries have mobilized significant resources to combat the wildfires. France has deployed more than 500 military personnel to assist firefighting teams, with additional soldiers being sent to the affected regions. The government has distributed 1.5 million face masks to protect people from harmful smoke exposure. France also requested its armed forces deploy an Airbus A400M military transport aircraft to help tackle the blazes.

The evacuation of popular tourist destinations, including the Cap Ferret peninsula on the Atlantic coast, has complicated response efforts during peak summer travel season. French Foreign Minister Éléonore Caroit urged travelers not to abandon plans for unaffected regions, stressing that "entire areas are fine" and that most of France remains unaffected.

Meanwhile, wildfires are not limited to France and Spain. Scotland's First Minister John Swinney declared a wildfire in Cairngorms national park a "very serious situation," prompting evacuation of the village of Nethy Bridge after police declared a major incident.

Nvidia has locked in its memory chip supply from South Korean manufacturer SK Hynix through a major artificial intelligence deal, the companies announced. The agreement reflects the accelerating global competition for semiconductor resources as AI technology reshapes technology markets worldwide.

The deal underscores the critical importance of memory chips in AI infrastructure. These specialized components are essential for training and operating large language models, and securing stable supplies has become a top priority for tech companies racing to expand their AI capabilities. Access to advanced memory chips now serves as a competitive advantage in the rapidly growing AI sector.

South Korea has positioned itself as a crucial player in global semiconductor manufacturing. The country's two largest chipmakers, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, have both joined the trillion-dollar company club alongside Taiwan's TSMC, driven primarily by explosive demand for AI chips. SK Hynix's stock price surged 1,000 percent over the past year, while Samsung climbed 500 percent.

This semiconductor boom has transformed South Korea's broader economy. The Kospi index, South Korea's main stock market, has surged 220 percent in the past year and recently reached an all-time high, vaulting the country past India to become the world's sixth largest share market. The index now exceeds the equity markets of the UK, Germany, and France.

However, analysts note concentration risks in South Korea's market expansion. Research indicates that Samsung and SK Hynix have contributed up to 70 percent of the Kospi's growth in 2026, leaving the index heavily exposed to fluctuations in global AI spending and potential supply chain disruptions. The Kospi's volatility index recently reached an exceptionally high level of 75, compared to its historical average of 20, suggesting investor nervousness despite the market rally.

The memory chip market serves different needs than Nvidia's advanced processors. Three companies dominate memory production: Samsung, SK Hynix, and American chipmaker Micron, which recently joined the trillion-dollar valuation club. Their success reflects AI's enormous appetite for these components.

Demand appears underpinned by major technology companies, often called "AI hyperscalers," including Meta, Amazon, Alphabet, and Microsoft. These firms have committed substantial capital to AI infrastructure development, potentially supporting continued growth in the semiconductor sector.

Japan has also experienced gains from the AI boom, with Tokyo's Nikkei 225 index reaching all-time highs. Semiconductor and AI-related stocks have driven much of the growth, with SoftBank Group recently toppling Toyota as Japan's most valuable listed company.

The semiconductor industry historically experienced boom-and-bust cycles, but analysts suggest AI demand may have fundamentally changed that pattern by creating sustained, large-scale chip consumption.

Del Toro's outspoken rejection of AI technology for this project represents a notable public position in an ongoing debate that has created divisions within the creative community. The dark fantasy film, which takes place during the Spanish Civil War, has maintained a devoted audience throughout the past two decades. The conversion to 3D and HDR marks a significant technical enhancement to the film since its original theatrical release.

Throughout his career, del Toro has established himself through visually striking films that blend fantasy and horror with practical effects and intricate production design choices. His insistence on relying on human craftsmanship for this re-release aligns with his broader creative philosophy, which prioritizes traditional artistic techniques over digital convenience.

The director's position on AI in this project reflects broader conversations happening throughout the entertainment industry about the role of artificial intelligence in creative work. While some see AI as a valuable tool for efficiency and cost reduction, others argue that it risks undermining the artisanal qualities that distinguish meaningful cinema. Del Toro's decision to exclude AI entirely from the Pan's Labyrinth re-release demonstrates his alignment with those prioritizing human artistic contribution in film restoration and enhancement work.

6:00 AM ET

The U.S. military is preparing for a potential escalation in military operations against Iran as President Trump considers limited strikes and has ordered a significant buildup of naval forces in the Middle East. The repositioning of aircraft carriers and other warships has raised concerns about the possibility of imminent conflict, though it remains unclear whether the military movements are intended as intimidation tactics to pressure Iran on nuclear negotiations.

U.S. military planning has reached an advanced stage, according to two U.S. officials who spoke to Reuters. Options under consideration include targeting individuals and pursuing leadership change in Tehran. Trump has given Iran a deadline of 10 to 15 days to reach a nuclear deal, warning of "really bad things" if the country does not comply. When asked about considering limited strikes to pressure Iran, Trump told reporters: "I guess I can say I am considering it."

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi rejected the prospect of military solutions to the nuclear dispute. He pointed to previous attacks on Iranian facilities last year that killed scientists but failed to stop the nuclear program. Araghchi held indirect talks in Geneva this week with Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. He said the sides had reached an understanding on main guiding principles, though this does not indicate a deal is close. Araghchi stated he would have a draft counterproposal ready within two or three days.

The current tensions reflect a long-running dispute over Iran's nuclear program. Trump exited the internationally backed nuclear deal during his first term, which had restricted Iran's activities. Following Trump's departure from the agreement, Tehran began expanding its nuclear program. Israel, encouraged by Trump's support for aggressive action, led a 12-day military campaign against Iran in June 2025, with U.S. participation.

Military and policy experts express doubt that an intensified air campaign would achieve the administration's stated objectives. Even strikes targeting critical infrastructure such as bridges, water facilities, and electricity plants are unlikely to break Tehran's resolve or force significant concessions, according to analysts.

Trump has also referenced a crackdown on protests in Iran, stating that 32,000 people were killed, figures that could not be verified. Iran's human rights monitoring group Hrana reports verifying 7,114 deaths with another 11,700 under review. The Iranian government published a list claiming 3,117 deaths from what it called a "recent terrorist operation."

Any decision to launch major military operations against Iran would carry significant risks, including potential retaliation against U.S. interests and allies in the region, disruption to global energy markets, and the possibility of broader regional conflict. The United Nations has expressed concerns about heightened rhetoric and increased military activities in the region.
