President Trump said Thursday he is considering using an executive order to extend Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act after Congress failed to pass a temporary extension of the surveillance program. Speaking in the Oval Office, Trump told reporters that Congress wants him to take action through executive authority. "Congress wants me to do it," Trump said when asked about the possibility.

Section 702 allows U.S. intelligence agencies to collect communications of foreign targets located outside the United States without a warrant. The provision has been central to counterterrorism and foreign intelligence operations, but it has also drawn criticism from privacy advocates who argue it sweeps up communications of American citizens in contact with foreign targets.

The House of Representatives blocked a short-term extension of the surveillance law in a 198-218 vote on Thursday. Democrats announced they would block renewal of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act in protest of Trump's recent decision to appoint an inexperienced loyalist as the country's top intelligence official.

Congressional debates over Section 702 have long centered on competing concerns about national security effectiveness and civil liberties protections. Previous reauthorizations have included various reform proposals aimed at adding safeguards for Americans whose communications may be incidentally collected.

Trump's statement that Congress wants him to act through executive order suggests lawmakers may be seeking a temporary administrative solution while they continue negotiating legislative language. However, using an executive order to extend a surveillance program that typically requires statutory authorization would likely face legal challenges. The president's comments left open whether he would actually proceed with such an order, saying only that he would see what happens. "Let's see what happens," Trump said.

The Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act has been reauthorized multiple times since its original passage in 1978, with Section 702 added in 2008. Intelligence officials have consistently argued that the program is essential for detecting foreign threats, while civil liberties groups have pushed for stronger protections against warrantless surveillance of Americans.

The failed vote represents a significant moment in the ongoing debate over surveillance authority. The controversy surrounding Trump's intelligence leadership appointment appears to have influenced the outcome, with Democrats using the failed extension as a protest measure against the personnel decision. The current impasse reflects ongoing tension between competing priorities in Congress regarding national security and civil liberties protections.

President Trump announced on Thursday that he would cancel fresh missile strikes against Iran, claiming that negotiations had reached the highest levels of Iranian leadership and progress was being made toward a peace agreement. The announcement came after two consecutive days of U.S. airstrikes on Iranian targets.

Trump made the announcement through Truth Social, the social media platform he owns. "Based on the fact that discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran have been brought to the highest level of Iranian leadership and approved, I have, as President of the United States of America, cancelled the scheduled strikes and bombings against Iran this evening," he wrote.

However, Iranian officials have not publicly confirmed that a deal is imminent. The semi-official Tasnim news agency stated that "until a potential understanding is announced by Iran, any news from Trump on this matter should be dismissed." The Fars news agency, which is close to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, said Iran had not yet given a final response, though it suggested the U.S. had accepted a text proposed by Iran.

This reversal represents another shift in Trump's approach to the conflict. He later walked back these threats in an interview with Fox News, saying he was uncertain whether America had the appetite for such action.

The conflict has drawn international concern as it threatens regional stability. UN Secretary-General António Guterres called on both countries to return to full implementation of a ceasefire negotiated in April and avoid further violence that could "trigger a full resumption of the conflict, with unpredictable consequences for the region and the world."

The two days of U.S. airstrikes targeted military installations across Iran, including surveillance capabilities, communication systems, and air-defense sites. The U.S. military also struck an oil tanker near the strait of Hormuz that it claimed was attempting to breach a blockade of Iranian ports. An Indian official reported that three Indian crew members were killed in one strike.

Iran responded by launching missiles and drones at Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan. An 11-year-old girl in Bahrain was injured by falling debris from intercepted projectiles.

The escalation was triggered by the downing of a U.S. helicopter above the strait of Hormuz. Trump said Thursday's assault was prompted by Iran stalling in negotiations aimed at converting the temporary ceasefire into a permanent peace agreement.

Despite the ongoing strikes, Iranian officials told Reuters that talks on a preliminary deal had intensified, with both sides exchanging messages on a memorandum of understanding. Significant obstacles remain, including how to unfreeze billions of dollars in Iranian assets.

Severe storms threaten millions across Midwest and Northeast

The storms began late Tuesday and continued into Wednesday, producing at least four confirmed tornado touchdowns in eastern Illinois and causing sign...

4:39 PM ET

A major weather system brought tornadoes, damaging winds, and heavy rain to millions of people across the central United States and Northeast, with forecasters warning that the severe weather threat would persist through midweek.

The storms began late Tuesday and continued into Wednesday, producing at least four confirmed tornado touchdowns in eastern Illinois and causing significant damage across the region into Indiana. The National Weather Service reported that a supercell storm system tracked from Kankakee County, Illinois, into Indiana late Tuesday, leaving a path of destruction. The small town of Lake Village, Indiana, was hit particularly hard, with officials reporting multiple homes destroyed. At least two people were killed in Indiana as a result of the storms.

The tornado near the Kankakee fairgrounds moved northeast into Aroma Park before continuing into Indiana. Downed trees and power lines across the area south of Chicago overwhelmed emergency services and 911 operators. In central Texas, a separate tornado brought 60 mph wind gusts and baseball-sized hail to Taylor County.

AccuWeather meteorologist Brandon Buckingham reported spotting at least 10 tornadoes across Illinois, Indiana, and Texas. He noted that nearly 200 filtered reports of severe weather spanned more than 2,500 miles from Texas to Michigan. The chain of storms was expected to peak midweek and could become "the most widespread and impactful severe weather outbreak so far this year," according to weather forecasters.

States from Oklahoma to Michigan remained under tornado watches as the system moved eastward. Andrew Lyons, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service's Storm Prediction Center, said the exact number of tornado touchdowns would not be confirmed until after officials completed damage assessments. He described the system as a fairly typical early spring strong storm pattern expected to move northeast toward the Atlantic coast.

The severe weather threatened to bring intense tornadoes, damaging winds, and very large hail from the southern plains to the southern Great Lakes. Forecasters warned that the system would likely reach the Washington DC area by Wednesday afternoon, bringing new threats of damaging winds and tornadoes. A line of storms was predicted to sweep east and move into Ohio and Tennessee, affecting cities including Cincinnati, Memphis, and Nashville.

Illinois Governor JB Pritzker said in a statement that he had been briefed on the storm and tornado damage. "Keeping in our thoughts all Illinoisans impacted by the severe weather - we'll be here to help them recover," he said on social media early Wednesday.

Emergency management officials and first responders faced challenging circumstances as they dealt with the storms' aftermath while warning the public to stay away from affected areas to allow rescue operations to proceed.

SpaceX finalized the price of its initial public offering at $135 per share, the company announced Thursday. The rocket and satellite manufacturer will sell more than 555 million shares, valuing the company at roughly $1.77 trillion in what becomes the largest public offering in history. Trading is set to begin Friday.

The IPO price represents a significant milestone for the aerospace company founded by Elon Musk. At the $135 share price, the offering will raise approximately $75 billion, surpassing previous IPO records. The public debut comes as SpaceX has expanded its operations across rocket launches, satellite internet service through Starlink, and government contracts with NASA and the Department of Defense.

The valuation has drawn scrutiny from market analysts who question whether the company can justify such a high price. SpaceX is currently spending heavily on expansion while operating at a loss, according to financial disclosures. The company has invested billions in developing its Starship vehicle and expanding its Starlink satellite network, which now serves millions of customers globally.

Retail investor interest has been substantial, with orders from individual investors topping $100 billion according to reports. The IPO is expected to create approximately 4,400 millionaires among SpaceX employees who hold stock options, including workers in non-executive roles such as cafeteria staff and manufacturing technicians who participated in employee stock programs over the past several years.

The offering notably excludes investors from China and Hong Kong, reflecting broader restrictions on Chinese investment in American aerospace and technology companies. SpaceX cited national security concerns and regulatory requirements in its decision to block Chinese participation. The public listing further consolidates Musk's position as the world's wealthiest individual, with analysts projecting he could become the first trillionaire once shares begin trading, given his significant ownership stake in the company.

Oracle has issued a security warning about a vulnerability that a cybercrime gang claims to have exploited as part of a widespread hacking operation. Google notified more than 100 organizations that had potentially vulnerable servers exposed to the threat.

The tech giant's alert comes after evidence emerged that attackers were actively using the security flaw to breach corporate systems. The cybercrime gang publicly stated it was exploiting the vulnerability as part of a coordinated mass-hacking campaign targeting Oracle customers.

Google's threat intelligence team identified the scope of potential exposure and reached out to affected organizations. The notification covered companies running servers that could be compromised through the security bug. The number of confirmed breaches has not been disclosed, but the scale of potentially vulnerable systems suggests significant exposure across multiple industries.

Security vulnerabilities in widely used enterprise software can affect thousands of organizations simultaneously. Oracle's products are deployed across major corporations, government agencies, and critical infrastructure providers worldwide. When flaws in such systems become known to criminal groups, the window for exploitation before patches can be applied creates significant risk.

The warning underscores ongoing challenges in enterprise cybersecurity, where the gap between vulnerability discovery and comprehensive patching can leave organizations exposed. Companies running Oracle systems will need to assess their exposure and apply security updates to protect against the active threat.
