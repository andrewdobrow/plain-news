Yesterday, 10:11 PM ET

A parasitic fly that feeds on living flesh has been detected in cattle in Texas, marking the first cases in the United States since the 1960s. The New World screwworm, eradicated from the country decades ago, has triggered an aggressive federal and state response aimed at preventing further spread.

The first case was confirmed in a three-week-old calf with an umbilical lesion in Zavala County, southwest of San Antonio. A second infection was discovered days later in a one-year-old calf approximately five miles away. The Texas Animal Health Commission established an infested zone in the area and implemented animal movement restrictions to contain the parasite.

The New World screwworm is a parasitic larva that burrows through the living flesh of warm-blooded animals and humans, causing a painful condition called myiasis. If left untreated, the infection can be fatal to infected animals.

Texas Governor Greg Abbott issued a statewide disaster declaration in response to the outbreak. "We're in the season for spread," Abbott warned, predicting an "extraordinarily challenging summer" for affected areas. State officials are coordinating with the federal government to slow the parasite's expansion.

The response includes releasing millions of sterile screwworm flies near the infection sites. These sterile male flies cannot reproduce with wild females, which prevents the population from expanding. The U.S. Department of Agriculture has deployed teams and is testing suspect cases to identify additional infections. Officials emphasized that this combination of sterile fly release, enhanced surveillance, movement restrictions, prompt wound treatment, and public education represents a proven and effective control strategy.

The Texas Animal Health Commission had spent more than two years preparing for a potential resurgence of the parasite. "We are putting these preparations into action, and we encourage all animal owners and caretakers to continue to remain vigilant for the presence of larvae in animal wounds and immediately report any suspicions," said commission director Dr Bud Dinges.

A widespread outbreak could significantly impact the U.S. cattle industry. The country has faced economic pressures since the disease appeared in Central America more than a year ago. The U.S.-Mexico border has been closed to live cattle imports during this period, shrinking the roughly 100 billion dollar beef industry to a 75-year low. The United States formerly imported more than one million cattle annually from Mexico, representing approximately 4 to 5 percent of all cattle sold for beef production.

Canada's food inspection agency announced a temporary ban on livestock from Texas, prohibiting cattle and horses that were in the state within 21 days before crossing the border. Though officials noted the parasite is unlikely to establish itself in Canada's colder climate, they warned farmers to monitor livestock for wounds accompanied by discharge or foul odor.

The USDA confirmed that the U.S. food supply remains safe, as the screwworm does not infest meat, fruits, vegetables, or other food sources.

Yesterday, 11:55 PM ET

The Pentagon has designated Chinese tech giant Alibaba, electric vehicle manufacturer BYD, and search engine Baidu as companies with alleged ties to China's military, according to the Trump administration's updated list released four months ago. The designation serves as a warning to American businesses about potential risks associated with working with these flagged companies.

The list identifies Chinese entities believed to support the People's Liberation Army or China's defense industrial base. Pharmaceutical company WuXi AppTec also appears on the expanded catalog. The Pentagon uses this list as an advisory tool to highlight companies that US officials believe operate in support of Chinese military modernization efforts.

While the designation does not directly impose sanctions or prohibit transactions, it signals regulatory risks and increased scrutiny for American firms maintaining commercial relationships with listed companies. The list guides US businesses and investors in their decision-making regarding potential partnerships or investments involving these Chinese corporations.

Some companies have appeared and disappeared from the Pentagon's catalog in previous versions. The restoration of major names like Alibaba and Baidu to the current list reflects shifting dynamics in US-China economic relations, potentially affecting recent diplomatic efforts between Washington and Beijing.

The timing adds pressure to Chinese companies already navigating complex international trade restrictions. BYD, one of the world's largest electric vehicle makers, has been expanding its global sales outside the United States. Meanwhile, Alibaba and Baidu remain dominant forces in Chinese technology and cloud computing sectors.

Notably, the Trump administration released the updated list four months ago but then quickly removed it without providing an explanation for the decision. The subsequent restoration of these companies to the list marks a significant shift in policy direction.

The Pentagon designation could influence how these companies approach investment decisions and partnerships as they operate within an increasingly fragmented global technology landscape. For BYD specifically, the listing may complicate its efforts to expand electric vehicle operations internationally, despite the company's position as a global leader in EV manufacturing.

The inclusion of these major technology and manufacturing firms on the military-linked companies list underscores ongoing tensions in the US-China technology competition. As American officials continue evaluating which Chinese companies pose risks to US national security interests, businesses on both sides of the Pacific face uncertainty regarding future commercial relationships and regulatory requirements.

The Pentagon has not provided detailed explanations regarding the specific military connections attributed to each company on the list. The companies themselves have not publicly responded to their designations.

Yesterday, 11:49 PM ET

Apple announced a major overhaul of its Siri digital assistant at its Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday, integrating the voice tool with the company's new artificial intelligence platform called Apple Intelligence. The upgraded Siri, set to launch in the fall, will function more like advanced AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than a traditional search-based question-and-answer tool.

The announcement represents a significant shift in Apple's approach to artificial intelligence after years of delays and consumer frustration. The company has faced criticism for lagging behind competitors like Google in deploying AI features to its devices.

Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained that the company rebuilt Siri from the ground up with generative AI at its core. The redesigned assistant will have its own dedicated app and offer more conversational interactions. Powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a partnership between the two companies, the new Siri can access Apple's native app ecosystem on phones and computers to assist with planning, information retrieval and directions.

During a product demonstration, Rockwell showed how Siri identified a beach location from a photograph, then retrieved a friend's address and provided directions to the beach in Santa Cruz, California with a stop at the friend's house, all within the Siri interface. The tool will also handle proofreading, shopping, calendar management and camera functions like extracting nutrition information from food images. Initially, the service will only be available in English.

Alongside the Siri upgrade, Apple announced new child safety features designed to give parents greater control over their children's device use. The company acknowledged both the benefits of children having devices and the associated risks, particularly for children under 13. Parents will use an "easy-to-use setup assistant" to customize content their children can access and gradually expand permissions as they age.

New safeguards include requiring children to request permission before browsing unfamiliar websites in Safari and automatically blurring messages containing gore or violence. The screen time interface has been simplified to show parents which apps their children use and for how long. Parents can set daily time allowances either manually or following recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics, which Apple partnered with on these features.

The conference also featured updates to iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, along with new Apple Intelligence features integrated into Safari, messaging and the Home app. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, addressed the company's previous lag in AI development, stating that some competitors appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve."

The timing of these announcements comes after Apple agreed to settle a class-action lawsuit in May for $250 million, with some consumers receiving up to $95 in payouts over allegations that the company falsely advertised Siri's capabilities.

The standoff threatens to delay the passage of annual funding bills that keep federal agencies and programs operati...

Yesterday, 9:31 PM ET

The Senate appropriations process has ground to a halt as Republicans and Democrats remain unable to reach agreement on overall spending levels. Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins stated that Democrats are not willing to work with Republicans on the issue.

The standoff threatens to delay the passage of annual funding bills that keep federal agencies and programs operating. Without agreement on top-line spending numbers, appropriations subcommittees cannot finalize individual spending measures for different government departments. The impasse reflects broader partisan divisions over federal spending priorities and budget levels.

The appropriations process typically begins after congressional leadership and the White House negotiate an overall budget framework that sets spending caps for discretionary programs. These negotiations establish the parameters within which appropriators can craft individual bills. The current deadlock suggests those preliminary negotiations have broken down or failed to produce a workable compromise.

Congress faces statutory deadlines for passing appropriations bills to avoid government shutdowns. When lawmakers cannot agree on full-year funding, they often resort to continuing resolutions that maintain spending at previous levels for limited periods. Such stopgap measures allow the government to keep operating but prevent agencies from starting new initiatives or adjusting to changed circumstances.

The Senate Appropriations Committee, one of the most powerful panels in Congress, oversees the drafting of twelve annual spending bills that together fund the entire discretionary federal budget. The committee's work requires bipartisan cooperation because appropriations bills need sixty votes to advance in the Senate. Collins, a Maine Republican, chairs the committee and has historically sought to build consensus across party lines on spending measures.

Apple announced a major overhaul of its Siri digital assistant at its Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday, integrating the voice tool with the company's new artificial intelligence platform called Apple Intelligence. The upgraded Siri, set to launch in the fall, will function more like advanced AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than a traditional search-based question-and-answer tool.

The announcement represents a significant shift in Apple's approach to artificial intelligence after years of delays and consumer frustration. The company has faced criticism for lagging behind competitors like Google in deploying AI features to its devices.

Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained that the company rebuilt Siri from the ground up with generative AI at its core. The redesigned assistant will have its own dedicated app and offer more conversational interactions. Powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a partnership between the two companies, the new Siri can access Apple's native app ecosystem on phones and computers to assist with planning, information retrieval and directions.

During a product demonstration, Rockwell showed how Siri identified a beach location from a photograph, then retrieved a friend's address and provided directions to the beach in Santa Cruz, California with a stop at the friend's house, all within the Siri interface. The tool will also handle proofreading, shopping, calendar management and camera functions like extracting nutrition information from food images. Initially, the service will only be available in English.

Alongside the Siri upgrade, Apple announced new child safety features designed to give parents greater control over their children's device use. The company acknowledged both the benefits of children having devices and the associated risks, particularly for children under 13. Parents will use an "easy-to-use setup assistant" to customize content their children can access and gradually expand permissions as they age.

New safeguards include requiring children to request permission before browsing unfamiliar websites in Safari and automatically blurring messages containing gore or violence. The screen time interface has been simplified to show parents which apps their children use and for how long. Parents can set daily time allowances either manually or following recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics, which Apple partnered with on these features.

The conference also featured updates to iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, along with new Apple Intelligence features integrated into Safari, messaging and the Home app. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, addressed the company's previous lag in AI development, stating that some competitors appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve."

The timing of these announcements comes after Apple agreed to settle a class-action lawsuit in May for $250 million, with some consumers receiving up to $95 in payouts over allegations that the company falsely advertised Siri's capabilities.

The government also tightened rules on test rescheduling after thousands of no-shows. Learners will only be allowed to swap their test to the three centers nearest to their original booking location, reducing flexibility but aiming to improve test availability and reduce wasted appointment slots.
