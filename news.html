A dangerous heat wave is affecting the East Coast as Americans prepare for Fourth of July celebrations this weekend. The extreme temperatures threaten holiday activities across major cities and regions already experiencing record-breaking heat.

Temperatures are expected to climb well above normal for early July, with forecasters warning of dangerous conditions that could persist through the holiday weekend. The heat dome gripping the region has prompted weather alerts from authorities concerned about public safety during the peak summer season.

The timing creates challenges for Independence Day festivities planned across the affected areas. Millions of people preparing for outdoor gatherings and celebrations will need to contend with the severe weather conditions. Weather officials have indicated that the extreme temperatures could disrupt traditional holiday plans and activities throughout the weekend.

Beyond the heat itself, meteorologists are tracking additional weather risks. Storm possibilities are increasing as the heat wave continues, adding another layer of complexity to the forecast. The combination of extreme temperatures and potential severe weather means residents and officials coordinating public celebrations face significant planning obstacles.

The current conditions represent part of a larger pattern of extreme summer temperatures affecting multiple regions across North America. Public health officials are urging precautions as dangerous heat persists. Residents should stay hydrated, limit outdoor activity during the hottest parts of the day, and check on vulnerable neighbors who may be at greater risk during such conditions.

Water safety has become a particular concern during heat events. Authorities note that people often seek relief in water during extreme heat, but risks remain significant. The sea and other bodies of water can still present dangers, with cold-water shock possible at lower temperatures. Officials encourage anyone swimming to take proper safety precautions, choose lifeguarded beaches when possible, and never swim alone.

The heat wave comes as the nation marks America's 250th birthday with special celebrations and events planned throughout the weekend. Despite the weather challenges, communities across the region are moving forward with Independence Day plans, though organizers must adapt to the dangerous conditions.

Meteorologists indicate that the extreme heat could affect not only typical holiday gatherings but also special anniversary events and commemorative activities planned for the semiquincentennial celebration. Travel plans may also be disrupted as the heat persists through the weekend.

Weather services continue to issue warnings and updates as conditions develop. Residents are advised to monitor forecasts closely and take appropriate precautions when spending time outdoors. The combination of extreme heat, potential storms, and large holiday gatherings makes this Fourth of July weekend particularly challenging from a weather perspective across the affected East Coast regions.

Iran has begun a lengthy funeral ceremony for Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who died in February during coordinated U.S. and Israeli strikes on the country. The six-day event, which started Saturday in Tehran and will conclude Thursday with his burial in Mashhad, is expected to draw millions of Iranians.

Khamenei, who led Iran for 36 years, was killed at age 86 in the opening phase of the military strikes. His granddaughter, who was 14 months old, and three other family members also died in the same attack on a government residence in Tehran on February 28.

The funeral has been carefully orchestrated as a display of national resilience and resistance. Mohammad Reza Aref, Iran's first vice-president and lead funeral organizer, called the ceremony "the most important event of this century" and predicted it would be the largest gathering in Iran since the 1979 revolution. Organizers estimate that as many as 30 million people may attend.

Khamenei's coffin was first displayed Friday at an indoor ceremony for families of those killed in the war. The emotion was intense as crowds pushed forward, throwing scarves for attendants to brush against the coffin. His body was then carried across Tehran to the Grand Mosalla mosque, where it lay in state for three days before Friday's official ceremony.

On Friday, Iran's political, judicial, and military leadership paid their respects as the coffin was covered with the sacred flag of the shrine of Imam Husayn. Senior IRGC commander Mohsen Rezaee wept openly, and President Masoud Pezeshkian shed tears during the proceedings.

A collection of foreign dignitaries also attended, though their participation reflected Iran's limited relationships with regional powers. Leaders from Iraq, Pakistan, Armenia, and Tajikistan attended at senior levels, along with twelve heads of parliament mostly from Arab states. No Western leaders were invited. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei criticized European countries, saying they stood on the "wrong side of history" regarding the U.S. and Israeli attacks.

The funeral serves multiple purposes beyond mourning. Iranian leaders used the occasion to reinforce messages of defiance and resistance. General Ahmad Vahidi, commander-in-chief of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, made his first public appearance since February 8, vowing that Iran would never surrender. Other hardline officials spoke of blood vengeance and resistance to Western powers.

Khamenei's body will also be carried through the Iraqi Shia cities of Kerbala and Najaf at the request of Iraqi politicians. The elaborate ceremony is designed to project continuity and national strength during the ongoing conflict with the United States and Israel.

A notable absence from the proceedings is Mojtaba Khamenei, the supreme leader's son and successor, who was severely injured in the same strike that killed his father.

Banks are collecting more revenue from overdraft fees after Congress eliminated a rule last year that limited how much financial institutions could charge customers who spent more than their account balances. The regulatory change has allowed banks to return to higher fee structures that had been curtailed under the previous cap.

Overdraft fees occur when a bank processes a transaction that exceeds the available balance in a customer's account. Banks typically charge a flat fee for each overdraft transaction, and some institutions charge additional fees if the account remains overdrawn for multiple days. The fees have long been a significant revenue source for banks, particularly as interest rate competition and other market forces have pressured traditional banking income streams.

The cap that Congress removed had been designed to limit the financial burden on consumers who overdraw their accounts. Consumer advocates have argued that overdraft fees disproportionately affect lower-income customers who may have less financial cushion to avoid occasional account shortfalls. The fees can accumulate quickly, with some customers facing multiple charges in a single day if several transactions process while an account is overdrawn.

Banking industry representatives have argued that the cap restricted their ability to offer overdraft protection services and that the fees reflect the cost and risk of extending short-term credit to customers. Some banks have modified their overdraft policies in recent years, offering grace periods or linking checking accounts to savings accounts to provide automatic transfers instead of charging fees. However, overdraft fees remain a standard practice across much of the industry.

The increase in fee revenue comes as banks navigate a complex economic environment with shifting interest rates and evolving customer expectations around digital banking services. While some financial institutions have moved away from overdraft fees as a competitive differentiator, others have maintained or increased their fee structures following the elimination of the federal cap.

The regulatory landscape around banking practices has shifted significantly in recent years. Some major banks have previously announced targets to reduce certain practices or restrict particular lending activities, but these commitments have faced pressure amid changing political conditions. The trend suggests that voluntary commitments from financial institutions may not achieve desired outcomes without stronger regulatory oversight.

Industry observers note that the gap between banks' stated environmental and financial commitments and their actual practices has widened. This pattern reflects broader questions about how financial regulators, legislators and policymakers should approach oversight of banking practices.

The change in overdraft fee policies affects millions of Americans who use checking accounts for everyday transactions and may occasionally exceed their available balances. As banks continue to adjust their fee structures in response to the regulatory change, consumer advocates are likely to push for new protections against excessive overdraft charges.

Sony announced it will discontinue production of physical game discs for PlayStation consoles starting in January 2028, marking a major shift toward digital-only distribution. Following that date, new games will be available exclusively through PlayStation Store and digital retailers.

The decision reflects changing consumer behavior, with more players purchasing and downloading games directly through online platforms rather than buying physical copies. The timing coincides with major releases like Grand Theft Auto VI launching as digital downloads only, accelerating the industry's move away from physical media.

Sony's disc manufacturing operations have been extensive. According to reports, the company's Thalgau plant in Austria produces 600,000 discs daily, with half destined for PlayStation. Over its lifetime, the facility has produced 24 billion discs. The company plans to repurpose its disc manufacturing facility into a micro optics laboratory after production ends.

The announcement has prompted concern from game developers and publishers. Industry representatives expressed dismay at the decision, with some describing themselves as profoundly disappointed by Sony's choice. Critics raised questions about game preservation and consumer ownership, pointing out that players who purchase digital games do not own the content in the same way they own physical copies.

The shift follows broader industry trends. Physical game sales have declined significantly as digital distribution becomes the dominant sales channel. PlayStation Store has become the primary way many players access titles on Sony's platforms.

However, not all console manufacturers are expected to follow Sony's lead immediately. Industry analysts suggest Nintendo is unlikely to abandon physical games in the near term, given its strong physical game sales on the Switch platform and its different market positioning. Nintendo's consumer base has continued to show robust demand for physical copies.

The end of PlayStation disc production raises important questions about long-term game accessibility. Players who prefer physical copies will need to rely on existing inventory and secondary markets. The move has also reignited debate about whether consumers truly own digital content purchased through online platforms, particularly given that digital licenses can be revoked or platforms can shut down.

The decision represents a significant milestone in gaming history. For decades, physical media served as the standard way consumers purchased games. The shift to digital-only distribution marks a fundamental change in how games are distributed, accessed, and potentially owned.

Players with concerns about digital-only access have a window until January 2028 to purchase physical copies of games they want to preserve. The secondary market for PlayStation discs is expected to remain active for years to come as collectors and players seek physical copies.
