5:00 AM ET

The federal government has canceled student loans for approximately 450,000 borrowers who claimed their colleges defrauded them. The debt relief concludes a class-action lawsuit that has been tied up in courts for years, with borrowers asserting that their schools made false claims about educational quality, employment outcomes, and other important factors.

These borrowers argued that their institutions engaged in deceptive practices, leaving them with debt but without the education or career opportunities they had been promised. The lengthy legal battle left many uncertain about whether their debts would ultimately be forgiven or if they would remain obligated to repay loans for what they considered fraudulent educational programs.

Borrower defense to repayment is a federal provision that allows students to seek loan forgiveness if their school committed certain forms of misconduct. This process has proven controversial over the years, with different administrations taking varying approaches to evaluating and approving claims. Some governments have supported broader relief while others have required stricter standards for proving fraud occurred.

The settlement affects 450,000 borrowers whose loans were held by the federal government. The resolution concludes a protracted legal dispute that left many borrowers in legal limbo for an extended period. The exact total amount of debt canceled has not been disclosed in available information.

This decision may influence how the government addresses borrower defense claims going forward. Thousands of additional claims remain pending from students at other institutions across the country. The settlement also comes as student debt policy continues to be actively debated in government and policy circles, with ongoing discussions about loan forgiveness programs, income-driven repayment plans, and measures to hold colleges accountable when they fail to deliver on their educational promises.

Consumer advocates and borrowers have long pointed out inconsistencies in how the government has handled these claims. Some borrowers have waited years for decisions on their applications while owing debt for degrees that did not lead to promised career outcomes. The settlement represents recognition that these borrowers faced genuine harm through institutional misconduct.

The timing of this resolution reflects broader concerns about college accountability and student borrower protections. As enrollment costs have increased substantially over recent decades, more students have taken on significant debt to finance their education. When institutions misrepresent their programs or employment outcomes, borrowers bear the financial consequences.

This settlement marks one of the largest single actions to discharge student debt based on borrower defense claims to date. It signals a shift in how the government may evaluate these cases and could encourage other borrowers who believe they were similarly defrauded to pursue their own claims. The outcome also highlights ongoing tensions between accountability for educational institutions and the broader debate over how much responsibility the government should bear for student loan debt.

An estimated 49,000 migrants entered Ceuta, a Spanish enclave on Morocco's northern coast, over a 24-hour period, according to Spanish interior ministry officials. The arrivals represent more than half the population of the city, which has approximately 83,600 residents. At least 18 people died during the crossing, with some drowning and others caught in crushes, according to reports.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez traveled to Ceuta to address the crisis, visiting the Tarajal border crossing and meeting with local authorities, border officials, and police services. The situation presents a significant challenge for his pro-immigration government. Spain deployed military forces to the enclave following the mass crossing, and both Spanish and Moroccan authorities subsequently halted the flow of migrants across the border.

Sánchez characterized the incident as "an attack and a violation of Spain's territorial sovereignty," calling it "deplorable" and worthy of "our strongest possible condemnation." He stated the government would use "all the mechanisms and resources available to the state" to resolve the situation.

According to Sánchez, a recent Spanish court ruling regarding people intercepted while attempting to enter Ceuta and Melilla by sea appeared to have triggered the mass crossing. He said that a "self-serving" interpretation of the ruling had "spread like wildfire" through human trafficking networks, creating a "surge" in attempted crossings.

The crossing far exceeds previous incidents at the border. The 2021 border crisis saw approximately 10,000 people enter the territory. The sudden arrival of tens of thousands in a single day overwhelmed local infrastructure and emergency services.

The crisis sparked reactions from across Europe. Finland's interior minister Mari Rantanen said Spain had "completely failed to protect the Schengen Area's external border" and called for suspending Spain's Schengen status. Sweden's prime minister Ulf Kristersson urged Spain to "act swiftly to regain control" and said Sweden would take necessary measures if the situation threatened its security.

France responded by strengthening border controls with Spain and activating its Rapid Intervention Border Force for in-depth checks, according to French interior minister Laurent Nuñez. French far-right politician Marine Le Pen called for France to strengthen controls at its borders, while former prime minister Michel Barnier emphasized that people in irregular situations must be "effectively returned."

Spain's opposition People's party called for a swifter and tougher response, with leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo saying the military and police reinforcements announced were insufficient. The far-right Vox party characterized the influx as "an invasion."

Ceuta is one of two Spanish territories on the African continent and serves as the only land border between Africa and the European Union in the region. Its position has long made it a destination for migrants seeking entry to Europe.

South Korea's KOSPI index experienced significant volatility this week, falling as much as 12.6% before rebounding slightly, driven by disappointing earnings from major semiconductor manufacturers. The sharp decline marks a dramatic reversal from recent gains and reflects growing investor concerns about the sustainability of artificial intelligence spending.

The sell-off was triggered by SK Hynix, a chipmaker essential to AI data center expansion, reporting results that fell short of investor expectations. Despite posting record profits for the second quarter, the company's performance disappointed markets. SK Hynix shares dropped as much as 20% before recovering to a 10% loss. Fellow chipmaker Samsung Electronics also tumbled, closing 5% lower. The two companies together account for more than half of the KOSPI's market capitalization, giving them outsized influence over the index's movement.

The KOSPI closed down 6% after falling almost 11% the previous day, reaching its lowest level since early April. This represents an almost 40% decline from the peak reached just over a month ago. Trading was halted twice in two consecutive sessions after an 8% plunge triggered a market-wide circuit breaker.

Analysts attributed the sell-off to investor disappointment about the long-term outlook for chip demand. Gary Tan, a portfolio manager at Allspring Global Investments in Singapore, said: "SK Hynix delivered strong results, but in today's AI market strong is no longer enough. Investors were looking for additional catalysts, particularly around long-term agreements and shareholder returns, to support a memory sector that has become the epicentre of the AI trade."

The downturn reflects broader concerns across global markets about whether technology companies can sustain their massive spending on AI infrastructure. Shares in US chipmakers including Intel, Advanced Micro Devices, and others fell on Wall Street, while Taiwan's TSMC dropped 3%.

Analysts noted that retail investors who had driven the earlier rally, many using borrowed money, accelerated the sell-off as they withdrew funds. This volatility highlights how small-time investors leveraging credit amplified both the recent gains and the subsequent losses.

South Korea's finance minister, Koo Yun-cheol, told the national assembly that the government was reviewing market stabilization measures in response to the turbulence.

The KOSPI's sharp swings demonstrate how semiconductor stocks have become central to investor sentiment about the AI sector. While chipmakers benefit from massive capital expenditures by technology companies building data centers and computing infrastructure, concerns about whether current valuations reflect realistic returns have created significant market uncertainty in recent weeks.

Sony announced it will proceed with ending physical disc production for PlayStation games, maintaining its shift to digital-only distribution despite widespread protests from consumers. The company faced petitions, organized protests, and social media blackouts from gaming communities opposed to the decision, but executives indicated the move aligns with broader industry trends toward digitalization.

During a recent earnings call, Sony's chief financial officer Lin Tao stated that while the company "put in a lot of thought and time" considering the opposition, the transition reflects changes across entertainment media. A Sony representative noted that digitalization is progressing not just for PlayStation but for all kinds of content, framing the shift as part of an inevitable industry evolution rather than a PlayStation-specific strategy.

The announcement came just days after Sony revealed it would delete 550 movies from customers' digital libraries due to a licensing deal ending, illustrating the risks consumers face with digital purchases. The timing intensified backlash, particularly given Sony's 2013 marketing campaign mocking Microsoft's digital-first Xbox strategy by highlighting the ease of sharing physical games with friends on PlayStation.

The decision carries significant implications for game pricing and consumer ownership. Without physical disc production, players will no longer be able to purchase used games, resell titles they have finished, or buy cheaper games from retail sales. Digital-only distribution also eliminates the ability to share games with friends or trade them toward new purchases.

Industry observers note that ending physical media gives Sony greater control over pricing through its digital PlayStation Store, where the company controls all sales and can set its own prices. The shift also eliminates manufacturing and distribution costs associated with discs and could enable a cheaper PS6 console without disc drives. Data suggests approximately 80 percent of PlayStation games are already purchased digitally.

However, consumer advocates have raised concerns about Sony's monopoly on digital pricing without competition from physical retailers. Players also worry about long-term game preservation, as digital licenses can potentially be revoked or become inaccessible if online services shut down.

Game developers have expressed mixed reactions. Creators of upcoming titles like Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 called the decision sad and noted they specifically pushed for physical releases to give players tangible ownership of their work.

The response from gaming communities has been substantial, with vociferous reaction videos circulating on TikTok and YouTube. Brands including KFC and Domino's posted satirical announcements mocking Sony's decision on social media, while the Onion published a parody story claiming snack brand Twinkies would go digital-only. The timeline for when disc production will fully cease remains unclear, though the transition is already underway. Sony has not indicated any plans to reverse course despite the consumer resistance.

Franco Baresi, one of the most recognizable defenders of Italian football's golden era in the 1980s and 1990s, has died at the age of 66. AC Milan confirmed his passing on Friday.

"The entire history of AC Milan is in tears following the passing of Franco Baresi," the Italian club said in a statement. "His example and integrity will be forever etched into the club's DNA, just as his iconic No 6 shirt is."

Baresi spent his entire career at AC Milan, becoming inseparable from the club's identity during one of its most successful periods. As captain and defensive leader, he shaped Milan's dominance in Serie A and European competitions across two decades. His style of play combined physical strength with tactical intelligence and technical ability, allowing him to read the game and organize the defense at the highest level.

On the international stage, Baresi represented Italy with distinction, establishing himself as one of the finest defenders of his generation. He featured prominently in Italy's World Cup campaigns and earned recognition as one of the greatest players to represent the Azzurri. His performances at major tournaments cemented his status among Italian football's most celebrated figures.

Baresi's influence extended beyond individual matches and trophies. He fundamentally changed how defenders were perceived in football, demonstrating that those playing at the back could be as influential and celebrated as attacking players. This shift helped define an entire era of Italian football and set standards of excellence that influenced generations of defenders who followed.

After his playing career ended, Baresi maintained close ties to AC Milan, serving the club in various capacities and representing it as an ambassador. His presence remained a constant connection to the club's storied history and traditions.

The football community responded with widespread tributes following the announcement. Baresi had undergone surgery on a pulmonary nodule, according to reports at the time of his death.

His legacy in football extends beyond the silverware he won during his career. Baresi demonstrated that defenders could be central figures in a team's success, worthy of the same recognition and celebration as any other player. He embodied both the values of AC Milan and the broader traditions of Italian football, representing an approach to the game that emphasized tactical sophistication and defensive excellence.

The loss represents a significant moment for the global football community, particularly for those connected to AC Milan and Italian football. Franco Baresi's name remains synonymous with defensive mastery and leadership, and his passing marks the end of an era in the sport's history.

The coordinated opposition represents the most significant challenge yet to Infantino's plan to bring outside capital into the g...

5:10 AM ET

All 55 member associations of UEFA have pledged to boycott FIFA soccer competitions if the organization's president, Gianni Infantino, moves forward with his proposal to sell stakes in FIFA competitions to private investors based in the United States. The coordinated opposition represents the most significant challenge yet to Infantino's plan to bring outside capital into the global soccer body.

The decision came after an emergency meeting that lasted nearly two hours on Thursday. UEFA members issued a statement saying they "unanimously and unequivocally reject" the proposals. Despite initial concerns that smaller nations might be attracted by financial incentives in Infantino's scheme, the European football associations maintained a unified front.

The Asian Football Confederation and the North, Central America and Caribbean football association, known as Concacaf, have also opposed the proposal. This coordinated resistance across three continental confederations represents a substantial portion of FIFA's global membership and raises serious questions about the viability of Infantino's privatization effort.

UEFA's statement was direct: "As a result of today's discussion, no UEFA national teams will participate in any FIFA competition for so long as these proposals remain alive, unless this proposal has been abandoned in its entirety and binding assurances have been given that FIFA will never again open its governance or competitions to private ownership."

The proposed boycott would begin with next year's Women's World Cup, with age-group tournaments following. The England Football Association fully supported the collective stance, with its president Debbie Hewitt making what attendees described as a "very tough" case for the boycott during the meeting.

A World Cup boycott by UEFA nations would eliminate many of the tournament's most prominent teams and largest television markets. European nations have won 12 of the 23 World Cups held since 1930. The region also generates significant broadcasting and sponsorship revenue for FIFA events.

Infantino has promoted the stake sale plan as a way to increase revenue and expand the organization's financial capacity. Critics argue that selling stakes to private investors could compromise FIFA's independence and prioritize profit over the sport's global development.

The threat of coordinated boycotts gives the opposing confederations considerable leverage in negotiations over FIFA's future structure. European national associations have reportedly held discussions with their respective governments about the matter, with political leaders voicing support for the boycott.

The growing opposition across multiple continents suggests Infantino faces an uphill battle to implement his plan. Sources within European football believe the UEFA decision increases the likelihood of a Europe-backed candidate running against Infantino in next year's FIFA election, which he had been expected to win without opposition.

Senate Republicans near deal to avert shutdown and limit White House budget authority

Senate Republicans have reached an agreement with Democrats to prevent a government shutdown and avoid a partial lapse in funding set to begin over the weekend. The deal represents a significant compromise after weeks of negotiation over spending priorities and immigration enforcement practices. Under the agreement, Senate leadership will split the funding package by separatin...

6:00 AM ET

Senate Republicans have reached an agreement with Democrats to prevent a government shutdown and avoid a partial lapse in funding set to begin over the weekend. The deal represents a significant compromise after weeks of negotiation over spending priorities and immigration enforcement practices.

Under the agreement, Senate leadership will split the funding package by separating the Department of Homeland Security bill from other appropriations measures. The DHS will receive funding for two weeks at current levels, while the remaining government agencies would be funded through September. This approach allows lawmakers more time to negotiate over contentious immigration enforcement issues that Democrats have raised.

The deal emerged after a key Senate vote failed Thursday to advance a comprehensive spending package. All 47 Democratic senators voted against the measure, joined by seven Republican senators, preventing the bill from reaching the 60-vote threshold needed to overcome a filibuster. Senate Majority Leader John Thune voted no as well so he could bring the measure back for future consideration.

Democrats had refused to approve DHS funding without reforms to federal immigration enforcement following two deaths of U.S. citizens in Minneapolis involving ICE agents. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called for legislation to "rein ICE in" and demanded the DHS bill be set aside for further work. Schumer noted that Democrats were prepared to pass five bipartisan funding bills covering 96 percent of the federal government but needed substantial revisions to the DHS measure.

The deaths of residents Alex Pretti and Renee Good prompted Democrats to seek specific changes to agent conduct, including ending mask-wearing by federal agents, implementing a code of conduct, and establishing independent investigations into violations. Tom Homan, the Trump administration's border czar, acknowledged that "certain improvements could and should be made" but deferred specifics to Congress.

President Trump endorsed the spending agreement in a statement, calling it a bipartisan effort to fund most of the government while extending DHS funding. He urged both parties to vote yes on the revised package.

The House of Representatives approved the original six-bill package last week but remains out of session. Speaker Mike Johnson initially opposed splitting the funding package but indicated the House would move quickly if necessary. Johnson acknowledged a short shutdown could occur over the weekend before the House returns Monday.

A DHS funding lapse is unlikely to halt ICE deportation operations significantly, as the agency received tens of billions in funding under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act passed last year. The White House could also direct employees to continue working through a shutdown.

The agreement reflects compromise between both parties facing pressure to maintain government operations while addressing concerns about immigration enforcement practices under the Trump administration.
