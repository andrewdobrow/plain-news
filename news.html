Trump acknowledged that U.S. military personnel were already conducting mine-hunting operations, but extensive sweeping will be necessary before commerce can resume fully.

Some critics argue that the deal demonstrates that the war itself was fundamentally a costly mistake. The war disrupted global energy markets, causing fuel price spikes and economic strain across multiple continents. Yesterday, 10:34 PM ET

A massive fire erupted at a cold storage facility in the Boyle Heights neighborhood of Los Angeles on Wednesday, forcing city officials to issue shelter-in-place orders for nearby residents. The blaze engulfed the solar panel-covered roof of the 500,000 square foot warehouse owned by Lineage, sending thick clouds of dark smoke and ammonia into the air.

About 110 firefighters from the Los Angeles Fire Department responded to the afternoon fire, joined by hazmat teams from both the city and Los Angeles County. Firefighters initially attempted to extinguish the flames using hand lines on the roof, but were forced to retreat after an ammonia refrigeration line burst. "At some point, an ammonia line was compromised and we had a large, pressurized off-gassing of ammonia," LAFD Chief Jaime Moore told NBC News 4.

Cold storage facilities use ammonia as a cost-effective refrigerant, but the chemical is both toxic and flammable. The dangerous conditions prompted Mayor Karen Bass to urge residents in the affected area to stay indoors immediately, close windows and doors, turn off air conditioning, and avoid unnecessary travel to the area.

The fire grew so large and hazardous that firefighters took the unusual step of deploying helicopters to battle it from above. At least three helicopters delivered more than half a dozen water drops onto the burning roof while firefighters evacuated the immediate area. "The amount of fire that they had was overrunning the amount of water we were able to put on it and our firefighters had to evacuate the area for a few minutes there while it was off-gassing," Moore said.

Chief Moore warned residents with health conditions to avoid breathing the contaminated air by remaining indoors with windows closed and air conditioning turned off while the city monitored air quality for ammonia off-gassing.

By evening, the roof fire had been largely contained, though the roof showed signs of sagging. The presence of solar panels on the building raised additional concerns about potential lithium ion batteries used to store solar energy. These batteries can undergo "thermal runaway," a process that makes them extremely difficult to extinguish.

"It's extremely hard to fight the fire, it's dangerous to be around the fire, and it's dangerous to be around the extremely hazardous smoke," said Jennifer Middleton, an LAFD spokesperson, regarding lithium ion battery fires. Moore called these batteries "one of our newest challenges in the fire service."

Authorities have not yet disclosed the cause of the fire. Lineage did not immediately respond to requests for comment about the incident.

Yesterday, 8:26 PM ET

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at its latest policy meeting while shifting its forward guidance to suggest rate increases could happen before the end of the year. The central bank maintained rates at a range of 3.5% to 3.75%, where they have remained since December, with unanimous support from the voting committee.

This was the first meeting overseen by Kevin Warsh, who became Fed chair in May. The decision prompted a sharp market reaction, with US stock markets dropping on Wednesday afternoon. The Dow closed 500 points lower, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each fell over 1.2%.

In its policy statement, the Federal Reserve's open market committee noted that "economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East." The committee acknowledged that "inflation remains elevated relative to the committee's 2% goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy."

The Fed's latest projections showed a significant shift in outlook. Nine of the voting members projected at least one rate increase this year. Warsh confirmed he was the sole board member who did not contribute to the projections. This marks a dramatic reversal from March, when 12 of 19 officials had projected at least one rate cut by year's end.

Current inflation stands at 4.2%, the highest level since 2023 and well above the Fed's 2% target, driven largely by energy prices following Middle East conflicts. However, core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, has increased only modestly to 2.9%. The labor market remains relatively strong, with unemployment holding steady at 4.3%.

At his first press conference as chair, Warsh announced plans to overhaul the central bank's operations. He will establish five new taskforces to assess monetary policy broadly, including communications, the Fed's balance sheet, data, productivity and jobs. One taskforce will specifically reassess how the Fed communicates with the public, potentially including changes to news conferences, dot-graph projections, and meeting transcripts.

Warsh emphasized that monetary policy "cannot have a very significant effect on particularly prices" and that oil prices, food costs and other commodity fluctuations do not have first-order consequences for Fed policy. He stated: "The price of oil in the markets or the price of a dozen eggs does not have first-order consequences to what we're doing. But we have an important job there. And it's to make sure that those changes in oil, or beef, or eggs or milk don't broaden in the economy."

The Fed's policy statement was notably shorter than previous statements, reflecting Warsh's stated desire to tighten the central bank's public communications on future guidance.

Apple plans to increase prices for its iPhones in response to rising memory chip costs, CEO Tim Cook said in a recent interview. Cook described the situation as unsustainable, noting that the company faces huge cost increases that it has been trying to shield customers from, but price increases are now unavoidable.

The company faces mounting pressure from its advanced artificial intelligence features, which demand more memory than previous iPhone generations. The upcoming iPhone 18 will require 12GB of RAM to support enhanced Siri capabilities. This increased memory requirement has intensified demand for chips from suppliers Samsung and SK Hynix at a time when global supply remains constrained.

The memory chip shortage affects the entire smartphone industry, but Apple's situation carries particular weight given its market position and the scale of its production. The company ships hundreds of millions of iPhones annually, making component cost pressures significant business challenges. Cook's public acknowledgment signals that the company views the supply constraints as a long-term challenge rather than a temporary disruption.

The planned price increases mark a shift for Apple, which has historically absorbed component cost fluctuations to maintain stable pricing. The company has not yet announced specific price points or which iPhone models will see increases. Cook did not indicate when prices will rise in his interview. Industry analysts note that higher iPhone prices could affect sales volumes, particularly in price-sensitive markets, though Apple's customer base has historically shown willingness to pay premium prices for new features.

The AI-driven memory requirements represent a broader trend in the smartphone industry, where manufacturers race to integrate more sophisticated machine learning capabilities. These features require substantial on-device processing power and memory, creating new bottlenecks in the supply chain. Samsung and SK Hynix, the primary suppliers of high-capacity mobile memory chips, face the challenge of scaling production to meet the surge in demand while managing their own capacity constraints.

The shift toward more powerful AI capabilities on iPhones has created significant demand for additional RAM. Reports indicate that the base model iPhone 18 is likely to support all of the new Siri AI features, meaning entry-level devices will need the higher memory specifications previously reserved for premium models. This universal requirement across the iPhone lineup has intensified pressure on memory chip supplies.

Apple's acknowledgment of pricing pressure comes at a time when the company is balancing its ambitions for AI-powered features with the practical constraints of component availability and manufacturing capacity. The decision to raise prices rather than reduce memory specifications reflects the company's commitment to delivering the full AI experience across its iPhone range, even as costs to produce these devices continue to climb.

The performer known for roles in The Ring and Lilo & Stitch died from sepsis following meningitis.

Yesterday, 6:55 PM ET

Daveigh Chase, the actress and voice actor best known for portraying the ghost Samara Morgan in the 2002 horror film The Ring and voicing Lilo in Disney's Lilo & Stitch, has died at age 35. She passed away in a Los Angeles hospital from sepsis following meningitis, according to her longtime manager.

Chase built her career in both live-action and voice acting throughout her time in the entertainment industry, establishing herself as a notable figure in Hollywood from her early years as a child star.
