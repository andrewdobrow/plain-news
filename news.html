Paraguay's President Santiago Peña was impeached and placed under house arrest Friday after lawmakers unanimously voted to remove him from office over the collapse of Ueno Bank, a digital bank with links to his private business interests. The bank's demise has left more than half of Paraguay's population—an estimated three million depositors—unable to access their savings and pensions, prompting widespread protests particularly in the capital, Asunción. Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as interim president and will serve until 2028.

The impeachment passed with 44 of 45 senators voting in favor. Peña, who until last year held shares in the company that owned the bank, had offered to step down Thursday. Rather than accept his resignation, senators impeached him and charged him with misconduct, stripping him of the immunity usually granted to former presidents. Senate President Basilio Núñez said Peña was found guilty of dereliction of duty.

The impeachment charges alleged Peña had conflicts of interest, manipulated regulatory oversight, diverted pension funds, and participated in an institutional cover-up involving the central bank. Prosecutor Silvia Margarita Gonzalez Vester said Peña is due to be questioned Saturday over allegations of breach of trust, influence peddling, and misappropriation of funds. Peña has denied any wrongdoing.

Senator Rafael Filizzola called the situation the biggest corruption scandal in Paraguay's democratic history. In an address to the nation Thursday, Peña said he was sorry and that it was never his intention to betray the trust placed in him. Alliana pledged that citizens affected by the financial collapse would be fully compensated.

Wes Morrill, the project's lead engineer, wrote on social media that Cybertruck was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab, appearing to suggest the vehicle is being discontinued. Tesla stock rose 4.7 percent Friday after the announcement.

Sales fell to 20,237 vehicles in 2025 from 38,965 the previous year, according to Kelley Blue Book. Musk had predicted annual sales of 250,000 after commercial production began in 2023. The vehicle received 146,000 orders when Musk announced it in 2019, though its public introduction was marred when glass Musk claimed was unbreakable shattered during a live demonstration.

The Cybertruck faced several recalls, including one in 2025 affecting more than 46,000 vehicles over concerns that part of the trim could fall off. The vehicle's price also climbed significantly. Musk told analysts the company needed to make it at a price people could afford, which ended up being $61,000 for a fully equipped model. In 2026, the Cyberbeast model costs $102,000.

Musk had called the Cybertruck the best product Tesla has ever made, but questions persisted about whether it could succeed in the competitive U.S. truck market. Yesterday, 4:56 PM ET

Blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis said hackers obtained sensitive information for all of its users, including names, usernames, password hashes, and live security tokens, following a breach of its network. The company's customer list includes government agencies such as the FBI, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and the U.S. Air Force, as well as private companies including Microsoft, Coinbase, and Barclays.

Chainalysis disclosed last Friday that an external actor used AI agent-assisted offensive techniques to gain unauthorized access to its systems and internal tools during an October cyberattack. In an email to affected customers, the company initially said some customer data such as user IDs, full names, email addresses, and organization role labels had been accessed, describing the information as non-sensitive.

In an update Wednesday, Chainalysis said the ongoing multifront cyberattack included an on-path attack that exposed a subset of customer passwords. The company said it had identified and fixed the attack vector. On-path attacks, also known as adversary-in-the-middle attacks, occur when a hacker gains access to infrastructure transmitting traffic between two parties.

Chainalysis has not disclosed where the infrastructure compromise occurred or whether the data obtained was encrypted using TLS. The company has not said whether the data theft exposed customers to additional security threats.

Wiles submitted her resignation Thursday night after Trump blamed her for the election results in which Republicans lost control of Congress. Trump will install Miller, currently deputy chief of staff, in the position starting Monday.

Wiles had offered to resign for two days before Trump accepted her decision Friday. A source who spoke with Wiles said Trump did not directly ask for her resignation but could not stop blaming her rather than himself for the midterm losses. The source said Wiles recognized that if she could not change Trump's view, there was no reason to remain.

Wiles was Trump's longest-serving chief of staff and the first woman to hold the position. She earned an extraordinary level of trust from Trump and often served as the last person he consulted before major decisions. Her influence stemmed from managing his 2016 Florida campaign and his political comeback after the Jan. 6 riot. Wiles had privately opposed the war in Iran and hardline immigration enforcement championed by Miller, policies that contributed to Tuesday's Republican losses.
