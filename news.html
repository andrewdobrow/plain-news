A few minutes ago

Stock futures declined in early trading after the United States launched military strikes against Iran in what the U.S. characterized as self-defense operations. The action triggered immediate responses across global financial markets, with investors moving toward safer assets.

Asian stock markets dropped as tensions in the Middle East escalated. Oil prices gained on concerns about potential supply disruptions from the region, which accounts for a significant portion of global crude production. The moves reflected investor uncertainty about how the conflict might develop and what economic consequences could follow.

According to reports from major financial outlets including CNBC, Reuters, and Bloomberg, Asian shares declined while oil advanced as the U.S. conducted strikes against Iran. The market reaction underscores how sensitive investors are to geopolitical developments in the Middle East, particularly events that could disrupt energy supplies.

Energy markets are particularly sensitive to Middle East tensions due to the area's role in oil production and shipping routes. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global energy transportation, has been a focal point of concern. Any prolonged conflict could affect supply chains and fuel costs worldwide.

The situation became more complex when President Trump confirmed that Iran had shot down a U.S. Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. Two Army aviators were rescued by a drone boat following the incident. Trump stated that the United States "must" respond to the attack, signaling potential for further military escalation.

U.S. stock futures showed losses across major indexes as the news spread through Asian trading hours. The retreat followed a period of relative calm in markets, with investors now reassessing their risk exposure. Financial analysts are closely monitoring developments that could affect trading when U.S. markets open for regular sessions.

The heightened tensions come at a time when global markets have been closely tracking geopolitical developments in the region. Energy sector stocks and defense-related companies are likely to see increased trading activity as investors adjust their positions based on the changing landscape.

Market participants are watching both the military developments and any potential diplomatic responses. The situation continues to evolve, with traders attempting to gauge how international actors might respond to the escalating tensions. The combination of military strikes, confirmed downing of military aircraft, and presidential statements about responding to Iran creates significant uncertainty for investors trying to assess the risks to their portfolios.

The market movements reflect broader concerns about how extended conflict in the region could impact global economic stability, energy prices, and supply chain resilience across multiple industries.

12:01 AM ET

The nation's 9-year-old students have shown progress in both math and reading, reversing years of declining performance, according to new assessment data. The improvements mark a significant shift for elementary school children who were largely unaffected by pandemic-era school closures.

The gains stand in contrast to older students, who continue to struggle with academic performance. Thirteen-year-old students have not shown similar improvements, reflecting the lasting impact of disruptions to middle school education during the pandemic years.

The National Assessment of Educational Progress, often called the Nation's Report Card, tracks student performance across different age groups and subjects. The recent data represents the first meaningful upward movement in elementary school achievement after several years of stagnant or declining scores. Younger students typically attend more structured classroom environments and may have benefited from stabilized schooling conditions in recent years.

Education researchers point to several factors that may explain why younger students have recovered while older students have not. Nine-year-olds who experienced school closures were in earlier grades when disruptions occurred, potentially making it easier to remediate learning gaps. Middle school students faced closures during more critical academic years and may have experienced more severe social and educational disruptions.

The divergent outcomes between age groups present both encouraging signs and ongoing challenges for American education. While elementary schools appear to be successfully supporting younger learners, the persistent struggles of adolescent students suggest that recovery efforts may need to be intensified or redesigned for older age groups. Education officials will continue monitoring whether the gains among 9-year-olds can be sustained as these students progress through the school system.

Yesterday, 10:04 PM ET

The Directors Guild of America has reached a tentative four-year agreement with major studios, concluding this year's labor negotiations between the union and production companies with minimal public conflict. The deal was finalized through discussions between the DGA and the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers.

The union has not released specific details about the agreement's terms. Before the deal can take effect, the DGA's board must approve it, followed by a membership vote for final ratification. The organization has not announced when these approval votes will take place.

The DGA represents directors, assistant directors, unit production managers, and other production professionals in the film and television industry. The new contract replaces the previous agreement that had expired, necessitating the current negotiation cycle. The contract will establish working conditions, compensation, and employment terms for guild members on studio productions.

This year's negotiation process proceeded without significant dispute or public drama, which marks a departure from some previous labor cycles in entertainment. The DGA has historically maintained a cooperative relationship with studios compared to other entertainment industry unions, and the smooth conclusion of negotiations reflects this pattern.

The agreement addresses how guild members will work with major studios through 2030. The four-year term covers numerous issues affecting thousands of production professionals working across film and television projects throughout the industry.

The timing of this deal comes as the entertainment sector continues responding to substantial shifts in how content is produced and distributed. The rise of streaming platforms and changing workplace standards have reshaped the industry landscape, and these developments influenced the scope of negotiations. The agreement establishes a framework for DGA members to work under conditions that account for these evolving industry practices.

The relatively seamless conclusion of the DGA negotiations stands out as significant for the entertainment industry, which has experienced more contentious labor disputes in recent years. The guild's ability to reach consensus without extended deadlines or public pressure demonstrates its established working relationship with production companies and studios.

The contract covers all DGA members and applies broadly to studio productions, ensuring consistent standards across the major entertainment production sector. The agreement will affect compensation structures, working hours, and other conditions of employment for the thousands of professionals the guild represents.

With board and membership approval pending, the DGA has successfully concluded its negotiations ahead of the new contract period. The four-year agreement provides certainty for both guild members and studios as the entertainment industry continues adapting to technological and market changes. Once ratified by the membership, the contract will govern labor relations between the DGA and major studios for the duration of the four-year term.
