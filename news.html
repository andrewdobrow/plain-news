12:42 AM ET

Asian stock markets fell sharply as technology stocks continued a global selloff and oil prices jumped following Iranian missile strikes on Israel. South Korea's Kospi index plunged more than 5%, while markets in Japan also declined after weeks of gains in tech-related shares. U.S. stock futures traded mixed as investors assessed the escalating tensions in the Middle East.

The technology sector bore the brunt of selling pressure across the region. South Korean tech heavyweights followed a significant decline in Wall Street's artificial intelligence-linked stocks. On Thursday, the U.S. benchmark S&P 500 and Dow Jones industrial average each closed down 1.7%, while the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.3%. Major tech companies like Nvidia, valued at $4.5 trillion, led stocks lower, declining 3.6% as it continues to face fallout from Japanese investor SoftBank's disclosure that it had sold its entire stake in the firm for $5.8 billion.

The selloff reflects broader concerns that technology firms may now be overvalued after an extraordinary rally driven by artificial intelligence optimism that had propelled global stock markets to record highs. Analysts described the current situation as a correction in the artificial intelligence sector combined with market rotation. "There's a lot of uncertainties about the state of the economy," said Peter Cardillo, chief market economist at Spartan Capital Securities. "What we're going through is a little bit of a correction in the market in the AI sector and we're seeing market rotation."

Oil prices climbed more than three dollars per barrel after Iran fired missiles at Israel, raising doubts about the fragile ceasefire in the region. The attacks put renewed pressure on energy markets already strained by the effective shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz, which has stranded vast amounts of the world's oil supply. The price surge came even as OPEC Plus announced plans to increase output by 188,000 barrels per day, a move analysts described as largely symbolic given current supply constraints.

The dual pressures of geopolitical risk and sector-specific weakness created difficult conditions across Asia-Pacific markets. Investors shifted focus from growth stocks to assessing the potential economic impact of prolonged Middle East conflict. The combination of rising energy costs and falling equity values raised concerns about broader market stability.

The developments marked a sharp reversal from recent optimism. Market confidence has also been tested by remarks from senior Federal Reserve officials on interest rate policy, as well as uncertainty surrounding the U.S. economy following a lengthy government shutdown. Analysts pointed to how quickly market sentiment can shift when geopolitical events intersect with sector-specific weakness, underscoring the interconnection between energy security and technology sector performance.

Yesterday, 10:00 PM ET

Nvidia and Microsoft announced new computer hardware designed for artificial intelligence at the Computex trade show in Taiwan, marking a significant shift in how the tech industry approaches personal computing.

Nvidia introduced the RTX Spark, a superchip that integrates both a microprocessor and graphics processing capabilities. The company developed the chip with help from Taiwan's MediaTek and describes it as purpose-built to run AI agents directly on laptops and desktop computers. Rather than relying on cloud-based processing, the RTX Spark enables AI applications to operate locally on devices while maintaining thin, lightweight designs.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced that major computer manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP will use the RTX Spark chip paired with Microsoft's Windows software. Huang stated the chip would "reinvent the PC" for the AI era, following three years of collaboration between the two companies. He emphasized that the technology could fundamentally change how people interact with computers, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interfaces with AI agents that navigate PCs autonomously.

The announcement represents Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in data center processors into the consumer PC market. The company is also developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents and already being tested by companies including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX. These moves demonstrate Nvidia's strategy to establish itself across multiple levels of the AI technology stack.

Microsoft showcased the Surface Laptop Ultra, a new device specifically designed to handle AI workloads locally. The laptop incorporates Nvidia's chip technology and represents the companies' shared vision of AI-capable personal computers becoming mainstream consumer products.

Industry analysts view the RTX Spark announcement as historically significant. Neil Shah, a co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the moment to the introduction of the iPhone and the emergence of ChatGPT, suggesting the chip could transform PCs from application-focused devices into "agentic AI personal computers." However, analysts also note that establishing a consumer PC business will take considerable time despite Nvidia's current dominance in AI semiconductors.

The announcements signal broader industry competition intensifying in consumer AI chips. Intel announced plans to release an AI chip later this year using cheaper memory and cooling technology, positioning itself against Nvidia and AMD. Intel also unveiled a new graphics processing unit called Xe3P, developed specifically for the emerging generation of AI agents.

During the conference, Huang addressed concerns about AI's impact on employment, stating that suggestions the technology would reduce job demand were "complete nonsense." He argued that AI would increase productivity and lead to greater hiring of software engineers rather than job losses across the industry.

Yesterday, 11:48 PM ET

Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman dominated the 2026 Tony Awards, claiming six wins and becoming the most awarded play revival in the ceremony's history. The stripped-back revival earned best revival of a play, with director Joe Mantello accepting the award and reflecting on how Miller's story "still talks to us through time."

Nathan Lane accepted the award on behalf of the cast, calling it a play that "continues to teach us who we are as humans and Americans." His co-star Laurie Metcalf won her third Tony Award for best featured actress in a play, defeating June Squibb and Aya Cash. Death of a Salesman's six wins surpassed the previous record for most awarded play revival. The production has won major recognition before, claiming best play in 1949 and best revival in 1984, 1999, and 2012.

John Lithgow made history as the oldest man to win a competitive acting Tony at age 80. The veteran actor won best leading actor in a play for his role as Roald Dahl in the antisemitism drama Giant, beating Nathan Lane, Mark Strong, and Daniel Radcliffe. Lithgow also won an Olivier award for the same role during the show's West End run. His victory marked his third Tony and came 53 years after his first win. "It's an extraordinarily important play of this moment," he said on stage.

Lesley Manville won best leading actress in a play for her Broadway debut in the stage adaptation of Oedipus. The actor defeated Rose Byrne and Carrie Coon, calling it "such a big deal" to win on her first Broadway appearance after previously winning an Olivier for the same performance.

Other major winners included Liberation, which won best play after recently earning the Pulitzer Prize for Drama. Playwright Bess Wohl called it "the honour of a lifetime." The victory marks the first time a female playwright has won since 2009, making Wohl only the fourth woman to receive the award.

The musical Schmigadoon! took home best musical along with three additional awards. Producer Lorne Michaels accepted the award, noting that "sometimes singing, dancing, a lot of jokes and a happy ending is really all you need."

Ragtime won four Tonys, including best revival of a musical. Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy won leading actor and actress in a musical, respectively. Henry won his first Tony after four previous nominations. "Every artist in this room, every artist at home, fight, fight, fight to be heard," he said during his acceptance speech.

Pink hosted the ceremony for the first time, opening with a star-packed performance of Lady Marmalade featuring Lea Michele and Megan Thee Stallion. The singer called herself "Broadway's biggest fan" and paid tribute to the theater community's hardest workers.
