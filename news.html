A few minutes ago

Crude oil prices surged past $90 a barrel after a two-month window for negotiations between the United States and Iran expired without progress, triggering renewed military exchanges and rattling global energy markets.

Brent crude rose above $90 for the first time since late July, reaching $91.63 by Tuesday morning. The escalation came after Iran signaled it would adopt a more aggressive military stance if talks failed, while President Donald Trump demanded Tehran "put up the white flag of surrender" in a Fox News interview. Trump also threatened to bomb Oman if it interfered with his efforts to resolve the conflict, marking the second time he has directed such a threat at the U.S. strategic partner.

The prospect of expanded conflict alarmed energy analysts watching the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Roughly a quarter of all seaborne oil normally passes through the waterway. An Iranian military official warned that vessels attempting transit would "find several beautiful holes in their hulls," and a cargo ship was attacked while traveling through the strait early Tuesday.

Ship traffic through the strait plummeted as tensions mounted. Just six cargo vessels transited on Monday, according to ship tracking company Kpler, compared to only five combined on Saturday and Sunday. Angeline Ong, a senior technical analyst at IG, suggested Trump's threat against Oman could mark a turning point for oil markets. "If Muscat pulls back from talks with Tehran, the diplomatic route to restoring normal flows through Hormuz narrows considerably," Ong said, adding that investors might shift from betting on temporary disruption to pricing in prolonged supply problems.

Deutsche Bank analysts interpreted the rising oil prices as evidence that investors were preparing for an extended closure of the strait. The economic consequences rippled across markets. U.S. gasoline prices remained elevated at 37 percent higher than before the conflict began, continuing to pressure household budgets and inflation. Bond yields climbed to multi-year highs as investors reassessed growth and inflation expectations.

Trump indicated he was not rushing to end the conflict, telling Fox News he had "no time schedule" and was "not in a hurry" to reach a deal, despite claiming months earlier that resolution was imminent.

Geopolitical strategists warned of compounding risks. Dan Alamariu at Alpine Macro cautioned that escalation through the fall could produce a "double shock" if combined with Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine. He predicted energy market volatility would spike with sustained, intense Middle East conflict, particularly affecting oil and gas prices.

The developments prompted G20 finance officials to convene in the United States for meetings centered on the economic fallout from the Iran conflict.

A few minutes ago

A major flash flood swept through Arizona's Grand Canyon on Saturday afternoon, killing one person and leaving approximately 15 others missing or unaccounted for, according to the National Park Service.

The flooding occurred in the Bright Angel Canyon area near Phantom Ranch. Park staff and the Arizona Department of Public Safety evacuated more than 60 people by Sunday morning. The body of a 46-year-old man was recovered Sunday evening near Crystal Rapids along the Colorado River.

The disaster caused extensive damage to park infrastructure. The flood knocked out the sole water pipeline that supplies up to 6 million annual visitors and about 1,600 residents. This pipeline had been undergoing a $208 million upgrade since 2023. Nearly all footbridges spanning Bright Angel Creek were destroyed, and the Colorado River is closed to river traffic due to metal structures and debris in the water.

The National Park Service closed several key areas "until further notice," including Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, the Phantom Ranch Canteen and cabins, and the entire North Kaibab Trail from the North Kaibab Trailhead to Phantom Ranch. Starting Monday, tourists will not be able to stay overnight at the park's lodges and hotels. Residents have been asked to conserve water.

Park officials have requested public assistance in locating missing people. They are asking anyone with information about hikers or backpackers in the inner canyon along the Bright Angel Creek corridor on Saturday to contact them. They also want to hear from anyone who held a campground reservation in the affected area.

The intense rainfall created dangerous conditions in the steep terrain. Justin Johndrow, a National Weather Service meteorologist in Flagstaff, explained that approximately half an inch to one inch of rain fell within about 30 minutes in the rocky area with steep elevation changes. "Most of it ran off instantly," Johndrow said. The flooding occurred on the north side of the Grand Canyon where Bright Angel Creek descends about 6,000 feet from the North Rim to the Colorado River.

The large boulders swept into the Colorado River during the flood illustrate the tremendous power of the water. Meteorologists warned that more rain and thunderstorms were possible in the area into Monday.

Flash floods represent a persistent hazard in the Grand Canyon, particularly during monsoon season when intense thunderstorms develop rapidly over the Southwest. The remote locations and steep terrain of the canyon make rescue operations difficult and dangerous for emergency personnel. The incident highlights the risks that hikers and visitors face in the park, especially those in backcountry areas during severe weather events.

Novak Djokovic's US Open campaign ended in distress during a first-round match that lasted four hours and 36 minutes. The 39-year-old Serbian star was defeated 7-6 (5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1 by Argentina's Mariano Navone around midnight, leaving the court in tears.

This marks only the third first-round exit of Djokovic's career and his earliest departure from a Grand Slam since the 2006 Australian Open, when he was 18 years old. The loss came at his record-extending 84th Grand Slam appearance.

The match revealed serious physical struggles for Djokovic. Barely 15 minutes into play, he was already visibly struggling with the conditions. Throughout the five sets, he repeatedly vomited into the towel bin, experienced severe cramping, fought for balance, and appeared disoriented on court. Between panting desperately from the baseline and battling physical deterioration, he looked increasingly helpless as the match progressed.

Djokovic attributed his difficulties to a health condition exacerbated by heat and humidity. He described the experience bluntly after the match: "It is something that I've been carrying, unfortunately, pretty much every match this year. You know, vomiting, throwing up, serious issue there, and then my whole body starts to collapse, basically cramping and pain, and the back just gave up in the end."

This was not an isolated incident. Two weeks earlier at the Cincinnati Open, Djokovic struggled similarly against another Argentine player in comparable evening humidity conditions, losing in three sets to Thiago Agustín Tirante, who appeared unaffected by the weather.

On paper, this should have been a favorable draw. Navone, a 25-year-old ranked just inside the top 50, had built most of his success on clay courts. However, the Argentine player proved equal to the challenge. As a longtime admirer of Djokovic, Navone had posted supportive tweets about the Serbian star as a teenager and approached the match with focused intensity. His parents even abandoned a Caribbean holiday to attend after learning of the draw.

Navone played a solid match, maintaining consistent depth from both wings and forcing extended exchanges that Djokovic's deteriorating body could not handle. The younger player chased down every ball and kept the pressure consistently applied.

Despite his physical distress, Djokovic's serving kept him competitive for much of the evening. His exceptional serve allowed him to secure a two-sets-to-one lead through the fourth set, but by that point his legs felt heavy and his first serve began to falter. He could not recover, ultimately walking off court with nothing left to offer.

The defeat underscores the mounting challenges Djokovic faces at 39, competing at tennis's highest level against younger, physically fresher opponents.

Country music legend Dolly Parton was laid to rest in Nashville alongside her husband following a private family funeral. The singer died on Tuesday at the age of 80 after suffering from cancer.

Parton's death marks the loss of one of the most celebrated and influential figures in American music history. Over a career spanning more than six decades, she became known for her distinctive voice and songwriting, but also for her humanitarian work and business ventures.

The private ceremony reflects the family's desire for an intimate farewell to the entertainer who had touched millions of lives. Parton had been married to Carl Dean, who remained largely out of the public eye throughout their decades-long marriage.

Parton possessed a distinctive soprano with a remarkable vibrato and east Tennessee twang that made her voice among the most recognizable in music. Her wit was equally famous, with self-deprecating humor that endeared her to audiences worldwide. Her maxim "It takes a lot of money to look this cheap" captured her appetite for glitz and mischief that defined her public persona.

She was a pioneering figure in taking country music into the mainstream, becoming one of the first country stars to achieve significant crossover success. At the 2014 Glastonbury festival, she attracted what was reportedly the largest crowd in the event's history, performing while wearing a crystal-studded white suit and tooting a rhinestone saxophone.

Her songwriting prowess earned her recognition across the industry, with 10 Grammy awards, 11 from the Academy of Country Music, and numerous other honors. Her composition "I Will Always Love You" became one of the best-selling singles of all time after Whitney Houston recorded it for "The Bodyguard" soundtrack in 1992. The song earned Parton enough money, she said, "to buy Graceland" and proved that her refusal to share publishing rights with Elvis Presley when he wanted to record it had been the right decision.

Beyond music, Parton's charitable work reflected her commitment to helping others. From 1988, she provided support to local schoolchildren, and from 1995, she ran an international book reading programme. She also provided aid for people affected by wildfires and hurricanes and supported medical research. Her Imagination Library became known worldwide for distributing free books to millions of children, inspired in part by her father's inability to read.

Though known for her glamorous exterior and larger-than-life persona, Parton grounded her public image with deep intelligence and honesty. She remained active in her career and charitable work until recent years, leaving behind a lasting impact on generations of musicians who followed in her footsteps and a legacy that extended far beyond music into film, television, and popular culture.
