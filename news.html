Police are continuing their search for at least two suspects after a shooting near the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio left twelve people wounded on Saturday evening. The search entered its second day Sunday with no arrests made.

The gunfire erupted just after 5:30 p.m. near the historic neighborhood festival, which features live music and architectural home tours. Two victims remain in critical condition, according to Toledo police deputy chief Joseph Heffernan. The ages of those injured ranged from 14 to 61, with most victims in their early twenties.

Heffernan stated that the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the violence occurred. The incident took place in front of several hundred festival attendees. Despite the large police presence at the event, including extra officers and off-duty personnel working security, authorities have not apprehended any suspects.

"It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years," Heffernan said.

Law enforcement officials have appealed to the public for assistance in identifying the shooters. Authorities have asked witnesses to provide any photos or videos taken on their phones that might offer leads in the investigation.

Video footage captured the immediate aftermath of the shooting, showing festival attendees scrambling for cover as gunfire erupted. Additional video posted online showed a wounded young man walking down the street with blood visible from his injury before sitting down.

Festival organizers announced Sunday that remaining events would be cancelled following discussions with Toledo civic and police authorities. In a statement, organizers expressed their heartbreak over the incident and acknowledged the broader concern about violence at community celebrations.

"Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood," the statement said.

Ohio Governor Mike DeWine responded to the shooting with concern for public safety. "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence," DeWine said in a statement.

Fire Chief Allison Armstrong noted that closed roads and traffic congestion as people fled the festival created challenges in transporting victims to area hospitals.

According to the Gun Violence Archive, the Toledo shooting was one of more than 170 mass shootings in the United States so far this year. The archive defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more people are wounded or killed.

The investigation remains active as authorities work to locate the suspects.

OPEC Plus agreed to increase oil production by 188,000 barrels per day, marking the fourth consecutive monthly quota hike since the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. The cartel's decision comes as the critical shipping lane has experienced a virtual standstill, stranding vast amounts of global oil supplies and forcing producers to seek alternative transportation methods.

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, typically handles about 20 percent of global oil and liquefied natural gas trade. The flow through this crucial chokepoint has nearly halted since the US-Israeli conflict with Iran began on February 28. Approximately 850 vessels remain trapped in the Gulf, unable to safely navigate the waterway.

The cartel's move to boost output appears largely symbolic, as the additional production cannot reach many key markets through traditional routes while the strait remains effectively closed. With oil prices already about 50 percent higher than pre-conflict levels, OPEC Plus's repeated quota increases demonstrate an attempt to signal willingness to increase supply once shipping routes reopen.

President Donald Trump recently announced "Project Freedom," a plan to use US military assets to guide stranded ships out of the Strait of Hormuz. Trump stated on his social media platform Truth Social that the operation would represent a humanitarian gesture on behalf of the United States, Middle Eastern countries, and Iran specifically. US Central Command confirmed that two US-flagged merchant vessels had successfully transited through the strait.

However, the shipping industry has expressed significant concerns about the plan's viability. Iran's Fars news agency reported that a US warship attempting to pass through the strait was hit by two missiles and turned back after ignoring an Iranian warning, though the US denied this claim. Brent crude oil rose more than 5 percent to $114.45 per barrel following reports of a missile threat to the United Arab Emirates and a fire on a South Korean vessel in the Gulf.

Sascha Meijer, general secretary of the seafarers' union Nautilus, questioned whether the protection offered would be sufficient. "Seafarers stuck in the strait would really appreciate protection to get ships sailing out. But is this protection sure? How about mines? Are the ships insured?" she asked.

Jakob Larsen, chief safety and security officer at shipping industry group Bimco, emphasized that without Iranian consent for commercial ships to transit safely, it remains unclear whether Iran's threats can be addressed. "There is a risk of hostilities breaking out again if 'Project Freedom' goes ahead," Larsen stated.

The US military plans to deploy guided-missile destroyers, over 100 land and sea-based aircraft, unmanned platforms, and 15,000 service members to support the operation. The effectiveness of both OPEC Plus's production increases and Trump's rescue plan will depend on resolving the underlying conflict and establishing safe passage through the vital shipping lane.

Zverev reasserted control in the third set, reestablishing his service rhythm while Cobolli struggled under pressure. The momentum appeared to be shifting decisively toward the German, but the approaching finish line intensified his nerves. During the fourth set, Zverev looked overwhelmed by tension and appeared to be in the early stages of cramps. Yet Cobolli, feeling the moment even more acutely, was unable to capitalize.

The fourth set went to a tiebreak that Cobolli nearly won. He shanked a straightforward smash on his first set point but responded with a running forehand winner to force a fifth set.

The victory marks a significant milestone for Zverev, who joins the ranks of active Grand Slam champions after multiple near misses. The win came in his fourth Grand Slam final appearance and validates his status among tennis's elite players.

Alexander Zverev earned his first Grand Slam championship after defeating Flavio Cobolli in a five-set match at the French Open.

A few minutes ago

Alexander Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Italy's Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 7-6 (5), 6-1 in the final. The second-seeded German won the match when Cobolli missed an overhead shot on the second championship point, prompting Zverev to drop to his back in emotional celebration.

The victory marks a breakthrough moment for Zverev after reaching three previous Grand Slam finals without winning. The 27-year-old had already achieved success at Masters 1000 events, the ATP Finals, and Olympic gold, but a major title had eluded him until now.
