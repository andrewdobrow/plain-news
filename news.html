Yesterday, 6:49 PM ET

The Seattle Times and Newsday filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging the companies used their journalism without authorization to train artificial intelligence models. The lawsuit argues that generative AI could leave the journalism industry broken beyond repair by functioning as a system that destroys the organizations producing the content it depends on.

The complaint describes AI products like ChatGPT and Copilot as consumers that take human-authored content and deliver back copies and derivative imitations to achieve commercial objectives. The publications characterized generative AI as a snake eating its own tail.

The lawsuit follows a 2023 copyright infringement case filed by The New York Times against OpenAI and Microsoft, which other publications have since joined. The Seattle Times case is notable because Microsoft and OpenAI have funded some of the newspaper's journalism projects and fellowships.

A Microsoft spokesperson said the company is surprised by the lawsuit but is always happy to explore solutions to this type of dispute. The case adds to growing legal challenges against AI companies over their use of copyrighted material for model training.

Historical stock market data shows that the 34 years featuring a Republican president and a divided Congress produced an average annual return of 7.33% for the S&P 500, according to an analysis of nearly a century of market performance. A divided government occurs when one or both houses of Congress are controlled by Democrats while a Republican holds the White House.

While the data indicates more modest returns under this configuration compared to other political arrangements, the average annual return remains decisively positive. The historical record shows no indication that stocks plunge when congressional control is split, and analysts note that the makeup of Congress is rarely responsible for sharp market downturns, which are typically driven by other factors such as margin debt levels and equity valuations.

Hamilton started from pole position and led for the opening half of the 17-lap sprint, maintaining a one-second advantage over Antonelli as the pair opened a five-second gap on their competitors. However, Antonelli's superior pace allowed him to close the gap steadily. By lap seven, he was within three-tenths of a second, and on lap nine he swept past Hamilton to take the lead.

The Italian driver then extended his advantage ruthlessly, crossing the line with a two-and-a-half-second lead to secure his first Formula 1 sprint victory. Hamilton held on for second place in a hard-fought effort, despite being unable to match the speed of his teammate's Mercedes.

Lando Norris claimed third for McLaren after a frenetic battle involving George Russell and Max Verstappen. Russell, who started fifth, moved up to fourth after exploiting Mercedes' power advantage to pass Verstappen on lap ten. Norris had shown strong pace during the sprint, briefly taking Antonelli's position before the Italian reclaimed it on the same lap.

The result extends Antonelli's advantage in the championship standings, leading Russell by 43 points and Hamilton by 47 points. This marks another impressive performance from the second-year driver, who had been narrowly beaten to pole by Hamilton but demonstrated great determination over the sprint distance.

Hamilton will find some encouragement in Ferrari's competitive showing throughout the weekend. Despite the disappointment of losing the sprint to his teammate, the seven-time world champion fought hard to secure second place in front of his home crowd. His resilience and tactical driving earned him credit despite the power deficit that ultimately proved decisive.

The sprint format, which awards points to top finishers and determines part of the starting grid for Sunday's main race, has set up an intriguing dynamic within the Mercedes team. Both drivers will start near the front of the grid for the feature event, with the sprint performance confirming that Mercedes possesses strong pace at their home circuit.

Antonelli's victory suggests he will carry momentum into the main Grand Prix, while Hamilton has the opportunity to respond in the longer race format. The competitive showing from both Mercedes cars at the front of the field indicates the team will play a central role in the weekend's outcome.
