Tropical Storm Bertha made landfall on the northern Gulf Coast on Wednesday, bringing heavy rain and wind to Louisiana and surrounding states. The storm crossed the Gulf of Mexico before reaching the Louisiana coast, leading to beach closures and emergency preparations across coastal communities.

According to the National Hurricane Center, storm surges up to 4 feet are possible along parts of the Louisiana and Mississippi coasts. Residents in affected areas filled sandbags and cleared debris from drainage systems ahead of the storm's arrival. Local authorities coordinated emergency response plans and urged residents to stay informed about changing conditions.

Damage from the storm appears limited so far. Meteorologists say the El Niño weather pattern is expected to reduce the intensity of the storm's impact by countering some of Bertha's worst effects. The storm has a messy and lopsided structure, which affects its strength and trajectory.

However, forecasters warn that unusually warm water temperatures in the Atlantic Ocean mean the risk of destructive storms remains high throughout the hurricane season. The combination of El Niño conditions and warm Atlantic waters creates an uncertain forecast for the remainder of the season. While El Niño typically suppresses Atlantic hurricane activity by creating wind shear that disrupts storm formation, record-warm ocean temperatures provide fuel for storm development.

Tropical Storm Bertha is the second named storm of the Atlantic hurricane season. The National Hurricane Center has been tracking the system as it moved through the Gulf, issuing warnings and advisories for coastal areas.

Despite the reduced threat from this particular storm, meteorologists continue to monitor conditions closely. The warm Atlantic waters represent a significant factor that could influence storm development throughout the remainder of the season. Officials emphasize that while Bertha's impact may be limited, the broader conditions that fuel hurricane formation remain in place.

Residents and emergency management officials across Louisiana and surrounding states remained on alert as the storm moved inland. The National Hurricane Center continued to provide updates and guidance as conditions evolved. Coastal communities took precautions to minimize potential damage and ensure public safety during the storm's passage.

The forecast for the hurricane season ahead remains uncertain as competing atmospheric conditions interact. Meteorologists will continue tracking how El Niño patterns and ocean temperatures influence storm activity in the coming weeks and months.

Speaking to Southeast Asian leaders in Manila on Wednesday, Rubio warned that Iran's demand to dominate the strait threatens global economic stability and freedom of navigation. "If we create a precedent in the Middle East where a nation state can decide that they are going to control an international waterway, charge a toll and if you don't pay them, blow up your ships, we have created a very dangerous precedent which will repeat itself in other parts of the world, including in this region," Rubio said.

Rubio accused Iran of breaking commitments made under a previous peace agreement. While acknowledging that Iran has contacted the United States both directly and indirectly about talks, Rubio offered no indication that Washington would consider returning to diplomatic negotiations.

The military pressure continues as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz declines sharply. On Tuesday, only three commodity vessels transited the narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman. No large crude carriers or liquefied natural gas tankers passed through the strait that day, according to shipping data from Kpler. The dramatic reduction in traffic reflects concerns about the escalating conflict near one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints, through which roughly one-fifth of global oil consumption flows.

Meanwhile, the conflict's economic impact is spreading. Gas prices in the United States have continued rising since the Iran conflict resumed this month, with the American Automobile Association reporting prices just over four dollars per gallon, nearly a dollar higher than a year ago. Brent Crude, the international oil benchmark, has climbed almost two percent to $90.92 per barrel.

The situation extends beyond the Persian Gulf. The Jordanian army reported intercepting four Iranian missiles on Wednesday, with two additional missiles landing in remote, uninhabited areas without casualties or property damage. In the Red Sea, a Houthi-run news agency claimed that six ships received warnings and were forced to reroute, though the claim could not be independently verified.

The repeated nights of American attacks represent a significant shift toward sustained military engagement rather than isolated strikes. The conflict carries substantial implications for regional stability and global energy markets. Continued military operations near critical shipping routes threaten oil supplies to major economies across Asia and Europe, while threats to Iranian nuclear facilities could trigger broader regional escalation and complicate international nuclear monitoring efforts.

The policy aims to encourage pharmaceutical manufacturing to return to the United States, with the two-year delay providing domestic producers time to establish production capacity before the duties take effect.

The tariff announcement triggered immediate market reaction, with shares of generic drug makers declining following the news. Generic drugs represent approximately 90% of all prescriptions filled across the country, with the majority currently manufactured overseas, particularly in India and China. The proposed tariffs would effectively double the cost of imported medications, raising significant concerns about affordability and supply chain stability.

Trump's administration framed the measure as part of a comprehensive strategy to reduce American reliance on foreign pharmaceutical manufacturing. By implementing the tariffs, officials believe companies will be incentivized to establish or expand production facilities within U.S. borders. The extended timeline before implementation acknowledges that building new pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity requires substantial capital investment and regulatory clearance.

However, industry experts expressed skepticism about whether domestic production could scale adequately within two years to replace current import levels without creating shortages or driving up prices. Pharmaceutical manufacturing involves intricate supply chains for both active pharmaceutical ingredients and finished products. A tariff rate of 100% could render certain medications unaffordable for uninsured patients.

The policy represents one of the most aggressive tariff proposals specifically targeting the healthcare sector. Previous tariff initiatives focused primarily on manufactured goods, technology products, and raw materials. As Trump continues announcing new tariffs regularly, observers have noted the escalating nature of these trade measures. Democratic lawmakers have begun pledging to repeal Trump's tariffs if given the opportunity.

Healthcare industry groups have not yet released formal statements responding to the announcement, though the initial market response demonstrates investor concern about the plan's economic consequences. The proposal comes amid broader trade tensions and reflects the administration's stated commitment to reshoring American manufacturing across multiple industries.

The tariff plan also emerges within a context of rising U.S. gas prices and international trade disputes. Gas prices have climbed to over $4 per gallon, nearly a dollar higher than a year prior, amid renewed conflict with Iran. These economic conditions may influence public and congressional reaction to the pharmaceutical tariff proposal.

The two-year implementation window gives stakeholders time to prepare responses and potentially challenge the policy through legislative or regulatory channels before 2028.

Born in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria in July 1999, Rodriguez developed through youth football before joining Atletico Madrid in 2017. She moved to Deportivo La Coruña ahead of the 2019-20 season, where she helped the club secure fourth place in their first top-flight campaign and became recognized as one of Spain's most promising young goalkeepers.

Arsenal's summer recruitment has been notably active. Rodriguez joins Georgia Stanway, who arrived from Bayern Munich, along with Selina Cerci from Hoffenheim, Ona Batlle from Barcelona, and Geraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt. Arsenal head coach Renee Slegers welcomed the addition to her goalkeeping unit. "Misa is a top goalkeeper who has performed consistently at a high level for several years," Slegers said. "As well as her technical attributes, she has great presence and communication on the pitch and we believe she'll be a strong addition to our goalkeeping unit, alongside Daphne van Domselaar, Anneke Borbe and Isabella Damm."

Rodriguez will compete with the existing goalkeepers in the squad as the club prepares for the upcoming campaign. This is a significant time for George, as he will begin secondary school at Eton College in September. The prestigious institution continues a family tradition, with his father Prince William having attended the same school.
