OpenAI Files for Stock Market Debut as AI Companies Rush to Public Markets

The artificial intelligence giant is expected to achieve one of the most highly valued listings in market history, with a valuation exceeding $850 billion. The company submitted a confidential S-1 filing to the Securities and Exchange Commission, which allows reg...

4:02 AM ET

OpenAI has filed confidentially to go public on the US stock market, according to an announcement from the company. The artificial intelligence giant is expected to achieve one of the most highly valued listings in market history, with a valuation exceeding $850 billion.

The company submitted a confidential S-1 filing to the Securities and Exchange Commission, which allows regulators to review financial disclosures before public disclosure. "We expect it to leak so we're just announcing it," the company said in a blog post. The company acknowledged that timing remains uncertain, noting that certain activities may be easier to pursue as a private company, but that going public could become advantageous depending on circumstances.

OpenAI's move comes one week after rival Anthropic, which produces the Claude chatbot, announced plans to file for a public listing. This intensifies competition among leading AI developers to access capital markets for funding their operations.

The IPO filing marks a significant milestone for OpenAI, which began as a nonprofit research laboratory in 2015 before converting to a for-profit structure. The company gained massive prominence after releasing ChatGPT in 2022, which rapidly accumulated hundreds of millions of users and transformed how people work across entertainment, healthcare, education, and other sectors.

The competitive landscape for AI company listings has expanded further, with SpaceX, which owns Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company xAI, also preparing to go public at an expected valuation of $1.75 trillion. OpenAI closed a funding round in March valued at approximately $852 billion, demonstrating investor confidence despite the company's difficulty achieving profitability and meeting certain revenue targets.

The path forward contains potential obstacles. OpenAI recently prevailed in a lawsuit brought by Musk, who sought to force the company to revert to nonprofit status. A jury panel ruled that the case fell outside the statute of limitations. However, the company faces more than a dozen additional lawsuits alleging that ChatGPT has exacerbated mental health crises and contributed to violent incidents.

Despite legal scrutiny, OpenAI has continued attracting major partnerships with Microsoft, Google, Nvidia, and the Trump administration. The company has also pursued expansion beyond its core chatbot, though with mixed results. It acquired Jony Ive's startup and launched video generation software called Sora before shuttering that application in April 2026.

The wave of AI company public offerings reflects broader investor appetite for the sector. China's recent export data showed semiconductor and technology shipments surging, partly driven by AI-related demand. The concentration of major AI firms seeking public listings within a compressed timeframe suggests a significant shift in how these technology companies finance growth, moving from private funding rounds toward traditional stock market access.

Israel carried out fresh airstrikes in southern Lebanon on Tuesday while also ordering residents of 16 villages to evacuate northward. The military operations targeted areas where Hezbollah remains active, continuing a conflict that has killed at least 2,534 people in Lebanon since fighting began on March 2.

The strikes followed Hezbollah's deployment of small, fiber-optic-guided drones against Israeli soldiers in southern Lebanon. These drones, which can travel up to 9 miles and evade Israeli air defenses due to their wired guidance systems, have been used to attack Israeli forces almost daily since a ceasefire was established on April 17. Hezbollah claimed the Tuesday attack wounded several Israeli soldiers, though the Israeli military did not confirm casualties beyond stating that interceptor missiles were fired at incoming drones.

The continued fighting undermines the nominal ceasefire agreement. A ceasefire text allows Israel to conduct strikes in self-defense, language identical to a 2024 agreement under which Israel struck Lebanon more than 15,000 times. Hezbollah has rejected the ceasefire negotiations, with group leader Naim Qassem calling direct talks between Lebanon and Israel a "grave sin" on Monday. Hezbollah stated it will not surrender its weapons, a key Israeli and Lebanese government demand.

The military operations coincide with stalled negotiations between the United States and Iran. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said any permanent truce must include a deal on Iran's nuclear program. This statement came after Tehran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil supply, in exchange for the United States lifting its blockade of the waterway.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Hezbollah still poses threats to northern Israel and promised continued strikes against what he described as Hezbollah infrastructure. Netanyahu told Trump that Israel needed to respond to Hezbollah attacks to restrain the group. The US subsequently asked Israel to ensure its response remained "calculated and limited."

The ceasefire was originally negotiated after the US requested Israel enter talks with Lebanon, apparently to prevent Israeli military operations from disrupting Iran negotiations. Since April 17, Israel and Lebanon have held two ambassador-level meetings, including one at the White House with Trump present.

Lebanon's government has limited ability to control Hezbollah's actions, a reality complicated by the country's history of inter-communal conflict. Analysts have questioned whether normalizing relations between Lebanon and Israel, which Trump seeks as a diplomatic achievement, remains realistic given widespread Lebanese opposition to Israel. A more achievable outcome may be a limited armistice agreement rather than full normalization.

Cancer survivors face lasting mental health challenges as survival rates improve

More Americans are surviving cancer than ever before, but advances in treatment and screening have revealed a growing challenge: many survivors face lasting mental health impacts including anxiety and depression that persist long after treatment ends. Improvements in cancer screening and treatment have significantly increased survival rates across the United States. Early dete...

5:00 AM ET

More Americans are surviving cancer than ever before, but advances in treatment and screening have revealed a growing challenge: many survivors face lasting mental health impacts including anxiety and depression that persist long after treatment ends.

Improvements in cancer screening and treatment have significantly increased survival rates across the United States. Early detection programs and more effective therapies have allowed patients to live longer, healthier lives after their diagnoses. However, as the population of cancer survivors has grown, medical researchers and clinicians have identified a persistent pattern of psychological distress that extends well beyond the completion of treatment.

Cancer survivors commonly report experiencing anxiety and depression that can last for years after their initial diagnosis and treatment. The psychological toll of facing a life-threatening illness, undergoing intensive medical procedures, and living with uncertainty about recurrence creates mental health burdens that do not simply disappear when treatment ends. These emotional challenges can affect quality of life, relationships, and overall wellbeing even as physical health improves.

The recognition of these lasting mental health impacts has prompted calls for more comprehensive survivorship care that addresses psychological needs alongside physical recovery. Medical professionals are increasingly acknowledging that successful cancer treatment must include support for the emotional and mental health consequences of the disease. As survival rates continue to improve and more people live with and beyond cancer, understanding and addressing these psychological challenges has become a priority for the healthcare system.

The growing awareness of mental health issues among cancer survivors reflects a broader shift in how the medical community approaches cancer care. Rather than focusing solely on eliminating the disease, providers are now working to support the whole person through treatment and beyond, recognizing that survival encompasses both physical and mental wellbeing.

Trump nominates Todd Blanche as attorney general

Bondi faced criticism over her handlin...

6:52 AM ET

President Trump has officially nominated Todd Blanche to serve as attorney general, positioning his former personal lawyer to lead the Department of Justice. The announcement came on Monday and will require Senate confirmation to become permanent.

Blanche has been serving as acting attorney general since April, when Trump fired Pam Bondi. Bondi faced criticism over her handling of files related to Jeffrey Epstein, an issue that has repeatedly affected the president. Trump praised Blanche in recent comments, calling him "a very talented guy."

The former federal prosecutor from New York has represented Trump in multiple legal cases. He defended the president against charges related to hush-money payments to adult film actress Stormy Daniels, in which Trump was convicted on 34 felony counts for fraudulently falsifying business records. Blanche also represented Trump in the federal classified documents case and election obstruction cases, both of which were resolved following Trump's electoral victory.

Since becoming acting attorney general, Blanche has pursued what he describes as an anti-weaponization agenda. In recent statements, he accused the Department of Justice of targeting pro-life individuals and promised to prevent such actions in the future. His priorities include addressing what he calls weaponization within the department and making America safe.

Republican Senator Chuck Grassley, chair of the Senate Judiciary Committee overseeing confirmations, called Blanche "well-qualified." Grassley stated he has worked effectively with the acting attorney general for over a year and praised his commitment to transparency and law enforcement.

However, Democratic opposition to the nomination is mounting. Senator Adam Schiff from California argued that Blanche has "been unable to put aside his role as Donald Trump's criminal defense lawyer and represent the American people instead." Schiff pointed to several controversial actions under Blanche's leadership, including the indictment of former FBI Director James Comey, an exemption from IRS audits and prosecution for Trump and his family, and approval of a 1.8 billion dollar fund to compensate Trump supporters who claim political prosecution.

Senator Chris Van Hollen, a Democrat from Maryland, raised concerns about Blanche's handling of Epstein-related disclosures at a recent hearing. Representative Rosa DeLauro from Connecticut also criticized the fund compensating Trump supporters, suggesting such actions undermine impartiality in the role.

The 1.8 billion dollar fund became particularly contentious, facing bipartisan opposition. Republican Brian Fitzpatrick from Pennsylvania drafted legislation with bipartisan support to block the fund's creation. Blanche abruptly announced the fund had been cancelled on Tuesday amid widespread criticism.

The Senate Judiciary Committee will hold confirmation hearings to examine Blanche's record and approach to leading the Justice Department. Lawmakers from both parties are expected to question him on prosecutorial independence and his relationship with the president.
