The timing of this announcement reflects growing concerns about the humanitarian and economic toll of regional instability. Researchers at the Penn Wharton Budget Model have calculated a specific threshold at which U.S. government debt becomes unsustainable, warning that interest payments could trigger a default crisis. The analysis comes as forecasters predict the country will reach its $41 trillion debt ceiling as soon as late winter.

The study focuses on the point where debt service costs consume such a large portion of federal revenue that the government cannot meet its obligations without fundamental fiscal changes. Interest payments on the national debt have grown substantially as borrowing levels have increased and interest rates have risen from their historic lows.

According to the Penn Wharton Budget Model, federal debt may reach unsustainable levels by spring 2026. Multiple forecasting organizations including the Bipartisan Policy Center are now tracking when the federal government will exhaust its borrowing authority under the current debt limit. The debt ceiling has become a recurring point of contention in Washington, with previous standoffs rattling financial markets.

The research provides lawmakers with data on the long-term fiscal trajectory and the constraints imposed by growing debt service. As the debt-to-GDP ratio climbs and interest rates remain elevated compared to the past decade, the share of federal revenue dedicated to interest payments continues to increase. This leaves less room for discretionary spending and could force difficult choices about taxation and government programs.

The analysis adds to a broader debate about fiscal sustainability and the federal budget. While some economists argue that the U.S. can safely carry higher debt levels given the dollar's reserve currency status and investor demand for Treasury securities, others warn that rising interest burdens create vulnerabilities. The research attempts to quantify where those risks become acute rather than theoretical.

The urgency of addressing debt sustainability is underscored by global economic conditions. Rising energy and fertilizer costs, along with increased global interest rates, are straining government budgets worldwide. The International Monetary Fund has warned that developing countries face particular exposure to rising interest rates and currency instability, challenges that could ripple through global markets.

As governments grapple with debt repayment obligations, budget constraints force difficult choices about spending priorities. When nations reduce public spending to accommodate higher debt costs, vulnerable populations often bear the heaviest burden. This dynamic highlights why policymakers must carefully consider the consequences of fiscal decisions.

The Penn Wharton research comes at a critical moment for U.S. fiscal policy. Congress will need to confront questions about raising or suspending the debt ceiling to avoid default on U.S. obligations. The findings suggest that addressing long-term fiscal imbalances requires more than temporary solutions, demanding instead fundamental reforms to taxation and government spending that reflect the nation's fiscal constraints going forward.

OpenAI has released a new security feature called Lockdown Mode designed to protect sensitive data from prompt injection attacks. The feature aims to prevent malicious prompts from tricking ChatGPT into revealing confidential information stored in its memory or shared during conversations.

Prompt injection attacks occur when users craft specific instructions that manipulate AI systems into bypassing security restrictions or leaking protected data. These attacks have become a significant concern as businesses increasingly integrate large language models into their operations. By exploiting weaknesses in how AI models interpret instructions, attackers can extract sensitive information or override safety guardrails.

Lockdown Mode represents OpenAI's attempt to reduce the risk of these exploits, particularly for enterprise users handling confidential data. The company acknowledges that even with the new protection, ChatGPT may still be vulnerable to some prompt injection techniques. The goal is not to eliminate the risk entirely but to reduce the likelihood that sensitive data gets shared when attacks occur.

The introduction of Lockdown Mode highlights the ongoing security challenges facing AI companies as their models become more widely deployed in sensitive environments. Organizations using AI chatbots to handle customer service, internal communications, or proprietary research face particular risks if attackers can manipulate these systems into revealing protected information.

OpenAI's transparent acknowledgment that vulnerabilities may persist even with Lockdown Mode reflects the difficulty of completely securing large language models against sophisticated attacks. Security researchers have repeatedly demonstrated that AI systems can be tricked through carefully crafted prompts, and the cat-and-mouse game between developers and attackers continues to evolve as the technology matures.

After landing the role of Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Head relocated to Los Angeles for five years while his partner Sarah and two young daughters remained in England. He managed the long-distance arrangement by returning home every three or four weeks whenever production schedules allowed. Despite his rising profile, Head remained grounded, telling The Guardian that he avoided getting caught up in the competitive nature of celebrity.

Following Buffy's conclusion, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain and appeared in the BBC production of Merlin. His daughters said their father "always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people in such wonderful productions across a career that spanned several decades."

Head's partner Sarah Fisher died in December 2025 at age 61. His legacy continues through the television shows and films he appeared in throughout his extensive career.

The US defense secretary used a speech in Normandy to attack Europe over migration policy.

7:52 PM ET

US Defense Secretary Hegseth delivered remarks in Normandy on the 82nd anniversary of D-Day, using the occasion to criticize European migration policies. He drew a comparison between the historic allied invasion and current immigration patterns in Europe, describing migration as a beach "invasion."

The speech drew attention for blending commentary on contemporary European immigration issues with a ceremony commemorating the 1944 operation that liberated Nazi-occupied northwestern Europe from Nazi occupation.

Scotland defeats Bolivia in World Cup preparation match

Scotland beat Bolivia ahead of a major World Cup match next week.

7:31 PM ET

Scotland defeated Bolivia 4-0 in a World Cup warm-up match, scoring four goals in the first half. The victory comes one week before their opening World Cup fixture against Haiti, marking Scotland's first World Cup appearance in 28 years.

Manager Steve Clarke's team displayed ruthless efficiency in the friendly. The commanding performance has boosted confidence ahead of what many consider the biggest game in Scottish football history. If Scotland can replicate this level of clinical play in the tournament proper, they have a genuine chance of advancing from the group stage for the first time ever.
