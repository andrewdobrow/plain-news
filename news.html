The Food and Drug Administration has authorized certain Zyn nicotine pouches to be marketed as presenting lower health risks than cigarettes. The decision allows manufacturers to make comparative claims about these products to consumers.

Nicotine pouches are small packets placed between the gum and lip that release nicotine without smoke or the need to spit. They come in various flavors and have grown significantly in popularity, particularly among young people. The products contain no tobacco but deliver nicotine, avoiding the tar and carcinogens produced by burning cigarettes.

Public health experts have expressed mixed reactions to the FDA's decision. Many acknowledge that nicotine pouches can serve as a harm reduction tool for current smokers trying to move away from combustible tobacco. Research suggests the pouches carry substantially lower health risks than cigarettes and may be less harmful than e-cigarettes.

However, significant concerns remain about youth adoption. Recent British research published in the Lancet Public Health found that nicotine pouch use among 16- to 24-year-olds jumped from 0.7% in 2022 to 4% in 2025. Overall use in Great Britain rose from 0.1% in 2020 to 1% in 2025, representing about 522,000 people. The sharpest increases occurred among young men.

Dr. Harry Tattan-Birch, lead author of the British research, attributed the growth to "aggressive advertising targeting this group on social media, billboards, in bars and train stations, and through sponsorships of motorsports and music festivals." He emphasized that while pouches pose lower risks than cigarettes, they remain "not harmless."

A troubling finding from the research showed that 16% of pouch users had never regularly smoked, suggesting the products are attracting people who would not otherwise use nicotine. Additionally, more than two-thirds of users also consumed other nicotine products, indicating concurrent use rather than replacement of cigarettes.

Public health advocates warn that marketing these products as lower risk could inadvertently appeal to adolescents and young adults who might not otherwise develop nicotine addiction. Caroline Cerny, deputy chief executive of Ash, called the marketing "heavy and indiscriminate," noting that "they shouldn't be used by children or people who don't already smoke, due to the addictive nature of nicotine."

Researchers stressed the importance of implementing age-of-sale legislation, advertising restrictions, and regulations on flavors, packaging, and nicotine content. The FDA continues balancing harm reduction for current smokers against preventing youth initiation of nicotine use as it evaluates tobacco products based on their broader public health impact.

Electronic Arts, the video game publisher behind franchises including The Sims and EA FC, has been acquired by Saudi Arabia's wealth fund alongside a group of investors for $55 billion. The deal was completed on Tuesday evening, shortly after the European Union provided final regulatory approval. The transaction takes the company into private ownership, ending its 36-year history as a publicly traded company.

The acquisition involves Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund working alongside Affinity Partners, a private equity firm run by Jared Kushner, Donald Trump's son-in-law, and Silver Lake Partners. Kushner commented on the purchase, stating "EA has created stories, characters, and communities that have become part of everyday life for hundreds of millions of people. We're excited to support the company as it continues to reach new audiences, inspire the next generation of creators, and expand the ways people around the world connect through play."

This represents one of the largest buyouts on record. Electronic Arts is best known for its blockbuster sports games, particularly EA Sports FC, which was previously branded as FIFA. The company's portfolio also includes Madden NFL, Battlefield military shooters, and The Sims life simulation series.

Founded in 1982 by former Apple employee William "Trip" Hawkins, EA has been led by chief executive Andrew Wilson since 2013. The Saudi royal family has shown increasing interest in the gaming and esports sectors in recent years, alongside investments in sports, media, and entertainment. Saudi Arabia's crown prince, Mohammed bin Salman, has a reputation as an avid gamer himself.

The acquisition comes as EA's growth has slowed. The company's annual revenues have stagnated in recent years, hovering between $7.4 billion and $7.6 billion. The gaming sector has become increasingly competitive, with mobile game makers like Epic Games gaining ground. Microsoft's 2023 acquisition of Activision Blizzard for almost $69 billion demonstrated the scale of consolidation in the industry.

Going private removes certain pressures that public companies face. As a private entity, EA will no longer be required to report its financial results each quarter, eliminating some scrutiny over meeting quarterly targets. However, privatizations sometimes lead to job cuts and cost reductions as new owners restructure operations.

EA has already undergone workforce changes in recent years. The company laid off approximately 5 percent of its workforce in 2024 and cut several hundred jobs in May 2025, though it maintained around 14,500 employees in March 2025. The company reported lower-than-expected revenues in its most recent quarter, which the company attributed to declining player engagement with its latest Battlefield release. There has been no indication from the new ownership that further significant job cuts are planned.

Yesterday, 8:16 PM ET

A four-ton piece of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket has crashed into the moon's surface at approximately 8,700 kilometers per hour in an unintentional collision that poses no danger to Earth. The rocket stage, which had been drifting in space since January 2025, struck the lunar surface on Wednesday as astronomers had predicted.

The object was part of a Falcon 9 rocket that launched a lunar lander for US company Firefly Aerospace toward the moon in January 2025. Because the lunar mission required more thrust than typical Earth-orbit operations, the rocket's second stage remained in space rather than falling back to Earth's atmosphere. The stage then became space junk among thousands of other debris pieces that satellites must avoid.

According to Julianna Scheiman, SpaceX's director of NASA science and Dragon programmes, the collision resulted from orbital mechanics. "What has happened is essentially a mixture of solar activity and gravity forces have put it on a path toward the moon," she said.

NASA estimated the impact would create a crater approximately 60 feet wide and 12 feet deep, ejecting dust and rock across the lunar surface. The space agency announced plans to observe the impact site before and after the collision to collect data. A NASA spokesperson said the agency would use this information to "better understand artificial impacts and their exploration implications" and to "refine techniques for tracking objects in space."

Professional and amateur astronomers using high-quality telescopes initially struggled to confirm the impact, possibly because the impact site was located near the moon's visible edges. The dust plume, while expected to stretch for miles, would be difficult to observe with the naked eye from Earth.

Lunar impacts from human-made objects remain rare. A Chinese rocket stage crashed into the moon in March 2022 after completing a lunar test mission. NASA intentionally crashed a rocket stage into the moon in 2009 to study the resulting dust plume. Several spacecraft intended to land softly on the moon have crashed in recent years, including Russia's Luna-25 mission in 2023 and India's Chandrayaan-2 lander in 2019.

Bill Gray, creator of widely used astronomy software who published a report on the stage's trajectory in April, noted the incident's limited scientific value. "This may be of some, probably minor, scientific interest, and we may learn some things from it. It doesn't present any danger to anyone, though it does highlight a certain carelessness about how leftover space hardware is disposed of," he said.

NASA and SpaceX are now discussing methods to prevent similar unintentional lunar impacts in the future. The incident underscores growing concerns about space debris management as launch activity increases globally.

A four-ton piece of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket has crashed into the moon's surface at approximately 8,700 kilometers per hour in an unintentional collision that poses no danger to Earth. The rocket stage, which had been drifting in space since January 2025, struck the lunar surface on Wednesday as astronomers had predicted.

The object was part of a Falcon 9 rocket that launched a lunar lander for US company Firefly Aerospace toward the moon in January 2025. Because the lunar mission required more thrust than typical Earth-orbit operations, the rocket's second stage remained in space rather than falling back to Earth's atmosphere. The stage then became space junk among thousands of other debris pieces that satellites must avoid.

According to Julianna Scheiman, SpaceX's director of NASA science and Dragon programmes, the collision resulted from orbital mechanics. "What has happened is essentially a mixture of solar activity and gravity forces have put it on a path toward the moon," she said.

NASA estimated the impact would create a crater approximately 60 feet wide and 12 feet deep, ejecting dust and rock across the lunar surface. The space agency announced plans to observe the impact site before and after the collision to collect data. A NASA spokesperson said the agency would use this information to "better understand artificial impacts and their exploration implications" and to "refine techniques for tracking objects in space."

Professional and amateur astronomers using high-quality telescopes initially struggled to confirm the impact, possibly because the impact site was located near the moon's visible edges. The dust plume, while expected to stretch for miles, would be difficult to observe with the naked eye from Earth.

Lunar impacts from human-made objects remain rare. A Chinese rocket stage crashed into the moon in March 2022 after completing a lunar test mission. NASA intentionally crashed a rocket stage into the moon in 2009 to study the resulting dust plume. Several spacecraft intended to land softly on the moon have crashed in recent years, including Russia's Luna-25 mission in 2023 and India's Chandrayaan-2 lander in 2019.

Bill Gray, creator of widely used astronomy software who published a report on the stage's trajectory in April, noted the incident's limited scientific value. "This may be of some, probably minor, scientific interest, and we may learn some things from it. It doesn't present any danger to anyone, though it does highlight a certain carelessness about how leftover space hardware is disposed of," he said.

NASA and SpaceX are now discussing methods to prevent similar unintentional lunar impacts in the future. The incident underscores growing concerns about space debris management as launch activity increases globally.
