The U.S. Treasury Department moved to cut off cryptocurrency exchange MEXC from the American financial system over allegations the platform processed $16 billion in illicit transactions linked to Iran, North Korea and Russia. The action targets MEXC Global for alleged sanctions violations.

The Treasury decision blocks MEXC from accessing U.S. financial infrastructure and conducting transactions with American counterparties. The enforcement measure represents one of the largest actions against a cryptocurrency exchange based on alleged illicit transaction volume.

The alleged $16 billion in flows involve transactions connected to sanctioned countries including Iran, North Korea and Russia. The Treasury did not specify the time period over which the transactions allegedly occurred or detail the nature of the illicit activity.

In response to the Treasury action, digital asset company NatGold Digital announced it would immediately delist its NATG token from MEXC. The move reflects the immediate market consequences for platforms cut off from the U.S. financial system.

Yesterday, 1:26 PM ET

Elon Musk told investors on November 6 that SpaceX could acquire a legacy telecommunications carrier and use its airwaves ten times more effectively than current operators, though he named no target and no transaction has been filed. Musk made the comments by video at the Baron Investment Conference in New York, adding that any purchase would be conditional on SpaceX using spectrum better than the incumbent. Verizon and AT&T each declined 0.89 percent following the remarks, while SpaceX rose 8.62 percent to $134.27.

Deutsche Telekom chief executive Tim Hoettges said on a November 5 earnings call that SpaceX would need at least 200 billion euros, or roughly $220 billion, to compete with established U.S. carriers. Hoettges, whose company controls T-Mobile, estimated SpaceX holds 76 megahertz of spectrum compared with approximately 400 megahertz for T-Mobile, and said leasing tower space alone would cost $20 billion to $30 billion. He added that his company must still assume SpaceX will enter the market.

Any acquisition of a major carrier would require SpaceX to assume significant debt, including Verizon's $166.2 billion or AT&T's $159.6 billion, along with labor agreements, retail operations, and landline networks. Both antitrust regulators and the Federal Communications Commission would need to approve such a transaction. The three major carriers together were valued at approximately $507 billion, while SpaceX was worth roughly $1.77 trillion.

SpaceX purchased AWS-4 and H-block licenses from EchoStar in September 2025 for approximately $17 billion and has agreed to buy nationwide 800-megahertz licenses, with closing expected in the first half of 2027. Analysts noted that at its current price, SpaceX stock likely already assumes its Starlink Mobile service will succeed, and a carrier acquisition would add substantial debt and regulatory complexity to that assumption.

Yesterday, 4:15 PM ET

NASA announced Saturday that it is accepting applications for a new astronaut class to support its Artemis program, which aims to establish a permanent lunar base. The space agency made the announcement during an air show at Kennedy Space Center focused on lunar exploration and aviation technology.

Aspiring astronauts have until December 3 to submit applications. NASA expects to make its final selection by late 2027, with new candidates beginning training by early 2028. The timing aligns with NASA's plan to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028 after more than fifty years.

The recruitment comes as NASA faces a personnel shortage relative to its expanding mission. The agency currently has only 32 active astronauts. Its last astronaut call two years ago drew more than 8,000 applicants, with 10 making the final cut in September 2025. Artemis III commander Randy Bresnik said the agency has far more work than available astronauts as it works to expand human presence beyond low-Earth orbit. The Artemis II crew completed a lunar flyaround in April, launching the lunar campaign.

Candidates must be U.S. citizens with college degrees and professional work experience. The mandatory two years of training can be grueling, and astronauts may wait several additional years before flying to space. Artemis III astronaut Frank Rubio, who holds the U.S. record for consecutive days in space at 371, cautioned that going to the moon represents about one percent of an astronaut's work, with most time spent contributing to mission support. This will be NASA's 25th astronaut class since the original Mercury Seven in 1959.

Australia will face New Zealand in the final in Brisbane next Sunday.

The match began with an intense confrontation during Samoa's ceremonial Siva Tau war dance, with players getting face-to-face with Australian opponents. Samoa took an early 3-0 lead when Stephen Crichton converted a penalty, but Australia quickly responded when Tom Dearden scored from a scrum. Australia added two more tries after Samoa discipline lapses gave them six-again advantages, with Jacob Preston and Tom Trbojevic scoring. Walsh added his first try and celebrated with a backflip, and Bradman Best scored before halftime to extend the lead.

Samoa's frustration grew as several refereeing decisions went against them. The most controversial came at the half-hour mark when Crichton appeared to have scored after Francis Manuleleua beat Walsh to a high ball, but replay review ruled Manuleleua had knocked on. Samoa's Spencer Leniu was sin-binned before halftime for speaking out of turn to referee Adam Gee. Samoa managed second-half tries from Crichton and Izack Tago, but Walsh responded quickly after both scores to maintain Australia's comfortable margin.

Australia, winners of the past three world titles, extended their dominance in the tournament. Walsh completed his first international hat-trick, and Bostock added a late try. Trbojevic's try was his eighth of the tournament, maintaining his position as joint-leading try scorer at the World Cup.

The three major carriers together were valued at approximately $507 billion, while SpaceX was worth roughly $1.77 trillion.

Oxford researchers demonstrated the Aharonov-Bohm effect in a lattice gauge theory using a quantum computer, a step toward simulating particle interactions beyond classical computing limits.

Yesterday, 1:00 PM ET

University of Oxford physicists used a trapped-ion quantum computer to simulate a lattice gauge theory in two dimensions, demonstrating how magnetic flux affects particle motion. The team used strontium ions' electronic states to represent gauge fields and their vibrations to encode matter particles, creating a closed loop where flux could be stored in entangled qubits.

In the experiment, particles tunneled freely between sites when flux was zero but remained frozen when flux was present, confirming the Aharonov-Bohm effect. The approach captures interactions between matter and gauge fields that earlier simulations could not, though the current single-loop system remains small enough for classical computers to solve. Researchers aim to scale to larger lattices that would exceed classical computing capabilities.
