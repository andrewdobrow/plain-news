The Italian 10th seed defeated fourth-seeded Felix Auger-Aliassime 4-6, 6-4, 6-4, 6-4 in the quarterfin...

1:49 AM ET

The French Open is guaranteed to crown a first-time Grand Slam champion this year after the men's and women's semifinals produced four players who have never won a major tournament.

In the men's draw, Flavio Cobolli reached the semifinals after a remarkable comeback victory. The Italian 10th seed defeated fourth-seeded Felix Auger-Aliassime 4-6, 6-4, 6-4, 6-4 in the quarterfinals, recovering from a set and a break down. This was Cobolli's first win against a top 10 opponent at a Grand Slam. His triumph set up an all-Italian men's semifinal for the first time in history, as Matteo Arnaldi also advanced when fellow Italian Matteo Berrettini retired injured.

Cobolli's path through the tournament has been efficient and authoritative. Through five matches, he has conceded just two sets while playing decisive tennis when it matters most. His game combines a destructive forehand with well-rounded skills that have allowed him to neutralize opponents' weapons. Against Auger-Aliassime, Cobolli pinned the Canadian in his backhand corner and narrowed the court with superior defensive abilities after breaking serve at 3-1 in the first set.

The loss represents a significant missed opportunity for Auger-Aliassime, who at 25 has been among the tour's longest-serving players without a major title. The Canadian was the highest-ranked player remaining in his half after Jannik Sinner's early exit, giving him a genuine chance to compete for the championship.

On the women's side, qualifier Maja Chwalinska reached the final with a stunning 7-6, 6-4 victory over 25th seed Diana Shnaider. Chwalinska became the first qualifier ever to reach a Roland Garros final. Her emotional response after the win, sobbing into her towel, reflected the magnitude of her achievement after coming through an extraordinarily difficult period in her life.

The pressure of competing for a first major title will test all four semifinalists in different ways. Cobolli and Arnaldi must navigate their historic matchup while maintaining the form that brought them this far. Chwalinska carries the weight of her journey and the expectations that come with being the first qualifier to reach this final. Her opponent in the women's final will also be pursuing their first Grand Slam title.

The absence of established champions in both semifinals means that Sunday's finals will feature players competing for major titles for the first time in their careers. How each handles the pressure and intensity of that moment could determine who leaves Roland Garros with their name etched into Grand Slam history.

Chinese President Xi Jinping will travel to North Korea on June 8-9 to meet with leader Kim Jong Un, marking his first visit to the country in five years. The trip occurs just weeks after Xi held separate meetings with the leaders of the United States and Russia, both of whom significantly influence North Korea's foreign policy direction.

The announcement of Xi's journey has drawn considerable attention among foreign policy experts. Satellite imagery had sparked early speculation about the visit before official confirmation came through. The timing suggests China wants to reassert its position in Pyongyang during a period when North Korea has grown closer to Russia in recent years.

Xi will encounter a more independent Kim Jong Un than in previous encounters. North Korea's strengthening alliance with Russia has diminished Pyongyang's traditional reliance on China for economic support and diplomatic backing. This shift has given Kim greater negotiating power with Beijing, which could complicate Xi's efforts to influence North Korean decisions on nuclear weapons and broader regional security matters.

The visit represents an uncommon face-to-face meeting between the two leaders. China has historically been North Korea's main economic supporter and has defended the country at the United Nations, but the relationship has experienced tension as Kim has pursued stronger connections with Moscow. The Russian-North Korean partnership has involved military cooperation and economic dealings that work outside traditional Chinese networks.

Xi's decision to travel to Pyongyang shows China recognizes the need to actively engage with Kim to preserve influence over the Korean Peninsula. The meetings will probably discuss regional security issues, economic partnership opportunities, and the shifting power dynamics in Northeast Asia as the United States, China, and Russia manage an increasingly strained geopolitical situation.

North Korea's emboldened position stems partly from its reduced dependence on Chinese support. As Russia has become a more significant ally, Kim has gained leverage in his dealings with Beijing. This dynamic makes Xi's visit particularly significant, as he attempts to maintain China's role as North Korea's primary international backer during a time of geopolitical realignment.

The visit underscores how the broader competition between major powers continues to reshape relationships across East Asia. China's need to court Kim demonstrates the stakes involved in maintaining regional influence. As these three powers navigate their competing interests, North Korea's position as a strategic actor has become more valuable and more contested than it has been in previous years.

Trump directs $700 million to coal industry under wartime authority

During a White House ceremony on Wednesday, Trump signed an executive order directing the...

Yesterday, 8:03 PM ET

President Trump has announced a major investment in the coal industry, framing it as a national security priority during the ongoing conflict with Iran. The funding represents a significant federal intervention in energy markets at a time when fuel prices are affecting American consumers.

During a White House ceremony on Wednesday, Trump signed an executive order directing the Department of Defense to purchase long-term power from coal plants for military installations and other critical facilities. "We're going to be buying a lot of coal through the military now," Trump said at the event, which included coal executives, miners, and Republican lawmakers.

The announcement included an additional allocation of $175 million from the Department of Energy to modernize and extend the operation of coal-fired power plants serving rural communities in West Virginia, Ohio, North Carolina, and Kentucky. The administration framed both initiatives as part of a broader strategy to increase domestic energy production and reduce reliance on foreign oil markets during wartime.

The coal industry has welcomed the support. James Grech, CEO of Peabody Energy, the largest coal company in the United States, praised Trump's actions. "We stand here today representing the thousands of coalminers across the country to express our deep gratitude to you, sir, for the actions you've taken to support our industry," Grech said.

This latest move follows other administration efforts to revive coal. In September, the White House announced $625 million for coal-fired power plants and opened 13.1 million acres of public land to coal mining.

The investment marks a significant policy shift toward fossil fuel production despite long-term industry trends. Coal has experienced sharp decline in recent decades. According to the Energy Information Administration, U.S. coal production in 2023 was less than half its 2008 level, and coal plants generated only about 15 percent of American electricity in 2024, compared to roughly 50 percent in 2000.

Energy analysts note that coal production faces structural challenges that extend beyond current market conditions. Aging infrastructure and competition from cheaper natural gas plants have created persistent headwinds for the industry. The federal investment could provide short-term support to coal producers, but economists question whether it can address the longer-term economic pressures reshaping the energy sector.

Trump stated that the administration aims to position the United States as "a massive energy exporter" and said the government is "lifting up our hard-working American miners like nobody has ever done before." The funding relies on presidential authority typically reserved for national security emergencies, with the administration citing energy supply disruptions from the Iran conflict as justification for the intervention.

Yesterday, 9:00 PM ET

Senate Republicans on Thursday blocked a Democratic attempt to prevent the creation of a $1.8 billion anti-weaponization fund, though the narrow vote revealed growing divisions within the president's own party.

Democratic Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer proposed language that would have barred the payouts as part of Republican-backed legislation to fund Trump's mass deportation campaign. The amendment failed 49-50 after a three-hour voting session that saw senators huddling on the chamber floor.

Three Republican senators broke ranks to support the amendment. Susan Collins of Maine, Dan Sullivan of Alaska, and Jon Husted of Ohio all voted with Democrats. All three represent seats that Democrats are targeting in the midterm elections. Husted faces particular pressure, trailing his Democratic challenger by eight points in a recent Fox News poll.

The fund proposal has sparked significant bipartisan concern. Trump's plan would issue financial settlements to people connected to the January 6 insurrection. Acting U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche told lawmakers earlier this week that the administration would not move forward with the fund. However, Schumer argued that a formal legal ban was necessary.

"Republicans are trusting the word of Todd Blanche, who built a career on lying," Schumer said. He pointed out that Trump had expressed his enthusiasm for the fund just the day before, calling it important. Schumer questioned whether Trump could resist using the fund for his own benefit.

Schumer proposed his amendment to a $70 billion bill funding immigration enforcement agencies through 2029. Republicans are using the budget reconciliation process to bypass Democratic filibuster rules and pass the legislation along party lines. This funding bill has been at the center of a major standoff between the parties since January, when Democrats refused to approve appropriations without reforms, leading to a 75-day partial shutdown of the Department of Homeland Security.

After Schumer's amendment failed, Republican Senator Thom Tillis proposed alternative language that would prevent payments from the anti-weaponization fund and redirect that money toward Justice Department fraud efforts. Tillis argued the move would eliminate an unpopular measure while funding important work.

"This bill is unpopular. This administration has said they're not moving forward with it. This is an opportunity for us to put it to bed and to also fund the fraud division, which I think is very important," Tillis said.

Democrats rejected Tillis's amendment, with Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon calling it insufficient. "Taking one slush fund and eliminating it and creating a new slush fund still under the control of the attorney general is not the way to go," Merkley said.

Tillis's proposal also failed, receiving support from only 11 Republicans and three Democrats.
