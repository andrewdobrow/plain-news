6:46 PM ET

The Federal Reserve kept interest rates unchanged at its first meeting under new chairman Kevin Warsh, while signaling that rate increases could come before the end of the year. The decision to maintain rates in the 3.5% to 3.75% range received unanimous support from the Fed's voting committee.

The announcement sent stock markets lower, with the Dow falling 500 points and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each dropping over 1.2%. The decline reflected market concern about the Fed's shift toward potential rate hikes rather than cuts.

In his inaugural press conference as chair, Warsh announced a comprehensive overhaul of the central bank's operations and communications. He plans to establish five taskforces to examine the Fed's approach to monetary policy, including how it communicates with the public, manages its balance sheet, and assesses economic data and employment trends. The groups will draw from "some of the very best minds, both inside and outside the economics profession," Warsh said.

One taskforce will specifically review the Fed's public communications, including news conferences, the dot-plot projections of future rate changes, and meeting transcripts. Warsh emphasized that he remains open-minded about potential reforms but declined to specify what changes might result from the review.

The Fed's policy statement was notably shorter than previous versions, reflecting Warsh's stated desire to tighten public communications about future guidance. Officials acknowledged that inflation remains elevated compared to the Fed's 2% target, partly due to supply disruptions that have driven up energy prices following the Middle East conflict. The committee said it remains committed to delivering price stability.

Fed policymakers released new projections showing a dramatic shift in outlook. Nine of the voting members projected at least one rate increase this year, reversing the March projections when 12 of 19 officials expected rate cuts. Warsh was the sole board member who did not contribute to the projections.

In comments during his press conference, Warsh noted that monetary policy cannot significantly influence many price movements, such as oil or food costs. "The price of oil in the markets or the price of a dozen eggs does not have first-order consequences to what we're doing," he said. However, he added that the Fed must ensure such shocks do not spread more broadly throughout the economy.

The Fed faces competing economic signals as it charts its course forward. While inflation stands at 4.2%, the highest level since 2023, core inflation has risen only modestly to 2.9%. The labor market remains relatively strong, with unemployment steady at 4.3%, though wage gains have been offset by higher prices over the past year.

Warsh takes the helm during an economically uncertain period marked by elevated inflation and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

Finland has lifted its long-standing ban on nuclear weapons, a significant policy shift designed to reinforce NATO's defensive posture as the Nordic nation confronts mounting security challenges. The decision reflects Finland's evolving approach to its own defense and its commitment to the alliance following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

The country shares an 830-mile border with Russia and completed its NATO membership in 2023, abandoning decades of military non-alignment that had defined Finnish foreign policy during the Cold War. That careful balance with its Russian neighbor became untenable after Ukraine's experience, prompting Finland to seek NATO protection.

The nuclear policy change does not mean Finland will develop or possess its own nuclear weapons. Rather, it permits NATO members to deploy nuclear assets through Finnish territory or station them there as part of the alliance's collective defense framework. This flexibility allows NATO to position strategic capabilities more effectively across Northern Europe in response to potential threats.

The decision represents a fundamental recalibration of Finnish defense priorities. For generations, Finland maintained its non-aligned status while managing a delicate relationship with Moscow. That approach proved incompatible with the security environment created by Russia's actions in Ukraine. The government framed the policy shift as necessary to fulfill its obligations as a full NATO member and to strengthen the alliance's deterrence capabilities.

Finland's geographical position makes it strategically crucial within NATO. Its long frontier with Russia places it on the frontlines of potential conflicts in the Arctic and Baltic regions. The policy change gives the alliance greater flexibility in monitoring and responding to Russian activities in these strategically sensitive areas.

The move also reflects broader anxiety among NATO members that border Russia. Several Eastern European and Nordic nations have grown increasingly concerned about Russian military activities and intentions. By allowing nuclear weapons transits and deployments on its territory, Finland signals its commitment to collective defense while contributing to NATO's ability to deter aggression.

Finnish officials stressed that the nuclear policy shift aligns with their responsibilities as a full alliance member. Rather than acting unilaterally, Finland is integrating itself more deeply into NATO's defense structures and strategic planning.

The decision carries symbolic weight beyond military considerations. It demonstrates that Russia's actions in Ukraine fundamentally altered the security calculations of nations on its periphery. Countries that once sought to maintain neutrality or non-aligned status increasingly view NATO membership and closer integration with Western defense structures as essential to their survival.

For NATO, Finland's willingness to permit nuclear deployments strengthens the alliance's deterrent posture in a region where Russian forces pose an immediate concern. The policy change underscores how profoundly the European security landscape has shifted since Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Apple plans to increase iPhone prices in response to rising memory chip costs, CEO Tim Cook revealed in a recent interview. Cook stated that price increases are unavoidable and described the situation as unsustainable, marking a notable shift for a company that has traditionally maintained stable pricing on its core products.

The shortage stems from Apple's expansion of artificial intelligence features in its Siri platform, which requires significant amounts of RAM in newer iPhone models. This increased memory demand has strained supply from major chipmakers Samsung and SK Hynix. The pressure on component costs has become substantial enough that Apple says it can no longer absorb these expenses without passing them on to consumers.

The memory chip crunch reflects broader industry pressures as smartphone manufacturers race to integrate on-device artificial intelligence. Apple's AI push has created unprecedented demand for high-capacity DRAM, benefiting chipmakers but creating supply bottlenecks that affect the entire sector. Rivals have similarly struggled to balance AI ambitions with manufacturing costs.

According to available reports, the base model iPhone 17 will not support all AI features included in iOS 27 because it lacks the necessary memory capacity. Apple has confirmed plans to upgrade RAM in future models to support the full range of Siri AI features across its product line. This represents a strategic commitment to AI functionality, though standardizing higher memory across the product lineup drives up manufacturing costs.

The company has faced criticism for fragmenting its AI feature set across different iPhone tiers, forcing customers to choose between budget constraints and access to new technology. By standardizing more memory in upcoming models, Apple aims to provide more consistent AI capabilities, but this comes at the expense of higher production costs.

Cook did not specify the extent of the price increases or which iPhone models would be affected. The announcement comes as Apple faces growing competition in the smartphone market, where component availability and cost remain significant challenges. The price hikes could test consumer tolerance at a time when device upgrade cycles have lengthened and economic uncertainty persists.

The memory shortage represents just one of several challenges affecting technology pricing. Across industries, manufacturers are grappling with component costs and supply chain pressures. For Apple specifically, the shift toward more memory-intensive AI features has made this particular constraint more acute than in previous product cycles.

The company's willingness to publicly acknowledge price increases reflects how serious the supply situation has become. Rather than attempting to absorb costs entirely or downplay the situation, Apple has chosen to be transparent about the market pressures driving its pricing decisions.

Jude Bellingham added a goal to re-establish England's lead in the second half, while Marcus Rashford sealed victory late on as a substitute.

The match proved dramatic in its opening stages. Croatia, ranked 11th in the world and the highest-ranked pot two nation in the tournament, equalled twice before halftime through Martin Baturina and Petar Musa. England's defending was described as overly open and generous during a seesaw first half that ended level.

The turning point came after Thomas Tuchel's half-time address. Kane revealed the manager's key message: "He told us to take the shackles off, calm down and let's go. He said what's the worst that can happen? Show the world who we can be." Bellingham confirmed the speech's impact, noting "it wasn't one of those where it was a big drama or standing up and shouting; it was what the team needed. We have a mature group with great leaders in there; everyone knew the level we had to get to."

England's second-half transformation was striking. "We came out in the second half full gas and they couldn't live with it, and that's the level we have to set in every game," Kane said. "The way we controlled the game once we went ahead, we never really looked like we were in danger and then scored on the counterattack."

Tuchel acknowledged his players had shown signs of nerves despite their early success. "Sometimes if you want to get it so perfectly right, you sometimes want it too much and you overthink it," he explained. "In the end, in doubt, we took the decision to go backwards, on and off the ball. We played way too many passes backwards, we played way too many back to our goalkeeper. It took us a while to find our confidence."

The manager also confirmed that midfielder Declan Rice was substituted as a precaution after feeling discomfort in his lower back and upper hamstring, though Rice reassured Tuchel afterwards that the issue appeared minor.

England's victory puts them in a strong position heading into their remaining group stage matches. The performance demonstrated the attacking capability that has sometimes proved elusive in past tournaments, with the team displaying what observers described as a Premier League style of aggressive, attacking play throughout the second half.

Senate Democrats are intensifying their criticism of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth during congressional hearings on the Pentagon's military budget, focusing on what they characterize as misleading statements about the Iran conflict.

At a Senate Armed Services Committee hearing, Jack Reed, the ranking Democrat from Rhode Island, accused Hegseth of failing to provide President Trump with accurate information about the war. "The problem with your statements, Mr Secretary, is they are dangerously exaggerated," Reed said, pointing out that Iran's government remains in power and its nuclear program continues despite Hegseth's claims of victory.

The conflict, which has lasted eight weeks, has become stalemated, with Iran closing the strategically important Strait of Hormuz. Reed emphasized that American families are paying the price through increased fuel costs for a war they did not support. "American families are bearing the cost of a war they wanted nothing to do with and have gained nothing from and yet, Secretary Hegseth, you declared victory a month ago," he said.

Reed also challenged Hegseth's inflammatory rhetoric about the conflict. Days after hundreds of Iranian schoolgirls were killed in a missile strike, Hegseth made statements about showing "no mercy" to Iranians. Reed characterized these as "dangerous statements that are counterproductive to the mission" and noted that such orders would constitute war crimes under international law.

The defense secretary also faced criticism from other Senate Democrats about public support for the war. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand of New York told Hegseth that he may not fully appreciate how strongly the American people oppose the conflict. "I don't know if you fully appreciate how much the American people do not support this war," she said. Opinion polls have consistently shown that a majority of Americans oppose the war, with many concerned about its economic impact and the risk of broader regional conflict.

Senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut challenged Hegseth's characterization of the military campaign as successful. "The American people aren't buying it," Blumenthal said, adding that successful wars require public backing.

Hegseth defended his position, dismissing critics as "reckless naysayers and defeatist" and characterizing the war as addressing a 47-year threat with historic significance. He argued that the conflict has achieved its goal of preventing Iran from developing nuclear weapons.

The hearing marked the second consecutive day of testimony from Hegseth and Joint Chiefs of Staff Chair Dan Caine before Congress. The proceedings were briefly interrupted by protesters who shouted slogans before being removed from the chamber.

The series starred Alfred Molina and Alfre Woodard in a story centered on a group of unlikely heroes. The Duffer Brothers, known for creating Stranger Things, served as executive producers on the project rather than in a showrunner capacity.

The cancellation comes on the heels of Stranger Things' final season, which became Netflix's biggest English-language debut ever. The fifth and final season of Stranger Things achieved 59.6 million views in its first five days, setting a new record for the platform. This represents a significant increase compared to the fourth season's performance in 2022, when Netflix measured viewership differently using stream time rather than view counts.

The massive audience for Stranger Things' conclusion was so large that it temporarily overwhelmed Netflix's systems. The platform had to increase bandwidth by 30 percent in advance of the premiere to prevent crashes, according to co-creator Ross Duffer. The surge in viewers even caused the platform to briefly freeze despite these precautions.

Before the final season dropped, all previous Stranger Things seasons entered Netflix's top 10 simultaneously, marking the first time any show had achieved this milestone on the platform. The show has maintained its status as one of Netflix's most popular English-language series since debuting in 2016.

Stranger Things helped launch the careers of numerous actors who have since become prominent in the industry. The cast includes Millie Bobby Brown, one of Netflix's first major in-house stars, along with Joe Keery, Natalia Dyer, Charlie Heaton, Finn Wolfhard, Noah Schnapp, Caleb McLaughlin, Sadie Sink, and Gaten Matarazzo. Series regulars Winona Ryder and David Harbour also returned for the final season.

The fifth season features a time jump from the show's typical 1980s setting to accommodate the actors who have aged since the series began. The story returns to Hawkins, Indiana, the fictional small town central to the series' narrative about otherworldly threats.

The co-creators, brothers Matt and Ross Duffer, indicated that the final season would deliver particularly intense moments, teasing "the most violent death of any season." Critic Jack Seale praised the fourth episode of the first half of the finale, calling it a "solidly thrilling 90 minutes" that effectively uses the show's substantial effects budget.

Stranger Things' conclusion continues throughout December, with the final three episodes set to premiere on Christmas Day and New Year's Eve.

Chase died from sepsis following meningitis at a Los Angeles hospital, according to her longtime manager and her boyfriend.

Federal Reserve releases revised policy statement under new leadership

Interest rates remained unchanged, but the language in the Fed's policy statement evolved considerably.

4:48 PM ET

The Federal Reserve kept interest rates unchanged at 3.5% to 3.75%, where they have held steady since December. However, the central bank's policy statement underwent notable revisions, reflecting the communication approach of new chairman Kevin Warsh. The Fed's voting committee unanimously supported the decision.

In the updated statement, officials described economic activity as "expanding at a solid pace." The shift in language signals the Fed may consider raising rates before year's end. Market reaction was negative, with US stock prices declining following the announcement.
