Oil prices fell sharply after President Trump confirmed a U.S.-Iran peace deal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz to tanker traffic. The agreement marks a turning point following more than 100 days of disruption to global energy supplies, with Brent crude tumbling to $82 a barrel and wholesale gas prices dropping around 6 percent.

The strait, which handles roughly 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied gas during normal times, had been closed as the conflict between the U.S. and Iran intensified. The blockade created one of the most severe disruptions to energy markets on record, driving fuel costs upward worldwide and creating shortages across multiple industries.

Financial markets reacted positively to the news. Stock indices climbed as investors welcomed the prospect of stabilized energy supplies returning to global markets. The Nasdaq prepared to rebound, while major indices including the Dow and S&P 500 posted gains. Analysts noted that optimism around the Iran deal helped offset concerns about hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve.

American consumers saw immediate relief at the pump. Gasoline prices fell below four dollars per gallon for the first time in months, representing a significant decline from the elevated costs that followed the conflict's start in February. The drop came as supply fears eased with the strait's reopening to tanker traffic.

The reopening also had broader diplomatic effects. Britain dropped its "do not travel" advisory for Dubai, where thousands of British travelers had been stranded when the conflict erupted. The Bank of England held interest rates steady, maintaining its December rate cut as the central bank continued monitoring the impact of high energy prices on the economy.

However, analysts cautioned that full recovery would take time. Energy markets noted that oil and gas prices were unlikely to return to pre-war levels for months, as buyers rushed to refill depleted emergency crude stockpiles. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remained stranded in the Gulf, according to the International Maritime Organization, though the reopening should eventually clear these backlogs.

The economic toll of the disruption had spread globally. Germany, Europe's largest economy, halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in extra aid for households and farmers facing elevated costs for fuel and other essential goods.

Asian markets prepared for mixed trading as investors weighed the positive supply news against lingering uncertainty around Federal Reserve policy and longer-term economic impacts from the extended energy crisis. The reopening of the strait represented a significant diplomatic and economic milestone, though full normalcy in global energy markets remained months away.

10:57 AM ET

Ukraine deployed nearly 200 drones in a massive assault on Moscow on Thursday, marking the largest aerial strike on the Russian capital since the war's full-scale invasion began. The attack set a major oil refinery ablaze and temporarily forced evacuations at Russia's largest airport.

The primary target was the Kapotnya oil refinery in Moscow's southeastern district, a facility that supplies approximately 40 percent of the capital's petrol and roughly half of its diesel fuel. Video footage captured a fuel tank detonating explosively, with flames consuming one of Moscow's most critical energy infrastructure sites. Beyond the refinery, Ukrainian drones struck a shopping center, residential buildings, industrial facilities and private homes. Russian authorities reported at least 17 people injured in the strikes.

The scale of this attack suggests Ukraine has significantly strained Moscow's air defense systems, which are the densest in Russia. Online videos showed Ukrainian drones flying over the capital with minimal interception, indicating some weapons successfully penetrated the layered defenses. Footage also captured Russian operators attempting last-minute countermeasures, including one soldier firing a shoulder-launched missile moments before a drone hit the refinery.

Ukraine appears to have employed a mix of conventional propeller-driven long-range drones alongside newer jet-powered missile systems. These faster, harder-to-intercept weapons, including the recently unveiled Bars hybrid drone-cruise missile, pose growing challenges for Russian air defenses. Moscow faces constraints in deploying its defensive systems in densely populated areas, where interceptor misses or falling debris could cause significant civilian casualties.

Residents in areas east of Moscow reported an unusual phenomenon following the refinery strike. Black rain, an oily residue from the burning refinery, coated cars, streets and buildings. This visual evidence brought the war directly to Moscow residents in unprecedented ways. Russian social media filled with videos of Muscovites reacting to the strikes, with some residents describing shaking windows and smoke-darkened skies.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy framed the attack as retaliation for Russia's earlier strike on a historic Kyiv monastery. "If Ukraine is going to burn, your Moscow will burn too," he stated in a voice message. "It is time to end the aggression, time to end this war."

The refinery damage could significantly affect fuel supplies throughout the Moscow region, though Russia may redirect supplies from other sources. Beyond the immediate practical consequences, the strike's psychological impact proved substantial. For the first time in years of conflict, Moscow's residents directly witnessed the war's consequences, with smoke visible from apartment windows and the air thick with the smell of burning fuel.

Russian officials have not yet formally responded. However, nationalist figures demanded retaliation, with some calling for escalated responses. Historical patterns suggest Moscow will likely answer with renewed waves of missile and drone strikes against Ukrainian cities.

A few minutes ago

The Supreme Court ruled in favor of a Texas man who challenged a federal law prohibiting drug users from possessing firearms, finding the statute violated the Second Amendment and was unconstitutionally vague. The decision marks another expansion of gun rights under the current court and limits the federal government's power to disarm individuals based on drug use.

The case centered on a Texas marijuana user who was prosecuted under the federal law after attempting to purchase a firearm. His attorneys argued that the prohibition infringed on his constitutional right to bear arms. The court agreed, determining that the law as applied in this case could not withstand Second Amendment scrutiny.

The ruling narrows the scope of federal firearms restrictions, building on recent Supreme Court decisions that have expanded gun rights. Legal experts note the decision could affect how federal authorities enforce existing gun laws, particularly in states where marijuana use has been legalized or decriminalized. The court found the statute's language too vague to provide clear guidance on who qualifies as a prohibited drug user.

The decision has significant implications for the intersection of drug policy and gun rights. With marijuana legal for recreational or medical use in many states but still prohibited federally, the ruling creates questions about how firearms laws will be enforced going forward. Federal authorities have historically used drug use as grounds to deny gun purchases or prosecute illegal possession.

The case represents the latest in a series of Supreme Court rulings that have strengthened Second Amendment protections. The court's majority has consistently ruled that federal and state governments must meet a high standard to justify restrictions on gun ownership. Civil liberties groups on both sides of the gun debate are analyzing the decision's potential reach, as it could affect other categories of people currently barred from firearm possession under federal law.

Waymo has issued a recall affecting nearly 4,000 of its autonomous vehicles after the company identified multiple instances where its robotaxis drove into highway sections that were closed for construction. The recall addresses a software issue that allowed the vehicles to navigate into restricted areas meant for construction workers and equipment.

The company has documented at least 13 separate occasions where its self-driving vehicles entered highway construction zones. The incidents raised safety concerns about the ability of the autonomous driving system to properly recognize and respond to temporary road closures and construction barriers. Waymo operates one of the largest commercial robotaxi fleets in the United States, providing rides to passengers in several cities.

The recall involves a software update to correct the navigation system that failed to properly identify and avoid construction zones. Waymo will deploy the fix to its fleet to prevent the vehicles from entering areas where active construction work is taking place. The nature of the software issue means the company can address the problem through remote updates rather than requiring physical modifications to the vehicles.

The construction zone incidents highlight ongoing challenges in autonomous vehicle technology, particularly in handling dynamic road conditions that change frequently. Construction zones present unique obstacles for self-driving systems because they often involve temporary signage, modified traffic patterns, and barriers that may not appear in standard mapping data. These environments require vehicles to process and respond to visual cues that differ from normal driving conditions.

Waymo has been expanding its robotaxi operations across multiple markets as it works to demonstrate the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles for commercial passenger service. The recall comes as regulators and the public continue to scrutinize the safety record of self-driving vehicles operating on public roads. The company has not disclosed whether any collisions or injuries resulted from the vehicles entering the construction zones.

The New York Knicks secured their first NBA title in over five decades with a stunning victory against the San Antonio Spurs in Game 4 of the Finals. The 107-106 win marked one of the most dramatic comebacks in basketball history.

Facing a 29-point deficit at one point during the game, the Knicks mounted an improbable rally to claim the championship. OG Anunoby's tip-in with 1.2 seconds remaining proved to be the decisive play. Jalen Brunson had missed a contested three-pointer in the closing moments, but Anunoby slipped through the defense to secure the victory.

"Right hand from God," said Knicks center Karl-Anthony Towns, describing Anunoby's winning basket.

The Spurs dominated most of the game. San Antonio buried 11 of their first 16 attempts from three-point range and held a 76-49 lead at halftime, marking the largest half-time advantage by a visiting team in NBA Finals history. The deficit reached 29 points with 9 minutes and 40 seconds remaining in the third quarter.

The Knicks' turnaround began in the third quarter when New York held San Antonio to just 14 points on 4-for-20 shooting. The fourth quarter brought an explosive 28-9 run over seven minutes as the team converted defensive stops into fast-break opportunities. Brunson delivered a key lay-up with 1 minute and 22 seconds left to give the Knicks their first lead of the game.

Anunoby finished with 33 points and made seven of nine attempts from beyond the arc, both playoff career highs. Brunson added 36 points and seven assists. Jose Alvarado provided crucial support off the bench with eight points during the fourth-quarter surge.

"You're down 29, you're thinking, 'OK let's get it to 20,'" said Josh Hart. "There's three minutes left in the third quarter, we're down 18, you're thinking, 'Let's get it to 10.' In the fourth quarter, you're like, 'This is winning time. Anything can happen.'"

Victor Wembanyama led San Antonio with 24 points and 13 rebounds, while Devin Harper contributed 21 points. The Spurs were outscored 58-30 in the second half and shot just 3-for-17 from three-point range after halftime.

The championship represents the franchise's first title since 1973 and the third in the team's 80-year history. No team had previously overcome a deficit greater than 24 points in an NBA Finals game. Only LeBron James's Cleveland Cavaliers in 2016 have overturned a 3-1 deficit to win the Finals.

The victory ended one of the longest title droughts in North American professional sports and sparked celebrations throughout New York City.

While Carson built a massive television audience during an era when viewing options were limited, Rogan has developed an equally influential platform in today's fragmented digital media landscape. Both figures became known for their ability to conduct extended, relatively informal conversations with guests that appealed to audiences across demographic and political lines.

Leno succeeded Carson as Tonight Show host in 1992, holding the position until 2009 and again from 2010 to 2014. His remarks acknowledge that the cultural center of conversational entertainment has moved from broadcast television to digital platforms, where Rogan's podcast reaches millions of listeners per episode without the restrictions of network standards or time slot limitations.

Political observers closely track Rogan's influence because his enormous listenership makes him a significant power broker for the digital age. His publicly stated voting decisions, including voting for Bernie Sanders in 2020 and Donald Trump in 2024, are considered as significant as traditional political endorsements. Rogan represents what some have called "America's most famous swing voter" because his views do not align neatly with either major political party. Trump's immigration policies have particularly driven this reconsideration among these influential media figures, suggesting that Rogan's cultural position continues to reflect and potentially shape the perspectives of millions of listeners.

A few minutes ago

The Supreme Court issued a unanimous decision stating that the federal government cannot restrict gun ownership based on marijuana use alone. The ruling came in a case involving a Texas man named Ali Danial Hemani who was prosecuted under a federal law that prohibits people classified as habitual drug users from possessing firearms.

The justices determined that the law violated Hemani's Second Amendment right to bear arms and found the statute to be unconstitutionally vague. The rare unanimous decision demonstrates broad agreement across the ideological spectrum on this constitutional issue.

Federal law has long contained a blanket prohibition on firearm possession by individuals deemed habitual drug users. This restriction has been applied to marijuana consumers regardless of whether they live in states where the drug is legal for medical or recreational purposes. The government argued that maintaining this restriction served legitimate public safety interests, but the court rejected this rationale.

The unanimous nature of the ruling suggests the justices identified fundamental constitutional problems with the law that transcended typical partisan disagreements on gun policy. This consensus across a divided court indicates concern about how the statute's language and application may infringe on constitutional protections.

The decision carries significant implications for federal firearms law enforcement. More than half of American states have legalized marijuana for medical use, and roughly two dozen states allow recreational use. However, the federal government continues to classify marijuana as a Schedule I controlled substance, creating a conflict between state and federal policy.

The court's ruling may force federal lawmakers to reconsider how gun restrictions interact with changing state marijuana laws. States that have legalized the drug now face questions about how to harmonize their policies with federal firearms regulations. The decision could affect numerous individuals across the country who use marijuana legally under state law but remain subject to federal gun restrictions.

The full implications of this ruling will become clearer as the court issues its complete written opinion and lower courts apply the precedent in future cases. The decision addresses a growing tension in American law as state-level marijuana reforms continue expanding while federal prohibitions remain in place.

This case represents a notable moment in the evolution of gun rights jurisprudence. It shows the court willing to strike down federal restrictions on firearm ownership even when tied to drug policy concerns. The ruling underscores how changing social attitudes and state laws around marijuana are forcing reconsideration of longstanding federal restrictions that were established when marijuana prohibitions faced virtually no state-level challenge.

Waymo has issued a recall affecting nearly 4,000 of its autonomous vehicles after the company identified multiple instances where its robotaxis drove into highway sections that were closed for construction. The recall addresses a software issue that allowed the vehicles to navigate into restricted areas meant for construction workers and equipment.

The company has documented at least 13 separate occasions where its self-driving vehicles entered highway construction zones. The incidents raised safety concerns about the ability of the autonomous driving system to properly recognize and respond to temporary road closures and construction barriers. Waymo operates one of the largest commercial robotaxi fleets in the United States, providing rides to passengers in several cities.

The recall involves a software update to correct the navigation system that failed to properly identify and avoid construction zones. Waymo will deploy the fix to its fleet to prevent the vehicles from entering areas where active construction work is taking place. The nature of the software issue means the company can address the problem through remote updates rather than requiring physical modifications to the vehicles.

The construction zone incidents highlight ongoing challenges in autonomous vehicle technology, particularly in handling dynamic road conditions that change frequently. Construction zones present unique obstacles for self-driving systems because they often involve temporary signage, modified traffic patterns, and barriers that may not appear in standard mapping data. These environments require vehicles to process and respond to visual cues that differ from normal driving conditions.

Waymo has been expanding its robotaxi operations across multiple markets as it works to demonstrate the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles for commercial passenger service. The recall comes as regulators and the public continue to scrutinize the safety record of self-driving vehicles operating on public roads. The company has not disclosed whether any collisions or injuries resulted from the vehicles entering the construction zones.

12:00 PM ET

The New York Knicks secured their first NBA title in over five decades with a stunning victory against the San Antonio Spurs in Game 4 of the Finals. The 107-106 win marked one of the most dramatic comebacks in basketball history.

Facing a 29-point deficit at one point during the game, the Knicks mounted an improbable rally to claim the championship. OG Anunoby's tip-in with 1.2 seconds remaining proved to be the decisive play. Jalen Brunson had missed a contested three-pointer in the closing moments, but Anunoby slipped through the defense to secure the victory.

"Right hand from God," said Knicks center Karl-Anthony Towns, describing Anunoby's winning basket.

The Spurs dominated most of the game. San Antonio buried 11 of their first 16 attempts from three-point range and held a 76-49 lead at halftime, marking the largest half-time advantage by a visiting team in NBA Finals history. The deficit reached 29 points with 9 minutes and 40 seconds remaining in the third quarter.

The Knicks' turnaround began in the third quarter when New York held San Antonio to just 14 points on 4-for-20 shooting. The fourth quarter brought an explosive 28-9 run over seven minutes as the team converted defensive stops into fast-break opportunities. Brunson delivered a key lay-up with 1 minute and 22 seconds left to give the Knicks their first lead of the game.

Anunoby finished with 33 points and made seven of nine attempts from beyond the arc, both playoff career highs. Brunson added 36 points and seven assists. Jose Alvarado provided crucial support off the bench with eight points during the fourth-quarter surge.

"You're down 29, you're thinking, 'OK let's get it to 20,'" said Josh Hart. "There's three minutes left in the third quarter, we're down 18, you're thinking, 'Let's get it to 10.' In the fourth quarter, you're like, 'This is winning time. Anything can happen.'"

Victor Wembanyama led San Antonio with 24 points and 13 rebounds, while Devin Harper contributed 21 points. The Spurs were outscored 58-30 in the second half and shot just 3-for-17 from three-point range after halftime.

The championship represents the franchise's first title since 1973 and the third in the team's 80-year history. No team had previously overcome a deficit greater than 24 points in an NBA Finals game. Only LeBron James's Cleveland Cavaliers in 2016 have overturned a 3-1 deficit to win the Finals.

The victory ended one of the longest title droughts in North American professional sports and sparked celebrations throughout New York City.

British authorities have dropped their "do not travel" advisory for Dubai following the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Thousands of British citizens had been stranded in the Middle East when the US-Iran conflict erupted in early 2026.

The diplomatic agreement to reopen the critical waterway enables the resumption of normal travel for British nationals to the region. The reopening comes after more than 100 days of disruption to global energy supplies, with oil markets responding positively to the peace deal.

World Cup Fans Complain About Hydration Breaks

Breaks added to each half of matches are drawing criticism from fans who say they enable more advertising.

A few minutes ago

Hydration breaks added to each half of World Cup matches have sparked complaints from fans. The pauses interrupt the flow of play during matches held in hot North American conditions in June.

Many viewers suspect the breaks serve a secondary purpose beyond player safety—allowing networks additional time to broadcast advertisements. Tournament organizers have defended the breaks as a necessary health measure for players competing in high temperatures.
