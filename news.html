The attack hit residential buildings, warehouses, a medical facility and an educational institution across multiple districts of the capital.

Among the targets was a children's hospital in the Solomianskyi district west of central Kyiv. The strike blew out windows and burned vehicles parked nearby. At least two residential buildings in the area were also reported hit. Explosions were heard from the city center around midnight Wednesday, earlier than typical Russian strike timing, with additional waves of attacks continuing into Thursday morning.

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched dozens of ballistic, cruise and hypersonic missiles along with 168 drones during the overnight bombardment. The weapons included Kinzhal and Zircon hypersonic missiles, Iskander and North Korean KN-23 ballistic missiles, and Shahed drones.

In the broader Kyiv region, an attack on the Brovary district killed one man and wounded another. Regional governor Tymur Tkachenko said an industrial facility was damaged and emergency services worked to extinguish fires at the site.

The strikes exposed Ukraine's vulnerability to continued Russian air attacks. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the assault demonstrated why stronger international support is needed. "While Moscow is investing in ballistic missiles and escalation, such strikes do not always receive the response they should from the world," Zelenskyy stated on social media. "And as long as Ukraine does not have enough anti-ballistic defence, Russia will not consider peace seriously."

The attack prompted responses from neighboring NATO countries. Poland's military mobilized aircraft and activated air defenses in areas bordering Ukraine. Romania's defense ministry reported radar detection of aerial targets near the border overnight and scrambled two Spanish fighter jets and a Romanian helicopter. A drone entered Romanian airspace near Galati before crashing in an unpopulated area in Tulcea county.

Russia reported a large Ukrainian drone attack in return. Moscow's mayor Sergei Sobyanin said 250 drones flew toward the Moscow region between Wednesday night and Thursday morning, with most destroyed at considerable distance from the capital itself.

The latest strikes come as Ukraine escalates its own retaliatory attacks on Russian targets, including facilities supplying Moscow's military infrastructure. The attack on Kyiv also coincided with domestic political turmoil, as Zelenskyy dismissed senior aide Iryna Mudra following reports of searches related to alleged money laundering, extending a corruption scandal involving high-ranking officials.

The technology addresses one of the longstanding limitations of OLED screens, which have historically struggled with brightness levels and degradation over time.

The FLiPP technology represents a significant advancement in display manufacturing. OLED screens are known for their superior contrast and color reproduction compared to traditional LCD panels, but lower peak brightness and concerns about burn-in have limited their adoption in certain applications. By improving both brightness and longevity, the new technology could expand the use of OLED displays across more device categories.

LG Display indicated the technology could be used in screens up to 100 inches in size, suggesting applications beyond smartphones and tablets. The company has been a leading supplier of OLED panels for televisions, computer monitors, and mobile devices. Competitors are also advancing display technology, with Samsung showcasing a 300Hz OLED display designed for gaming laptops at the same time.

The timing of the announcement coincides with growing demand for high-quality displays across consumer electronics. Brighter screens improve visibility in outdoor settings and well-lit rooms, while longer lifespans reduce the total cost of ownership for consumers and manufacturers. The technology could influence product development cycles for companies that rely on LG Display as a supplier.

LG Display has not provided a timeline for when FLiPP-based screens will appear in commercial products or disclosed which manufacturers have committed to using the technology. The company also has not specified the exact mechanisms behind the brightness and lifespan improvements or whether the technology requires changes to manufacturing processes.

The Dutch driver had been linked with potential moves to Mercedes and McLaren and had previously suggested he might retire from Formula One altogether due to his dissatisfaction with the new regulations introduced this season. However, ahead of his home race at the Dutch Grand Prix, the 28-year-old confirmed he would continue his partnership with Red Bull through the remainder of the decade.

Verstappen's previous contract was set to expire at the end of 2028 but included performance-based break clauses that would have allowed him to leave if he chose to do so. The new deal extends his commitment by two additional years.

"The team is like a second family for me and I feel very at home," Verstappen said in a statement. "I came to the team hoping to be able to win races. What we went on to achieve together has been simply lovely and we have shared many incredible moments and won four world championships."

The timing of the announcement is particularly significant given Red Bull's current struggles. The team has faced challenges adapting to the new technical regulations this season, while Mercedes has emerged with a dominant car. Verstappen currently sits sixth in the world championship standings, 110 points behind leader Kimi Antonelli, with only two second-place finishes to his name so far this year.

Verstappen has been vocal about his dislike for the new regulations and their emphasis on energy management, sentiments shared by many other drivers on the grid. Despite these frustrations, he expressed confidence in Red Bull's ability to recover and return to championship contention.

The driver also highlighted his positive working relationship with Laurent Mekies, Red Bull's team principal who took over from Christian Horner last July. "Getting to work with Laurent now for over a year has also been great," Verstappen said. "I see a clear vision he has for the team. Everyone in Milton Keynes believes in what we are building and I am looking forward to the next chapter, fighting for more victories and competing for championships."

Mekies emphasized the importance of securing Verstappen's future with the team. "Very few partnerships in sport achieve what Max and this team have," the team principal said. "Since the very beginning, Max has embodied everything that makes Oracle Red Bull Racing special: uncompromising competitiveness, relentless determination and the courage to challenge convention."

Verstappen will be 33 years old when his contract expires in 2030, potentially allowing him to pursue additional years in Formula One beyond that point should he wish to continue racing.
