5:33 PM ET

A hacking group known as ShinyHunters has claimed responsibility for compromising Oracle PeopleSoft servers at more than 100 organizations, including numerous universities. The cybercriminals announced the breach, which targets enterprise resource planning software widely used for human resources, finance, and student information management.

Oracle PeopleSoft is deployed across thousands of institutions worldwide to manage sensitive employee and student data. The software handles payroll information, personal identification details, financial records, and academic information. A successful breach of these systems could expose confidential data for millions of individuals across affected organizations.

ShinyHunters has a history of high-profile data breaches and typically sells stolen information on underground forums. The group has previously claimed responsibility for breaches at major corporations and has established a reputation within cybercriminal circles for targeting large-scale databases. Universities represent particularly attractive targets due to the volume of personal information they store and often limited cybersecurity resources compared to private sector organizations.

The full scope of the breach remains under investigation. Organizations running PeopleSoft installations must determine whether their systems were compromised and what data may have been accessed. Many universities store decades of records in these systems, meaning a breach could affect current students and employees as well as alumni and former staff members.

The incident highlights ongoing vulnerabilities in enterprise software systems that serve as central repositories for sensitive information. Organizations relying on PeopleSoft for critical operations face potential disruption to payroll, benefits administration, and academic record management if systems must be taken offline for security assessment and remediation. Affected institutions will need to notify individuals whose information may have been compromised and implement enhanced security measures to prevent future intrusions.

The United States launched airstrikes against Iran for a second straight night following the downing of a U.S. Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump blamed Tehran for the incident and said the military action would continue until a deal is reached.

The helicopter went down early Tuesday off the coast of Oman while on patrol. A U.S. official told the Associated Press the aircraft collided with an Iranian drone, though it remains unclear whether the collision was intentional. The two crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat after spending about two hours in the water. Trump later downplayed the incident, telling the Wall Street Journal that the helicopter being downed "wasn't a big deal" and that the "pilot is fine."

The strikes came after Iran launched retaliatory attacks Wednesday morning, targeting U.S. assets in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan. Tehran said it used long-range missiles and drones to strike American bases, including the al-Azraq base in Jordan. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed they targeted F-35 fighter jet hangars and a command-and-control center. However, U.S. officials told Reuters that nearly all missiles and drones launched by Iran were intercepted. Jordan's armed forces said they shot down five missiles before impact.

U.S. Central Command announced that Trump directed the military to launch strikes beginning at 10 p.m. UK time Tuesday in response to the helicopter downing. Media reports citing White House officials indicate the strikes targeted radar and air defense facilities along the Strait of Hormuz. Centcom described the response as "proportional" to what it called "unjustified Iranian aggression."

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded on social media, warning that Iran "will leave no attack or threat unanswered. Leave our region if you want to be safe." His statement suggested Iran prefers "the language of diplomacy," but added that Iran speaks "other languages far more fluently."

The escalating military action threatens a ceasefire announced in April between the two countries. Before the helicopter incident, Trump had expressed optimism about negotiations, saying there was "a good chance" of signing a deal within "two or three days." He also warned about the costs of continued bombing, saying "a lot of people are going to be killed."

The conflict has already strained the global economy and pushed up energy prices worldwide. Since fighting between the U.S., Israel and Iran began in February, many essential goods including food have become more expensive. Federal authorities executed a search warrant Wednesday at a Southern California aerospace facility where a chemical tank overheated in late May, triggering the evacuation of 50,000 residents. The facility, GKN Aerospace Transparency Systems in Garden Grove, manufactures cockpit windows and other aircraft components.

The overheated tank contained 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in manufacturing plastics and coatings like Plexiglas and dental prosthetics. Authorities feared the pressurized tank could explode, prompting the large-scale evacuation as a precautionary measure. Most evacuees were able to return home over the Memorial Day weekend after a crack formed in the tank, relieving pressure and preventing a potential explosion.

The federal judge-signed warrant, issued last week, authorized seizure of records related to the storage, use and disposal of methyl methacrylate. Agents were also instructed to collect samples of the substance from any tanks, containers or vessels suspected of containing it, along with records of cooling equipment and temperature control systems used for the chemical.

The Orange County district attorney's office is conducting a separate criminal investigation into the incident. Prosecutors have ordered the aerospace company not to modify or destroy any evidence. According to the Environmental Protection Agency, exposure to methyl methacrylate can cause serious respiratory problems, neurological issues and irritation to the skin, eyes and throat.

At a community meeting Tuesday, GKN senior vice-president Steve Carlin apologized to the Garden Grove community for the disruption and distress caused by the incident. "I'm sorry that this event and this incident occurred. I understand what a disruptive event it was and how unsettling it is to the greater community," Carlin said. He thanked firefighters and local leaders for their response.

When city leaders and residents asked the company to consider relocating the methyl methacrylate tanks, Carlin said it was too early in the investigation to make such a determination.

About a dozen evacuated people and businesses have filed lawsuits against GKN since the May incident. Some locals reported strong odors, respiratory irritation, headaches and dizziness. Orange County health officials said no contamination or fumes were released and committed to monitoring air quality for several months while checking sewer and storm drains.

A GKN spokesperson stated the company is cooperating with authorities. "We are cooperating with authorities at our Garden Grove facility and will continue to do so," the spokesperson told the Associated Press.

The California incident occurred five days before a similar hazardous chemical emergency at a Washington paper mill, where a corrosive chemical tank ruptured and imploded, killing 11 people.

6:56 PM ET

Consumer prices jumped to an annual rate of 4.2 percent in May, marking the fastest pace of inflation in three years as energy costs climbed during the ongoing conflict between the US and Israel against Iran. The increase represents a significant acceleration, with the personal consumption expenditures price index showing its biggest monthly rise since 2023.

Energy prices drove much of the increase, reflecting disruptions from the conflict. Before the war began, inflation was running at 2.4 percent, but the closure of the Strait of Hormuz affected global energy supplies and pushed gasoline prices higher. Oil markets have remained volatile, with Brent crude trading around $94.50 after initial reports suggested the US and Iran had reached a preliminary agreement on a 60-day ceasefire extension and nuclear negotiations, though President Trump's final approval remained pending.

President Trump responded to the inflation figures by saying he loves the inflation, expressing that he was not concerned about the rising prices. Speaker Mike Johnson attempted to defend the president's comments following the remarks.

Companies have shown reluctance to pass the full extent of rising costs on to consumers, but households are still feeling the strain of higher prices across multiple categories. Workers face particular pressure as wage growth lags behind the inflation rate. According to Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union, the situation is unsustainable for many Americans. "Inflation is at a three-year high and personal savings has cratered to one of the lowest levels in the past 20 years. Many Americans are spending more than the income they have coming in. This is not sustainable, especially for lower-income and middle-class households."

The acceleration in inflation complicates the economic outlook and has affected market sentiment. Stock markets initially reacted negatively to the data before turning positive following reports of potential US-Iran negotiations. The broader economic picture shows challenges beyond inflation, with GDP growth revised downward to 1.6 percent annually at the start of the year, down from the initially estimated 2 percent.

The administration continues to maintain that its economic agenda is working despite mounting evidence of consumer distress. The combination of elevated inflation, stagnant wage growth, and geopolitical uncertainty has created a challenging environment for policymakers and consumers alike. Household spending patterns are shifting as families adjust to the new price reality, with many seeking lower-cost alternatives while retailers and manufacturers navigate their own cost pressures. These economic headwinds come as the midterm elections approach, adding political urgency to inflation concerns.

The restructured agreement surpasses that previous deal and pushes past Dallas Cowboys quarterback Dak Prescott to make Mahomes the NFL's highest-paid player. With incentives and escalators included, the contract could reach beyond $520 million.

The move represents a significant commitment from the Chiefs organization to maintain their championship window with Mahomes under center. Since the quarterback was drafted in 2017, Kansas City has built its roster around him and appeared in four Super Bowls, winning three during his tenure as starting quarterback.

"The restructuring allows the Chiefs to manage their salary cap while securing their franchise quarterback for years to come," according to multiple reports on the agreement. The deal gives Mahomes the highest average annual value among all NFL quarterbacks.

The contract sets a new benchmark for quarterback compensation across professional football. As other elite quarterbacks approach their own contract negotiations, teams and players will likely reference Mahomes' deal as a ceiling for discussions. The rising NFL salary cap has enabled franchises to offer increasingly substantial contracts to quarterbacks deemed essential to championship contention.

The Kansas City organization's ability to restructure contracts while maintaining competitive rosters has been central to their sustained success in recent years. This latest extension demonstrates the team's determination to keep Mahomes as the centerpiece of their long-term strategy.

The exact breakdown between guaranteed money and total contract value was not immediately disclosed by the team or the quarterback's representatives. However, sources confirmed the total package exceeds $500 million, making it a historic moment for player compensation in the NFL.

Mahomes has already established himself as one of the most valuable players in professional football. As a two-time MVP, he has cemented his place among the league's elite quarterbacks and continues to lead the Chiefs' pursuit of additional championships.

The quarterback's new deal reflects broader trends in the NFL where franchise quarterbacks with proven championship success command unprecedented financial packages. As the salary cap continues to grow, similar record-breaking contracts may become more common in coming years.

Johnson told CNN's Manu Raju at the Capitol that Trump's comment was taken out of context and that people understood what the president meant. "It was totally out of context. You know what he was talking about," Johnson said.

The remark drew immediate criticism as new Labor Department data showed consumer prices increased 4.2 percent over the last year, marking a three-year high. The inflation jump represents the third consecutive monthly increase since the start of the conflict with Iran. Energy costs have risen substantially due to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has disrupted global oil supplies. Before the conflict began, inflation was at 2.4 percent.

Johnson indicated that Trump remained focused on addressing the economic situation. "The president is laser-focused on the domestic economic situation," Johnson said. "He is working to bring down prices; he's working to get the strait of Hormuz reopened."

The timing of Trump's comment proved problematic for the administration, as inflation and affordability remain the dominant concern for voters in an election year. Democrats have already criticized the administration's handling of inflation and its impact on consumers.

The inflation report also revealed the broader impact of the Iran conflict on the U.S. economy. In addition to energy price increases, the military situation has created uncertainty in global markets and affected supply chains that influence consumer prices across multiple sectors.

Trump's statement about loving inflation appeared disconnected from the concerns facing ordinary Americans struggling with higher costs for food, fuel, and other necessities. The president's comment suggested he viewed the inflation through a different lens than most economists and policymakers, who typically work to keep inflation moderate and stable.

Johnson's defense represented an attempt to reframe the president's words for a public already concerned about purchasing power and economic security. The House speaker's interpretation suggested Trump may have been referring to benefits he believed would emerge from the conflict or other developments, rather than expressing satisfaction with price increases themselves.

The controversy highlighted the growing tension between the administration's policies and their economic consequences. As the Iran war continued to affect energy markets and push prices higher, the administration faced pressure to demonstrate it was taking steps to relieve the burden on consumers while maintaining its foreign policy objectives.
