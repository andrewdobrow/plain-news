A few minutes ago

A 7.8 magnitude earthquake struck Indonesia's Ternate island on Thursday morning, killing at least one person and damaging buildings across the region. The quake, which occurred at 6:48 a.m. local time with a depth of 35 kilometers, was initially recorded at 7.8 magnitude by the United States Geological Survey. Its epicenter was located 127 kilometers west-northwest of Ternate, an island in Indonesia's North Maluku province.

The earthquake triggered tsunami warnings across the Pacific basin. The US tsunami warning system initially alerted coastal areas within 1,000 kilometers of the epicenter, including Indonesia, the Philippines, and Malaysia, warning of possible waves reaching 0.3 to 1 meter above tide level on some Indonesian coastlines. About two hours after the quake, authorities confirmed the tsunami threat had passed.

A 70-year-old woman died in North Sulawesi's Minahasa district, and another resident was injured. Strong shaking lasting 10 to 20 seconds was felt across multiple areas, including Bitung on Sulawesi island and Ternate city. An Agence France-Presse journalist in Manado reported that the shaking woke residents who rushed outdoors in panic, with schoolchildren evacuating their classrooms.

Tsunami waves were recorded in five locations, with the highest reaching 0.75 meters in North Minahasa in North Sulawesi province. Authorities documented 11 aftershocks, with the largest measuring 5.5 magnitude.

Initial damage assessments showed minor to moderate destruction to buildings in Ternate, which has a population of about 205,000. A church on Batang Dua Island and two houses in South Ternate were damaged. Images showed a sports complex in North Sumatra with mangled wall panels and metal bars scattered across the ground. Damage assessments were ongoing in Bitung.

Indonesia's disaster management agency urged the public to remain vigilant and instructed residents to stay away from beaches and coastal areas until authorities confirmed it was safe to return. "At this stage, caution is still required, particularly for communities living along the coast," an agency spokesperson said.

Japan's meteorological agency reported that only slight sea level changes might occur along Japan's coast with no tsunami damage expected. The Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology and Australia's Bureau of Meteorology confirmed there was no tsunami threat to their territories.

Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, making it prone to frequent seismic activity. In 2022, a 5.6 magnitude earthquake killed at least 602 people in West Java's Cianjur city. The 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami killed more than 230,000 people across multiple countries, with Indonesia's Aceh province suffering the heaviest losses. According to the US Geological Survey, nine earthquakes with magnitude higher than 7 have occurred within 250 kilometers of Thursday's epicenter over the past 50 years.

3:39 PM ET

Nvidia has launched the RTX Spark, a new processor combining microprocessor and graphics chip capabilities for Windows laptops and desktop computers. The chip, developed in collaboration with Microsoft and Taiwan-based MediaTek, is designed to run artificial intelligence agents locally on personal computers rather than relying on cloud computing.

The RTX Spark will reach consumers this year through major computer manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP. According to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, speaking at the Computex conference in Taiwan, the chip represents a fundamental reimagining of personal computers "for the first time in 40 years." The company spent three years collaborating with Microsoft on the project.

The chip's most significant feature is its ability to enable AI agents to navigate computers autonomously, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interactions. Despite its power for running complex AI workloads, the chip maintains a design that allows computers to remain thin and light. Die analysis reveals the processor uses modified Cortex-X925 cores, with design elements adapted from MediaTek's Dimensity 9400 and Dimensity 9500 chips, modified specifically to handle typical PC workloads.

Microsoft demonstrated the technology through the Surface Laptop Ultra at Computex, showcasing how integrated AI processing can become fundamental to computer architecture rather than an optional feature. This collaboration signals a strategic shift in how PC manufacturers approach hardware design and product development.

Nvidia's move into consumer PC chips represents a significant expansion beyond its dominant position in data center graphics cards and AI infrastructure. By focusing on efficient local AI processing, Nvidia directly competes with established PC processor makers including Intel, Apple, Qualcomm and AMD. The company is simultaneously developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents, with early adoption from companies including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Industry analysts have compared the significance of this moment to major technology shifts. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, stated the RTX Spark "looks to transform the traditional app-centric PC to a real useful agentic AI personal computer which will eventually be in every home in coming years as private edge AI agents become pivotal."

Jensen Huang addressed concerns about AI's impact on employment, stating that suggestions AI would reduce demand for software engineers represented "complete nonsense." He argued the technology would increase hiring by making workers more productive, claiming that software engineer hiring numbers were actually increasing rather than declining.

The move opens a new business line for Nvidia, though analysts note this will develop over time. The chip wars intensified as Intel announced plans to ship an AI-focused graphics processor later this year, featuring cheaper memory and cooling technology than competitors. Meanwhile, Arm CEO Rene Haas stands to receive a pay package worth more than one billion dollars if the company achieves exceptional growth targets by 2031.

Alexander Zverev captured his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Italy's Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in a tense five-set final. The victory marks a remarkable turnaround for the German player, who four years earlier was carried from the same court in a wheelchair after suffering severe injuries.

The second-seeded Zverev had previously reached three Grand Slam finals without winning, despite achieving success at every other level of professional tennis. He had won Masters 1000 events, claimed the ATP Finals twice, and earned an Olympic gold medal in Tokyo in 2020. The French Open title had eluded him until now.

Zverev's path to the title became clearer when top seed Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were eliminated from the tournament in consecutive days, while Carlos Alcaraz withdrew due to injury. Cobolli, ranked 14th in the world, emerged as the only top-25 player Zverev faced on his route to the final.

The final itself proved emotionally draining for both competitors. Cobolli, reaching his first Grand Slam final without playing a semifinal match after his Italian compatriot Matteo Arnaldi withdrew with a virus, felt the pressure intensely from the start. He was overwhelmed in the opening set but regrouped to take the second.

Zverev showed his dominance in the third set by completely outserving Cobolli, but nerves crept in as the finish line approached. He struggled with his forehand and second serve in the fourth set, forcing a tiebreak that Cobolli won with a running forehand winner to level the match. However, the Italian's energy was depleted, and he lost his opening service game in the final set without ever recovering.

When Cobolli missed an overhead on the second championship point, Zverev dropped to his back and began sobbing with emotion.

In his victory speech, Zverev addressed his team: "We've been through injuries. We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're grand slam champions now and that's what counts."

He reflected on the court's significance in his life, saying it had given him both the best and worst moments. He referenced the corner where he lay with seven broken ligaments and two fractured bones during his 2022 exit, and his loss in a Grand Slam final two years prior. "But now, finally, it's a happy end," Zverev said.

The Philippe-Chatrier crowd enthusiastically received his victory, marking the culmination of his long journey back from injury to claiming tennis's biggest prize.
