The Senate passed a $70 billion immigration enforcement funding bill after an overnight vote. The legislation authorizes substantial funding for border security and immigration operations, advancing through the chamber after Republicans rejected Democratic efforts to permanently ban what has been called Trump's settlement fund.

The bill moved through the Senate during an extended voting session known as a vote-a-rama, where lawmakers considered numerous amendments before final passage. Democrats attempted to include provisions that would place permanent restrictions on a fund they associate with the previous administration, but those efforts failed to gain sufficient support.

The $70 billion authorization represents a significant investment in immigration enforcement infrastructure and operations. The funding is intended to support border patrol activities, detention facilities, immigration courts, and related enforcement programs. The bill now heads to the House of Representatives for consideration.

The legislation comes as immigration policy remains a priority issue in Congress. Republicans have pushed for increased enforcement funding, while Democrats have sought to include oversight measures and restrictions on how the money can be used. The overnight voting process reflected the tensions surrounding immigration legislation, with both parties attempting to shape the final bill through amendments.

Former first lady Jill Biden discussed her husband's 2024 campaign in a separate interview, reflecting on the political landscape that has shaped current policy debates. The passage of the immigration bill marks a significant legislative development, though its ultimate fate depends on action in the House and potential reconciliation with other immigration proposals under consideration.

7:13 AM ET

Efforts to broker a cease-fire between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon have stalled despite a new agreement announced by the United States. Fighting between the two sides has intensified even after President Trump announced on Monday that both Israel and Hezbollah had agreed to stop all military operations.

The situation deteriorated quickly. Within hours of Trump's announcement, Israeli warplanes launched dozens of strikes across southern Lebanon. Lebanon's National News Agency reported 30 Israeli strikes on Tuesday alone. Near the city of Sidon, rescue workers recovered the bodies of six family members, including two children and a woman, following one strike. The Israeli military also issued new evacuation warnings for the southern city of Nabatiyeh.

Israel accused Hezbollah of violating the ceasefire, citing what Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called "repeated and ongoing violations." Netanyahu had ordered strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs, a densely populated Hezbollah stronghold, claiming the group had broken the terms of an earlier ceasefire officially in place since April 17.

Trump reportedly intervened during this escalation. According to news reports, Trump called Netanyahu over the strikes on the Beirut suburbs, and the two reached an agreement that Netanyahu would refrain from attacking that area if Hezbollah ceased its attacks on Israel. However, the situation remained volatile as both sides continued military operations.

Hezbollah has rejected the ceasefire framework. The group maintains it will not accept what it views as a one-sided arrangement requiring it to stop fighting first while Israel continues operations. Mahmoud Qmati, a senior Hezbollah official, told news agencies that the group "will not accept a partial ceasefire" and warned that any aggression against Beirut's southern suburbs "could lead to a deeper and stronger response."

Adding to the complexity, Hezbollah has not been a direct party to the negotiations, complicating Washington's mediation efforts. The group is not formally represented at the talks, leaving unclear who can commit the organization to any agreement.

The latest round of violence represents a significant escalation in the conflict, which began in March following Hezbollah's retaliation for the killing of Iran's supreme leader. Israeli troops have pushed deep into southern Lebanon, raising their flag over Beaufort Castle in what marks their deepest incursion since their occupation ended in 2000.

The breakdown in negotiations has raised concerns about regional stability. Iran's political leadership suspended further negotiations with the United States, declaring that a ceasefire in Lebanon was a precondition for broader talks. Israeli and Lebanese delegations have begun a fourth round of talks in Washington, but without agreement on fundamental terms, prospects for lasting peace remain uncertain.

The US labor market added 172,000 jobs in May, significantly outpacing economist predictions of around 80,000 new positions. The unemployment rate remained steady at 4.3%, according to data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

The stronger-than-expected job growth demonstrates continued resilience in the labor market despite rising inflation and economic uncertainty linked to ongoing Middle East conflicts. The May figures also prompted revisions to earlier months, with March and April combined seeing an additional 93,000 jobs added compared to initial reports.

The leisure and hospitality sector drove much of the employment gains as the country prepares to host the World Cup. The Bureau of Labor Statistics identified these industries as significant contributors to May's job creation. Consumer spending on travel and entertainment continues to remain elevated, supporting ongoing hiring in these sectors.

The jobs report provides important data for Federal Reserve policymakers as they assess the economy's direction. With the labor market demonstrating strength rather than weakness, officials have less immediate pressure to cut interest rates. Instead, the Fed has shifted its focus toward addressing resurgent inflation, which continues to squeeze consumers and complicate monetary policy decisions.

The solid employment numbers come after a challenging period for the labor market. Job growth had weakened considerably in recent months, though May's figures suggest a potential recovery is underway. However, economists warn that slow workforce growth may constrain how rapidly the overall economy can expand going forward.

The strong jobs report arrives amid broader economic concerns. Inflation remains unstable, with prices continuing to affect consumer sentiment. The Federal Reserve has indicated that it is proceeding cautiously on interest rate decisions, balancing the need to support employment with efforts to control price increases.

Young people entering the job market continue to face particular headwinds in securing entry-level positions. Economists have raised concerns that these early career obstacles could have lasting effects on lifetime earning potential for this generation of workers.

The May employment data underscores a labor market that remains fundamentally sound even as underlying economic pressures persist. Policymakers will likely reference these figures in upcoming discussions about the appropriate course for monetary policy. The combination of solid job growth and steady unemployment suggests the labor market has found some stability, though inflation concerns remain a central focus for Federal Reserve officials weighing their next moves on interest rates.

Yesterday, 6:48 PM ET

Valve announced it will release the Steam Machine, its new gaming console, this summer. The company made the confirmation after years of delays and speculation about when the device would reach consumers.

The Steam Machine represents Valve's latest effort to expand beyond its digital storefront into hardware. The console runs on a version of SteamOS and aims to bring PC gaming into the living room. Valve also announced the Steam Frame alongside the main console, though specific technical specifications for either device were not detailed in the announcement.

The company outlined new certification requirements for games on the platform. Titles seeking Steam Machine Verified status must meet specific performance standards. For virtual reality games running in standalone mode, Valve set a minimum frame rate of 72 frames per second, establishing a baseline for VR performance on the system.

The announcement comes as the gaming hardware market continues to evolve, with traditional console makers and PC manufacturers competing for players. Valve previously attempted to enter the console space with an earlier Steam Machine initiative that launched in 2015, which saw multiple hardware partners create systems running SteamOS. That effort largely faded as partners discontinued their models.

This new version appears to be a direct Valve-manufactured product rather than a partnership with multiple hardware makers. The summer launch window suggests the company aims to have units available for the peak gaming season, though Valve has not specified an exact release date or pricing information for either the Steam Machine or Steam Frame.

5:50 AM ET

Maja Chwalinska, a Polish qualifier, has reached the French Open final in one of the most improbable runs in Grand Slam history. The 24-year-old defeated Diana Shnaider 7-6 (4), 6-4 in the semifinals to secure her place in Saturday's championship match.

Chwalinska's achievement is historic. She is the first qualifier ever to reach the French Open final and only the second qualifier to reach any Grand Slam final, following Emma Raducanu's run at the 2021 US Open. During her three-week journey through the qualifying draw and main draw, she has won nine matches while losing just one set.

The emotional toll of her success was evident after her victory. "Like a dream, honestly. I don't know what's going on," Chwalinska said. "I don't know what to say." She later reflected on the improbability of her run, saying "Let's not pretend someone expected it. I mean, I was outside top 100 and now I'm in the finals of a grand slam, so I feel like it's a big thing. So it's hard to process it."

Before this tournament, Chwalinska had competed professionally for a decade without reaching the top 100. Her current ranking of 114 sits just one spot behind her career high. She had won only one main draw match at a Grand Slam prior to Roland Garros, coming at Wimbledon in 2022.

Her opponent in the final will be Mirra Andreeva, the eighth seed, who defeated Marta Kostyuk 6-1, 6-3 in her semifinal. The matchup sets up a final between two players who were not considered title favorites at the tournament's start.

The financial reward for Chwalinska's run has been substantial. Her prize money of $1,626,744 nearly doubles the $864,030 she had earned throughout her entire career. The substantial paycheck comes after she faced uncertainty during her tournament run about being able to afford her hotel expenses until Polish drinks company Oshee stepped in to help.

Despite her physical disadvantages on the court, Chwalinska has succeeded through tactical skill and intelligence. At 1.64 meters tall, she is diminutive for professional tennis and lacks the power of many competitors. She has compensated by using slices, angles, and drop shots to confound opponents and disrupt their rhythm.

Her semifinal opponent, Shnaider, had pulled off a stunning upset herself by defeating world number one Aryna Sabalenka in the quarterfinals after recovering from 6-3, 4-1 down. However, Shnaider could not back up that monumental win against Chwalinska's crafty and intelligent play.

The French Open final will determine which player claims their first Grand Slam title.

A few minutes ago

The Senate voted early Friday to pass a $69.5 billion budget reconciliation package that funds immigration enforcement operations through 2029, following an all-night legislative session. The bill advances a key component of President Trump's immigration agenda, providing multiyear funding for border and interior enforcement activities.

The overnight session included votes on multiple amendments, with some Republican defections complicating passage. GOP senators broke ranks on various measures, including attempts to prohibit funding for President Trump's anti-weaponization fund and a protest vote against William Pulte's appointment to lead a federal agency. The final vote came just before dawn after hours of debate and procedural maneuvering.

The reconciliation process allowed Republicans to pass the legislation with a simple majority rather than the 60 votes typically required to overcome a filibuster. This legislative mechanism has been used by both parties in recent years to advance major spending and tax bills when they control both chambers of Congress and the White House. The $69.5 billion price tag represents a significant investment in immigration enforcement infrastructure and personnel.

The legislation now heads to the House of Representatives, where Republican leadership will need to navigate their own narrow majority to send the bill to President Trump's desk. House passage is expected but not guaranteed, as conservative and moderate factions within the GOP caucus have differed on immigration policy details throughout the current session. The bill's multiyear funding structure aims to provide stability for enforcement operations beyond the typical annual appropriations cycle.

The vote caps months of negotiations over immigration policy and spending levels. Republican leaders had prioritized the legislation as part of their broader agenda on border security, while Democrats largely opposed the measure. The overnight session reflected the tight timeline facing Senate leadership as they work to clear legislative priorities before an upcoming recess.

Maja Chwalinska, a Polish qualifier, has reached the French Open final in one of the most improbable runs in Grand Slam history. The 24-year-old defeated Diana Shnaider 7-6 (4), 6-4 in the semifinals to secure her place...

9:00 AM ET

Maja Chwalinska, a Polish qualifier, has reached the French Open final in one of the most improbable runs in Grand Slam history. The 24-year-old defeated Diana Shnaider 7-6 (4), 6-4 in the semifinals to secure her place in Saturday's championship match.

Chwalinska's achievement is historic. She is the first qualifier ever to reach the French Open final and only the second qualifier to reach any Grand Slam final, following Emma Raducanu's run at the 2021 US Open. During her three-week journey through the qualifying draw and main draw, she has won nine matches while losing just one set.

The emotional toll of her success was evident after her victory. "Like a dream, honestly. I don't know what's going on," Chwalinska said. "I don't know what to say." She later reflected on the improbability of her run, saying "Let's not pretend someone expected it. I mean, I was outside top 100 and now I'm in the finals of a grand slam, so I feel like it's a big thing. So it's hard to process it."

Before this tournament, Chwalinska had competed professionally for a decade without reaching the top 100. Her current ranking of 114 sits just one spot behind her career high. She had won only one main draw match at a Grand Slam prior to Roland Garros, coming at Wimbledon in 2022.

Her opponent in the final will be Mirra Andreeva, the eighth seed, who defeated Marta Kostyuk 6-1, 6-3 in her semifinal. The matchup sets up a final between two players who were not considered title favorites at the tournament's start.

The financial reward for Chwalinska's run has been substantial. Her prize money of $1,626,744 nearly doubles the $864,030 she had earned throughout her entire career. The substantial paycheck comes after she faced uncertainty during her tournament run about being able to afford her hotel expenses until Polish drinks company Oshee stepped in to help.

Despite her physical disadvantages on the court, Chwalinska has succeeded through tactical skill and intelligence. At 1.64 meters tall, she is diminutive for professional tennis and lacks the power of many competitors. She has compensated by using slices, angles, and drop shots to confound opponents and disrupt their rhythm.

Her semifinal opponent, Shnaider, had pulled off a stunning upset herself by defeating world number one Aryna Sabalenka in the quarterfinals after recovering from 6-3, 4-1 down. However, Shnaider could not back up that monumental win against Chwalinska's crafty and intelligent play.

The French Open final will determine which player claims their first Grand Slam title.
