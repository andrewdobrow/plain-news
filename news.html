The FBI has disrupted an alleged threat targeting President Trump's UFC event at the White House, with multiple suspects now in custody, FBI Director Kash Patel announced Tuesday.

Federal officials became aware of a potential threat from individuals outside the Washington, D.C. area on June 10, according to Patel. In a statement, he said that "thanks to the rapid action of this FBI, our partners, and the Department of Justice in a multi-state operation, multiple individuals are now in custody and allegedly planned attacks were stopped cold."

The disrupted plot represents the latest security concern for Trump, who has faced multiple attempts on his life during his presidency. Recent incidents include a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner. Law enforcement agencies across the country are managing what officials describe as an elevated period of political violence.

Patel did not immediately disclose details about the nature of the alleged threat or provide the identities of those taken into custody. He indicated that additional information about the plot would be released once charges are unsealed.

The UFC event took place on the White House south lawn over the weekend, coinciding with Trump's 80th birthday celebration. The event, called UFC Freedom 250, featured a title fight between lightweight champion Ilia Topuria and interim champion Justin Gaethje. The White House had billed it as a historic national celebration, with 4,000 ticketed guests and a fan festival on the Ellipse expected to draw up to 100,000 people.

Sources reported that the alleged threat involved a potential drone attack, though official confirmation of the threat's specific nature remained limited.

The event drew significant attention not only for its security implications but also for statements made by a UFC fighter at the event who made disparaging comments about former first lady Michelle Obama while standing near the president. The incident drew widespread condemnation and raised questions about event protocols.

The FBI's counterterrorism and protective intelligence divisions have intensified monitoring of potential threats against government officials and public events in recent months. Federal law enforcement has increased coordination with state and local agencies to identify and investigate individuals who may pose security risks to high-profile political figures and gatherings.

Meanwhile, UFC president Dana White indicated that another White House fight night may not happen again, citing weather concerns, logistical challenges with constructing the cage and staging events at federal landmarks, and soaring costs that made the event a one-time affair.

The White House has not commented on whether security protocols for future events will be adjusted following the disrupted threat.

The incident occurred at the military installation located in Kern County, approximately 100 miles north of Los Angeles in the Mojave Desert.

Edwards Air Force Base serves as a primary testing site for military and experimental aircraft. The base has historically been the location for numerous aviation milestones and development programs since its establishment. The B-52, a long-range strategic bomber that entered service in the 1950s, was conducting a test flight when the crash happened.

All eight crew members aboard the aircraft were killed in the incident. The base has not yet released the identities of the deceased or specific details about what caused the accident. While the B-52 typically carries a crew of five during standard operational missions, test flights often require additional personnel to conduct specialized monitoring and evaluation activities.

The crash represents one of the deadliest military aviation accidents in recent years. The B-52 fleet continues to serve as a critical component of the United States strategic bomber force. Despite the aircraft's age, the Air Force regularly updates and maintains the planes through ongoing modifications and testing programs designed to extend their operational lifespan.

The Air Force has not announced whether it will ground other B-52 aircraft while investigations proceed. Investigators from the Air Force and other agencies have begun examining the wreckage and flight data to determine the cause of the crash. The investigation process will involve analyzing multiple factors that may have contributed to the accident.

Edwards Air Force Base regularly conducts test flights of both new and existing aircraft as part of ongoing military aviation development and maintenance operations. The remote location and extensive facilities at the base make it well suited for this testing work. The installation has been instrumental in evaluating military aircraft for decades.

The loss of eight crew members represents a significant tragedy for the military aviation community. The incident underscores the inherent risks associated with test flight operations, which often involve pushing aircraft to their limits to evaluate performance and safety systems. Families and colleagues of the deceased crew members are grieving the loss of these service members.

As the investigation continues, more information about the circumstances surrounding the crash is expected to emerge. The Air Force will likely release additional details once investigators have gathered sufficient evidence and completed preliminary analyses of the available data.

Industry analysts noted that the transaction reflected both the premium valuations commanded by AI companies and investor enthusiasm for SpaceX's post-IPO prospects.

SpaceX plans to integrate Cursor's AI coding capabilities across its operations. The technology could accelerate software development for rocket systems, satellite networks, and ground support infrastructure. Musk has previously pursued artificial intelligence investments through various ventures, and this acquisition brings substantial AI capabilities directly under SpaceX's corporate structure.

The company indicated in its IPO materials that it sees a $26 trillion addressable market opportunity in artificial intelligence, suggesting the Cursor acquisition aligns with long-term strategic ambitions. The rapid execution of the deal following the public market debut demonstrated SpaceX's aggressive expansion strategy in technology sectors beyond traditional aerospace.

The combined company will leverage Cursor's engineering team and technology platform to strengthen SpaceX's competitive position in enterprise AI applications. By acquiring established AI talent and existing product capabilities, SpaceX avoided the longer timeline associated with building such technology independently.

The all-stock transaction closed quickly, reflecting the confidence SpaceX's leadership and investors placed in the company's newly public valuation and future prospects.

The announcement marks a significant moment for Serena, who is returning to professional tennis after four years away from competition. The 44-year-old did not request a singles wildcard and has remained noncommittal about whether she will compete in singles play at the tournament. Venus, who is 46, has also not received a singles wildcard. She has continued to play sporadically on the professional tour since her debut in 1994, taking time away only due to health-related issues.

The Williams sisters are among the most successful doubles partnerships in tennis history. Together, they have won 14 Grand Slam doubles titles, the second-highest total in the Open Era. They have claimed six women's doubles titles at Wimbledon specifically, the highest total in the Open Era at that venue. Their partnership has also yielded three Olympic gold medals in women's doubles, and they reached the number one ranking in WTA doubles.

The wildcard entry allows the sisters to bypass the standard qualification process and compete directly in the main draw. Wimbledon organizers typically reserve wildcard spots for players who may not meet current ranking requirements but have exceptional circumstances or significant historical connections to the tournament. The Williams sisters clearly fit this category given their storied record at the All England Club.

Serena recently returned to competition at Queen's Club, where she won her first match alongside Victoria Mboko against Erin Routliffe and Nichole Melichar Martinez. However, Mboko subsequently withdrew from their next match and the remainder of the grass court season after suffering a torn medial collateral ligament. Serena is continuing her comeback preparations by competing at the Berlin WTA tournament this week alongside world number 10 singles player Karolina Muchova.

The wildcard announcement also included other notable decisions. Maja Chwalinska, who reached the French Open women's singles final as a qualifier, received a wildcard despite not meeting the rankings cutoff. Her sharp recent rise to number 21 in the world rankings will make her an unusual combination of both wildcard and seeded player at the Grand Slam tournament. Additionally, retiring players Stan Wawrinka and Grigor Dimitrov received singles wildcards, while British veteran Dan Evans received a doubles wildcard with compatriot Henry Searle.

The appearance of the Williams sisters will generate significant attention for the tournament and their presence on court together has historically drawn large crowds and television audiences.

A few minutes ago

The Trump administration's Justice Department has approved the $111 billion merger between Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery, concluding an eight-month antitrust investigation by the department's career staff.

The department announced Friday that it had "completed its analysis of the proposed merger" and determined the transaction would not likely harm competition or consumers across streaming video on demand, linear television, or theatrical film distribution. The approval represents a significant victory for the deal, which combines two major entertainment conglomerates controlling major film studios, cable networks, and streaming services.

The merger combines Paramount's assets including CBS News and Paramount Pictures with Warner Bros. Discovery's holdings of HBO, CNN, and DC Comics properties. Skydance, controlled by the Ellison family, is providing financing for the acquisition. Three Gulf sovereign-wealth funds have committed a combined $24 billion to support the deal.

The Justice Department stated that over the investigation period, it "received from the Parties over two million documents from over 80 custodians, substantial productions of data, as well as extensive documents, data, and advocacy from third parties across the media and entertainment ecosystem."

Paramount responded to the approval by stating the deal is "pro-competitive, resulting in a stronger company better positioned to compete against dominant technology platforms in an industry increasingly defined by intense competition for audiences, talent, technology, and investment."

However, the merger faces continued scrutiny domestically and internationally. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened its own investigation and set an August 7 deadline to determine whether a more in-depth review is necessary. Australian regulators have already approved the deal. More significantly, California Attorney General Rob Bonta indicated Friday that his office's investigation remains ongoing, leaving open the possibility of state attorneys general filing a lawsuit to block the transaction.

Opponents of the merger have criticized the approval. Craig Aaron, co-chief executive of Free Press, stated the "fix was in at the Trump Justice Department from the start," adding that the deal "will undermine competition, destroy jobs, slant the news and endanger our democracy." Senator Elizabeth Warren called the approval "terrible news for every American who doesn't want Trump-aligned billionaires to control what they watch and how much they pay."

The merger also faces internal concerns from journalists. Staff at both CBS News and CNN have expressed worry about potential network consolidation, which could result in significant job cuts. The companies have projected $6 billion in synergies from combining operations. Additionally, some CNN staff have raised concerns about the Ellison family's influence on editorial direction, despite pledges that CNN's editorial independence would be protected.
