2:09 AM ET

Tropical Storm Arthur made landfall along the Gulf Coast on Thursday, marking the first named storm of the Atlantic hurricane season to reach the United States. The system is bringing heavy rainfall and life-threatening flood risks to coastal communities across the region.

Weather forecasters have issued warnings about dangerous flash flooding conditions as Arthur moves inland from the Gulf. Multiple coastal areas are experiencing significant rainfall, with precipitation expected to continue throughout the day and potentially persist for several days as the storm travels further into the Gulf states.

The primary hazard from Arthur remains flooding, particularly in low-lying coastal regions where storm surge can combine with rainfall accumulation to create dangerous conditions. Storm surge warnings have been issued for affected areas as officials urge residents to stay alert to changing weather conditions.

According to forecasters, Arthur is not expected to strengthen further as it continues inland. The storm's intensity is being closely monitored by weather officials as it tracks across the region. However, heavy rain bands associated with the system could persist across Gulf state areas for an extended period.

Residents in affected areas have been advised to follow local emergency management instructions carefully. Authorities recommend that people in vulnerable locations prepare for potential evacuations if conditions deteriorate. Those in flood-prone areas should take precautions to protect property and ensure personal safety.

The arrival of Arthur comes earlier in the hurricane season than typical for significant Gulf Coast impacts. The tropical storm represents the beginning of what could be an active Atlantic hurricane season ahead.

As Arthur moves further inland and distances itself from the warm waters that fuel tropical systems, the storm is expected to gradually weaken. Meteorologists predict that normal June weather should return to the Gulf Coast region by the weekend as Arthur dissipates completely.

Officials continue to monitor the storm's path and intensity as it progresses northward. Residents should remain vigilant about weather updates and heed all guidance from local emergency management agencies. The National Weather Service and other forecasting agencies are providing regular updates on rainfall totals, storm movement, and any changes to the storm's strength.

The flooding threat posed by Arthur underscores the importance of preparedness during Atlantic hurricane season. Communities along the Gulf Coast are implementing emergency response plans and mobilizing resources to assist residents who may be affected by heavy rain and rising water levels in the coming days.

A few minutes ago

The Federal Reserve kept interest rates unchanged at its first meeting under new Chair Kevin Warsh, maintaining the benchmark rate in a range of 3.5% to 3.75%. However, the central bank signaled a notable shift in thinking, with nine of the Fed's voting members now projecting at least one rate increase before the end of the year.

The decision marked a significant departure from the Fed's outlook just three months earlier. In March, twelve of nineteen officials had projected rate cuts by year's end. The new projections represent a complete reversal in the committee's monetary policy direction as inflation concerns have intensified.

Stock markets reacted negatively to the announcement. The Dow dropped 500 points, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each fell more than 1.2% following the news.

The Fed's statement acknowledged economic challenges facing the country. "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East," the committee said. Inflation has climbed to 4.2%, the highest level since 2023, driven primarily by energy price spikes related to Middle Eastern tensions. This remains well above the Fed's 2% target, though core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, has risen only modestly to 2.9%.

Warsh used his first press conference as chair to outline plans for reshaping the central bank's operations. He announced five new taskforces to examine the Fed's monetary policy approach, including its communications strategy, balance sheet management, data analysis, productivity assessments, and employment focus. One taskforce will specifically evaluate how the Fed communicates with the public, including its news conferences, dot-plot projections, and meeting transcripts.

The Fed's policy statement under Warsh's leadership was notably shorter than previous statements, reflecting his stated intention to tighten public communications on future guidance.

Notably, Warsh was the sole board member who did not contribute to the rate projections released by the committee. During his press conference, he declined to discuss whether he had met with President Trump since taking office.

The Fed chair emphasized that monetary policy has limited direct impact on commodity prices like oil and food costs. "The price of oil in the markets or the price of a dozen eggs does not have first-order consequences to what we're doing," he said. "But we have an important job there. And it's to make sure that those changes in oil, or beef, or eggs or milk don't broaden in the economy."

Meanwhile, the labor market has remained relatively resilient, with unemployment holding steady at 4.3%. However, hourly earnings growth has slowed to 0.7% on a seasonally adjusted basis, suggesting that wage gains have been eroded by rising prices.

NASA announced a public-private partnership with Relativity Space to develop a Mars orbiter mission, marking a significant step in the agency's approach to planetary exploration. The rocket manufacturer, acquired by former Google executive chair Eric Schmidt in 2025, will privately develop the Aeolus mission to Mars orbit.

The partnership represents a new model for NASA's Mars science program, combining government objectives with private sector resources and technology. Relativity Space will shoulder much of the development cost and operational responsibility for the orbiter, which is designed to advance Mars research. The mission structure mirrors approaches NASA has used for commercial cargo and crew transportation to the International Space Station.

Relativity Space faced setbacks in its initial business model before Schmidt's acquisition. The company originally focused on 3D-printed rockets for satellite launches but struggled to reach orbit on schedule. Under new ownership, the firm shifted strategy toward larger rockets and deep space missions. The Mars contract represents the company's most ambitious project to date.

The announcement sets up a competitive dynamic in Mars exploration. SpaceX, led by Elon Musk, has publicly stated Mars colonization as a core company goal and is developing the Starship vehicle partly for Mars missions. NASA has worked with SpaceX on multiple projects, including crew transportation and lunar lander development for the Artemis program. The agency now has partnerships with both companies pursuing Mars objectives through different approaches.

NASA did not announce a timeline for the Aeolus mission launch. Mars missions require precise launch windows when Earth and Mars are favorably aligned, which occur roughly every 26 months. The orbiter will conduct science observations from Mars orbit, though specific instruments and research priorities were not detailed in the announcement.

Yesterday, 9:20 PM ET

The United States and Iran have signed a memorandum of understanding to end their military conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump signed the agreement remotely ahead of its originally scheduled signing ceremony in Switzerland, with the deal now in effect.

The 14-paragraph memo includes provisions to end fighting, establish that Iran will never develop nuclear weapons, and allocate a $300 billion redevelopment package for Iran. Trump confirmed the signing during a press conference at the G-7 summit in France, where he defended the agreement while appearing to lower his earlier expectations.

The accord aims to stabilize global energy markets and restore shipping through a critical waterway through which roughly 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passed before the conflict. For two months, Trump pursued this deal to address the severe economic consequences of the war.

However, significant obstacles remain. An estimated 5,000 mines that Iran placed in the strait during the conflict will delay reopening shipping lanes. Trump said the U.S. military was already searching for some of these mines. The continued presence of extensive mining will complicate efforts to restore normal traffic quickly.

The global economic impact has been severe. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remain stranded in the Gulf. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in additional aid for households and farmers. Asian countries dependent on Gulf oil have faced shortages of fuel, fertilizer, and raw materials.

Peace negotiations faced significant delays. Pakistan had attempted to mediate talks in Islamabad, but Iran never publicly accepted an invitation and a U.S. delegation led by Vice President JD Vance never left Washington. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf had stated that reopening the strait would be "impossible" while U.S. and Israeli actions continued, citing a U.S. naval blockade and other violations of the ceasefire.

Trump has warned he could authorize military strikes against Iran again if nuclear negotiations fail. Critics argue the deal simply returns conditions to their pre-war state, questioning whether the conflict's costs were justified. Trump had initially promised total victory over Iran but ultimately agreed to a negotiated settlement rather than the decisive outcome he initially sought.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu opposed ending the conflict through negotiation, calling instead for continued military action. He had promised the Israeli public "total victory" but had to settle for Trump's memorandum. However, even the United Arab Emirates, previously the most hawkish Gulf state, joined the regional consensus supporting the deal.

The agreement represents a significant shift in U.S. policy toward Iran following weeks of military confrontation and comes as Trump faces pressure to demonstrate concrete achievements from the military action.
