Yesterday, 3:17 PM ET

The Supreme Court ruled 8-1 on Monday to allow the Trump administration to proceed with border wall construction in the Big Bend region of southwest Texas, lifting a lower court injunction that had halted the project. The court found that the federal judge who blocked construction lacked authority to do so and said the administration was extremely likely to prevail on that issue. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the lone dissenter.

The dispute centers on three waivers issued by Homeland Security secretaries Kristi Noem and Markwayne Mullin between February and July that allow the government to bypass environmental and conservation laws for the project. The planned construction covers 341 miles in the sparsely populated area along the Rio Grande, including parts of Big Bend National Park. The administration said 89,000 people have been detained attempting to cross there in recent years.

District Judge Kathleen Cardone had invalidated the waivers in October, ruling that the Homeland Security secretary lacked such broad powers under a 1996 law. The administration argued that Congress granted it wide authority to build border infrastructure without regulatory delays. The Supreme Court said the government has compelling interests when border security policies are challenged.

The case was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity, Friends of the Ruidosa Church, and a local property owner who argued construction would permanently damage the landscape and threaten historic and cultural sites. They contended the Big Bend area has the lowest migrant apprehension rates along the southern border, making the waivers inappropriate. Government data confirms Big Bend has the fewest encounters between federal agents and migrants of any border section. The administration has said completing the wall there is necessary to prevent the area from becoming an entry point if other sections are built.

The gunman suspected of killing six people in central Krakow and three family members at their home died in a hospital early Monday morning, Polish police said. The 24-year-old Polish man was shot by police officers within three minutes of opening fire in the city's Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon. He had been hospitalized with injuries sustained during a gunfight with responding officers.

Polish prosecutors said Monday that investigations into the suspect's motive remain ongoing, walking back earlier statements by a government minister who described the gunman as inspired by far-right ideologies and ultra-nationalism. Przemysław Nowak, a spokesperson for Poland's National Prosecutor's Office, told reporters the motive inquiry continues. The suspect has not been officially named.

The attack killed four Polish citizens, a British national, and an Austrian woman in the Main Market Square. Police later found three bodies at the gunman's home, identified as his 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother, and 21-year-old sister. Authorities seized four firearms from the suspect's car, including two AR-15 rifles and two handguns, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition. Polish media reported the suspect held a firearms permit.

Sixteen people were hospitalized after the shooting, including two police officers who were injured during the gunfight with the suspect. A surgeon who was eating nearby when the attack began rushed to the scene and helped treat victims before ambulances arrived. Professor Michał Pędziwiatr said his colleague used his hand to stem bleeding from a severely injured police officer, and that intervention undoubtedly saved the officer's life. Both wounded officers have since emerged from comas.

Poland declared two days of national mourning following the attack. Prime Minister Donald Tusk visited Krakow and said the country had not previously experienced such drastic terrorism.

Yesterday, 7:27 PM ET

Immigration and Customs Enforcement detained an 8-year-old Haitian boy at a routine appointment in downtown San Diego last week, separating him from his uncle and transferring him to federal custody, according to school officials and an advocacy group. The boy, a third grader at Fay Elementary School, lost his Temporary Protected Status protections this summer after a Supreme Court decision allowed President Donald Trump to end TPS for Haitians. He was summoned to meet with ICE officers Wednesday, and his uncle brought him to the appointment to comply with the law, according to a letter San Diego Unified School District officials sent Friday to the Department of Homeland Security and the Department of Health and Human Services.

ICE officers determined the boy to be an unaccompanied minor after the uncle could not establish legal guardianship, according to a DHS spokesperson. The agency said it removed the boy from the household out of caution to ensure his safety. The boy was subsequently transferred to the Office of Refugee Resettlement, which takes custody of children deemed unaccompanied minors. As of Monday, his family did not know where he was being held, according to Representative Juan Vargas.

The boy had been living with his uncle and cousins after his father was deported last year, according to the school district and the Haitian Bridge Alliance, an immigrant rights advocacy group. TPS is a humanitarian designation that had shielded hundreds of thousands of Haitians from deportation following the country's 2010 earthquake. The protections were renewed multiple times as Haiti descended into crisis, with gangs now controlling much of the capital after the president was assassinated in 2021.

The school district said it is sending crisis counselors to Fay Elementary School this week because the boy's classmates have expressed concern and fear for him. School officials asked DHS and HHS to reconsider how immigration enforcement policies are affecting students, but as of Monday had received no response. The Office of Refugee Resettlement said it does not comment on specific cases. The boy is among 1,640 children considered unaccompanied minors currently in HHS care.

Yesterday, 8:38 PM ET

Qantas will outsource 850 corporate jobs to consulting firm Accenture, with most of the positions moving to India, as the Australian airline accelerates investment in artificial intelligence and information technology modernization. Chief Executive Vanessa Hudson announced the plan in an internal email to staff on Tuesday, saying the job cuts will begin in mid-2027.

The outsourcing will start with finance, procurement, human resources, and marketing roles shifting mostly to teams in India and some to Australia. Hudson said the airline is seeking to modernize its IT systems and cited the rapid pace of technological change. Qantas spends more than 1 billion Australian dollars annually on technology and operates more than 1,000 legacy applications that were not designed to work together, she said.

Under the multi-year deal with Accenture, Qantas aims to redesign and simplify corporate functions and reduce duplication so staff can focus on work that creates value for customers and the business, Hudson said. The company said the work transferred to Accenture will eventually be transformed further with automation and artificial intelligence.

Qantas employs approximately 28,000 staff in Australia and 4,000 globally. The announcement comes days after the airline issued a market update indicating fare increases and flight reductions due to higher fuel costs. Hudson acknowledged the difficulty of the news, telling staff she was sorry for the uncertainty it brings. The airline said it is in talks with unions and staff on the plans.

Wyndham exposed millions of customer records after hardcoded password found in Android app

Hotel giant Wyndham acknowledged a security incident after a researcher discovered a hardcoded password in its Android app that granted access to an internal database containing personal information for millions of rewards members. Security researcher Robert Wiggins found the credential embedded in the app's source code, which allowed access to a Salesforce-hosted database stor...

Yesterday, 6:03 PM ET

Hotel giant Wyndham acknowledged a security incident after a researcher discovered a hardcoded password in its Android app that granted access to an internal database containing personal information for millions of rewards members. Security researcher Robert Wiggins found the credential embedded in the app's source code, which allowed access to a Salesforce-hosted database storing customer names, email addresses, member numbers, and login tokens.

The database contained millions of entries from Wyndham's 129 million enrolled rewards members, though Wiggins could not determine an exact count of exposed records. The credential was revoked after Wiggins contacted Wyndham through a journalist because the company had no public channel for reporting security vulnerabilities. A Wyndham spokesperson said the company addressed the issue and found no evidence of unauthorized access, but declined to say whether server logs existed to verify that claim.

Wyndham would not disclose how long the password was embedded in the app, whether it plans to notify affected customers, or whether it will report the incident to the Securities and Exchange Commission as required for material cybersecurity events at public companies. Hardcoded credentials are considered a critical security weakness because anyone with the software can extract and use the password, and such vulnerabilities typically receive maximum severity ratings.

This marks the second major discovery of hardcoded credentials by Wiggins in two weeks, following a similar flaw in the Applebee's app. Wiggins used artificial intelligence tools to scan decompiled app code for such weaknesses. Wyndham's cybersecurity director provided an email address for researchers to report issues, but the company declined to say whether it would establish a formal vulnerability disclosure program or update its website to publicize the reporting channel.

Wyndham experienced a separate security incident in 2024 when hotel check-in computers were found running remote-access spyware that leaked guest records to the internet. That spyware company was later hacked and shut down, and its owner was convicted of making and selling unlawful spyware.

Yesterday, 7:57 PM ET

Cincinnati Reds first baseman Sal Stewart won the National League Rookie of the Year award, the Baseball Writers Association of America announced Monday. Stewart received 20 of 30 first-place votes and was the only player in the NL field to appear in the top five on every ballot. The award also earns Cincinnati a bonus pick after the first round of the 2027 draft through the Prospect Promotion Incentive.

Stewart led NL rookies with 700 plate appearances and hit .263/.330/.457 with 32 home runs and 112 RBI. His home run total was 12 more than any other rookie in the league, while his RBI count was nearly 40 ahead of any other first-year player. He finished eighth in the NL in home runs and trailed only Cardinals first baseman Alec Burleson in RBI among all league hitters. Stewart started 110 games at first base, 33 at third base, 11 as a designated hitter, and seven at second base.

Mets outfielder Carson Benge finished second with seven first-place votes and Cardinals middle infielder JJ Wetherholt placed third with three first-place votes. Benge hit .282/.346/.430 with a 20-20 season and was one of the best hitters in the NL after the All-Star Break. Wetherholt appeared to be Stewart's top competition for much of the season but struggled in the second half around a two-week absence due to right wrist tendinitis. Both Benge and Wetherholt were Gold Glove finalists at their positions.

Park adjustments for Cincinnati's hitter-friendly stadium and Stewart's lack of defensive value limited his standing in advanced metrics. Both FanGraphs and Baseball Reference graded Stewart at two wins above replacement, about half the totals for Benge and Wetherholt. The majority of voters prioritized Stewart's power production. All three finalists met Prospect Promotion Incentive criteria and opened the year on their teams' MLB rosters, meaning the winner would secure a bonus pick. Stewart is the Reds' second Rookie of the Year winner this decade and ninth in franchise history.

In 1939, he starred in...

Yesterday, 5:56 PM ET

Donnie Dunagan, the child actor who voiced the title character in Disney's 1942 animated film Bambi, has died of lung cancer, his agents confirmed. Dunagan died Nov. 4. He was 92.

Dunagan's brief acting career spanned just seven films over a few years but included two enduring roles. At age four, he made his film debut in 1938's Mother Carey's Chickens. In 1939, he starred in Son of Frankenstein alongside Boris Karloff and Bela Lugosi, playing the son of Baron Wolf von Frankenstein. Walt Disney personally selected Dunagan to be the facial model for Bambi because of his brown eyes, having animators sketch his expressions before offering him the voice role. Dunagan later called the work the thrill of his young life.

After his acting career ended, Dunagan volunteered for the Marines in the early 1950s and served 21 years, including combat in the Korean and Vietnam wars. He earned a Bronze Star and Purple Heart and rose to the rank of major. He kept his Bambi role secret throughout his military service, fearing it would undermine his authority. He said in 2005 that if the troops he led had found out about Bambi, they would have ridden him out on a rail.

Disney tracked Dunagan down in the early 2000s for a DVD release of Bambi, returning him to public life. He became a Disney ambassador and appeared at fan conventions. His agents said Dunagan loved his fans, rallied other veterans, and had an infectious smile and warmth that never lost the childlike spirit of his acting youth.
