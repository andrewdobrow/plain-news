12:56 AM ET

The Trump administration announced plans to revoke non-immigrant visas for foreigners who entered the country as short-term visitors but subsequently applied for or are seeking asylum status, according to a State Department spokesperson. The move would represent the largest mass visa revocation in U.S. history.

A State Department representative said the administration is coordinating with the Department of Homeland Security "to identify and revoke the non-immigrant visas of foreigners who have come to the United States claiming to be short-term visitors, but then file for asylum to stay here permanently."

The targeted visas include B-1 business visas and B-2 tourism visas issued between 2016 and 2026. Individuals holding these visas who have filed asylum applications or are in the process of seeking asylum protection would lose their legal authorization to remain in the country. Officials characterize the action as closing what they view as a loophole in the immigration system.

The scale of this proposed action exceeds any previous mass visa cancellation by the federal government. Those affected could face removal proceedings. The administration has not yet announced the specific timeline or implementation details for revoking the visas.

People who arrive on temporary business or tourism visas are not authorized to work long-term or reside permanently in the United States. However, asylum seekers typically apply for protection after arriving in the country, claiming they face persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. The asylum application process frequently takes months or years to resolve, and applicants generally remain in the country while their cases are pending.

The revocation plan could affect asylum seekers at various stages of the process, including those who recently filed applications and those awaiting court hearings. Immigration advocates have previously argued that many asylum seekers who enter on temporary visas do so because they lack alternative legal pathways to reach the United States and request protection.

The administration's proposal represents a significant escalation in its immigration enforcement approach since taking office. It comes as the Trump administration pursues multiple policies intended to tighten immigration procedures, including new restrictions on visa duration for foreign journalists and foreign students.

The revocation effort aligns with the administration's broader immigration crackdown, which has also included deportation agreements with other countries. Earlier this month, the first group of deportees under a new agreement arrived in Liberia as part of arrangements that could ultimately send as many as 1,200 people from the United States to the West African nation.

NPR's senior climate editor has provided guidance to help journalists and the public understand the connection between the current heat wave and broader climate patterns. This guidance addresses how to interpret extreme weather events within the context of long-term climate data and trends, helping news organizations provide accurate information about the heat wave's intensity and expected duration.

The current heat wave follows a pattern of increasing extreme weather events affecting the country in recent years. Scientists use such episodes to study how rising global temperatures influence the frequency and severity of weather patterns. Each extreme event provides researchers with additional data points for tracking how climate change affects everyday weather conditions.

Public health officials in affected areas have issued advisories urging residents to take precautions during the high temperatures. The heat wave's timing and geographic spread are being carefully documented as part of ongoing climate monitoring efforts. Weather services continue tracking the system's movement and intensity as it develops.

Research from across Europe demonstrates an important concern that applies broadly to heat waves: the impact is not equally distributed. A recent European heat wave laid bare significant disparities in how different populations experience extreme temperatures. Those living in poverty disproportionately bear the brunt of such events, lacking access to air conditioning, adequate housing insulation, or green spaces. Some face additional exposure through their work in agriculture and construction, while others struggle to access adequate healthcare during heat emergencies.

According to climate researchers, extreme temperatures amplify existing vulnerabilities in society. Heat kills far more people annually than other causes, with research suggesting that the combination of extreme temperatures and inequality could account for more than 100,000 deaths per year in some regions. Recent European heat waves resulted in thousands of additional deaths, underscoring the serious public health consequences.

The episode highlights the growing need for clear communication about climate science and extreme weather. As temperatures climb, understanding the distinction between individual weather events and longer-term climate trends becomes increasingly important for public safety planning and policy decisions. This understanding is essential for developing appropriate responses that address not only immediate dangers but also the underlying inequalities that determine who suffers most during extreme heat events.

The heat wave is expected to continue affecting regions across the country in the coming days, making timely public health guidance and community preparation critical.

This collaborative approach strengthens ongoing research in astronomy and astrophysics.

The mission reflects NASA's continued commitment to investing in space science and developing advanced exploration technology. By placing the Roman Space Telescope in orbit, NASA aims to answer fundamental questions about the cosmos while pushing the boundaries of what is possible in astronomical observation.

The launch from Florida continues the state's role as a primary location for American space operations. The facility has hosted numerous significant missions and remains central to the country's space exploration efforts.

Scientists and space enthusiasts worldwide anticipate the Roman Space Telescope's deployment, expecting it to provide unprecedented insights into the structure and composition of the universe. The telescope's advanced capabilities promise to revolutionize our understanding of distant galaxies, exoplanets, and other cosmic phenomena.

NASA's preparation for this launch demonstrates the agency's ongoing dedication to space science. With comprehensive coverage plans in place, the public will have the opportunity to witness this important moment in the history of space exploration and scientific discovery.
