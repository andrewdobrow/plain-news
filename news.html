At least 12 people were shot during the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio, when gunfire erupted just after 5:30 p.m. on Saturday. Police continue searching for at least two shooters who fled the scene, with the investigation entering its second day.

Toledo police deputy chief Joseph Heffernan said the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the violence occurred at the annual community gathering, which features live music and architectural home tours. Two of the 12 victims are reported to be in critical condition. The ages of those wounded ranged from 14 to 61, with most in their early 20s.

Several hundred people were present at the festival when shots rang out. Witnesses described scenes of chaos as attendees scrambled for safety. One video posted online showed a young man bleeding from a wound in his side as he walked down the street before sitting down.

With no suspects in custody, law enforcement officials have appealed to festival attendees to provide any photos or videos from their phones that might help identify the shooters. Heffernan noted that authorities had assigned extra police officers to the festival, and a large number of off-duty officers were working the event itself.

The Toledo fire chief, Allison Armstrong, said it was difficult to transport victims to area hospitals because of closed roads and traffic congestion as people left the festival.

Following the shooting, festival organizers announced the cancellation of all planned events on Sunday. In a statement, organizers expressed heartbreak over the injured attendees, saying "Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood." After consulting with Toledo civic and police authorities, they determined it would be "not compassionate, responsible or possible to continue the festival."

Deputy Chief Heffernan commented on the impact of the violence, stating: "It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years."

Ohio Governor Mike DeWine issued a statement saying "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence." Ohio Senator Jon Husted called the incident "senseless" and thanked law enforcement for acting quickly to protect residents and investigate the shooting.

According to the Gun Violence Archive, there have been more than 170 mass shootings in the United States so far this year, defined as incidents in which four or more victims are wounded or killed. The Toledo shooting adds to the nation's continuing pattern of gun violence at public gatherings.

Yesterday, 10:00 PM ET

Microsoft and Nvidia announced a new generation of Windows personal computers designed to run artificial intelligence tasks locally on devices at the Computex technology conference in Taiwan. The collaboration marks what Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang described as a reinvention of the PC "for the first time in 40 years."

The centerpiece of the announcement is Nvidia's RTX Spark chip, a combination microprocessor and graphics chip developed with help from Taiwan's MediaTek. Unlike previous approaches that rely heavily on cloud computing, the RTX Spark is designed to run AI agents directly on laptops and desktop computers. According to Huang, the chip could replace traditional mouse and keyboard interactions by allowing AI agents to navigate PCs autonomously.

The RTX Spark will launch this year and will be integrated into computers from major manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP. Microsoft's Windows software will power these machines as part of the three-year collaboration between the two companies. Despite the chip's processing power, Nvidia emphasized that computers using it will remain thin and light.

The move represents Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in AI semiconductor markets. The company is pushing into integrated chips that power entire computers, opening a new business line in the consumer PC industry. Nvidia is also developing the Vera central processing unit, designed for AI agents and targeted at early adopters including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Industry analysts see significant implications in the announcement. Neil Shah, a co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the RTX Spark moment with major technological shifts like the iPhone and ChatGPT. "The RTX Spark looks to transform the traditional app-centric PC to a real useful agentic AI personal computer which will eventually be in every home in coming years as private edge AI agents become pivotal," Shah said.

The announcement intensifies competition in the PC chip market. Intel plans to begin shipping an AI chip later this year using cheaper memory and cooling technology than competitors. The company announced a new graphics processing unit called Xe3P, codenamed Crescent Island, which Intel says is "purpose-built for this upcoming AI generation of agents."

During his Computex presentation, Huang addressed concerns about AI's impact on employment. He dismissed suggestions that artificial intelligence would reduce demand for software engineers, arguing instead that it would increase hiring by making workers more productive. "The number of engineers, software engineers, is actually increasing," Huang said. "People talk about AI reducing jobs – complete nonsense. It's causing more software engineers to be hired."

The partnership signals a shift in how personal computers will integrate AI capabilities, embedding AI processing directly into hardware rather than treating it as a cloud-based feature accessed through internet connections.

A stripped-back production of Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman dominated the 79th annual Tony Awards, winning six awards including best revival of a play. The reimagined classic claimed more honors than any other production at the ceremony, which was hosted by singer Pink at Radio City Music Hall.

The production made history as the most awarded play revival in Tony Awards history. Director Joe Mantello praised Miller's story as one that "still talks to us through time." Nathan Lane accepted the award on behalf of the cast, calling it a play that "continues to teach us who we are as humans and Americans."

Laurie Metcalf won best featured actress in a play for her role in the revival, marking her third Tony Award. She beat out June Squibb and Aya Cash for the honor. The production has now won multiple times at the Tonys, having previously won for best play in 1949, best revival in 1984, 1999, and 2012.

In other acting categories, Lesley Manville won best leading actress in a play for her performance in Oedipus, a Broadway transfer of the classical drama. She previously won an Olivier Award for the role. "I'm a bit overwhelmed, it was my first time on Broadway so this is such a big deal," Manville said on stage.

John Lithgow made Tony history by becoming the oldest man to win a competitive acting Tony at age 80 for his role as Roald Dahl in the antisemitism drama Giant. He beat out Nathan Lane, Mark Strong, and Daniel Radcliffe for the award. "It's an extraordinarily important play of this moment," Lithgow said on stage, winning his third Tony.

Schmigadoon!, the stage version of the cancelled Apple TV series, won best musical. Producer Lorne Michaels said in his speech, "Sometimes singing, dancing, a lot of jokes and a happy ending is really all you need." The show also picked up three additional awards.

Bess Wohl's Liberation, an intergenerational feminism epic that recently won the Pulitzer Prize for drama, was named best play. "This is the honour of a lifetime," Wohl said. It marks the first time a female playwright has won the award since 2009, with Wohl becoming only the fourth woman to win in this category.

A reimagined version of Ragtime won four Tonys, including best revival of a musical and leading actor and actress in a musical for Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy. It was Henry's first win after four previous nominations. The Lost Boys, an adaptation of the 1980s vampire film, also won four trophies including featured acting awards from a musical.

Pink opened the ceremony by flying in as Peter Pan before leading a star-packed version of "Lady Marmalade" with guests including Lea Michele and Megan Thee Stallion. She called herself "Broadway's biggest fan" and wanted to pay tribute to the hardest-working people in showbiz. The awards came after Broadway recorded nearly 1.91 billion dollars in ticket sales this season.

The RTX Spark will launch this year and will be integrated into computers from major manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP. Microsoft's Windows software will power these machines as part of the three-year collaboration between the two companies. Despite the chip's processing power, Nvidia emphasized that computers using it will remain thin and light.

The move represents Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in AI semiconductor markets. The company is pushing into integrated chips that power entire computers, opening a new business line in the consumer PC industry. Nvidia is also developing the Vera central processing unit, designed for AI agents and targeted at early adopters including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Industry analysts see significant implications in the announcement. Neil Shah, a co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the RTX Spark moment with major technological shifts like the iPhone and ChatGPT. "The RTX Spark looks to transform the traditional app-centric PC to a real useful agentic AI personal computer which will eventually be in every home in coming years as private edge AI agents become pivotal," Shah said.

The announcement intensifies competition in the PC chip market. Intel plans to begin shipping an AI chip later this year using cheaper memory and cooling technology than competitors. The company announced a new graphics processing unit called Xe3P, codenamed Crescent Island, which Intel says is "purpose-built for this upcoming AI generation of agents."

During his Computex presentation, Huang addressed concerns about AI's impact on employment. He dismissed suggestions that artificial intelligence would reduce demand for software engineers, arguing instead that it would increase hiring by making workers more productive. "The number of engineers, software engineers, is actually increasing," Huang said. "People talk about AI reducing jobs – complete nonsense. It's causing more software engineers to be hired."

The partnership signals a shift in how personal computers will integrate AI capabilities, embedding AI processing directly into hardware rather than treating it as a cloud-based feature accessed through internet connections.
