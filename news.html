Police are continuing their search for at least two gunmen who opened fire at the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio on Saturday evening, wounding 12 people. Festival organizers have cancelled Sunday's planned events as the investigation enters its second day.

The shooting erupted just after 5:30 p.m. near the Old West End festival, an annual community gathering featuring live music and architectural home tours. Toledo Police Deputy Chief Joseph Heffernan said the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the gunfire broke out. Several hundred people were attending the festival at the time.

Two victims are reported to be in critical condition. The ages of those shot ranged from 14 to 61 years old, with most in their early 20s. The fire chief reported that it was difficult to transport victims to area hospitals because closed roads and traffic from people leaving the festival hampered emergency response efforts.

With no suspects yet in custody, law enforcement officials have asked festival attendees to come forward with any photos or videos from their phones that might provide leads. Police noted that extra officers had been assigned to the festival, and a large number of off-duty officers were working the event itself.

Heffernan expressed frustration about the violence disrupting a longstanding community tradition. "It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years," he said.

Festival organizers released a statement expressing heartbreak over the incident. "Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood," the statement read. After consulting with Toledo civic and police authorities, organizers determined that continuing the festival would not be "compassionate, responsible or possible."

The incident drew responses from state officials. Ohio Governor Mike DeWine said in a statement, "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence." State Senator Jon Husted called the violence "senseless" and thanked law enforcement for acting quickly and working to locate suspects.

This shooting is among more than 170 mass shooting incidents that have occurred in the United States so far this year, according to the Gun Violence Archive, which defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more victims are wounded or killed. The Toledo incident reflects the ongoing challenge of gun violence at public gatherings across the country.

Yesterday, 8:30 PM ET

Astronomers have identified the source of mysterious radio signals that have puzzled scientists for two decades. The signals originate from a binary star system consisting of a white dwarf star accreting material from a companion star. The discovery was published in the journal Nature and has been described as a breakthrough in understanding these cosmic phenomena.

A student astronomer played a key role in pinpointing the location of these periodic radio emissions. The signals repeat in a predictable pattern, which had made them particularly intriguing to researchers trying to understand their origin. The system involves what scientists describe as a vampire star, a white dwarf that pulls material from its companion, creating the conditions that produce both radio and X-ray emissions.

The binary system generates both radio waves and X-rays as the white dwarf accretes matter from its partner star. This combination of emissions provided researchers with what they called a Rosetta stone for decoding the signals. The predictable nature of the emissions allowed astronomers to trace them back to their source after years of observation and analysis.

White dwarfs are the remnants of stars that have exhausted their nuclear fuel and collapsed to roughly the size of Earth while maintaining significant mass. When a white dwarf is part of a binary system, it can pull material from its companion through gravitational forces. This process, known as accretion, generates intense energy that can be detected across vast distances of space.

The identification of this source helps astronomers understand similar phenomena observed elsewhere in the universe. Repeating radio signals from space have sparked scientific interest and speculation about their origins. This discovery provides a natural explanation for at least one class of these signals and gives researchers new tools for identifying and studying similar systems throughout the galaxy.

Yesterday, 11:48 PM ET

Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman became the most awarded play revival in Tony Awards history, winning six awards at the 79th ceremony held at Radio City Music Hall and hosted by singer Pink.

The production claimed the award for best revival of a play. Director Joe Mantello praised Miller's work as a story that "still talks to us through time." Nathan Lane accepted on behalf of the cast, calling it a play that "continues to teach us who we are as humans and Americans." The six wins surpassed the previous record for a play revival, with the production having previously won for best play in 1949 and best revival in 1984, 1999 and 2012.

John Lithgow won best leading actor in a play for his role as Roald Dahl in the antisemitism drama Giant, making history at age 80 as the oldest man ever to win a competitive acting Tony. The previous record was held by Roy Dotrice, who won at 77 in 2000. This marked Lithgow's third Tony Award, coming 53 years after his first win. "It's an extraordinarily important play of this moment," he said on stage.

Lesley Manville took home the leading actress award for the Broadway transfer of Oedipus, defeating Rose Byrne and Carrie Coon. Making her Broadway debut, Manville said: "I'm a bit overwhelmed, it was my first time on Broadway so this is such a big deal."

Laurie Metcalf won best featured actress in a play for her role in Death of a Salesman, capturing her third Tony Award. She beat out June Squibb and Aya Cash for the honor.

Bess Wohl's Liberation was named best play after recently winning the Pulitzer Prize for drama. "This is the honour of a lifetime," Wohl said. It marked the first time a female playwright has won the award since 2009, making Wohl only the fourth woman to have won.

The musical Schmigadoon! won best musical, with producer Lorne Michaels noting: "Sometimes singing, dancing, a lot of jokes and a happy ending is really all you need." The show captured three additional awards.

Ragtime's new take on the classic musical won four Tonys, including best revival of a musical. Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy won for leading actor and leading actress respectively. This marked Henry's first win after four previous nominations. "Every artist in this room, every artist at home, fight, fight, fight to be heard," Henry said.

The Lost Boys, an adaptation of the 1980s vampire film, won four awards including featured actor and featured actress from a musical. Cats: The Jellicle Ball, a queer ballroom reinvention of Andrew Lloyd Webber's classic, won three awards including best direction of a musical.

Alden Ehrenreich won best featured actor in a play for his Broadway debut in a revival of the dating comedy Becky Shaw.

Pink opened the ceremony as Peter Pan, later leading a star-packed performance of her Moulin Rouge hit Lady Marmalade with guests including Lea Michele and Megan Thee Stallion.

The signals repeat in a predictable pattern, which had made them particularly intriguing to researchers trying to understand their origin. The system involves what scientists describe as a vampire star, a white dwarf that pulls material from its companion, creating the conditions that produce both radio and X-ray emissions.

The binary system generates both radio waves and X-rays as the white dwarf accretes matter from its partner star. This combination of emissions provided researchers with what they called a Rosetta stone for decoding the signals. The predictable nature of the emissions allowed astronomers to trace them back to their source after years of observation and analysis.

White dwarfs are the remnants of stars that have exhausted their nuclear fuel and collapsed to roughly the size of Earth while maintaining significant mass. When a white dwarf is part of a binary system, it can pull material from its companion through gravitational forces. This process, known as accretion, generates intense energy that can be detected across vast distances of space.

The identification of this source helps astronomers understand similar phenomena observed elsewhere in the universe. Repeating radio signals from space have sparked scientific interest and speculation about their origins. This discovery provides a natural explanation for at least one class of these signals and gives researchers new tools for identifying and studying similar systems throughout the galaxy.

A few minutes ago

A volunteer diver captured footage of a Great White shark in the Mediterranean Sea between Tunisia and Sicily. The diver said he was shaking as he filmed the endangered species during the encounter.

Great White sharks are rarely seen in the Mediterranean, making this sighting significant for documenting the species in these waters.
