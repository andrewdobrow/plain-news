A few minutes ago

A federal judge has invalidated the Trump administration's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, ruling that the policy violated federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page decision in response to a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general challenging the fee that Trump announced in September. The ruling vacated the sweeping charge, which represented a 20-to-50 fold increase over existing rates for the visa program that allows U.S. companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations.

The H-1B program is widely used by technology companies and other industries facing domestic worker shortages to hire highly skilled professionals in fields such as engineering and computer science. Opponents of the fee had argued that the administration exceeded its executive authority by imposing such a significant cost without congressional approval. They contended the policy would harm American businesses competing for global talent and increase costs for employers seeking to fill specialized positions.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta joined other state attorneys general in bringing the challenge and declared victory following the decision. The legal battle centered on whether the executive branch had the power to unilaterally implement such substantial fees. The judge found that the Trump administration's approach constituted an unlawful tax that violated established legal procedures.

The H-1B visa program operates under an annual cap on the number of visas available. Competition for these slots typically far exceeds the positions available, already making the visa process demanding for employers. The $100,000 fee would have added a major financial barrier for companies seeking to use the program.

The decision affects thousands of companies across the United States that rely on H-1B visas to fill positions in specialized fields. Technology companies in particular have historically been major users of the visa program and have expressed concerns about how additional fees would impact their ability to recruit talent globally.

The ruling represents a significant setback for the administration's immigration agenda. The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the decision. It remains unclear whether the administration will pursue modified fee structures or attempt other approaches to limit the H-1B program.

The outcome will have implications for how the administration implements its immigration policies going forward and whether courts will uphold other executive actions that face legal challenges. The decision also demonstrates ongoing tension between the executive branch's efforts to reshape immigration policy and judicial review of those actions.

"We have not decided on timing yet; it may be a while because there are things we want to do that are likely easier as a private company. But it's a complicated set of tradeoffs and this gives us the option to go public sooner if that ends up being best."

OpenAI's path to an IPO follows a remarkable expansion since its founding as a nonprofit research lab in 2015 under the leadership of CEO Sam Altman. The company converted to a for-profit structure and publicly released ChatGPT in 2022, which rapidly accumulated hundreds of millions of users and transformed multiple industries from entertainment to healthcare to education.

The company recently closed a funding round valuing it at approximately $852 billion in March, according to the Wall Street Journal. This valuation came despite the company's difficulty achieving profitability and missing key revenue and new user targets.

OpenAI has also faced legal challenges related to its structural transition. Elon Musk sued the company over claims it illegally converted from nonprofit to for-profit status, seeking to have it revert to nonprofit organization status. A jury ruled that the case fell outside the statute of limitations and found OpenAI not liable. The trial exposed details about the company's complex corporate history and leadership questions.

Additionally, OpenAI has been sued in more than a dozen cases where individuals allege that ChatGPT has contributed to mental health crises and violent incidents. Despite these challenges and public scrutiny, OpenAI continues to secure major partnerships with companies including Microsoft, Google, and Nvidia.

The IPO announcement occurs during a significant year for artificial intelligence company public offerings. Elon Musk's SpaceX, which owns xAI, is also preparing for a public listing with an expected valuation of $1.75 trillion. The simultaneous IPO filings from OpenAI and Anthropic indicate both companies view public markets as crucial for their continued growth and development.
