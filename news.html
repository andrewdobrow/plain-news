The United States launched military strikes on Iran after President Trump said Iranian forces shot down a U.S. Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran claimed it launched attacks on U.S. fleet assets in response to the American strikes.

The escalation began when the Apache helicopter was reportedly downed in the vicinity of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments. President Trump confirmed the incident and said the U.S. would respond to what he characterized as an Iranian attack on American forces.

Following the strikes, Iranian officials claimed they had targeted U.S. naval vessels in the region. The exchange represents a significant escalation in tensions between Washington and Tehran, which have been locked in a standoff over Iran's nuclear program and regional military activities. The Strait of Hormuz has been a flashpoint for confrontations between the two nations, with previous incidents involving seizures of commercial vessels and drone attacks.

The helicopter downing and subsequent military exchanges raise concerns about a broader conflict in the Persian Gulf region. Roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum passes through the Strait of Hormuz, making any military confrontation in the area a potential threat to global energy markets and international shipping.

Neither side has reported casualties from the exchanges. The incident comes amid ongoing diplomatic efforts by other nations to prevent a wider war in the Middle East. The U.S. military presence in the Gulf includes aircraft carriers, destroyers, and thousands of personnel stationed across multiple bases in the region.

2:00 AM ET

Anti-immigration protests erupted into violence in Northern Ireland after far-right activists called for demonstrations in response to a stabbing attack. The unrest began Tuesday night, hours after prominent agitators including Elon Musk and Tommy Robinson used social media to encourage people to take to the streets.

Crowds of masked men burned vehicles and houses and blocked roads across Belfast and surrounding areas. Protesters hijacked and burned a Glider bus on Newtownards Road in east Belfast and torched cars near Shankill Road and in Newtownabbey. Two phone shops were looted and an African shop was set on fire. In one incident near Shankill Road, a group stormed a home occupied by a family from an ethnic minority background.

The violence followed a stabbing attack Monday night outside a block of flats in north Belfast. Police charged a 30-year-old asylum seeker from Sudan with attempted murder. Video shared on social media showed a man straddling another man on the ground and striking at his head and neck. A kitchen knife was recovered from the scene. The victim, in his 40s, suffered serious injuries to his eyes, face and back. The suspect also faced charges for possession of a blade in a public place and threats to kill.

Police Chief Constable Jon Boutcher said the suspect was granted leave to remain in the UK on September 28, 2023. The man traveled from Sudan to Paris and then to Dublin before arriving in Belfast by bus on February 10, 2023, where he claimed asylum. Boutcher noted the suspect had no record on national security databases and was previously unknown to police.

Officials across the UK and Northern Ireland condemned the violence and called for calm. First Minister Michelle O'Neill described the attacks as "disgusting cowardice" and warned against "dangerous attempts to exploit" the stabbing. She urged people not to be persuaded by social media accounts stoking tensions.

Assistant Chief Constable Ryan Henderson appealed for peace: "We are urging everyone to remain calm, act responsibly, and avoid any activity that could place themselves or others at risk." He asked community voices to encourage peaceful protest while discouraging violence.

Sinn Féin MP John Finucane called the scenes "shameful," stating there is no place for such disorder. Alliance Justice Minister Naomi Long said the demonstrators were "intent on wreaking destruction on the very communities they claim they are trying to protect."

Smaller protests also occurred in other UK locations. About 60 people gathered in Parliament Square in London, with some shouting anti-immigrant slogans. Most protested peacefully, though some attempted to provoke police officers.

A few minutes ago

Congress has approved a Republican-backed bill providing funding for Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Border Patrol through the end of President Trump's second term. The measure represents a significant victory for the administration's immigration enforcement agenda, locking in resources for agencies carrying out deportation and border security operations for the next two years.

The legislation removes immigration enforcement funding from annual appropriations battles that have previously triggered government shutdown threats. By securing multi-year funding, Republicans aim to insulate Trump's immigration priorities from future budget negotiations. The bill passed along party lines, with Democrats opposing the measure.

Despite resolving the ICE funding dispute, the passage does not eliminate the threat of future government shutdowns. Numerous other partisan spending battles remain unresolved as the midterm elections approach. Congressional leaders face ongoing disputes over various budget priorities that could still trigger funding crises.

Democratic Representative James Walkinshaw of Virginia criticized the bill, raising concerns about the future direction of immigration enforcement under extended funding authority. The debate over the legislation highlighted deep partisan divisions on immigration policy that continue to define congressional negotiations.

The funding commitment extends through January 2029, covering the remainder of Trump's current presidential term. Republicans framed the bill as providing stability for border security operations, while Democrats argued it removes necessary congressional oversight of enforcement activities. The measure now awaits the president's signature.
