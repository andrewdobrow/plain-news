The strikes targeted radar installations along Iran's southern coast following the drone launches in the strategic waterway through which much of the world's oil passes.

The confrontation represents a significant escalation in tensions between the United States and Iran. US forces intercepted the Iranian drones before they could reach their intended targets in the strait. Reports indicate that Kuwait and Bahrain also came under attack during the flare-up, though details about those incidents remain limited. The strikes come amid broader regional tensions and ongoing uncertainty surrounding US-Iran diplomatic talks. Iran has previously threatened to disrupt shipping through the strait during periods of heightened conflict with Western powers. The US maintains a significant naval presence in the Persian Gulf to ensure freedom of navigation through the waterway.

The timing of the incident adds another layer of complexity to the regional security situation, occurring as Israel continues military operations in southern Lebanon and as diplomatic efforts to reduce tensions face obstacles. US military officials have not provided details about the extent of damage to Iranian facilities or whether there were any casualties from the American strikes.

Researchers discover exploit allowing hackers to take over PCs through USB-connected Bluetooth speakers

5:00 PM ET

Security researchers have discovered a vulnerability in Creative's USB-connected Bluetooth speakers that allows nearby attackers to take control of connected computers without any user interaction. The exploit, dubbed 'Pwnd Blaster,' takes advantage of how these speakers connect to PCs via USB while simultaneously maintaining Bluetooth connectivity.

The attack works by exploiting the dual connectivity of the speakers. When a Creative speaker is plugged into a PC via USB, it also broadcasts a Bluetooth signal that can be detected by nearby devices. Researchers found they could send specially crafted Bluetooth commands to the speaker that would then be relayed to the connected PC through the USB connection, effectively bypassing normal security controls that would prevent unauthorized Bluetooth access to the computer.

The vulnerability is particularly concerning because it requires no action from the victim. Traditional attacks typically require users to click a malicious link, download infected software, or accept a connection request. In this case, simply having the speaker connected to a PC and powered on creates an attack surface that hackers within Bluetooth range can exploit to gain system access.

The discovery has prompted alarm among PC security professionals and users. The revelation that common peripheral devices can serve as entry points for attacks represents a blind spot in typical security practices. Most users focus on protecting their systems from network-based threats or malicious software, but rarely consider that accessories like speakers could provide attackers with a pathway to compromise their machines.

Creative has not yet issued a public statement about the vulnerability or announced plans for a firmware update to address the issue. Security experts recommend that users concerned about the exploit either disconnect affected speakers when not in use or disable their Bluetooth functionality if possible. The research highlights the growing challenge of securing computers as peripheral devices become more sophisticated and incorporate multiple connectivity methods that can interact in unexpected ways.

Justice Department ends Trump anti-weaponization compensation fund

The filing...

9:57 PM ET

The Trump administration's nearly $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund will not continue, the Justice Department confirmed in a court filing on Friday. Two DOJ attorneys notified a federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia that the compensation program, established to pay individuals claiming they were targets of government overreach, has been terminated.

The filing came as the Justice Department simultaneously asked the court to dismiss a legal challenge to the fund brought by a former federal prosecutor and several other plaintiffs. The lawsuit had questioned how the fund would operate and who would be eligible for payments.

The fund faced swift legal and political opposition since its announcement last month. U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema had temporarily blocked the administration from transferring money from the fund after Democracy Forward filed a lawsuit seeking to dissolve it. The decision to end the program comes just two weeks after it was announced as part of a settlement in the president's lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service over the leak of his tax returns.

The compensation program drew intense scrutiny over concerns about lack of oversight and eligibility criteria. Administration officials indicated that payees could include pardoned January 6 rioters, sparking alarm among Republicans and Democrats alike.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune told reporters he believed the best approach would be for the administration to shut the fund down themselves. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called it "corruption in broad daylight" and vowed that Democrats would force Senate Republicans to vote on blocking any payments.

Former Vice President Mike Pence criticized the possibility of compensating Capitol rioters as "deeply offensive," telling NBC's Meet the Press that he believed most Republicans and Americans shared his concern. Several states, including California, Illinois, New York, and Connecticut, proposed tax measures targeting any fund distributions to their residents.

The program also faced criticism over its lack of transparency. The fund's terms did not require disclosure of who received payments or how much they were given. Critics viewed it as a secretive and loosely controlled mechanism that could be used for political favoritism.

The termination of the fund represents a significant reversal for the Trump administration, which had promoted it as a key initiative addressing what supporters characterized as politicized federal law enforcement. The decision appears to reflect the mounting political pressure from both parties concerned about the fund's structure and potential use.

The Justice Department's filing requesting dismissal of the lawsuit means the court has not yet ruled on whether to accept the government's request to end the case now that the program has been discontinued.
