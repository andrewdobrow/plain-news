Multiple US states are preparing a lawsuit to block Paramount's $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, according to sources familiar with the matter. The proposed deal would combine two major Hollywood studios and has drawn significant opposition from industry workers, competitors, and regulators.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta indicated his state is actively reviewing the merger. "Paramount/Warner Bros is not a done deal," Bonta said on social media. "These two Hollywood titans have not cleared regulatory scrutiny. The California Department of Justice has an open investigation, and we intend to be vigorous in our review." Bonta also noted he was in conversation with fellow state attorneys general about potential action.

Antitrust experts say a coordinated lawsuit by state attorneys general could pose a serious threat to the deal's completion. Bill Baer, former assistant attorney general in charge of the Department of Justice's antitrust division, told the Guardian that such a challenge "presents a real threat." However, he acknowledged it would be harder for states to prevail if the federal government approves the merger.

Alvaro Bedoya, who served on the Federal Trade Commission, said state attorneys general "definitely have a shot at stopping it if they pool their resources to make a challenge." He pointed to potential labor market effects as grounds for legal action, noting that layoffs are expected as the combined company pursues cost-cutting measures.

The merger would combine extensive film libraries, television networks, and streaming platforms. Paramount announced plans to merge HBO Max and Paramount+ streaming services, a move that could reduce competition in the marketplace. The company said it would maintain separate Warner Bros. and Paramount film studios.

The Writers Guild of America opposes the deal, stating the merger "must be blocked" because "the loss of competition would be a disaster for writers, consumers and the entire entertainment industry." US senators Elizabeth Warren and Richard Blumenthal similarly warned that the deal would allow one family to dominate American entertainment, controlling numerous media properties including CNN, CBS News, and multiple cable networks.

Paramount CEO David Ellison expressed confidence the merger would receive regulatory approval. He stated the company was "absolutely confident" the deal would "expeditiously pass regulatory muster both in the US and abroad" and that it does "not come close to hitting any of the metrics that would be problematic."

Paramount has already responded to some regulatory concerns by offering to sell children's channels to address objections from European Union regulators. The company completed at least two information requests from the Department of Justice, with the mandatory waiting period expiring in February.

The deal remains under active review by US antitrust officials, according to recent reporting.

Yesterday, 4:32 PM ET

OpenAI introduced Lockdown Mode for ChatGPT, a security feature designed to protect sensitive user data from prompt injection attacks. The company announced the update as a defense measure against techniques that manipulate AI systems into revealing confidential information through carefully crafted prompts.

Prompt injection attacks exploit how language models process instructions. Attackers embed hidden commands in external content that AI systems read, potentially causing them to leak private data or ignore safety guidelines. These attacks have grown more sophisticated as AI adoption increases across industries handling sensitive information.

Lockdown Mode aims to reduce the likelihood that ChatGPT shares protected data when subjected to such attacks. The feature adds safeguards to how the system handles instructions, particularly when processing external inputs alongside user data. OpenAI acknowledged that the protection is not absolute and that ChatGPT remains potentially vulnerable to injection techniques.

The company positioned the release as part of ongoing efforts to secure AI systems for enterprise and high-stakes applications. Organizations using ChatGPT to process confidential business information, personal data, or proprietary content face risks if attackers successfully manipulate the system through injected prompts. Lockdown Mode provides an additional layer of defense for users working with sensitive material.

OpenAI did not specify the technical mechanisms behind Lockdown Mode or detail which types of injection attacks it counters most effectively. The announcement reflects broader industry concerns about AI security as language models become embedded in workflows handling private information. Security researchers have demonstrated various prompt injection techniques, prompting AI companies to develop countermeasures while acknowledging that fully preventing such attacks remains an unsolved challenge.

Yesterday, 7:10 PM ET

Golden Tempo Completes Historic Triple Crown Victory

Golden Tempo won the 158th Belmont Stakes on Saturday at Saratoga Race Course, capturing thoroughbred racing's most prestigious achievement just five weeks after winning the Kentucky Derby. With jockey Jose Ortiz in the saddle, Golden Tempo charged down the stretch to claim victory at 6-1 odds, holding off Commandment at the finish line while favorite Renegade finished third.

The victory makes Golden Tempo only the 14th horse in history to win the Triple Crown, joining an exclusive group that includes Secretariat, American Pharoah, and most recently Justify in 2018. The win also marks a historic milestone for trainer Cherie DeVaux, who becomes the first woman to train a Triple Crown winner.

Golden Tempo's path to the Triple Crown spanned five weeks and three different distances across three states, a feat often called the most difficult achievement in American sports. The horse began its journey in May with the Kentucky Derby victory at Churchill Downs before facing what experts described as a significant pace challenge heading into the Belmont at Saratoga.

The Belmont Stakes traditionally serves as the final test of the Triple Crown, requiring horses to navigate a longer distance than either the Derby or the Preakness Stakes. Golden Tempo's performance demonstrated the stamina and speed necessary to succeed across the extended course, completing a remarkable season for the horse and its connections.

The race came before a crowd at Saratoga that witnessed a historic chapter in thoroughbred racing. Golden Tempo's victory extended a tradition of Triple Crown achievement that dates back to Sir Barton's sweep in 1919. Other notable winners in the 107-year history of the achievement include War Admiral, Whirlaway, Citation, Seattle Slew, and Affirmed.

The Triple Crown schedule typically spans five weeks, with the Kentucky Derby held first, followed by the Preakness Stakes two weeks later, and the Belmont Stakes three weeks after that. This timing structure has increasingly drawn scrutiny in recent years, as more Derby winners have chosen to skip the Preakness due to the short turnaround between races.

Golden Tempo's Triple Crown completion comes during a period of significant change in thoroughbred racing's landscape. Churchill Downs recently acquired the intellectual property rights to the Preakness Stakes in an $85 million deal, consolidating ownership of multiple Triple Crown races under one corporate entity. Industry discussions have included potential adjustments to the Triple Crown calendar, including moving the Preakness one week later to create a three-week gap between it and the Derby starting in 2027.

Despite broader challenges facing the sport, the Triple Crown remains one of American sports' most celebrated competitions, and Golden Tempo's achievement adds another legendary name to its storied history.

British actor Anthony Head has died at age 72 from complications related to pneumonia, surrounded by his family. His daughters Emily and Daisy shared the news in a statement, describing it as "an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many."

Head gained international recognition for his role as Rupert Giles, the mentor and father figure to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character throughout Buffy the Vampire Slayer's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued: "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Head's career extended far beyond Buffy. Born in Camden, London, to artistic parents, he began his professional work on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in various theatrical productions including the original West End production of Chess in 1988 and The Rocky Horror Show in 1990. He also appeared in films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie.

In the early 1980s, Head sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album. However, he became arguably best known in the UK and US for his role in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements, which featured a slow-burn romance between a British couple.

During his five years in Los Angeles filming Buffy, Head maintained a long-distance relationship with his partner Sarah and their two young daughters in England. He told the Guardian that he would travel home every three or four weeks whenever possible, spending considerable money on airfares to maintain family connections.

After Buffy concluded in 2003, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain, playing a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant played by David Walliams. He also appeared in the BBC production Merlin. More recently, he featured regularly in Ted Lasso as Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, where he worked closely with co-creator Jason Sudeikis and earned praise for their on-screen chemistry.

His daughters emphasized that Head "loved his job very much and he always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people, in such wonderful productions, across a career that spanned several decades." They concluded: "We know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of and in the audiences that love them."
