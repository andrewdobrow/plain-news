Yesterday, 10:00 PM ET

Multiple U.S. states are preparing to file an antitrust lawsuit aimed at blocking Paramount's proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, according to sources familiar with the matter. The effort represents a significant challenge to what would be one of the largest media consolidations in recent years.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta signaled the state's intent to challenge the deal, stating on social media that "Paramount/Warner Bros is not a done deal" and that the California Department of Justice has an open investigation. Bonta indicated he was in conversation with fellow state attorneys general about coordinating action against the $110 billion transaction.

Legal experts suggest state-level challenges pose a genuine threat. Alvaro Bedoya, who served on the Federal Trade Commission between 2022 and 2025, said he believed state attorneys general "have a shot at stopping it if they pool their resources." Bill Baer, former assistant attorney general in charge of the antitrust division at the Department of Justice, told the Guardian that "a combined lawsuit by state attorneys general presents a real threat," though he acknowledged it would be harder for states to succeed if federal regulators approve the deal.

The proposed merger has drawn opposition from entertainment industry workers and major organizations. The Writers Guild of America issued a statement opposing the deal, arguing that the loss of competition "would be a disaster for writers, consumers and the entire entertainment industry." Several U.S. senators, including Elizabeth Warren and Richard Blumenthal, have raised concerns about the consolidated power the deal would create, warning that one family would control Warner Bros., Paramount Plus, HBO, CBS News, CNN, and numerous other networks and channels.

Antitrust experts point to specific concerns. Bedoya noted there is "an even more compelling case that a whole lot of people are going to be put out of work," given that the combined company carries $79 billion in debt and will likely undertake significant cost-cutting measures including layoffs. Competition experts have also highlighted how merging HBO Max and Paramount Plus could reduce competition in the streaming market, though Paramount says it will maintain both the Warner Bros. and Paramount film studios.

Paramount's chief executive David Ellison has expressed confidence that the merger will pass regulatory review, claiming the combination "does not come close to hitting any of the metrics that would be problematic." Paramount has already completed at least two information requests from the Department of Justice, and the mandatory 10-day waiting period under federal antitrust law expired in February.

The state-level legal action would add another layer of complexity to an already complicated approval process. If multiple states file suit, the litigation could significantly delay or derail the transaction even if federal regulators ultimately allow it to proceed.

OpenAI Delays Adult Mode Launch as Company Faces Competing Priorities

OpenAI has postponed its planned "adult mode" for ChatGPT to focus on more urgent development work, the company announced this week. The decision comes after the startup's chief executive Sam Altman had previously committed to rolling out the feature alongside age verification tools.

"We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive," OpenAI said in a statement.

The delay reflects shifting priorities at the company, which serves more than 900 million ChatGPT users globally. Since announcing adult mode plans last October, Altman has declared a "code red" initiative to improve ChatGPT's capabilities amid intensifying competition from Google and Anthropic.

While adult mode launch has been postponed, OpenAI is rolling out age prediction technology to determine whether users are under 18. When the system identifies a minor, it activates enhanced safety features that limit exposure to graphic violence and sexually explicit roleplay content. In the United Kingdom, OpenAI must implement strict age verification for any pornographic images ChatGPT generates under the Online Safety Act.

On the principle behind the delayed feature, OpenAI stated: "We still believe in the principle of treating adults like adults, but getting the experience right will take more time."

The announcement comes as OpenAI navigates broader organizational challenges. This week, Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over concerns regarding the company's Pentagon partnership.

Kalinowski cited worries about domestic surveillance of US citizens and AI-guided autonomous weapons systems. "This wasn't an easy call," she wrote on social media. "AI has an important role in national security. But surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got."

Kalinowski emphasized respect for company leadership while expressing frustration with the pace of execution. "My issue is that the announcement was rushed without the guardrails defined. It's a governance concern first and foremost. These are too important for deals or announcements to be rushed," she explained in a follow-up post.

The Pentagon deal had drawn immediate scrutiny. OpenAI later amended the contract to explicitly exclude domestic surveillance applications after Altman acknowledged the agreement appeared "opportunistic and sloppy."

An OpenAI spokesperson responded to the resignation concerns: "We believe our agreement with the Pentagon creates a workable path for responsible national security uses of AI while making clear our red lines: no domestic surveillance and no autonomous weapons. We recognise that people have strong views about these issues and we will continue to engage in discussion with employees, government, civil society and communities around the world."

The divide is even sharper among independent voters, with 57% preferring candidates who oppose Trump's deportation approach.

Foreign policy challenges compound these problems. Two-thirds of Americans disapprove of Trump's handling of the war with Iran, with only one-third approving. A majority of 61% said the US should not take any further military action in Iran.

Despite these broad struggles, Trump maintains significant support among his party base. The NBC poll found that 83% of Republicans still approve of Trump, though this represents a 4-point decline from earlier in the year. Among Republicans, 52% strongly approve of his economic handling, down from 58% previously.

The political landscape presents stark challenges for Republican candidates defending their congressional majorities. Two-thirds of Americans believe the country is on the wrong track, compared to just one-third who believe it is moving in the right direction. The economy continues to be the top issue for voters at 29%, followed by threats to democracy at 24%.

There is one area where Trump and Republicans find stronger support. Three-quarters of Americans support government-issued photo ID requirements for voting, and 61% believe such documents should include proof of citizenship.

As Trump prepares for his Sunday interview, these polling numbers underscore the political headwinds his party faces in the approaching midterm elections.

The technical achievement marked one of the early milestones in space exploration, revealing the moon's far side years before any astronaut would orbit it.

OpenAI Delays Adult Mode Launch as Company Faces Competing Priorities

OpenAI has postponed its planned "adult mode" for ChatGPT to focus on more urgent development work, the company announced this week. The decision comes after the startup's chief executive Sam Altman had previously committed to rolling out the feature alongside age verification tools.

"We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive," OpenAI said in a statement.

The delay reflects shifting priorities at the company, which serves more than 900 million ChatGPT users globally. Since announcing adult mode plans last October, Altman has declared a "code red" initiative to improve ChatGPT's capabilities amid intensifying competition from Google and Anthropic.

While adult mode launch has been postponed, OpenAI is rolling out age prediction technology to determine whether users are under 18. When the system identifies a minor, it activates enhanced safety features that limit exposure to graphic violence and sexually explicit roleplay content. In the United Kingdom, OpenAI must implement strict age verification for any pornographic images ChatGPT generates under the Online Safety Act.

On the principle behind the delayed feature, OpenAI stated: "We still believe in the principle of treating adults like adults, but getting the experience right will take more time."

The announcement comes as OpenAI navigates broader organizational challenges. This week, Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over concerns regarding the company's Pentagon partnership.

Kalinowski cited worries about domestic surveillance of US citizens and AI-guided autonomous weapons systems. "This wasn't an easy call," she wrote on social media. "AI has an important role in national security. But surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got."

Kalinowski emphasized respect for company leadership while expressing frustration with the pace of execution. "My issue is that the announcement was rushed without the guardrails defined. It's a governance concern first and foremost. These are too important for deals or announcements to be rushed," she explained in a follow-up post.

The Pentagon deal had drawn immediate scrutiny. OpenAI later amended the contract to explicitly exclude domestic surveillance applications after Altman acknowledged the agreement appeared "opportunistic and sloppy."

An OpenAI spokesperson responded to the resignation concerns: "We believe our agreement with the Pentagon creates a workable path for responsible national security uses of AI while making clear our red lines: no domestic surveillance and no autonomous weapons. We recognise that people have strong views about these issues and we will continue to engage in discussion with employees, government, civil society and communities around the world."
