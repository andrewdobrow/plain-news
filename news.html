Israeli ground forces advanced into central Gaza beyond their occupied zone on Wednesday to dismantle what the military described as a terror tunnel posing an immediate threat to troops. The operation, which took place west of the Yellow Line marking the boundary of the more than 60 percent of Gaza under Israeli control, prompted evacuation orders for residents near Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps. Hamas accused Israel of a flagrant violation of the October 2025 ceasefire agreement.

Witnesses reported tanks advancing east of Nuseirat refugee camp and moving onto the main north-south Salah al-Din road under artillery cover. The Israeli military said the targeted operation aimed to remove an immediate threat and prevent Hamas from harming Israeli civilians or troops. The military body Cogat warned Palestinians in the area to stay away for their own safety and announced the closure of a section of Salah al-Din road.

Footage verified by BBC Verify showed families fleeing near Nuseirat camp, with residents reporting indiscriminate gunfire and shelling. One displaced resident said Israeli forces fired randomly at areas sheltering displaced people, forcing families to leave behind all belongings. Gaza's Hamas-run Civil Defence agency reported three people killed in separate Israeli drone strikes on Wednesday, including one man in Nuseirat. The Israeli military told BBC it had struck several military operatives.

At least 1,658 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza since the ceasefire began last year, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry. More than 1.9 million people, about 90 percent of Gaza's population, remain displaced, with 1.4 million living in tents or makeshift shelters. Reconstruction has not begun in Gaza, where an estimated 82 percent of structures have been destroyed or damaged during the war. The disarmament of Hamas remains a key obstacle to advancing a U.S. peace plan.

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share, above its projected range of $70 to $78, valuing the artificial intelligence company at $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. The company will begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT.

The pricing reflects strong investor appetite for the AI company despite recent market volatility following the departure of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The $1.5 trillion valuation marks a significant increase from the $965 billion Anthropic reached in May when it raised $65 billion in its last private funding round, though it falls short of the $2 trillion the company reportedly sought several months ago.

Amazon and Google hold the largest stakes in Anthropic, owning nearly 15 percent and almost 14 percent of the company respectively. A group of major technology companies including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC Partners agreed to purchase $26 billion worth of non-voting shares through a private placement alongside the IPO. Among venture capital investors, Sequoia and former Google CEO Eric Schmidt each reportedly own approximately 1 percent of the company, as does Singaporean sovereign wealth fund GIC.

The IPO delivers a substantial windfall to Anthropic's numerous backers, though none of its venture capital investors holds more than a 5 percent stake. Each of the company's seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, sold nearly 3.4 million shares in the offering, bringing in about $282 million per founder according to the S-1 filing. The company's performance is expected to influence expectations for other private AI companies, including rival OpenAI, which was last valued at $1.25 billion and has not announced IPO plans.

The lawsuit was filed Wednesday in Los Angeles County Superior Court. Frank Cascio and his...

3:54 PM ET

Five siblings who allege Michael Jackson sexually abused them as children have sued entertainment attorney Bryan Freedman for legal malpractice, claiming he deceived them into signing a 2019 agreement that silenced their abuse allegations while shielding Jackson's estate from liability. The lawsuit was filed Wednesday in Los Angeles County Superior Court.

Frank Cascio and his siblings Dominic, Marie-Nicole, Edward, and Aldo allege Freedman exploited their vulnerability to secure the agreement after Jackson's estate sought to contain fallout from the documentary Leaving Neverland. The siblings claim Freedman, who was already representing two family members in unrelated matters, assured them he was acting in their best interests while discouraging them from hiring independent counsel. They say he failed to disclose provisions barring them from publicly discussing the alleged abuse and releasing the estate from liability related to it.

Under the agreement, the estate was to pay each sibling $690,000 annually for five years, with disputes subject to mandatory arbitration. The lawsuit alleges Freedman never explained the payment was inadequate compared to amounts Jackson and the estate paid other alleged victims, nor did he disclose a penalty provision requiring the entire family to return all payments if one member disclosed Jackson's alleged crimes. The siblings also claim Freedman later returned in 2024 calling the original agreement "toilet paper" and said the estate wanted to pay them more.

The siblings recognized a conflict of interest, hired new representation, and informed the estate they would challenge the agreement. They filed a child sex trafficking lawsuit in February that was ordered into arbitration in August. The new complaint against Freedman, filed by attorney Howard King, includes claims for breach of fiduciary duty, fraud, and negligent misrepresentation. A lawyer for Jackson's estate has previously called the siblings' allegations a "desperate money grab" and noted the family defended Jackson for more than 25 years.

Microsoft released a public preview of Windows Server 2028 with security configurations aligned to Defense Information Systems Agency hardening standards enabled by default.
