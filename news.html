Stock futures slipped in early trading following reports that the United States launched military strikes against Iran. The action, described by officials as self-defense measures, triggered immediate market reactions as investors assessed potential economic fallout from escalating tensions in the Middle East.

Asian markets declined broadly as the news spread across trading floors. Oil prices gained on concerns that the conflict could disrupt supply from one of the world's key energy-producing regions. The price movements reflected trader anxiety about potential impacts on global energy markets and broader economic stability.

The strikes represent a significant escalation in hostilities between the two nations. Market analysts noted that geopolitical events in the Middle East have historically created volatility in energy markets, which can ripple through to other sectors of the economy. Technology stocks, which had recently rebounded on renewed investor enthusiasm for artificial intelligence developments, faced renewed pressure.

Investors are now watching for signs of how the situation will develop and what retaliatory measures might follow. The timing of the strikes, coming during Asian trading hours, meant that US markets had not yet opened to fully price in the news. Futures contracts, which allow traders to bet on where markets will open, showed declines across major indexes.

The market reaction underscores the delicate balance between geopolitical stability and economic confidence. Energy sector stocks are expected to see particular volatility as traders weigh potential supply disruptions against increased production from other sources. Financial analysts cautioned that sustained conflict could affect consumer confidence and spending patterns, particularly if oil price increases translate to higher costs at the pump and for other petroleum-based products.
