John Bolton, who served as National Security Advisor under President Trump, has reached a plea agreement with federal prosecutors over charges related to the mishandling of classified national security information. The former diplomat is expected to enter a guilty plea to illegal retention of sensitive national security information, according to sources familiar with the matter.

The agreement, subject to court approval, marks a significant legal development for Bolton, who held one of the most sensitive positions in the U.S. government from April 2018 to September 2019. During his tenure, he had access to some of the nation's most closely guarded secrets regarding national security, foreign policy, and military operations.

The Justice Department filed charges against Bolton in 2025 as part of what sources describe as an onslaught against the president's critics. The case underscores ongoing concerns about the handling of classified materials by senior government officials and enforcement of laws designed to protect national security information from unauthorized disclosure or retention.

Bolton's case adds to a growing list of high-profile national security officials who have faced legal consequences for improper handling of classified information. Charges of retaining classified documents carry potential penalties including fines and imprisonment, though plea agreements typically involve reduced sentences in exchange for a guilty plea.

Before his time in the Trump administration, Bolton served in various government roles spanning several decades, including as U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations under President George W. Bush. After leaving the White House in 2019, he published a memoir that itself became the subject of legal disputes over whether it contained classified information. The Trump administration unsuccessfully sought to block the book's publication.

Bolton fell out with Trump during his first administration and was ousted from his national security adviser position in 2019. The two have maintained a political feud ever since. Bolton has also been critical of Trump's recent handling of the Russia-Ukraine war. After a Trump-Putin meeting in Alaska failed to yield progress on ending the war, Bolton wrote on social media that it appeared "Putin got to Trump."

Early in 2025, Trump revoked the security clearances of more than four dozen former intelligence officials, including Bolton. Bolton was also among a trio of former Trump officials whose security details were canceled by the president.

Bolton's book, The Room Where It Happened, became a bestseller despite the Trump administration's attempts to ban its publication. In it, he detailed what he described as impeachable conduct and accused Trump of asking China to help secure his re-election.

The Justice Department has not publicly released details about the specific classified materials involved in the case. Bolton has not been detained or charged with additional crimes beyond those covered by the plea agreement.

The wireless carriers challenged the FCC's system for levying fines, arguing that the agency's in-h...

5:25 PM ET

Supreme Court Backs FCC Authority to Fine Wireless Carriers

The Supreme Court upheld the Federal Communications Commission's power to impose penalties against telecommunications companies, ruling 8-1 against AT&T and Verizon in a case about the agencies' enforcement procedures.

The wireless carriers challenged the FCC's system for levying fines, arguing that the agency's in-house proceedings violated their constitutional right to a jury trial. The justices rejected this argument, affirming that the FCC can penalize companies without requiring a jury to decide the case.

The case centered on whether companies have the right to a jury trial when federal agencies impose financial penalties through their own internal processes. AT&T and Verizon contended that the Constitution guaranteed them this right, but the court disagreed with only one justice dissenting from the majority opinion.

The ruling represents a significant victory for federal regulators seeking to enforce rules governing how telecommunications companies operate. The decision strengthens the FCC's ability to pursue enforcement actions against carriers without facing procedural obstacles that could delay or limit the penalties imposed.

The underlying dispute involved the carriers' handling of customer location data. The FCC had fined AT&T and Verizon for selling smartphone location information to third parties without adequate safeguards. Location data is particularly sensitive because it can reveal where people live, work, and travel, raising substantial privacy concerns about how wireless providers protect customer information.

The decision comes as privacy has become an increasingly important issue for regulators overseeing technology companies and service providers. Federal agencies have faced growing pressure to police how organizations collect, use, and share personal information. This ruling gives the FCC clearer authority to hold wireless carriers accountable for privacy violations without companies successfully challenging the enforcement process itself.

The case also has broader implications for regulatory authority across the federal government. By upholding the FCC's in-house penalty system, the court affirmed that agencies can enforce rules through their own proceedings rather than always going through the court system. This allows regulators to act more efficiently when companies violate the rules governing their industries.

The telecommunications industry has faced mounting scrutiny over data privacy practices in recent years as consumers and policymakers have become more aware of how much personal information carriers collect and can access. The Supreme Court's decision suggests that regulators will have stronger tools to address these concerns without companies being able to delay consequences through constitutional arguments about jury trials.

The decision also came as the Trump administration was in office, and the ruling backed the FCC's enforcement authority, demonstrating that the court's support for regulatory power extends across different political administrations.

Garrett, 30, is a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection. Since Cleveland drafted him first overall in 2017, he has recorded 125.5 sacks, 413 pressures, and 149 tackles for loss. Last season, he set a single-season sacks record with 23 performances, earning All-Pro recognition despite playing for a struggling team.

The trade came after Garrett requested a move from the Browns following their disappointing 3-14 season in 2024. Though he signed a four-year, $160 million extension with Cleveland shortly after his trade request, which made him the highest-paid non-quarterback at the time, the team's continued struggles prompted him to seek a fresh start. The Browns finished the 2025 season with a 5-12 record and missed the playoffs for the second consecutive year.

To facilitate the trade, the Browns and Garrett agreed to modify his contract, deferring option payments across the 2026-28 seasons. The change moved a payment of around $10 million from March 28 to the start of the regular season, enabling the deal to progress.

Browns General Manager Andrew Berry acknowledged the difficult decision in a statement. "As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" Berry said.

The Rams, who lost to the Seattle Seahawks in last season's NFC Championship game, have a history of making bold roster moves. In 2021, they acquired Matthew Stafford from Detroit, and he led the team to a Super Bowl victory in his first season. That championship was won at their home SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, where next February's Super Bowl LXI will also be played.

Verse, the 25-year-old linebacker sent to Cleveland, was a first-round pick by the Rams in 2024 who earned Defensive Rookie of the Year honors. He recorded 12 career sacks and 22 tackles for loss while earning Pro Bowl selection this past season.

The acquisition creates a historic pairing in Los Angeles, as Matthew Stafford won the AP NFL MVP and Defensive Player of the Year awards last season, marking the first time these honors will belong to teammates.
