The U.S. Treasury Department moved to cut off cryptocurrency exchange MEXC from the American financial system over allegations the platform processed $16 billion in illicit transactions linked to Iran, North Korea and Russia. The action targets MEXC Global for alleged sanctions violations.

The Treasury decision blocks MEXC from accessing U.S. financial infrastructure and conducting transactions with American counterparties. The enforcement measure represents one of the largest actions against a cryptocurrency exchange based on alleged illicit transaction volume.

The alleged $16 billion in flows involve transactions connected to sanctioned countries including Iran, North Korea and Russia. The Treasury did not specify the time period over which the transactions allegedly occurred or detail the nature of the illicit activity.

In response to the Treasury action, digital asset company NatGold Digital announced it would immediately delist its NATG token from MEXC. The move reflects the immediate market consequences for platforms cut off from the U.S. financial system.

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has tasked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming a new coalition government, giving the former military chief 28 days to build an administration after last week's election produced no clear majority. Herzog announced the decision after consulting with political parties represented in the incoming Knesset, Israel's parliament. Eisenkot received 67 recommendations for the premiership compared to 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The election left Netanyahu's right-wing Likud party and its far-right and ultra-Orthodox allies with 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset. Eisenkot's centrist Yashar party and three other Jewish opposition parties in his bloc won 48 seats. Two Arab-led alliances, the United Arab List and the Joint List, won 19 seats following a surge in turnout among Arab voters, positioning them as potential power brokers.

Eisenkot now faces the challenge of assembling a coalition within 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. Three paths appear possible: a national unity government involving both Eisenkot and Netanyahu, a right-wing coalition built on defections from Eisenkot's bloc to Netanyahu, or an Eisenkot-led government relying on the Arab parties. However, all but one of the Jewish parties pledged before the election not to work with Arab parties to form a government, though observers have speculated an agreement could be reached.

No political party in Israel has ever won an outright majority, requiring all governments to be coalitions. Between 2019 and 2022, Israel held five separate elections as Netanyahu and his rivals repeatedly failed to form stable coalitions. Herzog said during his consultations that preventing another election round would require parties to recognize that not every campaign commitment could be fulfilled in the current reality.

Stephen Colbert and cellist Yo-Yo Ma were rushed out of a Harvard University event Thursday after a fire alarm was pulled during their appearance, which had drawn hundreds of union protesters outside who objected to the pair crossing their picket line. The Harvard Graduate Student Union had urged both performers to cancel the talk in solidarity with their strike, which began October 27. Despite the protests and chanting outside the venue that made it difficult to hear the speakers, Colbert and Ma proceeded with the event.

The fire alarm forced organizers to end the event during the question-and-answer portion and escort Colbert out of the building. The union denied responsibility for the alarm, stating in a social media post that it was unrelated to their picketing activity and that members were relieved no one inside was at risk.

The union had sent multiple formal communications to Colbert and Ma's teams in advance, requesting they move the event off-site or virtually, reschedule, or cancel to avoid crossing the picket line. Representatives from Colbert's union, SAG-AFTRA, and Ma's union, the Boston Musicians Association, also reached out on the graduate union's behalf with the same request. The union said Colbert and Ma refused to speak with union members as they left and continued the event without regard for the picket line.

The graduate student union has been on strike demanding a contract that includes wage increases and protections for non-citizens and against harassment and discrimination. Graduate classes have gone untaught since the strike began. Author John Green moved his own Harvard event to accommodate the strike, calling it an easy decision and donating his proceeds to the union.
