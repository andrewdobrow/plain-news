1:40 AM ET

A federal judge has cautioned the Justice Department not to restore an $1.8 billion fund established to compensate people claiming they were harmed by federal government actions, even as the department maintains the program remains suspended.

The "anti-weaponization" fund has drawn intense criticism from lawmakers across the political spectrum, with many calling it a "slush fund" and questioning its legitimacy. The fund was created as part of a settlement in a lawsuit brought by Donald Trump and his sons against the Internal Revenue Service over the leak of their tax returns by a former contractor.

The judge's warning came after a federal judge in Miami reopened Trump's case against the IRS, responding to concerns raised by a group of 35 former federal judges. Those retired judges filed a motion asserting that the settlement "is a product of collusion and is itself a fraud on the court."

Judge Kathleen Williams noted that Trump had effectively used his lawsuit to obtain what she called "unlawful private benefits" for himself and his family while creating a mechanism to distribute taxpayer money without proper congressional or constitutional authority. The judge expressed concern that Trump rushed the settlement to avoid judicial oversight of its terms.

Williams ordered Trump's legal representatives to respond by June 12 regarding whether the parties truly had opposed interests in the case and whether Trump colluded with his own government to settle the dispute and escape scrutiny. The judge also questioned a provision signed only by the acting attorney general that shields the Trump family from future IRS audits, indicating uncertainty about its enforceability.

The anti-weaponization fund has generated controversy partly because it appears designed to benefit people convicted of crimes during the Biden administration, including supporters of Trump who were prosecuted for their involvement in the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack. However, the fund contains no explicit political limitations on who may seek compensation.

Some legal observers have noted that former Trump attorney Michael Cohen, now critical of the former president, has indicated he will apply for compensation, claiming he experienced similar financial and personal persecution.

The fund already faces separate legal challenges, including a temporary block on its establishment and any payouts issued by a federal judge in Virginia's eastern district.

The situation represents a significant legal development in Trump's efforts to use the IRS lawsuit settlement and the compensation fund. Williams emphasized that she possesses the power to investigate serious misconduct in cases before her court and indicated her willingness to call Justice Department officials, potentially including the acting attorney general, to testify about the settlement's circumstances and beneficiaries.

The strikes followed the downing of a US Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz, which President Trump blamed on Iran and said the country "could not allow such an event to go unanswered."

The military exchange represents a significant deterioration in a ceasefire that the two countries had announced in April. After US forces launched strikes beginning Tuesday evening targeting radar and air defence facilities along the Strait of Hormuz, Iran responded Wednesday morning with retaliatory strikes. Tehran's Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted the US al-Azraq base in Jordan using long-range missiles and launched drones at other locations in the region, including Kuwait and Bahrain. US officials told Reuters that nearly all the missiles and drones launched by Iran were intercepted.

US Central Command characterized the strikes as "a proportional response to unjustified Iranian aggression," signaling that officials were not seeking a return to full-scale war. However, the incident highlighted tensions that have simmered since a broader conflict began in February, when the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran.

The oil market reaction reflected immediate concerns about potential disruptions to energy supplies flowing through one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints. The region accounts for a significant portion of global petroleum production and transport. Traders appeared to be pricing in elevated risk premiums as they anticipated that further escalation could substantially disrupt energy flows across the Middle East.

Beyond commodity markets, the conflict carries broader economic consequences. Energy prices have already been climbing since fighting intensified in February, contributing to inflation pressures worldwide. The surge has made fuel and food more expensive for consumers and raised transportation costs for businesses. The national average gas price in the US stood at approximately $4.16 per gallon on Tuesday, according to AAA, roughly one dollar higher than the same period last year.

Despite the military exchanges, Trump suggested the situation remained manageable. In an interview with the Wall Street Journal, he called the helicopter incident "wasn't a big deal" and noted that "the pilot is fine." The two crew members were rescued in what the US military described as an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat. Earlier, Trump had expressed renewed optimism about negotiations, saying there was "a good chance" of signing a deal with Iran within days, though he did not provide details.

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded to the strikes on social media, stating that Iran "will leave no attack or threat unanswered." With diplomatic efforts appearing stalled and military tensions escalating, energy markets remain sensitive to any developments that could further disrupt global supply chains.

The collaboration addresses Apple's historical lag in AI development compared to competitors like Google, which has introduced advanced AI features more rapidly in recent years.

Yesterday, 9:33 PM ET

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman defended the decision to select an all-male crew for the upcoming Artemis III mission, acknowledging the criticism the announcement has generated. In a post on social media platform X, Isaacman wrote that he had observed reactions to the crew selection ranging from disappointment to outrage among those concerned about the lack of female astronauts on the flight.

The four-person crew consists of three American astronauts and one Italian astronaut from the European Space Agency. Randy Bresnik, a marine colonel and former commander of the International Space Station, will serve as mission commander. Frank Rubio, an army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the record for the longest single spaceflight by a NASA astronaut at 371 days. Andre Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer with no previous spaceflight experience, completes the American contingent. Luca Parmitano of the European Space Agency will pilot the mission.

Scheduled to launch in 2027, Artemis III represents an important stepping stone in NASA's broader lunar exploration goals. Rather than landing on the moon itself, this two-week mission will remain in lower Earth orbit, where the crew will test docking procedures and life support systems for two competing lunar landers from Blue Origin and SpaceX. These tests will prepare systems for the actual lunar landing planned for Artemis IV in 2028, which will mark the first crewed moon landing since 1972.

During the mission announcement event at NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston, Isaacman emphasized the scale and ambition of the agency's vision. He described the convergence of multiple spacecraft in orbit simultaneously, including the Dragon, Shenzhou, Soyuz, possibly Starliner, Starship, and Blue Origin landers, calling it "the beginning of Earth's first Starfleet." He praised the crew, saying they carry forward "the hopes and dreams of the next generation, just as the Apollo astronauts did for so many of us."

In defending his personnel decisions, Isaacman pointed to his own spaceflight experience, noting that he has personally traveled to space twice with crews that were 50 percent female. However, the administrator did not provide additional details about the specific selection process or criteria used for choosing the Artemis III team members.

The Artemis program itself is named after the Greek goddess Artemis, sister of Apollo in classical mythology. The program represents NASA's strategy to establish sustainable lunar exploration and eventual human missions to Mars. The selection of the Artemis III crew comes approximately two months after the successful Artemis II mission, which looped around the moon in April and tested NASA's Space Launch System rocket and Orion crew capsule.
