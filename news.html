5:00 AM ET

The Supreme Court is entering its final weeks, a period when justices typically race to complete their most significant rulings before the summer recess. The court has issued several consequential decisions as the term winds down, with more opinions expected in the coming days.

One of the most significant recent rulings came in a case involving Alabama's congressional map. In a 6-3 decision, the court's conservative majority allowed Alabama to use a redrawn map that eliminates one of the state's two majority-Black districts in upcoming midterm elections. The unsigned opinion marked another major blow to voting rights protections and represented a significant victory for Republicans.

The decision came after the court's landmark April ruling in Louisiana v Callais, which struck down a critical provision of the Voting Rights Act. In that case, the court made it nearly impossible to win voting rights claims by requiring plaintiffs to prove intentional discrimination. The Alabama ruling applied this new standard, reversing a lower court's May 26 decision that had blocked the state's map as intentionally discriminatory.

The court's conservative justices argued that the three-judge panel had failed to properly reconsider the case under the Callais decision and other recent rulings weakening the Voting Rights Act. They noted that the lower court had failed to give the legislature a presumption of good faith and had not properly evaluate whether an alternative map offered by plaintiffs could perform as well as the state's map on neutral redistricting criteria.

The three liberal justices issued a sharply worded dissent. Justice Sonia Sotomayor, joined by Elena Kagan and Ketanji Brown Jackson, accused the majority of causing chaos and abandoning the rule of law. She contrasted two paths forward: one with an orderly election under a map protecting Black voters' rights, and another with a chaotic election under a map intentionally discriminating against Black voters. "The majority chooses the second path and disregards both democratic values and the rule of law," Sotomayor wrote.

The ruling concluded a lengthy legal battle over Alabama's congressional map. After the 2020 census, Alabama created a plan with only one majority-Black district. Black voters challenged this, and courts previously ordered the state to create two majority-Black districts. When Alabama Republicans redrew the map in 2023, they again created only one majority-Black district. The court blocked that map and appointed a special master to draw a compliant plan with two majority-Black districts, which was used in the 2024 election.

Following the Callais decision, Alabama sought to reinstate its 2023 map, leading to the latest court battle. The Supreme Court's decision allows the state to proceed with its plan despite the lower court's finding of discriminatory intent.

The tensions have undermined attempts to use the Lebanon ceasefire as a stepping stone to wider regional peace.

He pledged further strikes against what he characterized as Hezbollah infrastructure. Israeli media reported that Netanyahu told President Trump that Israel needed to respond to maintain restraint from the armed group. The US subsequently asked Israel to ensure any response remained "calculated and limited."

The ceasefire agreement included a clause allowing Israel to conduct strikes in self-defense, a provision Israel has used extensively. OpenAI has confidentially submitted paperwork to go public on the US stock market, the company announced Monday through a blog post. The artificial intelligence company behind ChatGPT is expected to become one of the most highly valued listings in market history, with a valuation exceeding $850 billion.

"We recently submitted a confidential S-1. We expect it to leak so we're just announcing it," OpenAI stated in the announcement. The company added that it has not yet decided on timing for the public offering, noting that "there are things we want to do that are likely easier as a private company."

The confidential filing, known as a Form S-1, allows OpenAI to submit its investor prospectus to the Securities and Exchange Commission for regulatory review while keeping financial details private until closer to the actual listing date. This approach gives regulators time to discuss and review the company's financial disclosures before the public sees them.

OpenAI's move comes just over a week after rival Anthropic, which produces the Claude chatbot, announced its own plans to go public. The back-to-back filings mark an acceleration in the competition between leading AI companies to access public markets as they scale operations and vie for dominance in the rapidly expanding artificial intelligence industry.

The timing reflects OpenAI's dramatic trajectory since its founding in 2015 as a non-profit research lab under CEO Sam Altman. After releasing ChatGPT publicly in 2022 and converting to a for-profit structure, the company rapidly accumulated hundreds of millions of users and fundamentally changed how people work across entertainment, healthcare, education, and employment sectors.

OpenAI achieved its current valuation of around $852 billion after closing a $122 billion funding round in March, according to the Wall Street Journal. The company reached this valuation despite reported difficulties turning a profit and missing key revenue and user acquisition targets.

The IPO announcement arrives as OpenAI has continued securing major partnerships with companies including Microsoft, Google, and Nvidia, as well as with the Trump administration. The company recently acquired iPhone designer Jony Ive's startup for billions in stock, though efforts to expand beyond ChatGPT have produced mixed results, including the shutdown of its Sora video generation app in April 2026.

OpenAI also recently prevailed in a lawsuit brought by Elon Musk, who sought to force the company to revert to non-profit status. A jury panel ruled that the case fell outside the statute of limitations.

Another major AI competitor, SpaceX and Elon Musk's xAI, is also expected to go public at a valuation around $1.75 trillion, promising to make this a significant year for AI company debuts on Wall Street.

Apple unveiled a significantly redesigned Siri at its annual developer conference, marking a major shift in the company's approach to artificial intelligence. The upgraded voice assistant, now called Siri AI, will be integrated with Apple's broader artificial intelligence tool known as Apple Intelligence and is scheduled for widespread release in the fall.

The new Siri functions more like modern AI chatbots such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini rather than a traditional web-based question-and-answer tool. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice president of Siri engineering, demonstrated the assistant's expanded capabilities during the conference, showing how it can handle complex tasks across multiple apps within a single interface. In one demonstration, Rockwell asked Siri to identify a beach location from a photo, find a friend's address, and then provide directions to the beach with a stop at the friend's house.

The revamped assistant represents a notable partnership strategy for Apple. The company is powering Siri AI through Google's Gemini model as part of a billion-dollar partnership between the two tech giants. This collaborative approach marks a shift for Apple, which traditionally develops core technologies internally.

Siri AI will function as a dedicated app available on iPhones, iPads, and laptops. The assistant can handle various tasks including proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events, and using the camera to extract information like nutrition data from food. Currently, the tool will only be available in English.

Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, acknowledged that some competitors have moved faster in the AI race. "Some appear to be racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve," he said. Apple has previously postponed Siri updates and faced legal challenges over past advertising claims about the assistant's capabilities, settling a class-action lawsuit for $250 million in May.

The announcements came during what will be Tim Cook's final keynote as CEO before stepping down in the fall after 15 years leading the company. Beyond Siri, Apple introduced new child safety features for its devices, including parental controls that limit what content children can access and who they can communicate with. Parents can use a setup assistant to customize restrictions and gradually expand access as children age.

Apple also integrated AI capabilities into Safari, messaging, the Home app, and photography tools. These features were developed in collaboration with Google, further demonstrating the company's shift toward strategic partnerships in its AI development strategy rather than pursuing all capabilities independently.

Victor Wembanyama delivered a breakout performance to lead the San Antonio Spurs past the New York Knicks 115-111 in Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden, snapping New York's 13-game postseason winning streak and cutting the series deficit to 2-1.

Wembanyama finished with 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks, his best effort of the series so far. Stephon Castle contributed 23 points and hit two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining in the third quarter. For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson scored 32 points and OG Anunoby added 28, but the rest of the roster struggled in the fourth quarter, going just 3 for 20 from the field.

The game unfolded against an extraordinary backdrop at Madison Square Garden, with the first NBA Finals game in New York since 1999 drawing high-profile attendees including the sitting U.S. president, Mayor Zohran Mamdani, and numerous celebrities. The atmosphere created an intense environment that tested both teams' focus.

After a difficult Game 2 in which he missed a potential game-winner, Wembanyama entered Game 3 with renewed energy. "Really tried to relax," he said of his approach after the previous loss. "The playoffs, it's like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water." He started strong, scoring nine points in his opening nine minutes.

The Spurs built an 11-point first-quarter lead before the Knicks responded aggressively, outscoring San Antonio 42-24 in the second quarter to take a 64-57 halftime advantage. The third quarter remained competitive, with neither team establishing a significant cushion.

The fourth quarter proved decisive. While Brunson contributed 12 points in the final period to keep New York close, his teammates couldn't provide enough support. The Knicks' frustration grew over foul call discrepancies, with chants of "refs, you suck!" breaking out multiple times. New York took just eight free throws in the second half compared to 24 for San Antonio.

Outside of Wembanyama and Castle, the Spurs benefited from the Knicks' 13 turnovers, which resulted in 21 points. Karl-Anthony Towns finished with just 11 points and eight rebounds after consecutive double-doubles in San Antonio, while the rest of the Knicks' supporting cast struggled.

Brunson acknowledged the setback but remained focused on improvement. "We've done our best to try to learn from wins over the past couple weeks. But now we have to learn from a loss," he said.

The series continues with Game 4 on Wednesday in New York. The Knicks need two more wins to clinch the championship, while the Spurs must win consecutive games to force the series back to San Antonio.

The first Wicked film became one of the highest-grossing musical films, earning $765 million in worldwide box office receipts and winning two Academy Awards. Her performance earned critical acclaim and led to her first Oscar nomination, cementing her status as a multifaceted entertainer capable of success across different mediums.

The end of their relationship comes as the Wicked sequel has performed markedly differently from its predecessor. Wicked: For Good took $541 million at the worldwide box office and received no Oscar nominations, a significant decrease from the first film's performance and accolades.

Recent weeks have seen notable developments surrounding the Wicked cast. During the Singapore premiere of Wicked: For Good, co-star Cynthia Erivo stepped in when a red-carpet invader rushed towards Grande. Erivo later discussed how social media reactions to the incident revealed problematic perceptions of Black women. A few minutes ago

President Trump formally nominated Todd Blanche to serve as attorney general on Monday, positioning his former personal lawyer to become the nation's top law enforcement officer. The nomination will require Senate confirmation to become permanent.

Blanche has been serving in an acting capacity since April, when Trump fired Pam Bondi from the role. Bondi faced criticism for her handling of the Epstein files, an issue that has followed the president for years. Trump previously described Blanche as "a very talented guy" while discussing the nomination on a podcast.

Blanche previously represented Trump in several high-profile legal cases. He was Trump's attorney in the hush-money case involving adult film actor Stormy Daniels, which resulted in Trump's conviction on 34 felony counts for fraudulently falsifying business records. Blanche also represented Trump in the federal classified documents case and election obstruction cases, both of which were dismissed following Trump's electoral victory.

As acting attorney general, Blanche has pursued controversial actions under the banner of combating what the administration calls "weaponization" of the Justice Department. Federal prosecutors have filed criminal charges against James Comey, the former FBI director, and escalated investigations into former CIA director John Brennan. The department also removed press releases about prosecutions related to the January 6 Capitol attack.

Republican Senate Judiciary Committee Chair Chuck Grassley called Blanche "well-qualified," stating he has "worked well with acting attorney general Blanche for more than a year and appreciate his commitment to transparency and support for law enforcement."

Democratic opposition is expected to be significant. Senator Adam Schiff of California argued that "at every turn, Todd Blanche has been unable to put aside his role as Donald Trump's criminal defense lawyer and represent the American people instead." Schiff highlighted several controversial decisions, including support for the Comey indictment based on an Instagram photo and approval of a 1.8 billion dollar fund to compensate Trump supporters who claimed they faced political persecution.

Blanche also signed a Justice Department memo granting Trump, his sons, and the Trump Organization immunity from IRS audits and tax prosecutions. Representative Rosa DeLauro, a Connecticut Democrat, criticized this action, saying that "when you are issuing memos granting the president, his children, and their companies immunity from audits or prosecution for tax offenses, your previous role becomes relevant information."

The controversial compensation fund faced bipartisan criticism. Republican Brian Fitzpatrick from Pennsylvania drafted legislation with bipartisan support to block its creation. Under mounting pressure, Blanche announced on Tuesday that the fund had been terminated.

Republicans hold the Senate majority, which could facilitate confirmation, though some lawmakers have indicated they plan to review Blanche's record closely. The Senate Judiciary Committee typically schedules hearings within weeks of a formal nomination.
