Yesterday, 6:49 PM ET

The Seattle Times and Newsday filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging the companies used their journalism without authorization to train artificial intelligence models. The lawsuit argues that generative AI could leave the journalism industry broken beyond repair by functioning as a system that destroys the organizations producing the content it depends on.

The complaint describes AI products like ChatGPT and Copilot as consumers that take human-authored content and deliver back copies and derivative imitations to achieve commercial objectives. The publications characterized generative AI as a snake eating its own tail.

The lawsuit follows a 2023 copyright infringement case filed by The New York Times against OpenAI and Microsoft, which other publications have since joined. The Seattle Times case is notable because Microsoft and OpenAI have funded some of the newspaper's journalism projects and fellowships.

A Microsoft spokesperson said the company is surprised by the lawsuit but is always happy to explore solutions to this type of dispute. The case adds to growing legal challenges against AI companies over their use of copyrighted material for model training.

A Greek air force F-4 Phantom jet crashed during an air show at Tanagra air force base north of Athens on Saturday, killing both pilots. The twin-seat aircraft flew into the ground during a low-altitude maneuver just meters above the surface as thousands of spectators watched during the annual Athens Flying Week event. No civilians were injured.

The jet burst into flames on impact and sent up a large plume of black smoke. Eighty-five firefighters assisted by 11 water-dropping planes and helicopters worked to extinguish the blaze. Video of the crash showed a second ball of fire nearby as the plane broke apart. The bodies of the pilots were later recovered. An eyewitness told ERT, Greece's state broadcaster, that the jet had been airborne for no more than three minutes.

Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said the sense of grief was widespread and that his thoughts were with the families of the two pilots. He described the crash as a reminder of the daily danger faced by Greek aviators who remain on constant standby to protect the country's skies. Cyprus Defense Minister Vasilis Palmas expressed sadness at the deaths and said he had called Greek Defense Minister Nikos Dendias.

Organizers canceled the remainder of Athens Flying Week following the incident and evacuated the area. The Greek air force declared three days of national mourning.

The Australian government plans to introduce legislation this week that would require social media platforms to allow users to switch off content-recommendation algorithms. Communications Minister Anika Wells said the digital duty of care laws would impose basic safety protections on an industry that has not been subject to such requirements. Companies including Meta, Google, and TikTok would face substantial penalties if they did not provide the opt-out option.

Wells said the government is seeking expert advice on whether users would be required to opt in or opt out of algorithmic feeds. The government previously passed legislation banning social media use for children under 16, though research indicates that most underage users who were on platforms before the ban took effect in December continue to use them. No tech company has been fined for failing to enforce that ban.

Some opposition members have criticized the proposal. Liberal Party leader Angus Taylor said he was highly skeptical of the plan and argued it could amount to censorship. Wells responded that the legislation is not about censorship but about requiring tech companies to identify and mitigate risks on their platforms. She said many Australians value algorithms for discovering content such as local businesses, and those users could choose to keep them active.

The draft legislation is also expected to require platforms to prevent the sharing of harmful content, including illegal pornography and material promoting disordered eating. Last week, consent advocate Chanel Contos called for an opt-in model for algorithmic feeds, arguing that algorithms contribute to sexual violence by exposing young people to misogynistic content.

Historical stock market data shows that the 34 years featuring a Republican president and a divided Congress produced an average annual return of 7.33% for the S&P 500, according to an analysis of nearly a century of market performance. A divided government occurs when one or both houses of Congress are controlled by Democrats while a Republican holds the White House.

While the data indicates more modest returns under this configuration compared to other political arrangements, the average annual return remains decisively positive. The historical record shows no indication that stocks plunge when congressional control is split, and analysts note that the makeup of Congress is rarely responsible for sharp market downturns, which are typically driven by other factors such as margin debt levels and equity valuations.

Carlos Alcaraz advanced to the US Open quarterfinals with a straight-set victory over Tommy Paul, winning 6-4 6-3 6-4 in his fourth competitive singles match since returning from a wrist injury that sidelined him for more than four months. The 23-year-old Spaniard, who missed both the French Open and Wimbledon this summer, defeated the American 20th seed and has dropped only one set in reaching the last eight in New York.

Alcaraz extended his Grand Slam match winning streak to 18, having won the previous two majors at last year's US Open and this year's Australian Open. He will face American eighth seed Ben Shelton in the quarterfinals. Alcaraz said he has focused heavily on recovery during the tournament to keep his wrist injury-free, even giving up golf during his days off.

Shelton advanced by defeating Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-4 6-3 6-4. Alcaraz has won all three of his tour-level matches against Shelton. Against Paul, Alcaraz demonstrated his shot-making ability with a cross-court forehand winner in the second set that was measured at 103 miles per hour.

Three American men also reached the quarterfinals. Frances Tiafoe defeated 2021 champion Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (7-1) 6-4 7-6 (8-6) to maintain his perfect 10-0 record on Louis Armstrong Stadium at the US Open. Alex Michelsen beat Tomas Martin Etcheverry 7-6 (8-6) 6-4 6-4 to reach his first Grand Slam quarterfinal, and will face Tiafoe next. The American advances mark the country's strongest showing as it seeks its first men's Grand Slam champion since Andy Roddick won the US Open in 2003.

Moore plays Mona Friedman, a meek housewife who transforms into a zealous method actor after being cast in a community theater production. The performance generated spontaneous mid-scene applause during the Saturday afternoon Telluride screening. Moore is a five-time Oscar nominee with one win for "Still Alice" more than a decade ago. A sixth nomination is considered automatic, with a second win viewed as plausible given the Academy's recent pattern of awarding repeat acting Oscars to performers including Anthony Hopkins, Emma Stone, Frances McDormand, and Sean Penn.

Giamatti portrays Jerry, a tyrannical community theater director, in what is described as the flashier role. The actor has never won an Oscar despite widespread admiration, including for his leading turn in "The Holdovers" last year and his performance in "Sideways." His strongest path to victory is considered to be the supporting actor category, the same category Kieran Culkin won for Eisenberg's previous film "A Real Pain."

Eisenberg wrote and directed the film, which is set inside a fictional community theater musical production at a New Jersey recreation center. The cast includes Bernadette Peters and Halle Bailey, with cinematography by Drew Daniels and a score by Emil Mosseri, an Oscar nominee for "Minari." Emma Stone serves as a producer. Giamatti saw the film for the first time at the Telluride premiere, where Rebecca Keagan of NBC News moderated a question-and-answer session with Eisenberg, Moore, and Giamatti.
