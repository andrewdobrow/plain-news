The US stock market suffered its steepest decline of the year on Friday, with the S&P 500 falling more than 2.6 percent and the Nasdaq plunging 4 percent in its worst single-day drop since April 2025. The sharp selloff ended a nine-week winning streak for major indexes and wiped out recent gains as investors fled technology stocks and recalibrated their expectations for interest rates.

The market turbulence followed a stronger-than-expected jobs report showing US employers added 172,000 positions in May, sustaining a vigorous pace of hiring that has persisted through recent economic shocks. The robust employment figures reinforced trader concerns that the Federal Reserve may raise interest rates to prevent the economy from overheating, with betting markets now pricing in higher odds of a Fed rate hike in coming months.

Technology stocks bore the brunt of the selling pressure, with chip manufacturers and artificial intelligence companies leading the decline. Broadcom and Intel shares dropped sharply, while other semiconductor stocks including Super Micro and Qualcomm also tumbled. The tech-heavy Nasdaq saw its biggest daily percentage fall since early 2025, reflecting growing nervousness about stretched valuations in the sector that has powered much of the market's recent rally.

Beyond equities, the selloff rippled through other asset classes. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since the November 2024 election, sinking to its lowest level since October 2024 as investors rotated away from speculative assets. The cryptocurrency's decline marked a sharp reversal from its earlier strength following the election outcome.

The market retreat highlighted tensions between strong economic data and investor preferences for lower interest rates. While the May employment report demonstrated the job market's resilience in pushing past recent strains, wage growth has not kept pace with higher prices, and consumer sentiment remains pessimistic. The Federal Reserve now faces renewed pressure to balance supporting growth against the risk of persistent inflation, a calculation that could shape monetary policy decisions in the months ahead and determine whether the market's extended rally can resume.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention released modeling showing that an ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo could sicken more than 20,000 people in the next three months without swift intervention. Health officials stress that immediate action is needed to prevent the outbreak from matching the scale of previous major epidemics.

The outbreak, identified in mid-May in the DRC, involves the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola. As of the latest reports, the virus has caused 344 confirmed cases and 60 deaths in the DRC, with 15 confirmed cases including one death in neighboring Uganda. However, the World Health Organization head Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus indicated the outbreak may have started much earlier than May. "It could be January, it could be February, March, April," he said, noting that the virus has had "a big head start." The first identified case involved a nurse who visited a health center on April 24.

Response efforts face significant obstacles. Contact tracing, a crucial element of controlling any infectious disease outbreak, remains a major challenge. Currently, only about 45 percent of contacts are being followed up, and Tedros said the number needs to exceed 90 percent to get ahead of the outbreak. Insecurity and population displacement in Ituri province, the most affected region, are hampering these efforts.

Community mistrust is another barrier to containment. Tedros reported that some community leaders in the DRC do not believe Ebola is real and worry that response measures will divert resources from other essential services. Additionally, blanket travel restrictions imposed by countries including the United States are disrupting supply chains and hindering the response, according to WHO officials.

Despite these challenges, there are signs of progress. Treatment centers have been established across Ituri province. Laboratory and diagnostic capacity is being scaled up in affected areas and neighboring regions. The number of suspected cases fell significantly when officials worked through a testing backlog, dropping from over 1,000 to 116 as cases were either confirmed or ruled out.

There is currently no vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain. However, Tedros highlighted an important development: six people in the DRC and two in Uganda have recovered from the virus, showing that survival is possible when patients receive proper care and seek treatment early.

Health officials have emphasized that early investment in outbreak response costs far less than managing a full-scale epidemic. The CDC's three-month projection window suggests there is still an opportunity to prevent escalation, but officials warn that the window for action is closing rapidly. The response is catching up to the outbreak, but swift international coordination and increased resources remain critical.

Astronauts shelter in safe area as Russian crew attempts to repair air leak on space station

A few minutes ago

Five astronauts aboard the International Space Station sheltered in their spacecraft Friday as Russian crew members attempted repairs on persistent air leaks in the station's Russian segment. The precaution was lifted after about two hours when repair efforts were paused for further assessment.

NASA ordered the crew to take shelter in the Dragon spacecraft and prepare for possible evacuation while Roscosmos worked on the leaks. The five astronauts included four members of NASA's SpaceX Crew-12 mission: American astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, French astronaut Sophie Adenot of the European Space Agency, and Russian cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev. American astronaut Chris Williams, who has been aboard the station for 190 days, also sheltered in place alongside Russian cosmonauts Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergey Mikayev.

The air leaks were discovered in the transfer tunnel of the Zvezda Service Module during pressurization procedures. Russian specialists detected two leak sites and successfully sealed one using a two-component sealant called Germetal-1. The second potential leak was located on the conical portion of the transfer chamber. Roscosmos stated that neither the crew nor the station's onboard systems faced immediate danger.

After the repair work was paused for additional measurements and data analysis, NASA instructed the crew to end their safe haven procedures and return to normal station operations. "Roscosmos has paused Friday's structural repair efforts inside the Zvezda service module transfer tunnel as more measurements and data is assessed," said Bethany Stevens, a NASA spokesperson. "Given this development, NASA has instructed the crew members inside the Dragon spacecraft to end the safe haven procedures and return to planned operations aboard the International Space Station. We look forward to working with Roscosmos on a collaborative approach to address the leaks."

The air leak problem represents an ongoing concern for the aging orbital laboratory. Roscosmos has been dealing with cracks and air leaks in its station compartments for more than five years, with NASA's office of inspector general identifying them as a top safety risk. In June, a chartered spaceflight carrying India, Poland, and Hungary's first astronauts in decades was postponed due to leak concerns.

The Zvezda Service Module, where the leaks occurred, is approximately 43 feet long and contains living quarters, life support systems, communications equipment, electrical power distribution, data processing systems, flight control systems, and propulsion systems. It was the first fully Russian contribution to the ISS and includes a docking port for Russian Soyuz and Progress spacecraft.

The International Space Station has maintained continuous human presence for 25 years and is operated by five partner space agencies: NASA, the Canadian Space Agency, the European Space Agency, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, and Roscosmos.

Jarrett secured NASCAR Cup Series championships in 1961 and 1965, establishing himself as a dominant force during the sport's early years. He competed during a formative period when stock car racing was building its reputation as a major American sport. His driving success and professional approach helped lay the groundwork for NASCAR's growth and wider appeal.

Following his retirement from competition, Jarrett transitioned into broadcasting, where his career proved equally successful. Known for his distinctive voice and insightful analysis, he became one of the sport's most celebrated commentators.

Jarrett's most iconic broadcasting moment occurred at the 1993 Daytona 500, when his son Dale Jarrett was competing in the closing laps of the race. The emotional call captured Jarrett encouraging his son toward victory, including direction to take the high side of the track. This broadcast moment became one of the most memorable and frequently replayed calls in NASCAR history. It exemplified both the sport's family traditions and the genuine emotion that made Jarrett an effective communicator with viewers. Broadcaster Mike Joy later discussed behind-the-scenes aspects of that famous call, highlighting how Jarrett maintained his professionalism while watching his son race for one of motorsports' most prestigious victories.

The NASCAR Hall of Fame inducted Jarrett, recognizing his substantial contributions to the sport. Beyond his personal accomplishments, he played a mentoring role for younger drivers and broadcasters throughout his later years. The Jarrett family name remains prominent in NASCAR circles through his son Dale, who also achieved success as a championship-caliber driver. Ned's influence continues to shape how racing is presented and consumed by fans across the sport.

Jarrett's passing represents the loss of one of NASCAR's most important figures from its foundational era. His ability to excel both as a competitor and as a storyteller made him a unique presence in motorsports history. The impact of his work in building the sport's audience and appeal continues to influence NASCAR today. His legacy encompasses not only his on-track achievements and announcing career but also his role in advancing the sport's popularity and helping establish NASCAR as a cultural institution in America.

British actor Anthony Head has died at age 72 from complications related to pneumonia. His daughters Emily and Daisy announced his passing in a statement, saying he "passed away peacefully of complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family."

Head achieved worldwide recognition for his role as Rupert Giles in the supernatural drama series Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Playing the school librarian and mentor figure to Sarah Michelle Gellar's title character, he became a defining presence throughout the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute on Instagram, referencing her character's famous line: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok," before adding, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Beyond Buffy, Head demonstrated his versatility across multiple entertainment formats. He appeared in the BBC sketch comedy show Little Britain as a prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant played by David Walliams. He also took roles in the BBC fantasy series Merlin and appeared in films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in the Apple TV Plus comedy Ted Lasso, where he earned praise for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Head's connection to entertainment began early. Born in Camden, London, he was raised by artistic parents. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother also pursued acting.

He began his professional career on the British stage, starring in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s, he appeared in various productions including the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and the 1987 film A Prayer for the Dying alongside Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. He took a lead role as Freddie Trumper in the original West End production of the musical Chess in 1988 and played Dr. Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show.

Head was also recognizable to British audiences for his iconic role in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements that aired throughout the 1980s, which featured a slow-burn courtship between a British couple.

During Buffy's production, Head lived in Los Angeles while his partner Sarah and two young daughters remained in England. He told the Guardian he would travel home every three or four weeks whenever the production schedule allowed.

His daughters expressed that "our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of and in the audiences that love them."

The Senate concluded an 18-hour voting marathon to pass an immigration enforcement package, ending a contentious legislative battle that tested the chamber's endurance and political divisions. The extended session, known as a vote-a-rama, required senators to cast dozens of votes on amendments and final passage of the legislation.

The immigration enforcement package represents a significant legislative achievement for Republicans pushing stricter border security measures. The vote-a-rama process allowed unlimited amendments, forcing senators to take positions on a wide range of immigration-related issues throughout the night and into the following day.

The lengthy session highlighted deep partisan divisions over immigration policy. Senators debated measures ranging from border wall funding to detention policies and asylum procedures. The process tested the physical stamina of lawmakers, many of whom remained on the Senate floor throughout the night to participate in successive votes.

The package now moves to implementation, though its passage through the Senate does not guarantee immediate policy changes. The legislation's specific provisions and their practical effects on border enforcement will depend on how federal agencies interpret and apply the new authorities granted by Congress.

The vote-a-rama concluded what Senate leaders described as one of the most demanding legislative sessions in recent memory. The extended voting period allowed both parties to force votes on politically sensitive amendments, creating a record that will likely feature in upcoming election campaigns. The final tally showed party-line divisions on most major provisions, reflecting the ongoing national debate over immigration policy and border security.
