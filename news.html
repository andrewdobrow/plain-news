Washington DC has altered its July Fourth weekend event plans in response to the oppressive conditions. The heat dome has trapped approximately 260 million people under scorching temperatures that are expected to last through the holiday. Major metropolitan areas including New York City, Philadelphia, Boston, and Washington DC are experiencing the most intense effects.

The Mid-Atlantic region has reached peak heat levels, with numerous temperature records falling across the eastern United States. Maryland faces extreme heat through July 4th, with forecasters warning of an increased risk of storms as the weather system continues. The combination of record-breaking temperatures and high humidity has created dangerous conditions for outdoor gatherings traditionally held during the Independence Day holiday.

Authorities are urging residents to take precautions during outdoor activities and to monitor vulnerable populations. The heat wave's timing coincides with one of the busiest travel and celebration periods of the year, when millions of Americans typically gather for parades, barbecues, and fireworks displays. Event organizers across affected regions have been forced to reassess safety protocols and make last-minute adjustments to scheduled activities.

The extreme weather pattern represents a significant disruption to the milestone anniversary celebrations. Public health officials continue to monitor the situation as the heat dome shows little sign of weakening through the holiday weekend.

6:45 AM ET

NASA launched a rescue mission to save the Swift space telescope, which has been falling back toward Earth and faces destruction if it reenters the atmosphere. A NASA-funded robot blasted off to intercept the telescope in mid-orbit and boost it back to a safe altitude.

The Swift telescope, which has been operating in space for years, has been gradually losing altitude due to atmospheric drag. Without intervention, the spacecraft would eventually burn up during reentry. The rescue mission represents an attempt to extend the operational life of the valuable scientific instrument.

The mission involves a robotic spacecraft that will rendezvous with Swift in orbit. Once the two spacecraft meet, the rescue vehicle will use its propulsion system to push Swift back into a higher, more stable orbit. This maneuver aims to prevent the telescope from succumbing to the increasing atmospheric resistance that has been pulling it downward.

The Swift observatory has provided crucial data for astronomical research during its time in space. Saving the telescope would allow scientists to continue using its instruments for studying cosmic phenomena. The rescue effort demonstrates NASA's commitment to preserving functioning space assets that still have scientific value.

Reports indicated that a last-minute technical problem caused a brief delay before the rescue mission successfully launched. The spacecraft is now on its way to attempt the complex orbital rendezvous and rescue operation that will determine whether Swift can continue its scientific mission or will be lost to atmospheric reentry.
