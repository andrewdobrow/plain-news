Yesterday, 8:12 PM ET

Anthropic's landmark $1.5B copyright settlement is approved

A federal court has approved a $1.5 billion settlement between AI company Anthropic and copyright holders, marking a significant legal resolution in the artificial intelligence industry. The settlement concludes a lawsuit over Anthropic's use of copyrighted material to train its AI models.

The approval represents an important financial and legal milestone for Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant. The settlement amount reflects the substantial volume of copyrighted content incorporated into large language models and the escalating legal risks facing AI companies as they expand their training datasets.

While this settlement resolves the immediate case, it does not establish broader legal precedent for how copyright law applies to AI training practices. The fundamental question of whether using copyrighted works to train AI models constitutes fair use or copyright infringement remains legally unsettled. Other AI companies, including OpenAI and Meta, face similar legal challenges from authors, publishers, and artists who contend their work was used without permission or adequate compensation.

The settlement arrives as AI companies work to develop more capable systems while operating within an uncertain legal framework. Building advanced AI systems requires enormous quantities of text, images, and other data, much of which is protected by copyright. The companies have maintained that this use qualifies as transformative fair use under copyright law, while rights holders argue it represents unauthorized commercial exploitation of their creative work.

Training data remains one of the most contentious issues in AI development. Companies need vast datasets to build competitive models, but sourcing this data legally while respecting copyright protections presents a significant challenge. The tension between AI development and copyright protection has created a complex landscape where companies operate without clear legal guidelines.

The approval of this settlement may influence ongoing negotiations in other copyright disputes against AI companies. However, because this case was resolved through settlement rather than court judgment on the merits, it does not provide legal clarity on the fundamental copyright questions at stake. The legal framework governing AI training data remains fundamentally unsettled.

Industry observers expect that Congress and courts will continue addressing how existing copyright law applies to artificial intelligence technology. As more AI companies face similar litigation, the need for legal clarity becomes more urgent. Courts may eventually need to establish clearer guidelines about what constitutes acceptable use of copyrighted material in AI training.

The case highlights broader tensions in the AI industry between rapid technological development and respect for intellectual property rights. While Anthropic has now resolved this particular lawsuit, the underlying legal questions will likely shape how AI companies approach data sourcing and training practices for years to come. Until courts establish clearer precedent or Congress passes new legislation, AI companies will continue navigating an uncertain regulatory environment.

Men now represent about one in eight nurses in the United States, a share that continues to grow as strong job prospects and targeted recruitment efforts draw more men into the profession. The trend reflects both the robust demand for nursing professionals and sustained campaigns to diversify a field historically dominated by women.

The shift appears particularly pronounced in the South, where healthcare systems have actively worked to attract male candidates to nursing programs and clinical positions. Men pursuing nursing cite job security and stable career paths as primary motivations for entering the field.

The nursing profession has experienced sustained growth in recent years, driven by an aging population, expanded healthcare coverage, and increasing demand for medical services. Job market conditions for nurses remain strong across most regions, with healthcare facilities reporting persistent need for qualified staff.

Recruitment initiatives have focused on reshaping perceptions of nursing as a career suitable for all genders. These efforts have included outreach to high schools and community colleges, mentorship programs pairing male nursing students with established professionals, and marketing campaigns highlighting the clinical, technical, and leadership aspects of nursing work.

Despite the growth, men still represent a small minority of the nursing workforce. The increase in male nurses follows broader patterns in other traditionally gender-segregated occupations, where economic factors and changing social attitudes have gradually shifted workforce composition over time.

5:16 AM ET

President Donald Trump announced 50% tariffs on most Canadian goods on Monday, claiming that Canada has unfairly discriminated against American automobiles, alcohol and dairy products. The White House said the new duties will apply to a broad range of items, including wine, hockey sticks and cement, and will even cover goods previously protected under the United States-Mexico-Canada trade agreement.

The tariffs exclude energy products, fish, critical minerals and potash. Products already subject to import taxes for national security reasons, such as steel and aluminum, are also exempt. The White House will implement the duties in 30 days, providing a window for potential negotiations between the two countries.

Trump justified the tariffs through three proclamations issued under Section 338 of the 1930 Trade Act. He argued that Canada maintained a 25% tariff on certain American motor vehicles and that several Canadian provinces blocked the sale of American alcoholic beverages while allowing imports from other nations. Trump also objected to what he described as Canadian discrimination against American cheese compared to European dairy products.

The White House official previewing the tariffs said Trump has also requested aides explore additional levies related to Canadian wildfires. Trump raised this issue Sunday while attending the World Cup final with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, saying Canada must stop fires from "poisoning our air."

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney responded by saying his government had submitted comprehensive proposals to resolve the trade dispute. He stated that Trump's previous tariffs violated their trade agreement and called for intensive negotiations to address the disagreement. Carney noted that the trade conflict has already increased costs for families on both sides of the border.

Political leaders across Canada expressed frustration with the action. Ontario Premier Doug Ford said that if the tariffs proceed, Canada should respond "tariff for tariff, dollar for dollar." Conservative opposition members called the tariffs an unjustified attack on Canadian workers and businesses.

Candace Laing, CEO of the Canadian chamber of commerce, described Trump's moves as regrettable but urged both countries to use the 30-day period before implementation to make meaningful progress in formal talks.

Economists warn the tariffs could trigger significant economic disruption. Higher import duties typically raise prices for American consumers on goods containing Canadian components. The two nations maintain deeply integrated supply chains, particularly in manufacturing, automotive and aerospace sectors. Analysts expect the tariffs to accelerate inflation concerns and potentially disrupt decades-old trading relationships developed under free trade agreements.

The new tariffs could carry serious political risks for Trump ahead of upcoming midterm elections, particularly if they cause market instability or consumer price increases similar to previous trade actions.

Notably, some goods previously protected under the United States-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) trade agreement will now face import taxes. Trump justified the tariffs by pointing to Canadian retaliatory measures against earlier U.S. tariffs. Additionally, most Canadian provinces and territories have stopped selling U.S. alcoholic beverages while permitting imports from other countries. Trump also objected to what he characterized as discriminatory treatment of American cheese compared to European dairy products.

The tariffs will take effect in 30 days, providing a window for potential negotiations between the countries. Trump signed three proclamations under Section 338 of the 1930 Trade Act to implement the increases.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney responded by stating that his government has made comprehensive proposals to resolve trade disputes with Washington. "This trade dispute has raised costs for families, particularly in the US," Carney said. "Canada stands ready to engage intensively to address outstanding issues with the US to the mutual benefit of our citizens."

Ontario Premier Doug Ford called for Canada to respond "tariff for tariff, dollar for dollar" if the measures proceed. Candace Laing, CEO of the Canadian Chamber of Commerce, described the Trump administration's actions as "regrettable" but urged both countries to use the 30-day period to make meaningful progress in formal talks.

During a World Cup final attendance Sunday, Trump raised concerns about Canadian wildfire smoke affecting U.S. air quality with Carney. Trump stated on social media that Canada needed to stop fires from "poisoning our air." A White House official indicated that Trump has requested aides explore additional tariffs specifically related to wildfire smoke impacts on U.S. air quality.

The tariffs carry significant risks for Trump ahead of midterm elections. Previous "liberation day" tariffs in April triggered financial market concerns about inflation and recession, prompting the administration to negotiate reduced rates. The Supreme Court ruled in February that Trump had illegally used emergency executive power for global tariffs, forcing the administration to pursue alternative legal authorities.

Lenders to Thames Water have offered a golden share arrangement in an attempt to prevent government takeover of the utility.

A few minutes ago

Thames Water's lenders have proposed a golden share structure to prevent the company from being nationalized by the Burnham government. The water supplier has faced significant financial difficulties in recent months.

The creditors are seeking to maintain private ownership while addressing concerns about the company's financial stability. If the government proceeds with nationalization, the BBC understands that lenders would pursue payment in full of outstanding debts, which amount to multi-billions of pounds.

Portland Trail Blazers reject city's proposed arena term sheet

Team officials say the proposal for Moda Center payments is a non-starter as arena negotiations stall.

Yesterday, 10:30 PM ET

A top Trail Blazers official has dismissed Portland's draft term sheet for a new arena deal, stating the organization has no interest in the payment structure proposed by the city. The breakdown in negotiations raises questions about the team's long-term future in Portland despite both sides expressing a desire to reach an agreement.

The city's proposal included specific payment terms related to the Moda Center that the Blazers and team owner Todd Dundon find unacceptable. Portland City Council President indicated the team has shown no willingness to engage with the payment framework outlined in the term sheet. Some fans view the ongoing negotiations as an important step toward securing the team's presence in the city.
