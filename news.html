The Senate approved legislation early Friday to fund immigration enforcement agencies through the end of President Trump's second term, following an intense overnight session. The final vote was 52-47, with Senator Lisa Murkowski of Alaska as the only Republican voting against the bill and Senator Michael Bennet of Colorado not voting.

The legislation provides approximately $70 billion in new funding for the Department of Homeland Security's immigration enforcement operations over three years. Senate Republicans secured passage after an 18-hour marathon voting session known as a vote-a-rama, a process where senators propose and vote on amendments to legislation passed through the budget reconciliation procedure.

Democratic leaders attempted to use the extended voting period to force Republicans to defend controversial provisions within the bill. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer had pledged to leverage the vote-a-rama to highlight what he characterized as priorities favoring Trump. According to Schumer, "Senate Republicans passed a rotten bill that makes their priorities painfully clear: more money for Donald Trump, more power for Donald Trump, and nothing to lower costs for working families."

During the voting marathon, Democrats introduced an amendment attempting to eliminate an "anti-weaponization fund" and block Trump from establishing a settlement fund worth $1.8 billion. The amendment narrowly failed with a vote of 49-50, after three Republican senators joined all Democrats in support. Democrats also succeeded in forcing Republicans to withdraw a separate proposal for $1 billion in security improvements for the White House ballroom.

Republican Senator Lindsey Graham expressed satisfaction with the outcome, stating he was "very proud of my Republican colleagues for sticking together and making sure that Border Patrol and ICE are fully funded." He added, "Well done to President Trump and my Republican colleagues."

If signed into law, the bill would remove ICE and Border Patrol funding from annual appropriations battles through January 2029. This locks in financing for immigration enforcement operations for the remainder of Trump's presidency without requiring annual congressional approval.

The party-line vote reflects deep partisan divisions over immigration enforcement spending and presidential authority over agency budgets. Throughout recent weeks, the legislation had faced significant uncertainty as some senators objected to specific provisions, particularly the anti-weaponization fund and the White House renovation expenses.

The legislation now moves to the House of Representatives for consideration. House passage would represent a substantial commitment to immigration enforcement infrastructure and personnel, solidifying funding levels for these agencies across the next three years.

The company's more recent hardware venture achieved far greater success. The handheld console's popularity suggests there may be broader interest in Valve's hardware offerings than earlier attempts indicated.

The gaming hardware landscape has become increasingly competitive. The Steam Deck's success appears to have given Valve confidence to pursue additional hardware initiatives.

The summer launch timing positions the Steam Machine and Steam Frame for a potential holiday season sales push later in 2026, when consumer spending on electronics typically increases. The simultaneous announcement of both devices suggests Valve plans to build a broader hardware ecosystem, though the company has provided minimal information about how these products would integrate with each other or existing Valve services.

The Steam Frame's announcement is particularly notable given the competitive VR headset market, which includes established players like Meta and other manufacturers. Valve has not revealed what form the Frame will take or how it would differentiate itself from existing options.

These announcements reflect Valve's broader strategy of diversifying beyond its successful digital storefront. While Steam remains the company's core business, the addition of hardware products like the Steam Deck and now the Steam Machine and Steam Frame indicates Valve's ambitions to control more of the gaming experience. Whether these new devices will fare better than the original Steam Machine remains to be seen, but the company appears committed to the strategy based on the Steam Deck's reception.
