8:26 PM ET

The Social Security retirement trust fund is now projected to deplete its reserves by 2032, one year earlier than previously forecast, according to a new report. Without intervention from Congress, the program will only be able to pay a portion of scheduled benefits once the fund runs out.

The accelerated timeline for the trust fund shortfall reflects updated projections about the program's financial health. When the retirement fund is exhausted, incoming payroll tax revenue would only cover a percentage of promised benefits, forcing automatic cuts unless lawmakers act to shore up the system. The report highlights the urgency of addressing Social Security's long-term financing challenges.

The Social Security system relies on trust funds that collect payroll taxes from current workers to pay benefits to retirees. The retirement trust fund has been drawing down its reserves as the baby boomer generation reaches retirement age, increasing the number of beneficiaries relative to contributing workers. The updated projection moves the depletion date closer, narrowing the window for potential legislative solutions.

Congress faces difficult choices to stabilize the program. Options include raising payroll taxes, reducing benefits, increasing the retirement age, or some combination of measures. The political sensitivity of Social Security reform has historically made bipartisan agreement difficult, even as actuaries and policy experts have warned about the program's financial trajectory for years.

The report underscores the scale of the challenge facing federal policymakers. Social Security provides essential income for tens of millions of retired Americans, making any reduction in benefits a significant economic and political concern. The 2032 deadline gives lawmakers a defined timeframe to develop and implement reforms before the trust fund reaches critical low levels and automatic benefit cuts would take effect.

7:02 PM ET

NASA has announced the crew for its Artemis III mission, revealing an all-male team of four astronauts selected to advance the agency's plan to return humans to the Moon. The announcement was made at NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston on Tuesday.

Commander Randy Bresnik will lead the crew, joined by European Space Agency pilot Luca Parmitano and NASA mission specialists Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas. The team is scheduled to launch in 2027 for a two-week mission in low Earth orbit.

Unlike previous Artemis missions, the Artemis III crew will not travel to the Moon or approach it closely. Instead, they will remain near Earth to test docking procedures and life support systems for two competing lunar landers: Blue Origin's Blue Moon and SpaceX's Human Landing System. These tests will prepare systems for Artemis IV, the planned first crewed lunar landing since 1972, currently targeted for 2028.

Bresnik brings extensive spaceflight experience to the mission. The Marine colonel previously commanded the International Space Station and flew on Space Shuttle missions. He holds the distinction of being one of only two NASA astronauts to have a child born while in space.

Parmitano, an Italian Air Force fighter pilot, survived a harrowing incident during a 2013 spacewalk when his helmet filled with liquid. He has also earned recognition as the first disc jockey to broadcast from space, and an asteroid bears his name.

Rubio, an Army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the NASA record for the longest single spaceflight duration at 371 days. He is based in Miami, Florida.

Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer, comes from Miami as well. He has not previously flown to space but trained as part of the backup crew alongside Artemis II astronauts during their preparation.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman praised the crew and the broader space program, describing it as "Earth's first starfleet." He highlighted the multiple spacecraft that will operate simultaneously during these missions, including vehicles from the United States, Russia, and China, calling it "the very beginning of Earth's first Starfleet."

The Artemis III mission depends on successful completion of ongoing development work by private contractors. Blue Origin recently experienced a setback when its New Glenn rocket was destroyed in a launchpad explosion on May 28 in Florida. Company officials have expressed confidence that both the rocket and launchpad will be ready for the 2027 flight, with NASA pledging active support to resolve technical challenges.

The mission represents a significant step forward in NASA's long-term strategy to establish sustained lunar exploration and eventually support missions to Mars.

In a statement, Scorsese explained his reasoning: "I'm interested in the intersection of technology and storytelling, and seeing how that can push the bounds of creativity to create deeper and richer experiences for audiences."

He emphasized that cinema is still a young medium and should remain open to evolution. Scorsese noted that for seven decades he has been creating his own storyboards and has long struggled with how to effectively communicate his visual ideas to cast and crew members. "Now with this tool, I can share what I'm visualising more clearly and efficiently to my creative team," he said. He added that testing the technology on a scene proved "creatively freeing" and allowed faster movement through preproduction without sacrificing quality.

The announcement sparked immediate backlash from storyboard and concept artists. Karla Ortiz, a concept artist who worked on multiple Marvel films including Black Panther and Avengers: Endgame, responded angrily on social media, stating: "He throws every single storyboard artist he's ever worked with under the bus. To use his legacy and power for this is just so disgusting."

Samuel Deats, director of the animated series Castlevania, also criticized the partnership: "There is absolutely no reason to need AI built on the stolen work of millions of artists to storyboard your vision, have some damn pride and respect your peers."

The Art Directors Guild released a formal statement on social media condemning Scorsese's decision, accusing him of abandoning the human artists who have been instrumental throughout his career in creating his most iconic films. The guild represents art directors and production designers working across film, television, and other visual media.

Scorsese's move places him among a growing number of prominent filmmakers embracing AI technology. Director Steven Soderbergh used AI-generated sequences in his recent documentary John Lennon: The Last Interview, while Jurassic World Rebirth director Gareth Edwards has praised AI as helpful to his work. Jane Rosenthal, co-founder of the Tribeca film festival, defended screening Dreams of Violets, a fully AI-generated film about protesters in Iran.

The partnership reflects broader tensions in the entertainment industry over artificial intelligence's role in creative work, with concerns mounting about its impact on employment and artistic integrity among writers, actors, and visual artists.

Rubio, an Army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the NASA record for the longest single spaceflight duration at 371 days. He is based in Miami, Florida.
