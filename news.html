Twelve people were wounded in a shooting near the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio on Saturday evening, with police continuing their search for at least two suspects on Sunday. Two victims remain in critical condition following the attack.

The gunfire erupted just after 5:30pm near the festival, an annual community event featuring live music and architectural home tours in the historic center of Toledo. According to Toledo police deputy chief Joseph Heffernan, the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the violence occurred. Several hundred people were attending the festival at the time.

The victims ranged in age from 14 to 61 years old, with most in their early 20s. No suspects had been taken into custody by Sunday as the investigation entered its second day. Law enforcement officials asked festival attendees to share any photos or videos from their phones that might provide leads in locating the shooters.

The shooting prompted festival organizers to cancel all planned Sunday events. In a statement, organizers expressed their heartbreak over the injuries and explained their decision to shut down the remainder of the festival. "We feel that it would not be compassionate, responsible or possible to continue the festival," they said, acknowledging that celebrations across the country have increasingly become targets for violence.

Police had assigned extra officers to the festival, and a significant number of off-duty officers were working as security for the event. Despite these precautions, the shooting still occurred. Fire chief Allison Armstrong noted that it was challenging to transport victims to area hospitals because roads were closed and traffic was congested as people fled the festival.

Deputy Chief Heffernan expressed frustration about the incident, saying it was unfortunate when individuals "decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years."

Ohio governor Mike DeWine responded to the shooting with a statement expressing deep concern about the violence. "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence," DeWine said. Ohio senator Jon Husted also commented on the incident, calling the violence senseless and thanking law enforcement for their quick response and ongoing efforts to locate the suspects.

The Toledo shooting represents one of more than 170 mass shootings that have occurred in the United States so far this year, according to the Gun Violence Archive, which defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more people are wounded or killed. The high frequency of such incidents has prompted repeated calls for stronger federal gun control measures, though Congress has not implemented substantial restrictions.

1:20 PM ET

A new analysis has found that the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, a critical ocean current system, is far more likely to collapse than previously thought. The research combined real-world ocean observations with climate models to assess the future of the AMOC, producing findings that scientists have described as "very concerning."

The AMOC is a major component of the global climate system that transports warm tropical water to Europe and the Arctic, where it cools and sinks to form a deep return current. Previous studies had already shown the system is at its weakest point in 1,600 years due to climate change, but the new research narrows uncertainty about how much further it will weaken.

Climate scientists use dozens of computer models to predict future climate conditions, but for the complex AMOC system, these models produce widely varying results. Some suggest no further slowdown by 2100, while others predict a deceleration of about 65 percent. By comparing models with actual ocean observations, researchers found the most reliable predictions indicate a slowdown of 42 to 58 percent by 2100, a level almost certain to trigger collapse.

Dr Valentin Portmann, who led the research, stated: "We found that the Amoc is going to decline more than expected compared to the average of all climate models. This means we have an Amoc that is closer to a tipping point."

Professor Stefan Rahmstorf from the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research called the finding "an important and very concerning result." He explained: "The 'pessimistic' models, which show a strong weakening of the Amoc by 2100, are, unfortunately, the realistic ones, in that they agree better with observational data."

Rahmstorf, who has studied the AMOC for 35 years, expressed growing worry about reaching a shutdown tipping point by mid-century. When scientists previously thought the risk of collapse was about 5 percent, he said the risk was already too high given the potential impacts. "Now it looks like it's more than 50 percent," he added.

A collapse would have catastrophic consequences across multiple continents. Western Europe could experience extreme cold winters and summer droughts. The tropical rainfall belt that millions rely on for food production would shift. Sea levels around the Atlantic would rise by an additional 50 to 100 centimeters.

The AMOC is slowing because rapidly rising Arctic air temperatures cause ocean water to cool more slowly. Warmer water is less dense and sinks more slowly into the depths. This allows more rainfall to accumulate in surface waters, further reducing their density and creating a feedback loop that slows the circulation even more.

The research, published in the journal Science Advances, used a statistical method called ridge regression to identify which climate models best reflect observed ocean conditions, particularly surface salinity patterns in the south Atlantic.

Alexander Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Italian player Flavio Cobolli in a grueling five-set final. The second-seeded German won 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in what was described as an emotionally draining match that tested both players' mental fortitude.

The victory represents a breakthrough moment for Zverev after three previous Grand Slam final losses. Despite success at Masters 1000 events, the ATP Finals twice, and an Olympic gold medal in Tokyo 2020, the sport's biggest titles had eluded him until now. After the match concluded, Zverev dropped to his back and wept following Cobolli's missed overhead on the second championship point.

The French Open final unfolded as a tense affair across two weeks of a chaotic men's tournament. Zverev's path to the title became clearer when top seed Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were eliminated in consecutive days, with Carlos Alcaraz absent due to injury. Cobolli, ranked 14th in the world, proved to be the only top-25 player Zverev faced in the draw.

Cobolli's journey to the final was unusual. He reached his first Grand Slam final without playing in the semifinals after his Italian compatriot Matteo Arnaldi withdrew due to illness. This meant the underdog felt the magnitude of the moment intensely from the start. He was completely outplayed in the opening set, winning just one game.

The match's second set saw Cobolli recover as he found his rhythm with the forehand and used his drop shot effectively. Zverev's familiar weaknesses emerged, particularly his forehand and second serve, which led to double faults at critical moments.

Zverev reasserted control in the third set, dominating on his serve while Cobolli managed only a 52% first-serve percentage and faced constant pressure. The fourth set became a turning point for the Italian, who recovered from down 5-4 to force a tiebreak. Cobolli's fighting spirit culminated in a running forehand winner that leveled the match at two sets each.

However, Cobolli's energy was depleted. He collapsed at the beginning of the fifth set, losing his opening service game and never recovering. Zverev closed out the match with conviction.

Addressing his team after winning, Zverev reflected on his journey: "We've been through injuries. We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're grand slam champions now and that's what counts."

The victory also held special significance for Zverev at Roland Garros specifically. Four years earlier, he suffered a severe injury on the same court during a semifinal, leaving in a wheelchair with seven broken ligaments and two fractured bones. His return as champion represented a dramatic turnaround at a venue that held both his worst and best professional memories.
