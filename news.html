The inflation reading represents the third consecutive monthly jump since the Iran war began.

The sharp acceleration in inflation comes as disruptions to global energy markets have driven up oil prices. Before the conflict started, inflation was running at 2.4 percent. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has been a major factor in rising energy costs worldwide.

Brent crude, the international benchmark for oil, climbed to $111.16 per barrel early in the trading week, its highest level in nearly two weeks. The price surge followed an attack on a nuclear power plant in the United Arab Emirates and stalled peace negotiations between the United States and Iran.

President Trump responded to the inflation report on Wednesday, saying "I love the inflation" and indicating he was not concerned about the price increases given recent developments in the conflict.

The elevated inflation figures have reverberated across global financial markets. Government bond yields have risen sharply in response to inflation concerns and expectations that central banks will need to increase interest rates. The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield hit 4.631 percent, its highest level since February 2025. In the United Kingdom, the 10-year gilt yield surpassed an 18-year high, reaching 5.19 percent before pulling back slightly.

Stock markets opened lower across major regions on Monday in response to the geopolitical tensions and inflation worries. Europe's Stoxx 600 index dropped 0.7 percent, while Japan's Nikkei fell about 1 percent. Hong Kong's Hang Seng index also declined 1 percent.

Meanwhile, finance ministers from the G7 nations gathered in Paris to discuss the economic effects of the Middle East conflict. In Japan, the government prepared to issue fresh debt as part of an effort to cushion the economic impact from the war.

Bond market volatility has also been influenced by political developments in the United Kingdom, where traders are anticipating potential leadership changes that could affect fiscal policy. However, some analysts suggested that if investors believe spending pressures will be controlled, bond yields could begin to retreat.

Economists and policymakers are closely monitoring whether the inflation spike proves temporary or signals a sustained period of elevated prices. The data will shape monetary policy decisions in coming months as central banks balance the need to control inflation against risks to economic growth.

Yesterday, 6:52 PM ET

Apple introduced a substantially redesigned Siri at its Worldwide Developers Conference 2026, marking a major shift in the voice assistant's capabilities and architecture. The new version, now called Siri AI, integrates with Apple Intelligence and will be available to all users in the fall as part of iOS 27.

The revamp represents a fundamental departure from Siri's previous design. Rather than functioning primarily as a web-based question-and-answer tool, the new Siri operates as a conversational AI assistant similar to ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained that the tool has been rebuilt with generative AI at its core, powered by Google's Gemini model through a billion-dollar partnership between the two companies.

One of the most significant improvements is Siri's ability to recognize when users have finished speaking. The assistant can now detect pauses in conversation and determine whether a user is simply taking a breath or has completed their thought. This contextual awareness allows Siri to respond at appropriate moments rather than interrupting prematurely, making interactions feel more natural and less mechanical.

The new Siri will function as a dedicated app capable of drawing information from Apple's native ecosystem across mobile devices and laptops. In a demonstration, Rockwell showed how the assistant could identify a location from a photo, retrieve a friend's address from contacts, and provide directions with a planned stop, all within a single interface. Beyond navigation, the app will handle proofreading, shopping, calendar management, and image analysis such as retrieving nutritional information from photos of food. Initially, the service will be available only in English.

Apple also announced expanded child safety features alongside the Siri update. The company introduced new parental controls that limit content visibility, restrict communication contacts, and regulate app access. A new setup assistant helps parents customize restrictions and gradually expand access as children age. Additional features include requiring permission before accessing new websites in Safari and proactively monitoring messages for violent or graphic content.

The announcements reflect Apple's broader effort to catch up in the AI race. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, acknowledged the company's previous lag in AI development compared to competitors. Apple has repeatedly delayed previous Siri updates originally announced in 2024, and the company settled a 250 million dollar class-action lawsuit in May over false advertising of Siri's capabilities.

The new Siri represents the third generation of Apple's foundation models and will integrate AI features into Safari, messaging, the Home app, and camera tools. All updates will roll out as part of the next operating system releases, with developers receiving preview access at the conference.

Tharp's achievement at the college level underscores the depth of talent in American collegiate athletics.

The American college championships serve as the culminating event of the NCAA outdoor track season, bringing together the nation's top collegiate athletes. Many Olympic medalists and professional track stars have emerged from the meet, which features competition across sprints, distance races, jumps, and throws. Tharp's record-breaking performance will likely elevate his profile ahead of future international competitions.

The breakthrough adds to what has been a competitive season in men's hurdles, with several athletes posting fast times throughout the spring. Atlantic Ocean Cold Spot Suggests Major Current System May Be Weakening

Scientists say an anomalous cold region in the Atlantic could signal changes to ocean circulation patterns.

Yesterday, 7:03 PM ET

Researchers have identified a persistent cold region in the Atlantic Ocean that may indicate the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation is approaching a tipping point. The AMOC is a system of ocean currents that moves warm water northward and cold water southward, influencing weather patterns across multiple continents.

The cold spot appears anomalous because ocean temperatures are rising globally due to climate change. Scientists say the cooler water could result from a slowdown in the circulation system, which would have significant implications for weather in North America and Europe. The AMOC has weakened in recent decades, and some climate models suggest further decline could trigger abrupt shifts in regional climates.
