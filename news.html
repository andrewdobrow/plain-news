A few minutes ago

Oil and gas prices surged sharply following escalated military strikes between Iran and Israel on energy infrastructure, triggering widespread concerns about sustained disruption to global energy supplies and spurring sharp declines across stock markets worldwide.

Brent crude, the global benchmark, initially jumped 10% to $119 a barrel before settling at $110, representing a 3.3% gain. Crude prices have soared 60% since hostilities began on February 28. The escalation centered on attacks against critical energy facilities. Israel targeted Iran's South Pars gasfield, prompting Iranian retaliation against Qatar's Ras Laffan, the world's largest liquefied natural gas facility.

QatarEnergy reported that Iranian strikes caused "extensive damage" to Ras Laffan and damaged facilities producing 17% of its LNG export capacity. Repairs could take three to five years. Shell's Pearl gas-to-liquids facility at the site also suffered damage, though the fire was quickly extinguished and no injuries were reported. Additionally, authorities in Abu Dhabi shut down operations at its Habshan gas facility and Bab oilfield following Iranian attacks.

Gas prices experienced even sharper increases. European gas prices jumped 24% to 68 euros per megawatt hour, their highest level since December 2022. UK wholesale gas prices more than doubled since late February, with the month-ahead price rising 23% to 172 pence per therm, its highest since August 2022. These price surges are likely to increase household utility bills significantly.

The energy consultancy Wood Mackenzie warned that attacks on Qatar's LNG hub have fundamentally altered the global gas market outlook. Initial expectations of a two-month disruption are now likely to be exceeded, with each additional month of disruption removing about 1.5% from annual global LNG availability.

The escalation triggered a sharp sell-off across global stock markets. Japan's Nikkei fell 3.4%, South Korea's Kospi dropped 2.7%, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng declined 2%. European markets followed suit, with the UK's FTSE 100 down 2.35%, Germany's Dax down 2.3%, and France's CAC 2.2% lower.

Analysts warned of broader economic consequences. Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, stated that higher energy prices will create "toxic repercussions worldwide," particularly affecting Europe's reliance on Qatari LNG exports as countries wean themselves from Russian dependence. European airlines including Lufthansa announced that fares would rise if elevated fuel prices persist.

Thomas Pugh, chief economist at RSM UK, cautioned that sustained high energy prices could trigger second-round inflationary effects, potentially pushing inflation toward 5% by summer and making interest rate increases more likely from the Bank of England.

The conflict has expanded the risk profile for Middle East energy infrastructure, with analysts now assessing threats to export terminals, offshore facilities, and crucial shipping routes throughout the region.

The Senate failed to extend a key intelligence surveillance program, leaving lawmakers just days to prevent its expiration. Seven Republicans joined Democrats in blocking the extension, creating uncertainty about the future of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act authorities.

The failed vote came after President Trump's appointment of Bill Pulte as acting director of national intelligence threw bipartisan renewal efforts into disarray. Pulte, a major Republican donor and heir to a home construction fortune with no intelligence experience, replaced Tulsi Gabbard in the role. Senior Democrats immediately warned that the appointment could collapse a fragile bipartisan agreement needed to renew section 702 of FISA before the June 12 deadline.

Mark Warner, the Democratic vice chair of the Senate intelligence committee, directly criticized Pulte's qualifications during a hearing. "What qualifications from my standpoint does Mr Pulte bring to the office? Well, he has shown that he is willing to do anything that President Trump wants, legal or otherwise," Warner said. In an NPR interview, Warner stated that Pulte's appointment had "upended what he described as an already difficult path to renewal of section 702," adding "I do not have the confidence I had yesterday."

Democratic sources indicated privately that a bipartisan deal could collapse if Trump refused to reverse the appointment. Warner reportedly asked Republican Senate Majority Leader John Thune to use his influence with the White House to block the nomination.

Even within Republican ranks, significant opposition emerged. Thune offered a notably cool response to his own president's pick, warning that Pulte would face "a lengthy road ahead of him" if nominated permanently and stating that "we don't need a weaponized" national intelligence director. Former Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell said that "anyone performing this role of such immense public trust must have the extensive national security experience required by statute, and no nominee who falls short of this requirement will earn my vote."

Other senior administration officials notably failed to defend the appointment. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent both declined to endorse Trump's decision during separate congressional hearings.

Chris Murphy, a Democratic senator from Connecticut, expressed concern about potential misuse of intelligence authorities. "The very nature of our collection is now going to be put in the hands of somebody who has a history of seeking out private information for political gain," Murphy said.

Section 702 permits US intelligence agencies to collect communications of foreign targets operating outside the country without a warrant. The program has long attracted criticism from civil liberties advocates concerned about privacy protections, particularly after the FBI used it to investigate Black Lives Matter protesters in 2020.

With Congress facing a compressed timeline to negotiate renewal, lawmakers must bridge significant differences between members supporting the program's extension and those demanding reforms or opposing renewal altogether.

He will be replaced by Amar Subramanya, a veteran AI researcher who previously served as corporate vice-president of AI at Microsoft and spent 16 years at Google, where he led engineering for the Gemini AI assistant.

The leadership transition signals Apple's intention to intensify its focus on AI development. Subramanya will report to Craig Federighi, Apple's head of engineering, who has taken on an expanded role overseeing AI efforts across the company. Tim Cook, Apple's CEO, emphasized that Federighi "has been instrumental in driving our AI efforts, including overseeing our work to bring a more personalized Siri to users next year."

Apple unveiled its Apple Intelligence product suite in June 2024 but has been slower than competitors like Google in integrating generative AI features across its products. The company has introduced some incremental updates, including real-time language translation in new AirPod earphones and an AI-generated voice for fitness app workouts, but more substantial changes remain pending.

The promised Siri overhaul has faced repeated delays. Craig Federighi addressed this during the company's developer conference in June, explaining that "this work needed more time to reach our high-quality bar." Cook later confirmed that Apple was "making good progress on a more personalized Siri" and committed to releasing it in the coming year.

Subramanya brings significant experience to the role. His work on Gemini at Google positioned him at a leading AI company, while his time at Microsoft exposed him to enterprise AI applications. The appointment reflects Apple's determination to strengthen its competitive position in a rapidly evolving landscape where voice assistants and AI capabilities have become central to consumer expectations.

Apple's historical approach has emphasized on-device processing and privacy protections, which could differentiate its AI strategy from cloud-dependent competitors. With billions of users across iPhones, iPads, Macs, and wearables, the company has substantial reach for deploying new AI capabilities.

The company announced that this represents "a new chapter" as it "strengthens its commitment" to artificial intelligence. The leadership change, combined with upcoming software updates and a focus on Siri improvements, suggests Apple is positioning itself to demonstrate meaningful progress in AI development during a period when consumer expectations and competitive pressure continue to accelerate.

Antonelli led from George Russell's Mercedes throughout much of the race, maintaining a buffer that grew to nearly ten seconds at one point. Lewis Hamilton, now racing for Ferrari, secured his first podium finish with the Scuderia in third place, behind Russell. The result extends Hamilton's impressive record at Monaco while marking a significant achievement in his Ferrari career.

Charles Leclerc finished fourth for Ferrari, with Oliver Bearman rounding out the top five. The midfield battle saw contributions from Pierre Gasly, Liam Lawson, and other competitors fighting for position throughout the 56-lap contest.

The race tested driver concentration and consistency as multiple incidents unfolded on the narrow Monte Carlo streets. Several drivers encountered mechanical difficulties, leading to retirements. Max Verstappen withdrew from the race after suffering electrical issues that caused his dashboard to shut down while exiting a corner. Oscar Piastri also retired, citing a power unit problem. Fernando Alonso, Lance Stroll, Lando Norris, Gabriel Bortoleto, and Alexander Albon joined the list of non-finishers.

Antonelli's dominant performance drew praise following the checkered flag. When interviewed, he expressed emotion and gratitude toward his Mercedes team for their support in achieving his goal. He acknowledged making some early mistakes during the race, including a moment where he ran wide and briefly went off track at turn 14, but recovered to maintain his commanding position.

Hamilton congratulated his replacement warmly on the broadcast, describing the race as one of the most enjoyable of his career. He highlighted a memorable battle with Leclerc that featured intense but fair competition, with only minimal contact between the two drivers. Hamilton thanked Ferrari for providing him with a competitive platform.

The chaotic nature of events at Monaco underscored the circuit's notorious challenges. The tight confines leave little room for overtaking and punish any mistakes harshly. Driver errors and mechanical failures can instantly end a competitive afternoon on the principality's demanding streets.

The championship will next head to Japan in a fortnight for the next scheduled event. The calendar will then feature an extended break due to the cancellation of races in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia. Teams will analyze data from Monaco as they prepare strategies for the upcoming races while attempting to address the considerable gap Antonelli has established at the top of the standings.
