The whistleblower claims the USPS is rolling out the portal without sufficient testing or safeguards, creating concerns about potential disruptions to voting access.

The online system is being developed in response to Trump's efforts to restrict mail voting. According to the whistleblower, the accelerated timeline could result in technical failures that prevent eligible voters from receiving or submitting their ballots. The official stated that the implementation could potentially "derail" the midterm election if courts allow the plan to move forward.

The USPS portal would represent a significant change to how mail-in voting is processed. The system's design and operational readiness have become central concerns as the midterm elections approach. The whistleblower's allegations indicate that the agency is prioritizing speed over reliability in launching the new technology.

The warning emerges as courts consider challenges to Trump's mail voting restrictions. If legal challenges are unsuccessful and the portal proceeds as planned, election administrators could encounter unprecedented complications when processing absentee ballots. The potential for widespread ballot rejections raises important questions about voter access and election integrity.

Election officials, voting rights advocates, and lawmakers will likely examine the USPS implementation plans closely in the coming weeks. The outcome could affect millions of voters who depend on mail-in ballots, particularly in states with substantial absentee voting populations.

The situation highlights broader concerns about the implementation of voting systems and the importance of adequate testing before launch. Election experts generally emphasize that new voting infrastructure requires extensive preparation and validation to ensure it functions reliably when it matters most.

This approach follows what analysts identify as China's established pattern for managing information during national disasters.

The flooding represents one of the deadliest natural disasters in the region in recent years. Rescue operations are expected to continue for days as teams work to reach remote areas and account for all missing persons. The full extent of the damage to infrastructure and communities in both countries is still being assessed as floodwaters recede.

A few minutes ago

Government bond yields have climbed to levels unseen in decades across major developed economies, driven by a combination of geopolitical instability, inflation concerns, and mounting fiscal pressures. The widespread sell-off has raised borrowing costs for governments globally and sparked debate about the long-term sustainability of public finances.

The surge in yields intensified after ceasefire negotiations between the United States and Iran collapsed on Monday night without reaching an agreement. Oil prices climbed above $91 a barrel following threats to escalate the conflict, rekindling investor fears about persistent inflation. The 30-year US Treasury yield rose above 5.33 percent on Tuesday, marking its highest level since June 2007. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed above 4.74 percent, while Japan's equivalent bond yield reached 2.945 percent, the highest in three decades. The UK's 10-year government bond yield topped 5.176 percent, Germany's rose to its highest since 2011, and France's hit a 16-year peak.

Multiple factors are driving the global bond market deterioration. Rising long-dated bond yields reflect not only expectations of higher interest rates and inflation, but also investor concerns about elevated government debt levels. According to Dan Coatsworth, head of markets at AJ Bell, "Rising long-dated bond yields are not driven solely by expectations of higher interest rates and inflation fears. They can also reflect concerns around high levels of government borrowing and investors demanding greater compensation for the risks of holding long-dated government bonds."

The volume of new debt entering bond markets compounds the pressure. Governments continue spending while corporate debt issuance remains substantial, particularly from artificial intelligence firms making large capital expenditures. Neil Wilson, a Saxo UK investor strategist, observed that "fixed income investors grow nervous about a range of factors, from inflation and the Iran conflict to deeper structural concerns and fiscal worries. Issuance is clearly a factor, both on the government side and on the corporate side."

Fiscal challenges are mounting as governments increase defence spending in response to geopolitical tensions. Leading European countries including Germany and the UK are expected to raise borrowing substantially. This spending comes at a time when governments already carry high debt levels accumulated from previous deficits.

The consequences extend across financial markets and the real economy. Higher government borrowing costs typically result in more expensive loans for businesses and consumers, potentially restraining investment and economic growth. The bond market turbulence has already created uncertainty in equity markets as investors assess the implications of rising interest rates.

Policymakers face a complex challenge balancing multiple competing pressures. They must address fiscal sustainability, manage inflation concerns, support economic growth, and respond to geopolitical instability. The combination of elevated energy prices, high government debt, and rising interest rates will shape economic policy decisions in the coming months.

3:02 AM ET

A Las Vegas jury found Duane "Keffe D" Davis guilty of first-degree murder for orchestrating the 1996 killing of rap icon Tupac Shakur. The jury reached its verdict in approximately three hours, bringing closure to a case that remained unsolved for nearly 30 years.

Davis, a 63-year-old former South Side Compton Crips gang leader, did not pull the trigger himself but was accused of planning and commanding the drive-by shooting. Prosecutors argued he orchestrated the attack in retaliation after Shakur's entourage attacked Davis's nephew, Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, earlier that evening at the Mike Tyson-Bruce Seldon fight.

The shooting occurred on September 7, 1996, when a Cadillac pulled up next to Shakur's vehicle near the Las Vegas strip and fired eight shots. Shakur was hospitalized but died days later from his injuries. The case had frustrated law enforcement and captivated hip-hop fans for three decades, spawning countless theories and investigations.

During the two-week trial, prosecutors presented evidence including video footage and audio recordings of Davis describing his alleged role in the incident. Chief Deputy District Attorney Binu Palal told jurors: "Let's be clear, Duane Davis did not pull the trigger. But he did plan the shooting in retaliation of the beating of his nephew." The prosecution's case relied heavily on Davis's own admissions, made through years of statements to investigators and interviews.

Davis's defense attorney Michael Sanft argued that after 30 years, authorities possessed very little credible evidence. Sanft suggested that when Davis previously spoke with law enforcement agencies including the FBI, investigators did not pursue his claims because they questioned their credibility.

A major factor in the case was Davis's 2019 memoir, in which he detailed his involvement in the shooting. Prosecutors said the book ultimately revived the Las Vegas investigation. Defense attorneys attempted to exclude the memoir from trial, arguing Davis and his co-author had exaggerated details to place himself at the center of events for personal gain. A judge denied this request.

The jury comprised six men and ten women selected the previous week. During opening arguments, witness testimony came from Garry Dale, a former Las Vegas officer who had stopped Shakur's vehicle before the shooting, and Ingrid Stokes, a woman in a nearby car who heard the gunshots.

Shakur, killed at age 25, was one of rap's most influential artists and a cultural icon. His death, alongside the subsequent killing of rival rapper Notorious B.I.G., became emblematic of the East Coast-West Coast hip-hop rivalry of the 1990s.

Davis faces sentencing following his conviction. The guilty verdict represents the first successful prosecution in a case that had remained open throughout the lives of an entire generation of hip-hop fans.
