A few minutes ago

Shipping traffic in the Persian Gulf dropped to its lowest level in more than a month as the ongoing Iran war continues to disrupt global energy supplies. Oil prices moved higher in response to the reduced shipping activity through one of the world's most critical energy transit routes.

The Persian Gulf serves as a vital shipping lane for global oil markets, with a significant portion of the world's petroleum exports passing through the waterway. The decline in shipping traffic reflects the growing impact of the conflict on energy supply chains, as vessels avoid the region or face delays due to security concerns.

The disruption comes at a time when global energy markets remain sensitive to supply shocks. Oil prices have responded to the reduced shipping activity, with traders pricing in the potential for further supply constraints if the situation in the region deteriorates. The price increases reflect market concerns about the security of energy supplies and the potential for wider disruptions to global oil flows.

The reduced shipping traffic marks a significant escalation in the economic consequences of the Iran conflict. While the full extent of the supply disruption remains uncertain, energy analysts are monitoring the situation closely for signs of further deterioration. The Persian Gulf's role as a critical chokepoint for global oil supplies means that any sustained reduction in shipping activity could have far-reaching effects on energy prices and global economic growth.

The situation underscores the vulnerability of global energy supply chains to regional conflicts. As shipping companies assess the risks of operating in the area, the potential for prolonged disruptions grows, raising concerns among policymakers and industry leaders about energy security and the broader economic implications of the ongoing conflict.

Smoke from more than 180 active wildfires burning in northern Ontario has triggered air quality alerts across more than 20 states, affecting millions of Americans. Chicago experienced the worst air quality in the world on Thursday evening, according to IQAir's global rankings, followed by Detroit and Minneapolis.

The smoke has drifted southeast from Canada across the US Midwest and Northeast, creating hazy skies from Minnesota to New York. NASA reported that winds carried the Ontario smoke "primarily south-east over much of the southern part of the province, as well as parts of Quebec and the US midwest and north-east, tinting the sky shades of gray and yellow and the sun orange in many areas."

Air quality alerts have been issued for North Carolina, Minnesota, Vermont, Connecticut, New York, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Delaware, West Virginia, Colorado, Virginia, Maryland, Washington DC, Michigan and Wisconsin. According to the US Environmental Protection Agency, air quality reached "hazardous" levels in parts of Pennsylvania, Michigan, Ohio, Illinois, Wisconsin and Minnesota on Thursday, with officials urging residents to stay indoors.

Health officials are advising residents to limit outdoor activities and remain inside when possible. Vulnerable populations face particular risks, including children, elderly people, pregnant women, and those with heart or lung disease. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani cautioned that at unhealthy air levels, "everyone may feel health effects. So today, every New Yorker should take precautions."

The impact on daily life has been significant. In Chicago, beaches and outdoor pools closed as measurements reached dangerous levels. New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced that more than 100,000 N95-style masks were being made available to counties in need, with additional masks distributed at Penn Station and Grand Central. New York officials also opened hundreds of cooling centers to address both the heat emergency and air quality crisis.

New York officials warned this could become the city's "most significant smoke event" since 2023, though they noted current conditions "do not indicate a repeat of 2023's peak levels." They continue monitoring for any deterioration.

Weather forecasters expect smoky conditions to persist through Friday and potentially into the weekend across much of the affected region. Rain is expected Saturday in New York, which could help clear the smoke before Sunday's World Cup final in New Jersey.

Massachusetts Senator Ed Markey emphasized the broader climate implications, stating "The climate crisis is here and it does not stop at our borders." He warned that with record El Niño forecasted for the year, more wildfire smoke days lie ahead.

Residents are encouraged to monitor local air quality reports and follow guidance from public health officials as conditions develop.

Yesterday, 6:45 PM ET

European regulators have ordered Google to grant competing artificial intelligence applications the same access to Android system features that its own Gemini AI assistant receives. The directive represents a significant regulatory move aimed at addressing what authorities view as unfair competitive advantages built into the world's most widely used mobile operating system.

The European Union determined that Gemini's preferential integration with Android creates barriers for rival AI developers. Under the new order, Google must provide equivalent system-level access to competitors, ensuring that other AI assistants can match Gemini's capabilities on Android devices.

The ruling reflects the EU's broader strategy to regulate technology companies and foster competition in digital markets. European officials have increasingly examined how major platforms leverage control over operating systems and app ecosystems to promote their own services. AI integration has become a focal point as companies race to embed chatbots and assistants into smartphones and other devices.

Google now faces the complex task of opening Android's system-level features to multiple AI providers while preserving the platform's functionality and security. The company must balance granting meaningful access to competitors without fragmenting the user experience or introducing technical vulnerabilities. This approach contrasts with regulatory frameworks in other major markets, where platform operators retain greater authority over system integration decisions.

The decision will affect hundreds of millions of Android users throughout Europe and may establish a precedent for how AI assistants compete globally. Developers including OpenAI and Anthropic could potentially gain access that allows their assistants to operate at comparable levels to Gemini on Android phones. This increased access could reshape the competitive landscape for AI assistants in mobile markets.

The ruling has attracted attention from major technology publications including MacRumors, Ars Technica, The New York Times, The Verge, and The Washington Post, suggesting it represents a significant development in the ongoing debate over technology regulation and platform competition.

Google has not yet announced a specific timeline for implementing these changes or indicated whether it plans to challenge the order. The company will need to develop technical solutions that satisfy regulatory requirements while maintaining the integrity of the Android ecosystem.

This decision adds to the growing list of EU regulatory interventions targeting major technology companies. It demonstrates European authorities' willingness to impose structural changes on dominant platforms to protect competitive markets and ensure fair access for alternative services. As AI technology continues advancing and becoming more central to smartphone experiences, regulators worldwide will likely scrutinize how major platform operators integrate these tools.

Welsh singer Bonnie Tyler has died at age 75 after a period of ill health. Funeral arrangements have been announced that will include a public viewing at her Mumbles home, with fans invited to line the streets to pay their respects as her coffin is brought to the residence.

Tyler became internationally known for her distinctive raspy voice and powerful ballads. She achieved worldwide success with hits including "Total Eclipse of the Heart" and "Holding Out for a Hero," which became defining songs of the 1980s. Her career spanned several decades, during which she sold millions of records globally and earned recognition as one of Wales's most successful musical exports.

Tyler's most famous song, "Total Eclipse of the Heart," was released in 1983. Written by Jim Steinman, the songwriter behind Meat Loaf's hits, the track became a global phenomenon. A gothic music video directed by Russell Mulcahy and filmed at Holloway Sanatorium in Surrey helped propel the song to number one in 10 countries and achieved 6 million sales worldwide. The video exceeded a billion views on YouTube by 2023. Tyler recalled that the song reached the top of Apple Music charts again following the North American total solar eclipse in April 2024. She said in an interview: "I can't tell you how many people have told me they had their first dance to this song, they fell in love to this song. I never get tired of it."

Born Gaynor Hopkins in Skewen near Swansea, Tyler grew up in a musical family. Her mother was known locally for her beautiful singing voice, and her father, despite being unable to work due to tuberculosis, supported her musical interests by purchasing a tape machine. Tyler herself survived tuberculosis as a child, discovered at age 12 after scarring appeared on her lungs.

After forming a group called Imagination in the 1970s, Tyler was spotted singing in a Swansea nightclub and signed to RCA in 1976. She had early chart success with "Lost in France" and "It's a Heartache" before her career dipped. When signing to CBS in 1981, she requested to work with Jim Steinman. After hearing new demos in a rockier style, Steinman agreed to collaborate, noting she had an "80s voice that hadn't been exploited." Tyler's distinctive huskiness resulted from incomplete recovery after a 1977 throat operation, worsened by a scream during recovery that delayed healing.

Beyond her signature ballads, Tyler's songwriting talent was recognized when other artists achieved success with her tracks, including Tina Turner's "The Best" and Cher's "Save Up All Your Tears." She turned down the 1983 James Bond theme "Never Say Never Again," later explaining she simply did not like the song.

The announcement of funeral arrangements reflects Tyler's deep connection to her hometown of Mumbles in South Wales, allowing supporters to acknowledge her substantial impact on popular music.

House Republicans propose fourth budget reconciliation bill as third stalls

House Republican leaders have begun discussing the possibility of a fourth budget reconciliation bill, even as their third such measure faces significant uncertainty on its legislative path. The conversation about using this special parliamentary process again reflects growing dissatisfaction among many House Republicans with the current proposal under consideration. Budget re...

6:00 AM ET

House Republican leaders have begun discussing the possibility of a fourth budget reconciliation bill, even as their third such measure faces significant uncertainty on its legislative path. The conversation about using this special parliamentary process again reflects growing dissatisfaction among many House Republicans with the current proposal under consideration.

Budget reconciliation bills allow legislation related to federal spending and revenues to pass with only Republican votes, bypassing the Senate filibuster and requiring just a simple majority. However, these bills must follow strict budgetary rules, and party unity becomes critical when Republicans hold narrow margins of control.

The uncertainty surrounding the current healthcare debate has prompted this discussion. With enhanced tax credits for Americans purchasing coverage through Affordable Care Act marketplaces set to expire at the end of the year, House Speaker Mike Johnson unveiled a Republican alternative on Friday. The proposal focuses on Republican priorities rather than extending the enhanced subsidies that help lower premiums for millions of Americans.

Johnson's package, described as more than 100 pages, emphasizes Republican proposals to expand access to association health plans and increase oversight of pharmacy benefit managers. Association health plans would allow small businesses and self-employed individuals to band together to purchase coverage, though critics argue such plans offer skimpier coverage than required under the Affordable Care Act. The proposal also includes requirements for more data collection from pharmacy benefit managers as a way to control drug costs.

The Republican plan does mention cost-sharing reductions for some lower-income people using ACA coverage, but these would not take effect until January 2027. Notably, the package does not extend the enhanced tax credit that currently helps millions of Americans afford marketplace insurance. That credit, implemented during the Biden administration in response to the pandemic, expires December 31, potentially doubling or more the out-of-pocket premiums for most families in the program.

President Donald Trump said Friday that Republicans will develop a better plan than the ACA, but he provided few specifics beyond his proposal to provide Americans with stipends to help purchase insurance rather than extending existing tax credits.

The Republican healthcare approach has put vulnerable House Republicans representing competitive districts in a difficult position. Some centrist Republican lawmakers have aligned with Democrats to push their own proposals for continuing the tax credits, at least temporarily. These lawmakers are co-sponsoring bills and signing discharge petitions that could force floor votes if they gather sufficient signatures.

Two discharge petitions targeting ACA subsidies had bipartisan support as of Friday. One petition by Pennsylvania Republican Brian Fitzpatrick proposed a two-year subsidy extension with fraud prevention measures. Another from New Jersey Democrat Josh Gottheimer supported a simpler one-year extension with new income caps. Both petitions had enough Republican backing to likely succeed if Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries encouraged his caucus to participate.

The dramatic decline in eel populations has made them one of the most threatened freshwater species, prompting conservation efforts across the UK to restore waterways and remove barriers to their lifecycle.
