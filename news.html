6:00 AM ET

President Trump has issued new executive orders designed to restrict birthright citizenship, marking his second attempt to curtail a constitutional right that has existed for over 150 years. The orders come just weeks after the Supreme Court struck down his first effort on this issue.

The new orders attempt to expand the categories of children born on U.S. soil who would be ineligible for automatic citizenship and target what Trump calls "birth tourism." The president claimed this practice involves "hundreds of thousands" of people annually. However, according to the Migration Policy Institute, actual numbers are far lower. Census-based estimates put birth tourism at between 22,000 and 26,000 babies born annually to mothers visiting the country, while government data from 2024 showed just 9,600 births to mothers with foreign addresses.

Trump has pursued this issue for over a decade. In a 2015 campaign event, he used the term "anchor babies" to describe children born to undocumented immigrants and promised to end birthright citizenship if elected. As president in 2018, he said he would use an executive order to deny citizenship to children born in the U.S. to non-citizens, claiming this could be accomplished without a constitutional amendment.

The legal argument centers on the 14th Amendment's guarantee that "all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to the jurisdiction thereof" are citizens. Trump's administration argues this language should be interpreted more narrowly to exclude certain children, a position supported by legal figures like John Eastman, a former Supreme Court clerk. This interpretation remains a minority view among legal scholars.

The Supreme Court's June decision striking down Trump's first order provided clear guidance. Chief Justice John Roberts stated the original order violated the 14th Amendment. Despite this ruling, the administration has crafted these new orders more narrowly, hoping they will survive legal challenges.

Civil rights groups and Democratic-led states have already signaled their intent to challenge the orders in court. The American Civil Liberties Union has indicated it will pursue legal action. Legal experts expect the case will ultimately reach the Supreme Court, where justices must determine whether the executive branch has authority to reinterpret the 14th Amendment without congressional action or a constitutional amendment.

The policy represents one of Trump's significant immigration initiatives during his second term. Any successful change would fundamentally alter how citizenship is determined for children born on American soil and would affect countless families. The outcome of the legal battle will likely define citizenship rights for years to come and test the boundaries of presidential power over immigration policy.

Diplomatic efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz have reached what officials describe as progressive stages, with mediators from multiple countries working to bridge significant gaps between Iran and the United States. However, Iran continues to insist on major American policy changes before agreeing to restore shipping through the critical waterway.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expressed cautious optimism about negotiations, telling CNBC that "there is a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the strait and move towards a more normalised position in this conflict." His remarks echoed similar statements from President Donald Trump, though Tehran quickly denied being in direct talks with Washington.

Multiple countries are involved in mediation efforts. Qatar, Pakistan, and Oman are coordinating diplomatic contacts and exchanging draft proposals between the two sides. A senior Pakistani security official said the mediators' goal is to make both parties agree to start talking. "A lot is happening in the background," the official told Reuters.

The immediate focus of negotiations centers on a temporary transit scheme that would allow ships to enter and leave the Persian Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz. Under the proposal, vessels would enter via a route near Iran and exit along the Omani coast, with both countries sharing control of the southern passage. A contentious issue involves Iran's demand to charge vessels tolls or fees, which would be negotiated during a 60-day period when Iran's nuclear program would also be addressed.

Oman has reportedly faced pressure from the United States, European nations, and Saudi Arabia to accept this arrangement. However, the Omani government has resisted the proposal, fearing it could become permanent and give Tehran lasting control over sea routes in Omani waters.

The situation reflects internal divisions within Iran's leadership. The foreign ministry appears eager to reach a deal and worries that Iran's leverage over the strait could diminish over time as shipping finds alternative routes. Regime hardliners oppose the agreement, arguing that the US cannot be trusted to honor financial commitments promised in exchange for reopening the waterway.

One regional diplomat said the regime recognizes the substantial control it holds over the strait and senses American desperation to reach an agreement. Another diplomat noted that Saudi Arabia is applying particularly heavy pressure on Oman, concerned that regional tensions could escalate into broader conflict because a significant faction in Iran opposes any deal.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted global energy markets. The waterway typically handles roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum supply. Vessels have been forced to use longer alternative routes, increasing shipping costs and contributing to oil price volatility.

While both sides describe recent technical and political discussions as positive, a breakthrough remains uncertain.

Two helicopter pilots died when their aircraft crashed while fighting a wildfire in Utah, officials confirmed Saturday. The Sikorsky Skycrane helicopter went down Friday morning in the Fishlake National Forest, according to the Sevier County Sheriff.

The pilots were employed by Helicopter Transport Services, which was contracted by the US Forest Service. Officials have not released their identities at this time.

The helicopter was actively engaged in firefighting operations when the crash occurred. The impact ignited a new fire that spread to connect with the existing blaze, expanding the overall burned area to nearly 174 square miles.

This tragedy is part of a larger crisis unfolding across the western United States. Three firefighters were killed and two others injured while responding to wildfires along the Colorado-Utah border, the US Wildland Fire Service announced Sunday. The fatalities occurred during a joint response to the Knowles and Gore fires on Saturday.

"The US Wildland Fire Service stands united with the USDA Forest Service in grief and in our unwavering support for the loved ones left behind," the agency said in a statement. "Their bravery, dedication, and sacrifice will never be forgotten."

Utah is experiencing one of its most severe wildfire seasons in recent history. The Cottonwood fire in Fishlake National Forest began Monday and had expanded to more than 92,000 acres by Saturday morning, remaining zero percent contained. Low humidity and strong winds have contributed to the rapid growth of fires across the region.

Nearly 3 million acres have burned across the country since the beginning of the year, exceeding the ten-year average. Utah has faced an especially severe threat due to its record-low snowpack and warmest winter ever recorded.

State and federal officials have declared emergencies and taken aggressive action to combat the fires. Utah Governor Spencer Cox declared a state of emergency due to severe fire conditions and authorized a statewide fireworks ban ahead of the Fourth of July holiday. Colorado Governor Jared Polis also declared a state of emergency and approved deployment of the national guard to assist firefighting efforts.

Power shutoffs have become increasingly common throughout the western United States as wildfire danger has grown. With extreme fire conditions continuing in Utah, Rocky Mountain Power shut off electrical lines serving Beaver County and surrounding areas.

The National Weather Service has issued red flag warnings across a broad section of the west, including California, Arizona and New Mexico. Experts warn that as this summer could become the hottest on record, wildfires will continue to pose a growing challenge to communities and natural resources across the region.

Alphabet is raising the record-breaking sum to fund its artificial intelligence infrastructure expansion. The company stated it needs the capital to meet "unprecedented customer demand" for its AI solutions and services, which are currently exceeding available supply. The fundraising will cover both AI compute infrastructure investments and other costs including tax obligations tied to employee equity awards.

The $80 billion equity raise dwarfs previous secondary share offerings and exceeds the size of the world's largest initial public offerings, according to analyst commentary. This marks a striking reversal for Alphabet, which had previously maintained a policy of repurchasing its own shares rather than issuing new equity.

Berkshire's $10 billion investment signals confidence in Alphabet at a moment when the company's shares initially declined following the capital raise announcement. The capital deployment reflects broader trends in the technology sector. Multiple major companies including Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceX are simultaneously pursuing public market funding as artificial intelligence development demands accelerate. Industry observers note that tech companies are increasingly viewing public equity as the cheapest available capital source in the current interest rate environment, reversing the trend of recent years when private funding dominated.

Alphabet's situation exemplifies the immense capital requirements driving the artificial intelligence race. The company's management emphasized that AI represents an "expansionary moment" and that the company is experiencing strong demand from both enterprise and consumer customers that outpaces current infrastructure capacity.

The Berkshire investment comes as the conglomerate simultaneously reported stronger-than-expected quarterly earnings and continued its share repurchase program. The company repurchased $4.5 billion of its own stock during the period, maintaining the capital deployment strategy that began under Abel's leadership.

Berkshire has historically maintained substantial cash reserves under Buffett's management, with the former CEO expressing difficulty identifying investment opportunities large enough to meaningfully impact the conglomerate's performance. Abel's willingness to commit $10 billion to Alphabet suggests he views current valuations and market conditions as presenting meaningful opportunities for shareholder returns.

The investment underscores the widening gap between companies positioned at the center of artificial intelligence development and those on the periphery, with major technology firms commanding enormous capital flows to support infrastructure expansion.

5:33 AM ET

A total solar eclipse will occur on August 12, 2026, bringing a rare celestial event to Spain and the Balearic Islands. This astronomical phenomenon will mark the first total solar eclipse visible from Spain in more than 120 years, drawing unprecedented attention to regions in the path of totality.

The small mountain village of Somosierra, located at 1,440 metres altitude in the Madrid region, sits in one of the best viewing locations. The village will experience one minute and 29 seconds of total eclipse, making it an ideal destination for eclipse observers. However, this distinction has created significant logistical challenges for the community of just 93 residents.

Authorities anticipate between 2,000 and 5,000 visitors could arrive on eclipse day, despite the village council offering only 400 official registration slots. Tourism manager Irma López Muñoz explained the difficulty of predicting attendance: "Many people are going to come without seeking out information beforehand. We can't predict that someone might be watching television and say, 'Well, I'm going to make some sandwiches and go see the eclipse with my children.'"

Local authorities are coordinating extensively with police and civil protection services to prepare for the influx. The council has hired El Nocturnario, an astrotourism company, to train volunteers about eclipse safety and logistics. Volunteer María Gradaille noted the value of this preparation: "I've learned so much about the eclipse. I didn't know it could burn your eyes so badly or just how dangerous it can be."

Practical concerns facing the village include parking arrangements, water supply, and food service. Local restaurants are preparing to accommodate hungry visitors, with questions about how much food will be needed for the crowds.

The eclipse has broader implications for Spain's tourism industry. The Spanish government established a special committee to oversee three upcoming eclipses: the August 2026 total eclipse, a six-minute "eclipse of the century" in August 2027, and a ring-of-fire eclipse in January 2028. Officials expect the August event alone to attract 446,000 additional visitors, generating approximately 342 million euros in tourist revenue.

In the Balearic Islands, authorities have announced traffic restrictions on 20 main roads across Mallorca, Ibiza, Menorca, and Formentera from 3pm to 9pm on eclipse day to prevent gridlock. According to Sarai de la Hoz of El Nocturnario, Spanish authorities initially underestimated the eclipse's significance but have since recognized its potential for astrotourism and economic benefit.

The total solar eclipse occurs when the moon passes directly between Earth and the sun, completely blocking the solar disk. During totality, observers can see the sun's corona, the outer atmosphere normally invisible to the naked eye.
