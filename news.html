The United States and Iran have signed a memorandum of understanding to end their conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with the agreement now in effect. President Trump signed the document remotely and later confirmed the signing. The agreement was originally scheduled to be signed in Switzerland on Friday, but officials accelerated the timeline to implement it sooner.

Trump presented the deal during an hour-long press conference at the Group of Seven summit in France. He appeared to lower expectations for what the agreement would accomplish, warning that he could authorize military strikes against Iran again if future nuclear negotiations fail. For two months, Trump had been pursuing a deal to end the war, reopen the critical shipping lane, and stabilize global energy markets.

The memorandum includes 14 paragraphs addressing an end to fighting and an agreement that Iran will never have a nuclear weapon. A 300 billion dollar redevelopment package for Iran is also part of the deal. However, critics argue that the agreement falls short of Trump's earlier promises regarding Iran's nuclear program. The president had previously demanded total Iranian surrender and full dismantlement of its nuclear capabilities, including no enrichment and no ballistic missile funding.

Despite the signed agreement, significant obstacles remain to reopening the waterway. Thousands of Iranian mines placed in the Strait of Hormuz during the conflict will delay the return of normal shipping traffic. Trump said the US military was already searching for an estimated 5,000 mines that must be cleared before commerce can fully resume.

Current tensions between the parties threaten to undermine the deal's implementation. Iran has seized two ships attempting to cross the strait, with Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and lead negotiator, declaring that reopening the waterway would be "impossible" while the US and Israel committed what he called "flagrant" breaches of the ceasefire. He criticized the US naval blockade as "the hostage-taking of the world's economy."

The US has also seized Iranian vessels, with the White House press secretary stating that Trump was "satisfied" with the naval blockade and believed Iran was in a weak position, losing approximately 500 million dollars daily through economic strangulation.

The conflict has created severe global economic consequences. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remain stranded in the Gulf. Countries dependent on Gulf oil have faced fuel shortages and price increases. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast, while Greece announced additional aid for households struggling with higher fuel and living costs.

Peace negotiations had been scheduled to continue in Pakistan, but talks never materialized. Iran did not publicly accept the invitation, and a US delegation led by Vice President JD Vance never departed Washington.

As the storm moves inland, the danger shifts from storm surge to heavy rainfall and flooding in areas well beyond the immediate coastline. Authorities have issued flood warnings for low-lying communities and are tracking expected rainfall totals as the system continues its inland progression.

The storm developed rapidly in the Gulf of Mexico before reaching the coast. Forecasters do not expect Arthur to strengthen further as it moves inland. The combination of storm surge and heavy precipitation has created hazardous conditions along the shore, with coastal infrastructure already sustaining damage from waves and debris.

Emergency management teams across coastal states activated their response plans as Arthur approached. Residents in affected areas have been instructed to monitor local emergency alerts and avoid travel through flooded regions. Highway 87, a key coastal route, has sustained damage as water and debris crossed the roadway, disrupting transportation in the area.

The early arrival of Arthur marks the beginning of what may be an active 2026 hurricane season for the Atlantic basin. The storm's impact brings renewed focus to hurricane preparedness along the Gulf Coast, a region that has experienced repeated impacts from tropical systems in recent years. Communities in the path of the storm have been dealing with worsening conditions as rainfall continues.

Authorities caution that conditions will deteriorate over the coming hours as the system moves through the region. The threat of inland flooding extends beyond coastal communities as the storm system pushes further into land. Residents have been advised to prepare for potential evacuations and to secure important documents and supplies.

The full extent of damage caused by Arthur will become clearer as conditions improve and assessment teams are able to survey affected areas. Recovery efforts will likely focus on clearing debris from roadways, restoring utilities, and addressing flood damage to homes and businesses. As the storm system moves inland, rainfall is expected to continue, raising concerns about flash flooding in areas that may not typically face such threats.

7:57 PM ET

The Federal Reserve kept interest rates unchanged at its first meeting under new chairman Kevin Warsh, but signaled a possible rate increase before the end of the year. The central bank maintained rates in a range of 3.5% to 3.75%, where they have remained since December. The decision received unanimous support from the Fed's voting committee.

US stock markets initially dropped following the announcement, with the Dow closing 500 points lower and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each falling over 1.2%. The Fed's statement acknowledged that "economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East."

The policy shift became clear in the Fed's new economic projections, which showed nine of the voting members now expect at least one rate increase this year. This marks a dramatic reversal from March projections, when 12 of 19 officials predicted at least one rate cut by year's end. The change reflects growing concerns about inflation, which the Fed noted "remains elevated relative to the committee's 2% goal."

Warsh used his inaugural press conference to announce a comprehensive overhaul of how the Federal Reserve communicates with the public. He plans to create five new taskforces to assess the central bank's monetary policy approach, including its communications strategy, balance sheet operations, data practices, and labor market analysis. One taskforce will specifically examine how the Fed communicates through news conferences, economic projections, and meeting transcripts.

The new chairman emphasized that the Fed's monetary policy "cannot have a very significant effect on particularly prices," a notable shift from his reputation as an inflation hawk during his previous tenure as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. He stated that while energy prices and food costs do not have first-order consequences for Fed policy, the central bank must prevent such changes from spreading more broadly throughout the economy.

The Fed's policy statement was notably shorter than previous statements, reflecting Warsh's stated desire to tighten the central bank's public communications. Warsh confirmed he was the only board member who did not contribute to the economic projections released Wednesday.

The decision comes as the US economy faces significant pressures. Inflation reached 4.2 percent, the highest level since 2023, driven largely by energy prices following conflict in the Middle East. Although hourly earnings dropped to a seasonally adjusted 0.7 percent, core inflation has risen only mildly to 2.9 percent. The unemployment rate has remained steady at 4.3 percent, indicating continued labor market strength.

Officials will continue monitoring economic conditions and inflation data as they determine whether rate increases become necessary in coming months.

The shortage stems from increased demand for high-capacity memory chips driven by artific...

5:00 PM ET

Apple plans to increase iPhone prices in response to a global shortage of memory chips, CEO Tim Cook said in a recent interview. Cook described the current supply situation as unsustainable, indicating the company can no longer absorb the rising costs of components needed for its devices.

The shortage stems from increased demand for high-capacity memory chips driven by artificial intelligence features. Apple's push to integrate advanced AI capabilities into its Siri assistant requires 12 gigabytes of DRAM in newer iPhone models, double the memory used in previous generations. This surge in demand has strained suppliers Samsung and SK Hynix, creating upward pressure on component pricing across the industry.

In a recent statement, Cook said the company is "doing our best to mitigate the huge increases that are being passed to us" but emphasized that "price increases are unavoidable." The company has been attempting to shield customers from these rising costs, but the situation has become untenable.

The base model iPhone 17, currently in development, will not support all of the new AI features planned for iOS 27 due to insufficient memory capacity. However, the iPhone 18, expected in a later release cycle, is confirmed to include 12 gigabytes of RAM in its base model, enabling full AI functionality across all variants of that generation.

Industry observers note that the memory chip crunch affects Apple's operations in multiple ways beyond just procurement costs. The company must balance the technical requirements of its AI ambitions against manufacturing constraints and consumer price sensitivity. The shortage has forced difficult decisions about which features to prioritize and which device tiers receive upgraded specifications.

Apple has not specified the size of the planned price increases or which iPhone models will be affected first. The company typically adjusts pricing based on regional markets and currency fluctuations, but a component-driven price increase would represent a shift from recent pricing strategies that held costs steady despite inflation in other areas.

The timing of these price increases remains uncertain as Apple continues negotiating with memory chip suppliers. The company's ability to manage costs while delivering the AI features consumers expect will be crucial during the coming product cycles. How aggressively Apple implements price increases and which markets see the largest hikes could significantly impact consumer demand and the company's competitive position in the smartphone market.

Actress Daveigh Chase has died at age 35 from sepsis following meningitis and blood infections. Her boyfriend, Roy Hernandez, confirmed the news after Chase was admitted to a Los Angeles hospital earlier this month.

Chase was best known for voicing the character Lilo in Disney's 2002 animated film Lilo and Stitch. That same year, she appeared in the horror film The Ring, playing Samara Morgan, the ghost at the center of the story. The Ring became a commercial success and established Chase as a recognizable talent in Hollywood during the early 2000s.

Her voice acting work extended beyond Disney. Chase also voiced Chihiro Ogino in the English dub of Studio Ghibli's acclaimed animated film Spirited Away. On screen, she had roles in the films Donnie Darko and appeared in the television series Sabrina the Teenage Witch. She also starred in the HBO drama Big Love, which ran from 2006 to 2011, where she played Rhonda Volmer in the series about a polygamous family in Utah.

Born on July 24, 1990, in Las Vegas, Nevada, Chase built her career across voice acting, film, and television from childhood into adulthood. Her work in Lilo and Stitch introduced her voice to a generation of children, while The Ring showcased her ability to handle darker, more challenging roles.

According to Hernandez, Chase had been struggling with serious health issues and personal difficulties. In a GoFundMe page set up before her death, he described how Chase had faced "a difficult childhood and a painful falling out with her family" and had been "bullied and struggled to find safety and happiness in downtown LA." Hernandez wrote that when they met, he promised to protect her and provide the love and comfort she deserved.

Meningitis is an inflammation of the protective membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord. When combined with blood infections, it can lead to sepsis, a life-threatening condition where the body's response to infection causes tissue damage and organ failure. The condition can progress rapidly and requires immediate medical treatment.

Chase's death represents a significant loss to the entertainment industry. She contributed to both family entertainment through her Disney voice work and horror cinema through The Ring. Her career demonstrated her versatility as a performer across multiple genres and mediums, from animated films to live-action television dramas. Those who knew her remember her contributions to popular culture during the early 2000s, when she was among the most recognizable young talents working in Hollywood.
