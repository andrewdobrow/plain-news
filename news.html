2:12 AM ET

A federal judge has struck down the Trump administration's $100,000 fee requirement for H-1B visa applications, ruling the policy unlawful under federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page ruling in a lawsuit brought by 20 Democratic state attorneys general who challenged the fee announced by Trump in September. The decision vacates the fee entirely, which represented a 20-to-50 fold increase over existing application costs.

The H-1B visa program allows U.S. employers to temporarily hire foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring advanced knowledge and skills. The program maintains an annual cap of 85,000 visas, with 20,000 reserved for workers holding advanced degrees from U.S. institutions. Technology companies, healthcare organizations, and other employers across multiple industries rely on the program to recruit talent in fields including engineering, information technology, mathematics, and medicine.

The Trump administration introduced the substantial fee as part of broader efforts to reshape the nation's legal immigration approach. Officials framed the policy as a way to prevent foreign workers from taking American jobs. However, critics argued the dramatic fee increase would make the program inaccessible to many legitimate employers and reduce the pool of skilled workers available to U.S. companies.

The ruling eliminates the fee requirement, allowing employers to continue applying for H-1B visas under the previous, significantly lower fee structure. This decision restores access to the program for a wider range of companies that had faced barriers under the higher cost.

The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the judge's decision. The case represents one of several legal challenges the administration has faced regarding its immigration policies.

The decision also addresses broader questions about visa program accessibility and the role of skilled foreign workers in the American economy. Employers who have already paid the $100,000 fee may pursue refunds, though the legal process for recovering those payments remains to be determined.

Judge Sorokin found that the fee violated federal administrative procedures and constitutional requirements. The 20 state attorneys general who brought the challenge argued the policy exceeded executive authority and would harm American competitiveness by restricting employers' ability to hire qualified specialists from abroad.

The ruling comes as the Trump administration continues implementing significant changes to immigration policy through executive actions. Legal experts anticipate further litigation over the administration's immigration initiatives as various groups challenge their implementation and scope.

1:08 AM ET

The Pentagon has designated several major Chinese technology and manufacturing companies as entities with alleged ties to China's military. The newly listed firms include electric vehicle manufacturer BYD, e-commerce platform Alibaba, search engine operator Baidu, and biotechnology company WuXi AppTec. The designation prevents these companies from securing U.S. defense contracts and signals to American businesses the potential national security risks of working with them.

The Pentagon maintains an official list identifying foreign companies operating in or doing business with the United States that allegedly have connections to foreign military organizations. Placement on this list triggers increased regulatory scrutiny and can discourage American firms from establishing business relationships with the flagged companies. The action reflects the Trump administration's ongoing concerns about technology transfer and national security in U.S.-China relations.

BYD has become one of the world's largest electric vehicle manufacturers and competes directly with American automakers in global markets. Alibaba operates major e-commerce platforms and provides cloud computing services that reach consumers throughout China. Baidu dominates China's search engine market and has invested significantly in artificial intelligence research and development. WuXi AppTec supplies drug development and manufacturing services to pharmaceutical companies worldwide.

The additions come amid escalating economic tensions between the United States and China over technology competition and national security. Previous Pentagon designations have targeted major Chinese telecommunications equipment makers and other technology firms. U.S. officials have raised concerns that Chinese companies might share sensitive technology or data with military organizations, creating potential vulnerabilities for American partners.

Being placed on the Pentagon list does not automatically impose sanctions or completely prohibit all business activities with American companies. Rather, the designation formally notifies U.S. firms that the government views these organizations as potential national security risks. The listing typically prompts American businesses to reassess their existing partnerships and review their supply chain relationships involving the named companies.

The listed companies retain the ability to contest their designation through administrative processes available under relevant regulations. However, the practical impact of the listing can be significant, as many American firms choose to distance themselves from flagged Chinese entities to avoid potential regulatory complications or reputational concerns.

The Trump administration released an updated version of this list four months earlier but quickly removed it without providing a public explanation. The current additions represent a renewed focus on designating Chinese companies the administration views as supporting China's military capabilities and interests.

Yesterday, 6:39 PM ET

Apple announced a major overhaul of its Siri digital assistant at its Worldwide Developers Conference, integrating the voice tool with Apple Intelligence and rebranding it as "Siri AI." The announcement marks a significant moment for the company, which has faced criticism for falling behind competitors in artificial intelligence development over the past several years.

The new Siri will function more like modern AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than serving as a traditional web-based question-and-answer tool. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained how the company rebuilt the assistant with generative AI at its core. The update represents a shift in Apple's approach to the technology, with Siri becoming more conversational and capable of pulling information from Apple's native app ecosystem across mobile devices and laptops.

During his presentation, Rockwell demonstrated Siri's new capabilities by showing a photo of a beach sunset and asking the assistant to identify the location. He then requested directions to a friend's house nearby and to Santa Cruz, California, all within the Siri interface rather than requiring switches between multiple applications. The assistant will also be able to proofread documents, help with shopping, add calendar events, and use the camera to retrieve information such as nutrition details from food photos. Initially, the tool will only be available in English, with a wider public release expected in the fall.

Siri's capabilities are powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a billion-dollar partnership between the two companies. According to Dipanjan Chatterjee, principal analyst at Forrester, a "fundamentally re-architected Siri" powered by Gemini would signal Apple's readiness to make AI the primary interface layer across its ecosystem.

Apple also introduced iOS 27, which includes a feature allowing users to remove the Now Playing widget from the Lock Screen. The operating system will support current iPhone models, though some older iPad models will lose compatibility. The company unveiled watchOS 27 with a dynamic app grid and new gesture controls, though the update drops support for several older Apple Watch models, supporting only Apple Watch Series 9 and newer devices.

Beyond Siri updates, Apple highlighted new child safety features designed to give parents greater control over their children's device usage. New safeguards limit content visibility, contact access, and app availability. A simplified Screen Time interface shows parents which apps their children use and for how long. Parents can also set daily time allowances or follow recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics, which Apple partnered with for these features.

The keynote emphasized Apple's measured approach to artificial intelligence. Craig Federighi, the company's senior vice-president of software engineering, stated that some competitors appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve."

Victor Wembanyama delivered his best performance of the series with 32 points, leading the San Antonio Spurs to a 115-111 victory over the New York Knicks in Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden. The win snapped New York's 13-game postseason winning streak and trimmed their series lead to 2-1.

Wembanyama's dominant display came after a disappointing Game 2 performance that left him searching for answers. "Really tried to relax after Game 2," he said. "The playoffs are like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water. I need some time off, let my brain cool down." He came out energized, scoring nine points in his first nine minutes and setting the tone for the rest of the night.

The 7-foot-4 French star added eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks to his scoring total. Stephon Castle contributed 23 points and hit two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining. For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson scored 32 points and OG Anunoby added 28, but their supporting cast struggled down the stretch. Outside of Brunson, New York shot just 3 for 20 from the field in the fourth quarter.

The Spurs built an 11-point lead in the first quarter before the Knicks responded by outscoring them 42-24 in the second quarter. The game remained closely contested throughout the third quarter, with neither team able to pull away significantly. The decisive fourth quarter saw the Spurs capitalize on turnovers, turning 13 Knicks mistakes into 21 points.

The atmosphere at Madison Square Garden added extra intrigue to the proceedings. President Donald Trump attended as the first sitting president ever to watch an NBA Finals game. He received heavy boos when his image appeared on the jumbotron during the national anthem. The event drew numerous celebrities to courtside, including Spike Lee, Timothée Chalamet and Ben Stiller, while Mayor Zohran Mamdani attended separately after purchasing a standing-room-only ticket.

The Knicks' frustration boiled over during the game, particularly regarding officiating calls. New York was whistled for three fouls in the first 64 seconds of the fourth quarter and took just eight free throws in the second half compared to San Antonio's 24. "Refs, you suck!" chants erupted multiple times throughout the evening.

Despite the loss, Brunson remained focused on improvement. "We've done our best to learn from wins over the past couple weeks," he said. "Now we have to learn from a loss. We knew there were things we were going to have to improve on going into the next game. So mindset stays the same."

Game 4 is scheduled for Wednesday night in New York.
