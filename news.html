Yesterday, 5:34 PM ET

Israeli ground forces advanced into central Gaza beyond their occupied zone on Wednesday to dismantle what the military described as a terror tunnel posing an immediate threat to troops. The operation, which took place west of the Yellow Line marking the boundary of the more than 60 percent of Gaza under Israeli control, prompted evacuation orders for residents near Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps. Hamas accused Israel of a flagrant violation of the October 2025 ceasefire agreement.

Witnesses reported tanks advancing east of Nuseirat refugee camp and moving onto the main north-south Salah al-Din road under artillery cover. The Israeli military said the targeted operation aimed to remove an immediate threat and prevent Hamas from harming Israeli civilians or troops. The military body Cogat warned Palestinians in the area to stay away for their own safety and announced the closure of a section of Salah al-Din road.

Footage verified by BBC Verify showed families fleeing near Nuseirat camp, with residents reporting indiscriminate gunfire and shelling. One displaced resident said Israeli forces fired randomly at areas sheltering displaced people, forcing families to leave behind all belongings. Gaza's Hamas-run Civil Defence agency reported three people killed in separate Israeli drone strikes on Wednesday, including one man in Nuseirat. The Israeli military told BBC it had struck several military operatives.

At least 1,658 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza since the ceasefire began last year, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry. More than 1.9 million people, about 90 percent of Gaza's population, remain displaced, with 1.4 million living in tents or makeshift shelters. Reconstruction has not begun in Gaza, where an estimated 82 percent of structures have been destroyed or damaged during the war. The disarmament of Hamas remains a key obstacle to advancing a U.S. peace plan.

Yesterday, 6:23 AM ET

Central Florida is under a flood watch through Friday as several more days of heavy rain threaten multiple counties already saturated by recent storms. The watch covers Volusia, Seminole, Orange, Osceola, Flagler, Okeechobee, Brevard, Indian River, St. Lucie, and Martin counties, with the heaviest additional rainfall forecast along coastal areas.

Over the past 24 hours, several communities recorded significant rainfall totals. Port Orange measured 3.32 inches, while Daytona Beach received 2.21 inches and Ponce Inlet recorded 2.18 inches. Cocoa and Viera saw 1.99 inches and 1.95 inches respectively. Wednesday's forecast called for periods of showers and thunderstorms, with heavier rain possible across portions of Volusia, Seminole, Orange, Osceola, and Brevard counties. Repeated rounds of rain increase the risk of localized flooding, particularly in areas that have already received substantial precipitation.

The weather system is expected to bring sustained winds between 15 and 25 miles per hour, with gusts reaching 30 miles per hour. Wednesday through Friday are being designated as severe weather warning days for coastal communities. Drier conditions are forecast to return Saturday, with rain chances dropping to 30 percent, followed by mostly sunny weather Sunday.

The flooding threat comes two weeks after Brevard County experienced more than 20 inches of rain in some areas. Palm Bay is still dealing with cleanup from that earlier storm system.

Yesterday, 2:09 PM ET

Nvidia interim chief executive Colette Kress told employees to move forward on Wednesday during the company's first all-hands meeting since founder Jensen Huang resigned, according to an attendee who spoke to CNBC. Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones and hyperscale and high-performance computing head Ian Buck, along with several other senior executives. The meeting lasted about an hour and was held in person and streamed on Microsoft Teams.

Kress opened with the message about moving forward and announced that the yearly review process would be moved up from March to December, when employees typically receive pay raises and stock grants. Jones discussed the search for the next chief executive. Nvidia shares are down 37 percent from their 52-week high.

Employees have spoken up on social media in recent days to call for the return of Huang as chief executive, and one senior employee reportedly sent a letter to the board warning Nvidia would lose irreplaceable talent if it does not consider reinstating Huang. One attendee said the general sentiment in the room was that employees want Huang to come back. Another person familiar with the matter disagreed, saying that employees welcomed the remarks from leadership and the visibility into company leadership.

Jones said during the earnings call on Tuesday that the Nvidia board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive. Some executives disagreed with the statement, with one employee questioning why Jones would make such a definitive statement while the investigation is still playing out. The meeting came one day after Huang said he was ready to return as chief executive should the board want him. Multiple employees said Huang's letter fired employees up. The executives did not answer questions during Wednesday's all-hands meeting.

The company will begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT. The pricing reflects strong investor appetite for the AI company despite recent market volatility following t...

Yesterday, 5:11 PM ET

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share, above its projected range of $70 to $78, valuing the artificial intelligence company at $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. The company will begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT.

The pricing reflects strong investor appetite for the AI company despite recent market volatility following the departure of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The $1.5 trillion valuation marks a significant increase from the $965 billion Anthropic reached in May when it raised $65 billion in its last private funding round, though it falls short of the $2 trillion the company reportedly sought several months ago.

Amazon and Google hold the largest stakes in Anthropic, owning nearly 15 percent and almost 14 percent of the company respectively. A group of major technology companies including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC Partners agreed to purchase $26 billion worth of non-voting shares through a private placement alongside the IPO. Among venture capital investors, Sequoia and former Google CEO Eric Schmidt each reportedly own approximately 1 percent of the company, as does Singaporean sovereign wealth fund GIC.

The IPO delivers a substantial windfall to Anthropic's numerous backers, though none of its venture capital investors holds more than a 5 percent stake. Each of the company's seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, sold nearly 3.4 million shares in the offering, bringing in about $282 million per founder according to the S-1 filing. The company's performance is expected to influence expectations for other private AI companies, including rival OpenAI, which was last valued at $1.25 billion and has not announced IPO plans.

Yesterday, 4:25 PM ET

Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr has asked the Southeastern Conference to take action against Louisiana State University over the school's decision to roster basketball player RJ Luis Jr., escalating a dispute over the former St. John's guard who signed contracts with NBA teams. Carr's letter accuses LSU of showing contempt for conference rules.

Luis, an All-American transfer, is at the center of a legal and administrative fight over his NCAA eligibility after signing professional contracts. LSU added Luis to its basketball roster despite the SEC's objections and an ongoing court battle over whether his prior NBA agreements disqualify him from collegiate competition. The conference has expressed frustration with LSU's actions.

The dispute has reached a point where some SEC members are considering boycotting games against LSU if head coach Will Wade plays Luis on the court, according to reports. The potential boycotts would represent an extraordinary escalation in the standoff between the conference and one of its member institutions.

The case tests NCAA rules governing player compensation and professional contracts at a time when college athletics is navigating rapidly changing policies around athlete earnings. LSU maintains it will keep Luis on its roster, while the SEC and state officials from other member states argue the university is violating eligibility standards that apply across the conference.

Cascio siblings sue Michael Jackson estate attorney for malpractice over 2019 abuse settlement

Five siblings who allege Michael Jackson sexually abused them as children have sued entertainment attorney Bryan Freedman for legal malpractice, claiming he deceived them into signing a 2019 agreement that silenced their abuse allegations while shielding Jackson's estate from liability. The lawsuit was filed Wednesday in Los Angeles County Superior Court. Frank Cascio and his...

Yesterday, 3:54 PM ET

Five siblings who allege Michael Jackson sexually abused them as children have sued entertainment attorney Bryan Freedman for legal malpractice, claiming he deceived them into signing a 2019 agreement that silenced their abuse allegations while shielding Jackson's estate from liability. The lawsuit was filed Wednesday in Los Angeles County Superior Court.

Frank Cascio and his siblings Dominic, Marie-Nicole, Edward, and Aldo allege Freedman exploited their vulnerability to secure the agreement after Jackson's estate sought to contain fallout from the documentary Leaving Neverland. The siblings claim Freedman, who was already representing two family members in unrelated matters, assured them he was acting in their best interests while discouraging them from hiring independent counsel. They say he failed to disclose provisions barring them from publicly discussing the alleged abuse and releasing the estate from liability related to it.

Under the agreement, the estate was to pay each sibling $690,000 annually for five years, with disputes subject to mandatory arbitration. The lawsuit alleges Freedman never explained the payment was inadequate compared to amounts Jackson and the estate paid other alleged victims, nor did he disclose a penalty provision requiring the entire family to return all payments if one member disclosed Jackson's alleged crimes. The siblings also claim Freedman later returned in 2024 calling the original agreement "toilet paper" and said the estate wanted to pay them more.

The siblings recognized a conflict of interest, hired new representation, and informed the estate they would challenge the agreement. They filed a child sex trafficking lawsuit in February that was ordered into arbitration in August. The new complaint against Freedman, filed by attorney Howard King, includes claims for breach of fiduciary duty, fraud, and negligent misrepresentation. A lawyer for Jackson's estate has previously called the siblings' allegations a "desperate money grab" and noted the family defended Jackson for more than 25 years.

Yesterday, 5:20 PM ET

Representative Laurel Lee announced Wednesday her bid to lead Republicans on the House Judiciary Committee, a position that would place her at the center of potential Democratic efforts to impeach President Donald Trump. The Florida Republican told colleagues in a letter that she understands the constitutional grounds for impeachment and can distinguish between policy disagreements and impeachable offenses.

Lee cited her background as a judge, federal prosecutor, and former Florida secretary of state as qualifications for the role. She said she is prepared to make distinctions about impeachment in committee hearings and explain them to the public. She also told colleagues she is equipped to respond to investigations that committee Democrats, led by incoming Chair Jamie Raskin of Maryland, have already indicated they plan to pursue.

If recommended by the House GOP Steering Committee, Lee would be among the few Republican women in leadership roles in the House. She was elected to Congress in 2022.

Lee is expected to compete against Representatives Lance Gooden of Texas and Scott Fitzgerald of Wisconsin for the position.
