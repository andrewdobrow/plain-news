US and Iran Exchange Military Strikes Over Gulf Region

The United States and Iran engaged in another round of military exchanges on Saturday, with American forces shooting down Iranian drones and striking radar installations, followed by Iranian attacks targeting bases in Kuwait and Bahrain.

Air raid sirens sounded in Bahrain as Iranian ballistic missiles and drones headed toward the Gulf states. Kuwait's military reported intercepting drones and missiles launched at the country. The Kuwaiti foreign ministry condemned the attacks as a "serious escalation" and "flagrant violation of its sovereignty," asserting its right to defend itself.

According to the US military's Central Command, Iranian attack drones posed an "immediate threat to regional maritime traffic." In response, American forces shot down four Iranian drones launched toward the Strait of Hormuz and struck Iranian coastal surveillance radar sites on Sirik and Qeshm Island to "defend against further attacks."

Hours later, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had targeted "enemy bases" in the Gulf. The IRGC claimed it fired seven ballistic missiles toward Kuwait and Bahrain. Centcom reported that six missiles were intercepted and a seventh did not reach its target. The command stated there were no reports of harm to US personnel and dismissed Iranian claims of damaging the US Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain as false.

The escalation marks the latest in a series of tense military encounters that threaten the fragile ceasefire between the two nations. Diplomatic negotiations aimed at ending the conflict have stalled, with both countries calling for changes to any agreement without showing willingness to compromise. US and Iranian negotiators have spent weeks attempting to extend a 60-day ceasefire and initiate new talks on Iran's nuclear program.

President Donald Trump told reporters on Friday that "the situation with Iran seems to be going quite well," while simultaneously stating the US would resolve the matter "very quickly" either through a deal or "the very tough way." Trump faces pressure to end the conflict before midterm elections, citing its negative impact on markets and domestic popularity.

The military exchanges also coincide with regional complications. A ceasefire agreement between Israel and Lebanon's government was announced this week following US-brokered talks, though Iranian-backed Hezbollah rejected the deal. Israeli airstrikes killed nine people in Lebanon on Saturday, including three army soldiers, prompting Lebanon's president to call the strike a "flagrant violation" of sovereignty.

Iran has demanded that any lasting truce extend to Lebanon, complicating efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end broader regional conflict. The continued military tensions have economic ripple effects, with the World Food Programme warning that millions face increased hunger due to soaring energy and food prices connected to the conflict.

OpenAI Delays Adult Mode for ChatGPT, Faces Internal Criticism Over Pentagon Deal

OpenAI has postponed the launch of "adult mode" for ChatGPT, shifting focus to what the company considers more urgent priorities. The delay comes as the startup manages competing demands from users, investors, and its own workforce.

Sam Altman announced last October that OpenAI would allow adult content on ChatGPT and implement age verification systems. The company has now reversed course on that timeline. "We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive," OpenAI said in a statement.

The company, which has more than 900 million ChatGPT users, acknowledged the principle behind the original plan. "We still believe in the principle of treating adults like adults, but getting the experience right will take more time," the statement continued.

Instead of rolling out adult mode, OpenAI is implementing age prediction tools that identify users under 18. When underage users are detected, stricter safety settings activate automatically, limiting exposure to graphic violence and sexual content. In the UK, if ChatGPT generates pornographic images deemed illegal under the Online Safety Act, OpenAI must use age verification to prevent minors from accessing that material.

The shift in priorities reflects pressure Altman has faced since declaring a "code red" to improve ChatGPT. The startup is competing intensely with Google, Anthropic, and other AI companies developing rival systems.

The delay announcement comes as OpenAI navigates internal dissent over a recently announced Pentagon contract. Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over the partnership, citing concerns about mass surveillance and autonomous weapons development.

"AI has an important role in national security. But surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got," Kalinowski wrote on X.

Kalinowski emphasized her objection centered on execution speed. "To be clear, my issue is that the announcement was rushed without the guardrails defined. It's a governance concern first and foremost. These are too important for deals or announcements to be rushed," she added.

OpenAI had moved quickly with the Pentagon contract after Anthropic, the previous AI contractor, was dropped from the position. Altman later acknowledged the deal appeared "opportunistic and sloppy."

Following the resignation and criticism, OpenAI amended its Pentagon agreement to explicitly exclude domestic mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. An OpenAI spokesperson stated the company believes the updated agreement creates a responsible path for national security applications of AI while maintaining clear boundaries.

"We recognise that people have strong views about these issues and we will continue to engage in discussion with employees, government, civil society and communities around the world," the company said.

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles on Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said.

Head portrayed the librarian and mentor Giles throughout Buffy the Vampire Slayer's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003, alongside Sarah Michelle Gellar. The role made him a beloved figure among fans of the supernatural drama series. Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute to her former co-star on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her Buffy character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Born in Camden, London, Head was raised by artistic parents. His father founded Verity Films, a documentary production company, while his mother was an actor who played Madame Maigret in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor known for his lead role in 1971's Sunday Bloody Sunday.

Head began his professional career on stage, starring in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s, he appeared in various projects including the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and A Prayer for the Dying, which featured Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. He also performed as Freddie Trumper in the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess and played Dr. Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show.

Head gained significant recognition in the UK and US for his role in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements during the 1980s, which featured a slow-burn courtship between a British couple.

During Buffy's production, Head commuted between Los Angeles and England, where his partner Sarah and their two young daughters lived. The production team adjusted his schedule to allow him to return home every three or four weeks.

After Buffy ended in 2003, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain, where he played a British prime minister. He also appeared in the BBC production of Merlin and the film The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played former football club owner Rupert Mannion in Apple TV Plus's Ted Lasso, where he starred alongside Jason Sudeikis.

His daughters said in their statement that Head "loved his job very much" and considered himself "incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people."
