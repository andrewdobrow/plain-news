Yesterday, 8:02 PM ET

Consumer prices in the United States are rising at the fastest rate in three years, according to new economic data released this week. The personal consumption expenditures price index jumped 3.8% in the 12 months through April, marking the largest increase since May 2023.

The acceleration in inflation reflects multiple pressures on the economy. The Middle East conflict has disrupted shipping routes and global supply chains, pushing energy prices higher. The national average retail gasoline price shot up 12.3% in April alone, with gasoline prices climbing more than 50% since the war began at the end of February. Beyond energy, consumers are also paying more for food, goods, and services across the board.

Inflation was already elevated before the war, largely due to Trump's sweeping import duties. Goods prices increased 0.7% in April, with food prices rebounding 0.5%. Services prices, including housing, utilities, and food services, also rose. Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy components, increased 3.3% year-on-year in April after rising 3.2% in March.

The rising prices are taking a toll on American households. Real income at the disposal of households, adjusted for inflation, dropped for a third straight month in April. Consumer spending patterns are shifting as families adjust budgets to accommodate higher costs. Consumers are depleting savings to maintain spending levels, with the saving rate dropping to 2.6% in April, the lowest level since June 2022.

The inflation surge comes as President Trump faces growing political challenges. Americans are increasingly frustrated with his handling of the economy. A Reuters and Ipsos survey showed the president's approval rating fell to nearly its lowest level since returning to the White House, hurt by a drop in support among Republicans. Trump won the 2024 presidential election largely on his promise to lower inflation.

The weak economic data extends beyond inflation. Overall gross domestic product growth was revised down to 1.6% in the first quarter from the previously estimated 2.0% pace. Consumer spending growth, which accounts for more than two-thirds of economic activity, was also revised downward.

The Federal Reserve will likely monitor inflation closely as it considers monetary policy decisions. However, policymakers face a difficult situation. Economic analysts note that while the Fed cannot fix a supply shock from the Middle East conflict, it cannot ignore one that is feeding into underlying inflation. Financial markets expect the Fed will hold its benchmark overnight interest rate steady well into 2027.

The combination of high inflation, slowing growth, and declining real incomes poses challenges for both households and policymakers as economic uncertainty persists.

Yesterday, 6:52 PM ET

Apple unveiled a substantially redesigned Siri at its annual developer conference, marking a major shift in the voice assistant's capabilities. The new Siri, rebranded as "Siri AI," integrates with Apple Intelligence and will be widely available in the fall.

The revamped assistant represents a fundamental reconception of how Siri functions. Rather than operating as a web-based question-and-answer tool, the updated version mimics modern AI chatbots like ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, demonstrated the assistant's new conversational abilities during the conference, showing how it can work across Apple's native app ecosystem to handle tasks like planning, navigation and information retrieval.

A key advancement enables Siri to operate within a single interface rather than shuffling between multiple apps. In a demonstration, Rockwell showed Siri identifying a beach location from a photo, retrieving a friend's address from the contact app, and providing directions to the beach with a stop at the friend's house, all without leaving the Siri interface.

The new Siri will feature its own dedicated app and can handle expanded functionality including proofreading, shopping, calendar management and extracting information from images using the camera. Initially, English will be the only supported language.

Apple achieved these improvements through a partnership with Google, leveraging Google's Gemini AI model. According to Dipanjan Chatterjee, a principal analyst at Forrester, this represents a significant moment for Apple. He noted that "a fundamentally re-architected Siri inside iOS 27 with a little help from Google's Gemini would represent more than an update – it would signal Apple's readiness to make AI the primary interface layer across its ecosystem."

The update comes after years of delays and false starts with Siri improvements. Apple had previously postponed releasing an updated version first announced in 2024, as its AI development lagged competitors. The company faced additional pressure from a 250 million dollar settlement in May following a class-action lawsuit alleging false advertising of Siri's AI capabilities.

Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, acknowledged during the conference that some companies appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people." This framing positions Apple's approach as more deliberately integrated into existing products rather than pursuing AI as a standalone technology.

Beyond Siri, Apple announced new AI features across Safari, messaging and the Home app, alongside camera and photo tools developed with Google. The company also introduced expanded child safety features allowing parents greater control over content access and app usage through an updated setup assistant and monitoring tools.

The atmosphere at Madison Square Garden reflected the magnitude of the occasion. The game marked New York's first NBA Finals contest at home since 1999. Notable attendees included President Donald Trump, who received heavy boos when shown on the jumbotron during the national anthem. Wembanyama and his teammates must regroup quickly to extend the series and avoid squandering their home court advantage.
