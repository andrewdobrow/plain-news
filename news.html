Yesterday, 4:32 PM ET

OpenAI released a new security feature called Lockdown Mode designed to protect sensitive data from prompt injection attacks. The feature aims to reduce the likelihood that ChatGPT will share confidential information when attackers attempt to manipulate the system through carefully crafted prompts.

Prompt injection attacks exploit how AI systems process instructions by inserting malicious commands that can cause the model to behave in unintended ways. These attacks pose particular risks when ChatGPT processes sensitive business data or personal information. An attacker might embed hidden instructions in documents or messages that trick the AI into revealing confidential details it should keep private.

Lockdown Mode represents OpenAI's response to this growing security concern as more organizations integrate ChatGPT into workflows that handle proprietary or personal data. The company acknowledged that even with the new protection, ChatGPT remains potentially vulnerable to prompt injections. The goal is not to eliminate the risk entirely but to make it significantly harder for attackers to successfully extract sensitive information through these techniques.

The security enhancement comes as AI systems face increasing scrutiny over data protection and privacy. Companies deploying ChatGPT for customer service, document processing, and internal communications need assurance that the system will not inadvertently leak confidential information. Prompt injection has emerged as one of the more concerning attack vectors because it exploits the fundamental way large language models interpret and follow instructions.

OpenAI did not specify the exact mechanisms Lockdown Mode uses to detect and block prompt injection attempts. The feature adds another layer to the company's security infrastructure as it continues to expand ChatGPT's enterprise and business applications where data protection requirements are strictest.

Yesterday, 5:50 PM ET

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said.

Head played Giles, the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's Buffy Summers, throughout the show's seven seasons from 1997 to 2003. The character became a fan favorite for his guidance and dry British wit, helping define the supernatural drama's mix of horror and heart that made it a cultural touchstone of the late 1990s and early 2000s.

Beyond Buffy, Head's career spanned decades across television and film. He appeared in the BBC sketch show Little Britain, the BBC production of Merlin, and films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in the acclaimed Apple TV+ series Ted Lasso, where he regularly appeared alongside co-creator Jason Sudeikis and earned praise for their on-screen chemistry.

Head's early career included stage work, beginning with the 1978 West End revival of Godspell. In the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in various theatrical productions including Chess and The Rocky Horror Show. He also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album.

Before his rise to international fame, Head was well-known in the UK and US for appearing in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements, which featured a slow-burn courtship between a British couple.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actor who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor.

During Buffy's production, Head commuted between Los Angeles and England, where his partner Sarah and two young daughters lived. "I'd try to go home to them every three or four weeks," he told the Guardian in 2016. "Every time I got the chance to have six days clear, I'd get on a plane."

Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute to Head on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her Buffy character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

His daughters noted that his legacy will live on through the shows he appeared in and the audiences who loved them.
