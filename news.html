A few minutes ago

A federal judge has invalidated the Trump administration's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, ruling that the policy violated federal administrative law and the Constitution.

U.S. District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page ruling in response to a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general. The decision struck down a fee that represented a dramatic increase from previous H-1B visa costs, amounting to a 20-to-50 fold increase on existing rates. The Trump administration announced the fee in September as a way to prevent foreign workers from taking American jobs.

The H-1B visa program allows U.S. companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring theoretical or technical expertise. The program is widely used by technology companies and other industries facing skilled worker shortages. By imposing such a substantial financial burden on companies seeking to hire foreign talent, the administration aimed to discourage the practice and create more opportunities for domestic workers.

Critics of the H-1B program have long argued that it allows companies to bypass qualified American workers in favor of cheaper foreign labor. Supporters of the administration's policy saw the fee as a tool to reshape hiring practices and prioritize American workers in the labor market.

However, the court found the policy legally insufficient to stand. Judge Sorokin determined that the fee functioned as an unlawful tax that violated federal administrative procedures. The ruling vacates the sweeping fee entirely, removing a major obstacle for companies that rely on foreign talent to fill specialized positions.

The decision affects how businesses can staff roles requiring specific technical skills and expertise that may be in short supply among the domestic workforce. It also has implications for foreign workers who had been seeking employment opportunities in the United States through the H-1B program.

The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the judge's decision. The legal challenge underscores ongoing tensions between immigration policy and business interests. While the administration sought to use the fee as a tool to restrict various immigration pathways and reshape the labor market through executive action, the court blocked the approach.

The ruling represents a setback for the broader administration efforts to restrict immigration. It demonstrates how executive actions on immigration policy face legal scrutiny when they conflict with established administrative law and constitutional principles. The case illustrates the continuing debate over how to balance business needs for specialized foreign workers with concerns about protecting domestic employment opportunities.

Yesterday, 11:55 PM ET

The Pentagon has expanded its roster of Chinese companies deemed to have connections to the Chinese military, adding major technology firms including Alibaba, electric vehicle manufacturer BYD, and search engine operator Baidu. The designation serves as a cautionary signal to American businesses and investors about potential national security risks linked to these organizations.

The updated list also includes biotech firm WuXi AppTec. While inclusion on the Pentagon's registry does not immediately trigger legal restrictions, it effectively warns U.S. companies about the dangers of engaging in business partnerships or investments with these flagged enterprises.

The three major companies added represent some of China's most prominent global players. Alibaba operates one of the world's largest e-commerce platforms alongside substantial cloud computing operations. BYD has emerged as a leading electric vehicle manufacturer with a growing international presence. Baidu controls the dominant search engine in China and has made significant investments in artificial intelligence and autonomous vehicle technology. Their addition marks a notable shift in the Pentagon's approach, expanding scrutiny beyond traditional military contractors to encompass major commercial technology firms.

The timing reflects escalating tensions between the United States and China. American policymakers have increasingly prioritized restricting Chinese access to cutting-edge technologies, particularly in semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and electric vehicles. Chinese officials have previously rejected such designations, characterizing them as politically motivated efforts designed to disadvantage Chinese companies competing in global markets.

The broader context for these moves involves growing concerns about technological dependencies and national security vulnerabilities. According to a report examining European reliance on Chinese green technology, the continent faces serious risks from over-dependence on Chinese suppliers across multiple sectors. China currently supplies approximately 98 percent of Europe's solar panels, 88 percent of its lithium-ion battery imports, and 61 percent of its inverters.

The report highlighted several potential threats stemming from such dependencies. These include the possibility of China using remote control mechanisms to disable critical infrastructure like solar panels or power grids, though experts characterized such a scenario as unlikely outside of wartime conditions. More probable risks identified in the analysis include supply chain disruptions and erosion of Western industrial competitiveness as China dominates key green technology sectors.

The report also noted that the defense sector relies on many of the same components and manufacturing techniques as green technology, meaning increased dependency on Chinese suppliers could extend into military applications. As China's role in global energy systems expands, its influence over international geopolitical dynamics would correspondingly increase.

The Pentagon's expanded list is expected to generate responses from Beijing and could further complicate ongoing trade negotiations and diplomatic discussions between Washington and China. The designation reflects broader American efforts to limit Chinese technological advancement and maintain strategic advantages in emerging technologies critical to future economic and military capabilities.

Yesterday, 11:49 PM ET

Apple introduced a significantly redesigned Siri powered by artificial intelligence at its Worldwide Developers Conference, marking a major overhaul of the digital assistant that has faced years of criticism for lagging behind competitors. The new Siri, called Siri AI, will be integrated with Apple Intelligence and operate more like modern AI chatbots such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini rather than a traditional search tool.

Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained that the company rebuilt the assistant with generative AI at its core. The new Siri will feature its own dedicated app and become more conversational, pulling from Apple's native app ecosystem across mobile devices and laptops to assist with daily planning, information retrieval, and directions. In a demonstration, Rockwell showed how Siri could identify a beach location from a photo, retrieve a friend's address, and provide directions with a stop at the friend's house, all within a single interface.

The upgrade represents Apple's response to falling behind competitors in the AI race. "Some appear to be racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it ultimately meant to serve," said Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering. The new Siri will launch in the fall and initially be available only in English, with capabilities including proofreading, shopping, calendar management, and using the camera to extract nutrition information from food images.

Apple partnered with Google to power Siri AI through Google's Gemini model in a billion-dollar deal. Dipanjan Chatterjee, a principal analyst at Forrester, noted that "a fundamentally re-architected Siri inside iOS 27 with a little help from Google's Gemini would represent more than an update. It would signal Apple's readiness to make AI the primary interface layer across its ecosystem."

Beyond Siri, Apple announced iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27 Golden Gate, and watchOS 27, all incorporating new AI features across Safari, messaging, the Home app, and camera and photo tools. The company also expanded its AI integration into child safety, introducing new parental controls that limit content exposure, monitor messages for inappropriate material, and allow parents to set app usage limits based on recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics.

The announcements come after Apple postponed previous Siri updates and faced a settlement in May over false advertising claims about the assistant's AI capabilities, which resulted in the company paying $250 million in a class-action lawsuit. Tech stocks declined following the announcements, reflecting broader investor concerns about AI spending across the technology sector.

Victor Wembanyama delivered his best performance of the series as the San Antonio Spurs defeated the New York Knicks 115-111 in Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden. The victory cut New York's series lead to 2-1 and ended the Knicks' 13-game postseason winning streak.

Wembanyama finished with 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks. Stephon Castle added 23 points and hit two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining. For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson scored 32 points and OG Anunoby contributed 28, but the rest of New York's roster struggled in the fourth quarter, combining to shoot just 3 for 20 from the field.

The atmosphere at Madison Square Garden added significant intensity to the proceedings. The venue hosted its first NBA Finals game since 1999, with ticket prices exceeding $10,000 and heightened security measures in place. Donald Trump attended as a guest of team owner James Dolan and was heavily booed when shown on the jumbotron during the national anthem. Mayor Zohran Mamdani attended separately after purchasing a standing-room-only ticket for approximately $1,000. Celebrities including Spike Lee, Timothée Chalamet, Ben Stiller, Tina Fey, Tracy Morgan and Larry David occupied courtside seats.

Wembanyama had struggled in the first two games, including missing a potential game-winner in San Antonio. He approached Game 3 with renewed energy, scoring nine points in his first nine minutes and setting an early tone. "Really tried to relax after Game 2," he said. "The playoffs, it's like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water."

The Spurs raced to an 11-point first-quarter lead before the Knicks responded, outscoring San Antonio 42-24 in the second quarter. The teams traded momentum throughout, with neither team managing to build a commanding lead in the third quarter.

The fourth quarter proved decisive. Brunson kept New York competitive with 12 points in the final period, but the Knicks struggled elsewhere. The Spurs benefited from New York's turnovers, scoring 21 points off 13 miscues. The Knicks took just eight free throws in the second half compared to San Antonio's 24, frustrating the crowd. Fans chanted "refs, you suck!" multiple times throughout the evening.

Outside of Wembanyama and Castle, the Spurs leaned on contributions from their bench and the Knicks' mistakes. Karl-Anthony Towns had a quiet night with 11 points and eight rebounds after consecutive double-doubles in San Antonio.

Game 4 is scheduled for Wednesday in New York. The Knicks still hold the advantage, needing just two more victories to claim the championship, while the Spurs have extended what could become a lengthy Finals series.

The race itself proved unusually competitive for California's Democratic stronghold. Steyer spent more than $200 million of his personal fortune on advertising but failed to secure a top-two finish.

The primary season saw dramatic shifts in momentum. Early in the contest, the race appeared it could produce two Republican nominees. However, Democrat Eric Swalwell's campaign collapsed following allegations of sexual assault and misconduct, which he has denied. He subsequently ended his gubernatorial bid and resigned from Congress.

Becerra faced significant headwinds during his campaign, including a viral video of him attempting to direct a TV reporter's interview and criticism from former Biden administration officials regarding his management style. He also faced scrutiny over his position on single-payer healthcare. Despite these challenges, he steadily gained support from Democratic voters as election day approached.

The general election winner will replace Governor Gavin Newsom, who is term-limited. A matchup between Becerra and Hilton sets up a contest where the Republican nominee must overcome substantial voter registration disadvantages and the fact that Trump remains deeply unpopular in California. Running with Trump's endorsement adds complexity to Hilton's path to victory.

As of the count's conclusion, election officials continued processing ballots to determine final placements. Many Democratic voters waited until the final days to cast ballots, causing late-arriving mail-in votes to still be counted as results were finalized.
