Apple unveiled a significant overhaul to its Siri voice assistant at its annual Worldwide Developers Conference, marking a major shift for the company's approach to artificial intelligence. The new Siri, branded as "Siri AI," will be integrated with Apple Intelligence and rolled out to users in the fall.

Tim Cook highlighted the Siri improvements during his final WWDC keynote, positioning the upgrade as a response to years of user frustration with the assistant's limitations. The revamped Siri will function more like advanced AI chatbots such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini rather than a traditional search tool that pulls information from the web.

The announcement represents a departure from Apple's historical practice of developing core technologies internally. The company focused on practical improvements to Siri rather than presenting flashy new features, though the underlying AI technology represents a substantial advancement.

Apple introduced its third generation of foundation models, which will power the new Siri capabilities and other features in iOS 27. These models are designed to handle a broader range of tasks and understand more complex user requests than previous versions. The foundation models were developed using infrastructure and expertise that goes beyond Apple's own research teams, signaling the company's recognition that advanced AI development increasingly requires external collaboration.

The new Siri will be available as part of iOS 27, Apple's next mobile operating system. Developers will gain access to testing versions of the software before a public release later this year. The company published technical details about the foundation models alongside the keynote announcement.

In addition to the Siri upgrades, Apple revealed a series of new child safety features amid ongoing scrutiny over apps that create sexual images of children. These safety features address concerns raised by UK Prime Minister Starmer, who called on Apple and Google to activate built-in protections that prevent children from accessing sexually explicit material on their devices.

The company also emphasized its traditional approach of prioritizing privacy and on-device processing, even as it pursues partnerships to accelerate AI development. This combination of external collaboration and privacy-focused design reflects Apple's attempt to balance rapid AI advancement with user protection.

The Siri AI announcement comes as technology companies compete to integrate increasingly powerful artificial intelligence into consumer products. Apple has faced questions in recent years about whether it is keeping pace with competitors in AI capabilities. The Siri improvements and related iOS 27 announcements suggest Apple is working to address these concerns while maintaining its distinctive approach to technology development.

GSK to Acquire Cancer Drugmaker Nuvalent for $10.6 Billion

British pharmaceutical company GSK has agreed to buy Nuvalent, a Boston-based cancer biotech firm, for $10.6 billion in what marks the company's largest acquisition ever. GSK will pay $124 per share in cash, sending Nuvalent shares surging 39 percent in premarket trading. The deal represents a significant expansion of GSK's oncology portfolio and reflects new chief executive L...

4:35 AM ET

British pharmaceutical company GSK has agreed to buy Nuvalent, a Boston-based cancer biotech firm, for $10.6 billion in what marks the company's largest acquisition ever. GSK will pay $124 per share in cash, sending Nuvalent shares surging 39 percent in premarket trading.

The deal represents a significant expansion of GSK's oncology portfolio and reflects new chief executive Luke Miels' aggressive push into cancer treatments. Miels, who took over at the start of the year, described the acquisition as part of a "brick-by-brick approach" to strengthen the company's drug pipeline.

Nuvalent specializes in developing precision oncology treatments, particularly for lung cancer. The acquisition gives GSK access to two late-stage drugs with major commercial potential. Zidesamtinib and neladalkib are being reviewed by the US Food and Drug Administration, with decisions expected in September and November. Both drugs target non-small cell lung cancer and are expected to launch later this year if approved. Analysts project these could become multi-blockbuster drugs, each generating several billion dollars annually.

The treatments target specific genetic mutations that drive lung cancer in non-smoking adults aged 40 to 50, predominantly women, affecting approximately 4,000 people in the US. Both drugs aim to provide longer effective treatment with improved tolerability and quality of life compared to current options. Miels noted that existing lung cancer treatments require seven to eight years of therapy and often cause patients to gain significant weight. "These are relatively young people. They've got kids, they lead active lives," he said. "Clearly there's a gap for a much better-tolerated product."

Nuvalent was founded in 2017 by Matthew Shair, a Harvard chemistry professor, and went public on the Nasdaq in 2021. Shair holds a 2.16 percent stake and is in line to receive just under $200 million from the sale. Deerfield Management, a New York-based healthcare investment firm, is the company's largest shareholder.

The deal was more than a year in the making, according to Miels, who praised Nuvalent's scientists as an "impressive group of people." GSK also expects to gain a platform for rapid expansion in lung cancer through a pipeline drug called Ris-Rez, which is in late-stage development and targets multiple cancer forms.

This acquisition follows GSK's earlier deal in January to buy California biotech company RAPT for $2.2 billion, which is developing a treatment for severe food allergies. The Nuvalent purchase positions GSK to improve profit contributions from 2027 and supports the company's target of more than 40 billion pounds in annual sales by 2031.
