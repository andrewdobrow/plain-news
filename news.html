10:30 AM ET

The Department of Justice has approved the $111 billion merger between Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery, removing a major regulatory obstacle for one of the entertainment industry's largest consolidations. The decision came after an eight-month investigation by the Justice Department's antitrust division, which examined whether the combination would harm competition.

The combined company will bring together Paramount's assets, including CBS, Paramount Pictures, and Paramount+, with Warner Bros. Discovery's properties such as HBO, Max, CNN, and the Warner Bros. film studio. According to the Justice Department, "the transaction is not likely to result in harm to competition or American consumers" in streaming video, linear television, or theatrical film production and distribution.

The merger represents a significant industry bet that scale will prove essential in the streaming era. Both companies have faced competitive pressures as they compete against dominant technology platforms. The combined entity is expected to pursue approximately $6 billion in cost savings through operational synergies.

However, the deal still faces additional regulatory hurdles. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened its own investigation with a July deadline to determine whether a more thorough review is needed. European regulators are also examining the funding behind the merger, which includes $24 billion in commitments from Gulf-based sovereign wealth funds. Australia has already approved the transaction.

The merger has generated significant opposition from media critics and politicians. Senator Elizabeth Warren called the approval "terrible news for every American who doesn't want Trump-aligned billionaires to control what they watch and how much they pay." Craig Aaron, co-chief executive of Free Press, stated that the investigation appeared predetermined, saying "the fix was in at the Trump Justice Department from the start."

Journalists at CBS News and CNN have expressed particular concerns about potential integration of the two newsrooms, which would likely result in significant job cuts given the promised cost savings. Some CNN staff have worried about editorial direction changes following the merger. David Ellison, who will control the combined company, pledged in March that CNN's editorial independence would be protected.

California's attorney general, Rob Bonta, indicated his office continues investigating the deal. A coalition of state attorneys general could still file a lawsuit to block the merger in coming weeks, potentially led by Bonta's office.

Paramount defended the merger in a statement, calling it "pro-competitive" and saying it would create "a stronger company better positioned to compete against dominant technology platforms." The company expressed commitment to completing the transaction while the remaining regulatory reviews proceed.

8:10 PM ET

Workers removed Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center's facade in Washington, D.C., after the performing arts venue missed a federal judge's deadline to complete the task. The removal began in the early hours of Saturday, following a judge's rejection of an emergency appeal to block the work.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper had ordered the Kennedy Center to remove all references to "Trump Kennedy Center" by Friday at 11:59 p.m. local time. Crews had erected scaffolding on Friday as onlookers gathered into the evening, though storms delayed the removal work until the following morning. The actual removal then proceeded behind a screen.

The building's exterior had displayed "The Donald J Trump and The John F Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts" as of Monday afternoon, before the work began. Following the removal, the facility will be known as "The John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts" or simply the "Kennedy Center."

In his 94-page ruling, Judge Cooper determined that the Kennedy Center's organic statute makes clear the institution is named for President Kennedy and cannot bear any other formal name or public memorial without congressional action. "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it," Cooper wrote.

The judge's order also addressed a broader dispute over the venue's governance. Trump's handpicked board had previously approved a $257 million "revitalization project" that would have closed the center for two years. Cooper temporarily blocked this renovation plan as part of his decision.

The Kennedy Center's general counsel issued a memo directing employees to immediately change email signatures, letterheads, and other documents to remove Trump's name. The deadline for completing changes to templates, forms, signage, brochures, and website pages was set for June 12, 2026.

The removal of Trump's name represents a significant change to the visual identity of one of Washington's most recognizable landmarks along the city's monumental core. The Kennedy Center operates as a federally chartered organization that also receives private donations and functions as the nation's cultural center and memorial to President John F. Kennedy.

Following the judge's decision, Trump responded with a lengthy statement on social media, expressing his position on the matter and the legal proceedings. The dispute centered on questions about the institution's authority to modify naming agreements and building signage based on its governing structure and governance guidelines.

"Iran no longer want a Nuclear Weapon, nor will they have one, either through purchase, development, or any other form of procurement," Trump said in his announcement. He added that the strait would open to all shipping immediately after the agreement is signed and suggested that the US relationship with Iran under his administration would be "much different and better one than previous Administrations have had."

However, Iran's foreign ministry cast doubt on the timeline. Esmaeil Baghaei, an Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson, cautioned that an exact signing date had not been finalized. "We will have to wait and see about the exact date of the signing of the memorandum of understanding, although it will not be tomorrow," Baghaei said. He noted that signing could potentially happen in the coming days.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed optimism about the negotiations, saying Islamabad was preparing for an electronic signing within 24 hours to be followed by technical-level talks. "We are closer to a peace deal than ever before," Sharif wrote on social media.

The two countries and mediators have been discussing a preliminary peace agreement to end the three-month conflict in the Middle East. However, there are significant disagreements about what the deal contains. According to Iran's semi-official Mehr news agency, the agreement would end conflict on all fronts including Lebanon, release 24 billion dollars of Iran's frozen assets, allow Iran to charge fees for passage through the strait, and suspend sanctions on Iran's oil sales. The agreement would establish a 60-day period for nuclear negotiations.

US officials provided a different account, stating that the deal requires Iran's nuclear material to be destroyed and its nuclear program dismantled. They said Iran's frozen assets would not be released until it meets certain demands and that Iran must stop supporting militant movements in the Middle East.

Trump has repeatedly claimed over the past three months that a deal was imminent. He reserved what he called "the ultimate alternative" if Iran refuses to sign, suggesting continued military action remains possible.

The announcement marks a potential turning point in decades of hostility between Washington and Tehran. The framework addresses multiple sources of tension, including Iran's nuclear program and control of the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquid gas supplies previously passed before Iran closed it to most shipping.

President Trump announced on Saturday that the United States and Iran plan to sign a peace agreement on Sunday. In a post on Truth Social, Trump stated that the deal would prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping.

"Iran no longer want a Nuclear Weapon, nor will they have one, either through purchase, development, or any other form of procurement," Trump said in his announcement. He added that the strait would open to all shipping immediately after the agreement is signed and suggested that the US relationship with Iran under his administration would be "much different and better one than previous Administrations have had."

However, Iran's foreign ministry cast doubt on the timeline. Esmaeil Baghaei, an Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson, cautioned that an exact signing date had not been finalized. "We will have to wait and see about the exact date of the signing of the memorandum of understanding, although it will not be tomorrow," Baghaei said. He noted that signing could potentially happen in the coming days.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed optimism about the negotiations, saying Islamabad was preparing for an electronic signing within 24 hours to be followed by technical-level talks. "We are closer to a peace deal than ever before," Sharif wrote on social media.

The two countries and mediators have been discussing a preliminary peace agreement to end the three-month conflict in the Middle East. However, there are significant disagreements about what the deal contains. According to Iran's semi-official Mehr news agency, the agreement would end conflict on all fronts including Lebanon, release 24 billion dollars of Iran's frozen assets, allow Iran to charge fees for passage through the strait, and suspend sanctions on Iran's oil sales. The agreement would establish a 60-day period for nuclear negotiations.

US officials provided a different account, stating that the deal requires Iran's nuclear material to be destroyed and its nuclear program dismantled. They said Iran's frozen assets would not be released until it meets certain demands and that Iran must stop supporting militant movements in the Middle East.

Trump has repeatedly claimed over the past three months that a deal was imminent. He reserved what he called "the ultimate alternative" if Iran refuses to sign, suggesting continued military action remains possible.

The announcement marks a potential turning point in decades of hostility between Washington and Tehran. The framework addresses multiple sources of tension, including Iran's nuclear program and control of the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquid gas supplies previously passed before Iran closed it to most shipping.
