Canada has banned cattle imports from Texas after a second calf infected with flesh-eating screwworms was identified in the state. Texas has declared a state of disaster in response to the outbreak.

The new world screwworm fly is a parasitic insect whose larvae feed on the blood and flesh of warm-blooded animals. The parasite was eradicated from the United States in the 1970s after causing tens of millions of dollars in losses to the cattle industry. It has since remained present in parts of Central and South America, particularly Mexico, where it has been advancing northward for more than a year.

The first confirmed screwworm case in Texas was found in a calf in the southern part of the state, approximately 50 miles from the Mexico border, according to Brooke Rollins, the agriculture secretary. This marked the first detection in Texas since 1966.

The outbreak poses significant economic concerns for Texas ranchers and the broader North American cattle market. Sid Miller, the Texas agriculture commissioner, criticized federal response efforts, noting that despite the USDA's deployment of billions of sterile flies, the screwworm has advanced over 1,100 miles from southern Mexico to Texas. Miller urged the Trump administration to deploy the screwworm adult suppression system, or Swass, which combines attractants, bait and targeted insecticides to reduce adult populations before sterile fly releases eliminate remaining infestations. This approach was successfully used to eradicate screwworm in previous campaigns.

Rollins defended her agency's response, stating "there is no threat of mass infestation" and expressing confidence that "there is no reason to believe this incursion will result in establishment of the pest in our country." She emphasized that only one case has been confirmed so far in Texas.

Female screwworm flies target open wounds on livestock to lay eggs. Once larvae hatch, they rapidly consume blood and flesh, often proving fatal if the animal goes untreated. While the flies pose no direct threat to food safety, officials worry about potential shortages if the parasite becomes established in cattle and poultry populations, particularly at a time when beef prices remain at record highs.

In March, Rollins announced that the USDA had partnered with the Army Corps of Engineers and Mortenson Construction to build a new sterile fly production facility at Moore airbase in Edinburg, Texas. This facility is intended to support ongoing containment efforts.

The US had previously shut down most of its sterile fly breeding facilities, leaving only one operating in Panama for decades, according to reporting from the Associated Press. In Mexico, ranchers facing high treatment costs have resorted to crude measures such as applying gasoline or lime to open wounds in attempts to remove worms.

The New York Knicks moved within two wins of their first NBA championship since 1973 by defeating the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 of the NBA Finals on Friday night. The victory gives New York a commanding 2-0 series lead heading back to Madison Square Garden.

The Knicks, riding an 11-game winning streak entering the Finals, secured their second consecutive road victory in this series. Jalen Brunson delivered when the game hung in the balance, sinking the go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining after a crucial turnover by Spurs star Victor Wembanyama. On the final possession, Wembanyama's clean look from the elbow at the buzzer caromed off the back rim, sealing the Knicks' victory.

The game mirrored Game 1's dramatic nature, with the Knicks fighting back in the closing moments. Brunson led New York with a strong performance in crucial situations, while OG Anunoby contributed significantly in the fourth quarter. The Knicks avoided turnovers in the fourth quarter while holding the Spurs to 28.6 percent shooting during that stretch.

For San Antonio, Wembanyama recorded 26 points, 12 rebounds, and three blocks but shot just 6-of-21 from the field. The Spurs committed five turnovers in the fourth quarter and wilted before their home crowd. "I was bad tonight, it's not more complicated than that," Wembanyama said afterward. He denied that the occasion or nerves affected his team's performance.

The Knicks became only the third team to win the first two games of an NBA Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets. Despite their commanding position, Brunson warned his teammates against complacency returning home. "Just sticking together - it wasn't really our night and wasn't really my night most of the night but we kept finding a way, kept chipping away," Brunson said. "Just knowing we have each other's back."

The series now shifts to Madison Square Garden on Monday for Games 3 and 4. The Knicks will seek to extend their advantage on their home court, where the atmosphere is expected to be intense. The Spurs face a significant challenge in climbing back into contention after losing both games in San Antonio.

Teams taking 2-0 leads in the NBA Finals historically go on to win championships the vast majority of the time. New York's balanced attack and late-game execution have been difficult for San Antonio to contain, while the Spurs must find answers if they hope to make this a competitive series going forward.

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said.

Head played Giles, the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's vampire slayer, throughout the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. The character became iconic as the tweedy father figure guiding Buffy and her friends through supernatural threats. Cast members from Buffy paid tribute following his death, with references to show themes reflecting the lasting bonds formed during production.

Sarah Michelle Gellar shared her tribute on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her Buffy character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you. Thank you to Daisy and Emily who not only shared their dad with me, but with the world."

Beyond Buffy, Head's career spanned decades across British and American television and film. He appeared in the BBC production of Merlin and took a recurring role in the sketch comedy show Little Britain. His film credits included roles in The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in Ted Lasso, where he worked alongside co-creator Jason Sudeikis and received praise for their on-screen chemistry.

Head was born in Camden, London, to artistic parents. His father founded the documentary production company Verity Films, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor known for the 1971 film Sunday Bloody Sunday.

Head began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in various stage productions including Chess and The Rocky Horror Show. He also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album The Circle & the Square.

Head became widely recognized in the UK and US for appearing in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements that featured a slow-burn romance between a British couple.

During Buffy's filming, Head spent five years in Los Angeles while his partner Sarah and their two young daughters remained in England. He traveled home every three or four weeks whenever possible, working with the production team to arrange his schedule around these visits.

His daughters noted that Head loved his work and considered himself fortunate to collaborate with talented people throughout his long career. "Our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of," they said.

Yesterday, 9:57 PM ET

The Justice Department announced in a court filing that the Trump administration's nearly $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund will not continue. The decision came as two DOJ attorneys filed notice in federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia, simultaneously asking the court to reject legal challenges to the program.

The compensation fund was created to provide financial relief to individuals who claimed they were targets of politically motivated investigations or prosecutions. The program generated significant criticism from legal experts, government watchdog groups, and members of both parties over concerns about its lack of oversight and undefined criteria for payouts.

The announcement came after a federal judge temporarily blocked the administration from transferring money from the fund. US District Judge Leonie Brinkema issued the temporary restraining order on Friday following a lawsuit filed by Democracy Forward seeking to dissolve the program entirely.

The fund faced mounting political backlash even from Republicans. Senate Majority Leader John Thune told reporters that the best approach would be for the administration to shut down the fund voluntarily. Several state governors, including California's Gavin Newsom, proposed punitive tax measures against any distributions to their residents.

Significant controversy surrounded the possibility that the fund could compensate January 6 Capitol rioters. Mike Pence, Trump's first-term vice president, called the prospect of federal compensation for individuals who assaulted police officers and vandalized the Capitol "deeply offensive." He suggested this view was widely shared among Republicans and Americans generally.

The fund had been announced as part of a settlement in Trump's lawsuit against the IRS over the leak of his tax returns. The IRS did not defend itself in the lawsuit, raising accusations of collusion and corruption.

Senate Democrats vowed aggressive opposition to the program. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer described it as "corruption in broad daylight" and a "maga slush fund." He pledged that Democrats would force Republicans to vote on measures to block payments and would press for preserved records and congressional hearings.

Schumer outlined multiple strategies for fighting the fund, stating that Democrats would work through reconciliation amendments, demand Senate floor votes, and oppose any attempts to obscure the issue through appropriations processes. He emphasized there would be "no escape hatch" or administrative workarounds to hide the program.

The Justice Department's court filing did not explain in detail the reasoning behind discontinuing the fund. It remains unclear whether any payments were made before the program ended or whether the administration plans to implement alternative measures to address concerns about past law enforcement practices.

Mountain Lion Captured in Pasadena Front Yard

Wildlife officials tranquilized the animal after it spent hours hiding in a residential area.

Yesterday, 10:10 PM ET

A mountain lion was tranquilized and captured in Pasadena after being found lounging in a front yard. The animal had been hiding in the residential area for hours before wildlife officials intervened.

The sighting prompted a shelter-in-place advisory in the neighborhood. Los Angeles area residents have reported multiple mountain lion encounters in recent weeks, with another sighting captured on camera in a San Diego backyard. Wildlife officials successfully removed the Pasadena animal without incident.
