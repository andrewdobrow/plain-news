1:07 AM ET

A federal judge has invalidated the Trump administration's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, ruling that the charge violated federal administrative law and the Constitution. US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page decision in a lawsuit brought by 20 Democratic state attorneys generals challenging the fee that Trump announced in September.

The fee represented a dramatic increase in costs for H-1B visas, raising the price by 20 to 50 times the existing rates. The administration had introduced the fee as a means of discouraging companies from hiring foreign workers for positions that could potentially be filled by American citizens.

The H-1B visa program allows US employers to temporarily hire foreign workers in specialty occupations, particularly in technology, engineering, and medicine. The administration argued that the substantial fee would encourage companies to make greater efforts to recruit domestically before turning to international talent.

Judge Sorokin's ruling vacated the entire fee structure. The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the decision.

Technology companies and business groups have been among the strongest critics of restrictions on the H-1B program. These industries argue that limiting access to global talent hampers innovation and American competitiveness in the international marketplace. Many firms depend on the visa program to fill specialized positions that are difficult to staff domestically.

The decision represents a setback for the administration's broader immigration policy agenda. Over the past several months, the administration has pursued various measures to restrict both legal and illegal immigration pathways. However, federal courts have blocked several of these initiatives in their early stages.

The H-1B visa program remains a contentious issue in American immigration debates. Business interests advocate for expanded access to foreign workers, while labor organizations and immigration restrictionists push for tighter controls and stronger protections for American workers.

The court challenge came at a time when the Trump administration has intensified its overall crackdown on immigration. The state department has revoked more than 100,000 visas since Trump returned to office, while the homeland security department reported that more than 605,000 people have been deported. An additional 2.5 million people left the country on their own.

This legal battle over the H-1B fee is likely to continue, given the administration's expected appeal and the ongoing policy disputes surrounding immigration. The outcome could significantly affect how many foreign workers companies can hire and at what cost, shaping the availability of skilled labor in key American industries for years to come.

Israel carried out new airstrikes across southern Lebanon following the rejection of a U.S.-brokered ceasefire agreement by Hezbollah. The strikes killed six people on Friday and forced thousands of residents to flee their homes after Israeli military issued evacuation orders for nine villages in the region.

The Israeli military ordered residents to leave multiple areas, including the village of Anqoun, which was sheltering at least 2,500 displaced people. Roads to Sidon, the nearest major city, became congested with families seeking safety. The airstrikes and artillery attacks targeted towns including Kfar Tebnit and struck vehicles in the Nabatieh area with drone fire. Israeli troops have also advanced deeper into southern Lebanon following the capture of Beaufort Castle earlier in the week.

The military operations intensified after Hezbollah rejected a ceasefire proposal that would have allowed Israel to continue carrying out airstrikes while the militant group stopped firing. Hezbollah called the agreement tantamount to "surrender." The rejection came despite negotiations involving the Israeli government and Lebanon, though Hezbollah was not a direct party to the talks.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated he would not seek cabinet approval for the ceasefire proposal unless Hezbollah first agreed to its terms. "At the moment, there is no deal," he told ministers. "Hezbollah is opposed, and therefore I am not making a decision."

Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam commented on the situation, saying "Lebanon can no longer be a field for wars fought for others, nor can the south and its people continue to pay the price for decisions they did not make." Parliament speaker Nabih Berri, who has served as Hezbollah's representative in negotiations, stated that Hezbollah would only withdraw from the area south of the Litani River if Israeli troops fully withdrew from Lebanon and a ceasefire was unconditional.

Israel currently occupies more than 608 square kilometers of Lebanese territory. The strikes represent a continuation of the military campaign that began on March 2, following Hezbollah's rocket attacks on Israel.

The Israeli campaign against Hezbollah has complicated ongoing U.S.-Iran peace negotiations. The United States has been working to broker an agreement with Tehran, but Israeli military operations against Iranian-backed groups in Lebanon create friction in the diplomatic process. President Trump has reportedly grown frustrated with Netanyahu's campaign in Lebanon as it complicates talks with Iran.

Fighting between Hezbollah and Israel has resulted in more than 3,500 deaths in Lebanon from Israeli strikes, while Hezbollah has killed at least 29 Israeli soldiers in Lebanon and three Israeli civilians. The conflict highlights the delicate balance Israel faces as it pursues security objectives while the United States attempts diplomatic progress with Iran.

The Supreme Court is approaching the conclusion of its current term, with justices working to complete decisions in several significant cases that remain unresolved. This period marks the final stretch of the Court's yearly calendar, when justices race to finish writing opinions and dissents before the term ends.

The Court typically issues its most consequential rulings in the final weeks of its term, which runs from October through late June or early July. The remaining cases on the docket will determine outcomes on issues affecting constitutional rights, federal policy, and legal precedent across the country.

The justices follow a traditional timeline that brings increased activity as the term concludes. Once a case is argued before the Court, the justices confer privately to discuss their positions, assign opinion writing responsibilities, and then draft majority opinions and any dissenting or concurring views. This process intensifies in the spring months as deadlines approach.

The Court's decisions during this final period often draw significant public attention because they tend to involve the term's most contentious or legally complex matters. The justices typically save cases requiring the most deliberation and writing for this concluding phase, rather than issuing decisions immediately after oral arguments.

As the term winds down, the Court will continue announcing decisions on opinion release days, typically occurring twice weekly. The pace of announcements accelerates as the justices work to clear the docket before recessing for the summer.

Yesterday, 11:49 PM ET

Apple announced a major overhaul of its Siri voice assistant at its annual developer conference, integrating the tool with Apple Intelligence and rebranding it as "Siri AI." The upgrade will become widely available in the fall, marking a significant shift in the company's approach to artificial intelligence.

The revamped Siri will function more like modern AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than a traditional web-based question-and-answer tool. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, demonstrated the assistant's new capabilities during the keynote, showing how it can handle complex, multi-step requests within a single interface. In one example, Rockwell asked Siri to identify a beach from a photograph, then requested a nearby friend's address and directions to the location, all completed without switching between apps.

The partnership powering Siri AI involves Google's Gemini model as part of a billion-dollar collaboration between the two tech giants. The new assistant will have a dedicated app and can access Apple's native applications across mobile devices and computers to assist with daily planning, information retrieval and navigation. The tool will also perform tasks including proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events and extracting nutrition information from photos of food.

Apple acknowledged that it has lagged behind competitors in deploying AI features over the past decade. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, noted that while some companies are "racing forward" with AI development, Apple prioritizes creating technology that serves users' actual needs.

The company's timing comes after a previous false start. Apple initially promised an updated Siri in 2024 but delayed the release, and later settled a class-action lawsuit for 250 million dollars over allegations that it had misrepresented the assistant's capabilities. Some consumers received payouts of up to 95 dollars.

Beyond the Siri upgrade, Apple introduced new child safety features designed to give parents greater control over their children's device usage. The company is implementing tools to limit content visibility, restrict who children can contact and control app access. A new safety feature in Safari will require parental approval before children can browse unfamiliar websites. The system will also blur messages containing gore or violence.

The child safety announcement follows increased scrutiny over harmful content, including so-called "nudification" apps. Apple partnered with the American Academy of Pediatrics to provide parents with recommended screen time limits.

The Siri AI announcement came during Tim Cook's final keynote as CEO. Cook is stepping down in the fall after leading Apple for 15 years, during which the company became one of the world's most profitable publicly traded corporations.

Victor Wembanyama delivered 32 points to lead the San Antonio Spurs past the New York Knicks 115-111 in Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden. The victory cut New York's series advantage to 2-1 and kept San Antonio's championship hopes alive.

The French center dominated the game after struggling in Game 2, when he ended the contest with a critical turnover and a missed potential game-winner. Wembanyama opened Game 3 with intensity, scoring nine points in his first nine minutes. He finished with eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks in a performance that silenced much of the Garden crowd.

"Really tried to relax after Game 2," Wembanyama said. "The playoffs, it's like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water."

Stephon Castle added 23 points for the Spurs and made two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining in the third quarter. San Antonio benefited from 21 points off 13 Knicks turnovers, which proved decisive in the tight contest.

Jalen Brunson led the Knicks with 32 points, while OG Anunoby contributed 28. However, New York's supporting cast struggled in the fourth quarter, shooting just 3 for 20 from the field in the period. Karl-Anthony Towns, who had recorded back-to-back double-doubles in San Antonio, managed only 11 points and eight rebounds.

The Spurs raced to an 11-point first-quarter lead before the Knicks responded with a 42-24 second-quarter run to take a 64-57 halftime advantage. The third quarter saw neither team pull away, with the largest lead just five points.

The fourth quarter proved contentious, with New York's players and fans expressing frustration over officiating decisions. The Knicks were whistled three times in the first 64 seconds of the period and took just eight free throws in the second half compared to San Antonio's 24. Brunson kept New York competitive with 12 fourth-quarter points, but his teammates couldn't provide enough support.

The loss snapped the Knicks' 13-game postseason winning streak and ended what had been a dominant playoff run. New York had entered the Finals with an air of inevitability, seeking the franchise's first championship since 1973.

Brunson acknowledged the setback but remained focused on improvement. "We've done our best to try to learn from wins over the past couple weeks. But now we have to learn from a loss," he said. "But I think the most important thing is that we are going to learn regardless, because we knew there were things that we were going to have to improve on going into next game."

Game 4 takes place Wednesday in New York, with the Spurs attempting to force the series back toward San Antonio.

The documentary presents a comprehensive portrait of the artist, depicting him as a New York child of privilege who became a driven prodigy and one of the art world's most celebrated figures. The film traces his evolution through multiple identities: a bohemian scavenger, downtown rock star, thrill-seeking addict, media celebrity, meditative soul, and spiky art genius whose work remains timeless.

Reviewers have praised the film for achieving what previous documentaries had not accomplished. The documentary explores the full scope of Basquiat's life and career, examining his development as a fixture of New York's downtown art scene in the 1980s and his eventual rise to prominence as both a cultural celebrity and influential artistic voice.

The film documents various dimensions of his existence, including his role in the bohemian art world, his connections to the downtown music scene, and his struggles with addiction. It also highlights his reflective nature and the enduring impact he has had on contemporary art.

Basquiat died in 1988 at the age of 27, yet his legacy continues to command substantial attention within the art world. His paintings regularly achieve record-breaking prices at auction, and his influence on contemporary artistic practice remains significant. The documentary arrives during a period of continued interest in examining his contributions to American art, with major exhibitions and academic research ongoing.

The Netflix deal guarantees that the documentary will reach audiences worldwide when it launches on the streaming platform. The acquisition was finalized following the film's Tribeca screening, though Netflix has not yet announced a specific release date for the project.

The filmmakers have focused their approach on presenting Basquiat as both an artistic genius and a complex individual. Their portrait reflects the creative vitality and difficulties that characterized New York's art scene during the 1980s, a formative period for both the artist and the city's cultural landscape.

The Icelandic producer based in the United States will receive the festival's Raimondo Rezzonico Award.

A few minutes ago

The Locarno Film Festival will present its Raimondo Rezzonico Award to Sigurjón Sighvatsson, a film, television, and music video producer. The award recognizes producers who exemplify independent filmmaking values.

Sighvatsson co-founded Propaganda Films with Steve Golin in 1986, building the company into a production house that created television series including Twin Peaks. The Icelandic producer has been based in the United States throughout his career in the entertainment industry.
