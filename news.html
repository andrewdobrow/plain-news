The House Oversight Committee released a Republican-led report Tuesday criticizing the Justice Department for its handling of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation over multiple decades and administrations, calling for federal investigators to reinterview key witnesses and associates of the late sex offender. The 137-page report stated that too many survivors and witnesses were never approached or properly questioned by federal authorities and urged the DOJ to interview or reinterview individuals who were part of the inner circle of Epstein and co-conspirator Ghislaine Maxwell.

Committee Chair James Comer said Epstein operated with impunity, shielded by powerful connections and a justice system that repeatedly failed his victims. The report called for the Justice Department to investigate Epstein associates accused of criminal activity and ensure survivors have access to their records. The Justice Department responded that the Attorney General has repeatedly said the Epstein case is not closed and that there is no statute of limitations on human trafficking, adding that the department will continue following all additional investigative leads.

The report also questioned whether the Justice Department has released all relevant documents under the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which mandated public disclosure of case-related records. The Republican report criticized the department for releasing names of some victims in documents while redacting the names of Epstein allies, and noted that survivors continue to ask the DOJ for access to their own personal files but have not been permitted to do so. The report called for legislation protecting the ability of individuals with knowledge of sexual abuse to speak freely regardless of nondisclosure agreements.

The top Democrat on the committee, Robert Garcia, said the Republican investigation was incomplete and that Democrats will hold public hearings when they take the majority in January. Garcia said the DOJ continues to illegally withhold over 2.5 million pages of documents. Democrats released their own report last month calling for an independent special counsel to investigate Epstein's ties to President Donald Trump, allegations the White House called untrue and politically motivated. The Republican report criticized Democrats for focusing on Trump, saying they were obsessed with the president and had engaged in an unfounded witch hunt.

The gunman suspected of killing six people in central Krakow and three family members at their home died in a hospital early Monday morning, Polish police said. The 24-year-old Polish man was shot by police officers within three minutes of opening fire in the city's Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon. He had been hospitalized with injuries sustained during a gunfight with responding officers.

Polish prosecutors said Monday that investigations into the suspect's motive remain ongoing, walking back earlier statements by a government minister who described the gunman as inspired by far-right ideologies and ultra-nationalism. Przemysław Nowak, a spokesperson for Poland's National Prosecutor's Office, told reporters the motive inquiry continues. The suspect has not been officially named.

The attack killed four Polish citizens, a British national, and an Austrian woman in the Main Market Square. Police later found three bodies at the gunman's home, identified as his 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother, and 21-year-old sister. Authorities seized four firearms from the suspect's car, including two AR-15 rifles and two handguns, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition. Polish media reported the suspect held a firearms permit.

Sixteen people were hospitalized after the shooting, including two police officers who were injured during the gunfight with the suspect. A surgeon who was eating nearby when the attack began rushed to the scene and helped treat victims before ambulances arrived. Professor Michał Pędziwiatr said his colleague used his hand to stem bleeding from a severely injured police officer, and that intervention undoubtedly saved the officer's life. Both wounded officers have since emerged from comas.

Poland declared two days of national mourning following the attack. Prime Minister Donald Tusk visited Krakow and said the country had not previously experienced such drastic terrorism.

3:43 PM ET

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission has approved a construction permit for X-energy to build four small modular reactors at a Dow chemical plant in Texas, the nuclear startup announced Monday. The permit marks the first approval for an advanced nuclear reactor at an industrial site in the United States. Each of the four Xe-100 reactors will be capable of generating 80 megawatts of electricity and 200 megawatts of heat, though the reactors are not expected to be operational until the early 2030s.

The nuclear industry has positioned small modular reactors as a faster and cheaper alternative to traditional nuclear plants, though those advantages remain unproven. No small modular reactor currently operates in the United States. X-energy says its design flows helium gas over uranium-containing fuel pellets, which the company claims is safer than many existing reactors.

Safety advocates criticized the approval process. Edwin Lyman, director of nuclear power safety for the Union of Concerned Scientists, said in a statement that the NRC achieved the approval "by pushing consideration of critical safety questions to a later date—issues the NRC should've fully resolved before green lighting construction."

X-energy has attracted significant backing from technology companies seeking electricity sources for data centers. Amazon's Climate Pledge Fund led the company's $500 million Series C-1 funding round, and Amazon has committed to buying 5 gigawatts of capacity from the startup. X-energy raised $1 billion in an April initial public offering, but investor enthusiasm has cooled. The company's shares are trading at less than half their initial offering price.

The Minnesota Vikings have acquired quarterback Kirk Cousins from the Las Vegas Raiders in exchange for a 2027 third-round pick, Yahoo Sports confirmed Tuesday. The trade reunites Cousins with the franchise where he last started in 2023 and addresses Minnesota's struggling quarterback situation following Carson Wentz's three-turnover, 81-yard performance in a Week 9 loss to Buffalo.

Cousins, 38, has completed his best season in years with the Raiders, throwing 20 touchdowns against eight interceptions through nine games. His performance helped Las Vegas rank eighth in points per game this season after finishing last in that category in 2025. Only Cincinnati's Joe Burrow has thrown more touchdown passes this season.

The Raiders, sitting at 4-5 and still in playoff contention, had previously signaled interest in keeping Cousins. The presence of 2026 first overall pick Fernando Mendoza, a quarterback from Indiana who has yet to start, ultimately led Las Vegas to change course and trade the veteran. The move signals the Raiders are ready to begin the Mendoza era.

For Minnesota, the acquisition represents an upgrade over the team's current options. Kyler Murray has failed to establish himself as the answer through six starts, and Wentz's Monday night collapse made clear the Vikings needed a change. With an elite defense already in place, the team views Cousins as the missing piece for a Super Bowl run. It remains unclear whether Cousins will start Sunday when Minnesota visits Green Bay at Lambeau Field, the same opponent and venue where he last started for the Vikings in Week 8 of 2023. The trade is pending Cousins passing a physical.

Additional women accused Walliams of inappropriate behavior at Britain's Got Talent, including invitations to his dressing room, and alleged he pursued relationships with junior employees at publisher HarperCollins.

In a letter to journalists, Walliams said he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law. He acknowledged behaving thoughtlessly in casual sexual relationships and said he regretted not taking people's feelings into account. He denied grooming or sexually harassing anyone and said he is devastated that some women he met 10 to 20 years ago have expressed hurt or anger. Walliams denied having sexual contact with the woman who alleged assault at age 15 and said he believed another woman was 18 when they had sex.

Walliams, 55, had brought legal action against ITV News and the Guardian to prevent publication, with his lawyers arguing it would be highly likely to lead him to kill himself. A High Court judge rejected the injunction request last week. Following publication, HarperCollins announced it was dropping Walliams as an author and would no longer publish his backlist. Sky also said it was dropping an upcoming adaptation of one of his books and removing his content from its platforms. New York opens AI safety registration portal, requiring disclosure from OpenAI and Anthropic

Major AI developers must register by January 1 under the RAISE Act, which mandates safety frameworks and incident reporting.

3:52 PM ET

New York opened a registration portal Tuesday for its RAISE Act, requiring large AI developers with more than $500 million in annual revenue to register by January 1, 2027. Companies including OpenAI and Anthropic must publish safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours, and issue public transparency reports. Violations can result in fines up to $1 million for a first offense and $3 million for subsequent violations.

The newly created Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust will oversee compliance. Governor Kathy Hochul also launched the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research at SUNY Binghamton University, which will independently evaluate AI systems and review companies' safety frameworks.
