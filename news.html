5:03 AM ET

U.S. government borrowing costs have climbed to their highest levels in nearly two decades following the Federal Reserve's decision to keep interest rates unchanged. The yield on the 30-year Treasury bond rose 14 basis points to approximately 5.24%, marking a 19-year peak, after the Fed maintained its key interest rate between 3.5% and 3.75% for the fifth consecutive meeting.

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh stated the central bank would "not waver" in its commitment to controlling inflation, directly addressing concerns that the Fed may lack sufficient resolve. "There is no soft implicit target: not on this committee's watch," Warsh said. "There's only a target and it's 2%. This Fed will not waver. Our credibility rests on performing our duties and delivering on our responsibilities."

Despite these assurances, financial markets reacted negatively to the Fed's decision to hold rates steady. Investors had anticipated a 30% probability of a rate increase before the announcement. Following the decision, traders reassessed expectations for future policy, reducing the likelihood of a September rate increase to approximately 57%, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool.

The decision to hold rates sparked debate among economists about the Fed's inflation-fighting approach. Felix Schmidt, a senior economist at Berenberg, noted that Warsh had not clearly explained why the Fed chose not to raise rates. Schmidt suggested the Fed chair may believe that higher bond yields, which have already increased borrowing costs across the economy, could help combat inflation in the near term.

Rising Treasury yields have immediate consequences for consumers and businesses. Borrowing costs are climbing for mortgages, corporate debt, and other forms of credit as yields surge across the financial system. U.S. stocks fell sharply on the day of the announcement, with the S&P 500 closing down 1.5%, the Dow Jones industrial average falling 2.2%, and the Nasdaq declining 1.7%.

The inflation concerns driving the market reaction stem partly from geopolitical developments. U.S. inflation had cooled to an annual rate of 3.5% in June after Washington and Tehran agreed to a temporary ceasefire. However, this truce has since collapsed, with both sides exchanging fire and pushing oil prices higher once again. Energy price increases add another layer to inflation pressures facing the economy.

The Fed's decision also marked a notable moment of internal disagreement. The Federal Open Market Committee voted 9-3 to maintain rates, with three members dissenting in favor of a quarter-percentage-point increase. This represented the first time in a decade that three board members shared dissent over a policy decision, highlighting divisions within the central bank over the appropriate response to persistent inflation.

A Russian cruise missile struck Polish territory during overnight attacks on Ukraine, creating a 10-meter-wide crater near the village of Tarnawa Kolonia, approximately 100 kilometers from the Ukrainian border. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk confirmed that early indications suggest the missile was a Russian Kh-101 air-launched cruise missile, marking the first time a Russian projectile has actually hit Polish soil during the war.

The incident occurred as Russia conducted large-scale strikes across Ukrainian cities, including the western city of Lviv. The attack killed at least eight civilians in Ukraine, including three children. At least six people died in a village near the central city of Kryvyi Rih, including a six-year-old girl and two boys aged 11 and 17.

Polish defense minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz confirmed that the Kh-101 was "the most probable hypothesis" regarding the projectile. Russia fired between 20 and 22 of these missiles at Ukraine during the overnight assault. Polish air force officials identified two missiles as potential threats to Polish territory. An F-16 fighter jet was scrambled to monitor one missile and observed its crash in an uninhabited area.

Tusk stressed that "there are no reasons to think Poland was the intended target for this missile." He noted that Poland's air force was prepared to shoot down the missile if it had threatened inhabited areas. Tusk emphasized that Poland suffered no casualties or significant damage from the incident.

The strike raises questions about NATO's collective defense obligations, though Polish officials have treated previous airspace violations as accidents rather than deliberate attacks. In November 2022, a Ukrainian surface-to-air missile accidentally strayed into Poland and killed two people. In December 2022, an unarmed Russian cruise missile briefly entered Polish airspace.

Tusk linked the incident to Ukraine's air defense shortages, saying "If the Ukrainians had much more of what they've been asking for all along, perhaps that ballistic missile wouldn't have been launched toward Poland." He called the next 100 days critical for Ukraine's outcome, describing it as a "50/50 chance."

Poland's foreign minister Radoslaw Sikorski warned against disinformation, noting that Kh-101 missiles are exclusively used by Russia's strategic air force. French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the Russian strikes on Ukraine and the airspace violation, pledging continued support for both Ukraine and Poland.

Ukraine has requested to join the Polish investigation into the incident, which officials say will be mapped and examined in detail. The US has expressed interest in participating in the investigation as well. Polish officials have maintained regular contact with NATO allies regarding the incident.

5:32 AM ET

A Georgia father has been found guilty of second-degree murder and other charges related to a mass shooting at a high school carried out by his teenage son. Colin Gray, 54, was convicted on more than two dozen charges following roughly two weeks of testimony and just a few hours of jury deliberation.

Gray had faced 29 counts including second-degree murder, involuntary manslaughter, and reckless conduct. Under Georgia law, second-degree murder in this case was defined as causing the death of a child through the crime of cruelty to children. Gray had pleaded not guilty to all charges.

The charges stem from a shooting at Apalachee High School on September 4, 2024, in which four people were killed and nine others were wounded. Prosecutors argued that Gray displayed criminal negligence by providing his 14-year-old son, Colt Gray, with access to a firearm and ammunition after receiving warnings that his son posed a danger to others.

The four victims were identified as teachers Richard Aspinwall, 39, and Christina Irimie, 53, and students Mason Schermerhorn and Christian Angulo, both 14. An additional teacher and eight students were injured in the attack.

Colt Gray is accused of carrying out the shooting with an assault-style rifle. He faces 55 counts, including four counts of malice murder and four counts of felony murder, and is being prosecuted as an adult. He has also pleaded not guilty and is awaiting trial.

Colin Gray will be sentenced at a later date and faces up to 30 years in prison. His attorneys are likely to appeal the conviction.

This case reflects a growing trend of holding parents criminally accountable for school shootings carried out by their children. Earlier in 2024, the parents of a Michigan high school student who shot and killed four students were both found guilty of involuntary manslaughter and sentenced to 10 to 15 years in prison.

The conviction of Colin Gray represents a significant legal moment in addressing parental responsibility in school shooting cases. Prosecutors presented evidence establishing that Gray had been warned of potential danger from his son before providing him access to weapons. The relatively swift jury decision to convict on multiple serious charges suggests the evidence of his negligence was persuasive.

The case has drawn national attention as communities grapple with questions about gun access, parental oversight, and accountability for mass violence. The dual prosecutions of both father and son extend criminal responsibility beyond the individual shooter to family members whose actions or inaction may have enabled the attack.

Yesterday, 6:41 PM ET

NASA plans to launch the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on August 30, the agency's next major space observatory designed to explore fundamental questions about the universe. The telescope is equipped with advanced technology including shape-shifting mirrors that could help scientists discover planets similar to Jupiter and investigate cosmic mysteries that current instruments cannot address.

The Roman Space Telescope represents a significant advancement in space observation capabilities. Its deformable mirror technology will allow the telescope to achieve observations currently impossible with existing equipment. This feature is particularly important for studying exoplanets and could reveal planetary systems resembling our own solar system.

NASA scheduled a media briefing to discuss the upcoming launch and the telescope's scientific objectives. The briefing will provide details about the mission timeline and the types of discoveries the agency expects the telescope to make once operational. The Roman Space Telescope is named after Nancy Grace Roman, NASA's first chief astronomer, who played a key role in developing the Hubble Space Telescope program.

The telescope's capabilities extend beyond planet hunting. Scientists expect the observatory to address fundamental questions about dark energy, dark matter, and the structure of the universe. Its wide field of view will allow it to survey large portions of the sky more efficiently than previous space telescopes, enabling studies of millions of stars and galaxies simultaneously.

The August 30 launch date positions the Roman Space Telescope as NASA's next flagship mission in space astronomy, following the success of the James Webb Space Telescope. Once operational, the Roman telescope will work alongside other observatories to provide a more complete understanding of the universe, from nearby exoplanets to the most distant galaxies.

Fleming left his position as head coach of Indian Premier League franchise Chennai Super Kings this month to take on the England role. More recently, he coached Southern Brave in the Hundred competition for two years until 2024.

The appointment marks a reunion with Brendon McCullum, his former teammate and friend, who continues as England's white-ball coach. McCullum previously played under Fleming's captaincy for New Zealand and has described him as a major influence on his own leadership approach. McCullum has praised Fleming's tactical awareness and man-management skills, noting that Fleming's apparent instinctiveness in the field was supported by detailed preparation and analysis.

Root returns to the captaincy after previously leading England in 64 Test matches. The Yorkshire batsman is England's highest run scorer in Test cricket history and his reappointment suggests the board views experience and continuity as essential to rebuilding the Test program.

The timing of Fleming's arrival will be staged. Marcus Trescothick will serve as interim coach for the three-Test series against Pakistan beginning at Headingley in August. Fleming is expected to arrive later in the summer and will assume full responsibility for the tour of South Africa beginning in December, which represents his first major assignment in the role.

Fleming previously turned down an approach from England in 2009, shortly after retiring as a player, believing the timing was not right. He returns to the position at a time when England is seeking to establish long-term stability in Test cricket leadership following a period of mixed results and several short-term coaching appointments.

The partnership between Fleming and Root will be instrumental in shaping England's Test cricket strategy and playing style going forward, with the coach responsible for team strategy, selection input, and developing the squad's approach in the longest format of the game.

The Senate Judiciary Committee postponed a scheduled vote on Todd Blanche's nomination for attorney general after two Republican members of the panel raised concerns about his qualifications. The vote had been set for Thursday but was pushed back as the committee works to address Republican objections.

Blanche, who currently serves as attorney general on an acting basis, is Donald Trump's former criminal defense lawyer. His nomination has faced unexpected resistance from within his own party, which controls the committee majority.

According to reporting on the delay, the two Republican senators withholding their support are seeking written confirmation on a specific issue: assurance that the Department of Justice will not proceed with a proposed 1.8 billion dollar fund designed to compensate Trump supporters who stormed the Capitol on January 6, 2021. Republican Senator John Cornyn stated he will not back the nomination without written assurances that this slush fund is dead.

The Department of Justice has not yet provided the written commitments that these key Republican senators are demanding, which has thrown Blanche's confirmation into doubt. Without their support, the nomination cannot advance out of committee to the full Senate.

Democrats have also raised objections to Blanche's nomination. Robert Garcia, a senior Democrat on the House oversight committee, issued a statement criticizing the delay, saying Republicans "were forced to postpone Todd Blanche's confirmation vote because they knew he was too corrupt and untrustworthy to get enough votes." Garcia alleged that Blanche went from being Trump's personal lawyer to running an Epstein files cover-up and orchestrating billion-dollar slush funds.

Senate Democrats including Dick Durbin, the senior Democrat on the Judiciary Committee, have urged the Senate to reject Blanche's nomination based on his handling of the Epstein files and his personal involvement in transferring Ghislaine Maxwell to a minimum-security prison.

The attorney general position serves as the nation's top law enforcement official, overseeing the Department of Justice and holding significant authority over federal prosecutions and civil rights enforcement. A prolonged confirmation fight could leave the position vacant or filled by an acting official for an extended period.

The Senate Judiciary Committee typically serves as the first formal hurdle for Justice Department nominees. A nominee who cannot secure committee approval faces a difficult path to confirmation. The committee has not announced when it plans to reschedule the vote or what steps Blanche might take to address the concerns raised by Republican senators. The delay comes as the Senate approaches its August recess, which could compress the timeline for his potential confirmation.

NASA plans to launch the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on August 30, the agency's next major space observatory designed to explore fundamental questions about the universe. The telescope is equipped with advanced technology including shape-shifting mirrors that could help scientists discover planets similar to Jupiter and investigate cosmic mysteries that current instruments cannot address.

The Roman Space Telescope represents a significant advancement in space observation capabilities. Its deformable mirror technology will allow the telescope to achieve observations currently impossible with existing equipment. This feature is particularly important for studying exoplanets and could reveal planetary systems resembling our own solar system.

NASA scheduled a media briefing to discuss the upcoming launch and the telescope's scientific objectives. The briefing will provide details about the mission timeline and the types of discoveries the agency expects the telescope to make once operational. The Roman Space Telescope is named after Nancy Grace Roman, NASA's first chief astronomer, who played a key role in developing the Hubble Space Telescope program.

The telescope's capabilities extend beyond planet hunting. Scientists expect the observatory to address fundamental questions about dark energy, dark matter, and the structure of the universe. Its wide field of view will allow it to survey large portions of the sky more efficiently than previous space telescopes, enabling studies of millions of stars and galaxies simultaneously.

The August 30 launch date positions the Roman Space Telescope as NASA's next flagship mission in space astronomy, following the success of the James Webb Space Telescope. Once operational, the Roman telescope will work alongside other observatories to provide a more complete understanding of the universe, from nearby exoplanets to the most distant galaxies.
