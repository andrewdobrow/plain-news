About 49,000 migrants cross into Spanish territory of Ceuta in one day

An estimated 49,000 migrants entered Ceuta, a Spanish enclave on Morocco's northern coast, over a 24-hour period, according to Spanish interior ministry officials. The arrivals represent more than half the population of the city, which has approximately 83,600 residents. At least 18 people died during the crossing, with some drowning and others caught in crushes, according to reports.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez traveled to Ceuta to address the crisis, visiting the Tarajal border crossing and meeting with local authorities, border officials, and police services. The situation presents a significant challenge for his pro-immigration government. Spain deployed military forces to the enclave following the mass crossing, and both Spanish and Moroccan authorities subsequently halted the flow of migrants across the border.

Sánchez characterized the incident as "an attack and a violation of Spain's territorial sovereignty," calling it "deplorable" and worthy of "our strongest possible condemnation." He stated the government would use "all the mechanisms and resources available to the state" to resolve the situation.

According to Sánchez, a recent Spanish court ruling regarding people intercepted while attempting to enter Ceuta and Melilla by sea appeared to have triggered the mass crossing. He said that a "self-serving" interpretation of the ruling had "spread like wildfire" through human trafficking networks, creating a "surge" in attempted crossings.

The crossing far exceeds previous incidents at the border. The 2021 border crisis saw approximately 10,000 people enter the territory. The sudden arrival of tens of thousands in a single day overwhelmed local infrastructure and emergency services.

The crisis sparked reactions from across Europe. Finland's interior minister Mari Rantanen said Spain had "completely failed to protect the Schengen Area's external border" and called for suspending Spain's Schengen status. Sweden's prime minister Ulf Kristersson urged Spain to "act swiftly to regain control" and said Sweden would take necessary measures if the situation threatened its security.

France responded by strengthening border controls with Spain and activating its Rapid Intervention Border Force for in-depth checks, according to French interior minister Laurent Nuñez. French far-right politician Marine Le Pen called for France to strengthen controls at its borders, while former prime minister Michel Barnier emphasized that people in irregular situations must be "effectively returned."

Spain's opposition People's party called for a swifter and tougher response, with leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo saying the military and police reinforcements announced were insufficient. The far-right Vox party characterized the influx as "an invasion."

Ceuta is one of two Spanish territories on the African continent and serves as the only land border between Africa and the European Union in the region. Its position has long made it a destination for migrants seeking entry to Europe.
