Yesterday, 11:02 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night, marking a significant setback for Jeff Bezos' spaceflight company and potentially impacting NASA's lunar ambitions.

The explosion occurred at 9 p.m. ET at Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 36 during a scheduled "hotfire" test. The massive fireball destroyed the launchpad, with the orange glow visible 185 kilometers to the south in Fort Pierce. Shockwaves rippled across Florida's space coast, causing homes to shake in Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach. Residents hundreds of miles north in South Carolina reported seeing the glow in the sky.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees at the facility were accounted for and unharmed. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" on social media. Bezos acknowledged the difficult day in a tweet, stating "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying."

The timing of the explosion is particularly consequential for NASA's lunar program. On Tuesday, just two days before the explosion, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the program timeline following the explosion.

The New Glenn rocket was designated to launch lunar landers for NASA, including the vehicles that will carry astronauts to the moon's surface as part of the Artemis program. Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which would mark the first human moon landing since 1972. Artemis III, scheduled for 2027, will test both Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander and SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System to determine which will transport astronauts to the lunar surface.

This explosion represents the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin. Last month, a payload from the company's third New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight the previous week.

Isaacman stated that NASA would work with its partners to support a thorough investigation and assess near-term mission impacts. The agency indicated it would provide updates on any impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs as information becomes available. Flames were still burning at the launchpad more than two hours after the explosion, and emergency officials reported no threats from fumes or other hazards.

Romania is a NATO member state, and the security alliance responded strongly to the strike. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte stated the alliance is "ready to defend every inch" of its territory, calling Moscow's "reckless behaviour a danger to us all." The European Union said Russia has crossed "another line" with the attack.

The drone was launched as part of a Russian military operation against Ukraine but deviated from its intended target and struck the residential building in eastern Romania near the Ukrainian border. Two people were inside the building when the impact occurred and sustained injuries.

This incident reflects a broader pattern of Ukrainian and Russian drones straying into NATO airspace due to Russian electronic jamming. Estonian officials recently shot down a Ukrainian drone that had been pushed off course by Russian jamming over the Baltic states. Estonia's foreign minister, Margus Tsahkna, explained that Ukrainian drones appear over NATO territory as a direct result of Russian electronic warfare pushing them from their intended routes. He stressed that Estonia has not permitted its airspace to be used for attacks against Russia.

Ukrainian officials have acknowledged these incidents and apologized for the unintended consequences. A spokesperson for Ukraine's foreign ministry stated that Kyiv remains in close cooperation with NATO countries to prevent such occurrences. Ukraine maintains that it deliberately targets only legitimate military objectives within Russia and has never intentionally used Baltic or Romanian airspace for drone operations.

Russia has responded to drone incidents over Baltic states by claiming Ukraine plans to launch attacks from those countries. However, officials from Latvia, Lithuania, and Estonia have denied these allegations. The Latvian president stated that his country has not permitted any nation to use its airspace or territory for attacks against Russia or other countries.

NATO has emphasized that it supports Ukraine's right to defend itself against Russian aggression. However, alliance members have urged Ukraine to exercise greater caution with drone routes given the Russian jamming activities that push these weapons off course. Estonia's defense minister noted that Ukrainian drone trajectories should remain as far from NATO territory as possible when targeting Russian positions.

The Romanian apartment strike adds to growing concerns about conflict spillover into neighboring countries. Romania has supported Ukraine by allowing grain exports through its ports and hosting refugees, but has not been directly targeted by Russian forces until this incident. NATO has not indicated whether this event triggers Article 5, the collective defense clause of the alliance's founding treaty, though the unified condemnation suggests the incident is being treated as a serious breach.

5:00 AM ET

The combined number of beef and dairy cattle in the United States has fallen to its lowest level in 75 years. The herd has shrunk to levels not seen since 1951, marking a significant contraction in domestic livestock populations.

Drought conditions have played a major role in reducing cattle numbers across the country. Ranchers in affected regions have faced persistent water shortages and diminished grazing lands, forcing difficult decisions about herd sizes.

Rising operating costs have also pressured cattle producers. Feed prices, veterinary care, and other expenses associated with raising cattle have increased, making it more expensive to maintain large herds.

Increased consolidation in the cattle industry has contributed to the decline as well. Smaller operations have struggled to compete with larger producers, leading to fewer independent ranchers and changes in how cattle are raised and distributed.

The shrinking herd size carries direct implications for beef prices. With fewer cattle available for processing, the supply of beef is tightening. Consumers may see higher prices at grocery stores and restaurants as producers and retailers respond to reduced availability.

Dairy cattle numbers are included in the overall decline, which could also affect milk production and dairy product pricing. The reduction spans both sectors of the cattle industry, reflecting broad pressures facing livestock producers.

The last time the U.S. cattle population was this small, the country had a significantly smaller human population and different agricultural practices. The current decline represents a reversal of decades of growth in the American cattle industry.

Industry observers note that rebuilding cattle herds is a slow process. Cattle have long gestation periods and it takes years to raise animals to market weight. Even if conditions improve, it will take considerable time before herd numbers recover to previous levels.

The timing of this contraction comes as farmers and ranchers navigate multiple economic pressures simultaneously. Environmental challenges like drought have collided with inflation in input costs, creating a difficult operating environment. Meanwhile, the trend toward larger, more consolidated operations means fewer family farms maintaining their own herds.

Experts emphasize that recovery from this decline will require patience. The biological constraints of cattle production mean that even with improved conditions and favorable economics, it will take years before herd sizes return to historical averages. This extended timeline suggests that any effects on beef and dairy pricing could persist for an extended period.

The cattle industry's current state reflects broader challenges in American agriculture, where producers face mounting pressure from multiple directions. Understanding these pressures is important for consumers who may experience the effects at the grocery store and in their grocery bills.

The Trump administration has opened a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll, the writer who accused President Trump of sexual assault, according to reporting by the New York Times and CNN. Prosecutors are examining whether Carroll, 82, committed perjury during a 2022 deposition in her civil lawsuits against Trump.

The investigation centers on Carroll's testimony that she did not accept outside financial support for her legal battles. Nearly six months later, before trial began, Carroll's attorneys informed the judge and Trump's legal team that a nonprofit funded by Reid Hoffman, the billionaire LinkedIn co-founder, had covered some legal fees and expenses. Trump's lawyers argued that Carroll concealed Hoffman's funding, claiming the omission undermined her credibility. Carroll's legal representatives stated she never met or spoke with anyone from the nonprofit. The judge permitted Trump's attorney, Alina Habba, to question Carroll again in a second deposition.

In 2024, a three-judge federal appeals court panel in New York dismissed the claim that Carroll had lied in her deposition. Carroll, a longtime advice columnist, alleged in her 2019 book that Trump assaulted her in a New York department store dressing room. Trump denied the allegations, saying she was "not my type" and accusing her of "totally lying."

Carroll filed a federal civil lawsuit against Trump for sexual abuse and defamation. A jury found Trump liable on both counts and awarded Carroll 5 million dollars in damages. A year later, a separate jury ordered Trump to pay 83 million dollars in another defamation case. Trump has appealed both judgments.

According to CNN and the New York Times, acting attorney general Todd Blanche has recused himself from the investigation due to his role representing Trump in Carroll's civil case.

The investigation represents the latest in a series of criminal probes launched by Trump's justice department against political opponents. Federal prosecutors have investigated former FBI chief James Comey, New York attorney general Letitia James, and Democratic members of Congress including Adam Schiff and Ilhan Omar. None of these investigations have resulted in convictions.

The timing creates a significant challenge for Senate Republicans, who will likely face questions about the probe when Congress reconvenes next month. Democrats are expected to raise concerns about whether the investigation appears retaliatory given Carroll's legal victories against the president. Republican senators must navigate balancing support for the Justice Department's independence with addressing questions about the appropriateness of investigating someone who successfully sued the president in civil court.

Neither the Justice Department nor Carroll's legal representatives immediately responded to requests for comment.
