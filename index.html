6:58 PM ET

The US government proposed new funding rules that would allow officials to cancel research grants at any time, according to documents released Friday. The proposal also makes peer review optional for grant decisions.

Under the proposed changes, political staff would screen grant applications for topics the administration considers forbidden. The rules represent a significant shift from current research funding practices, where grants typically undergo peer review by independent scientists and maintain guaranteed funding periods once awarded.

The proposal comes as federal research agencies distribute billions of dollars annually to universities, hospitals, and research institutions across the country. These grants support scientific work ranging from basic research to clinical trials.

Peer review has served as the standard process for evaluating grant applications at agencies like the National Institutes of Health and National Science Foundation for decades. Scientists submit proposals that undergo assessment by other researchers in their field, who evaluate the work's merit without political considerations.

The new rules would fundamentally alter this system. Making peer review optional would allow agency leadership to approve or reject grants without independent scientific assessment. The ability to cancel grants at any time would eliminate the funding stability researchers currently rely on for multi-year projects.

The proposal does not specify which topics would be considered forbidden or who would make those determinations. It also leaves unclear whether existing grants could face retroactive cancellation or if the rules would apply only to new awards.

Research institutions depend on federal grants to fund laboratory operations, pay graduate students and postdoctoral researchers, and purchase equipment. Sudden cancellations could force labs to shut down experiments mid-stream and dismiss staff.

The proposed rules remain subject to a public comment period before any final implementation. Federal agencies have not announced a timeline for when the changes might take effect or provided estimates of how many grants could be affected.

A Russian drone struck an apartment building in the Romanian city of Galati on Friday, injuring two people in what officials confirmed as the first such attack on Romanian soil to cause injuries. Romania is a NATO member state.

The drone hit the residential building during an overnight Russian assault on neighboring Ukraine. The impact ignited a fire on the building's roof and forced the evacuation of several residents. Emergency services responded to the scene, and police confirmed the two injuries.

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte responded swiftly to the incident. "Russia's reckless behaviour is a danger to us all," Rutte wrote on social media following a call with Romanian President Nicuşor Dan. He affirmed that "NATO stands ready to defend every inch of allied territory."

Romanian officials took immediate action. President Dan convened an emergency meeting of the country's supreme council of national defence on Friday. The foreign ministry summoned Russia's ambassador and described the incident as a "grave and irresponsible escalation" and serious breach of international law.

Romanian Foreign Minister Oana Țoiu stated on social media that Romania would "officially communicate the consequences that this lack of responsibility on the part of the Russian Federation will have for the diplomatic relations between our countries, as well as the next steps at the European level regarding sanctions packages."

The foreign ministry requested that NATO accelerate the transfer of anti-drone capabilities to Romania. Officials also suggested the country would invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty, which triggers formal discussions within the alliance about threats to a member state's security.

The Romanian military scrambled two F-16 fighter jets and a helicopter authorized to engage targets in response to the incident. The defense ministry said the drone was tracked by radar in Romanian airspace before crashing into the residential building in Galati, which sits on the Danube River west of the borders with Moldova and Ukraine.

President Dan stated that Romania would not accept the war being transferred to its citizens and instructed the foreign ministry to prepare a package of measures regarding Romania's relationship with Moscow that would be "proportionate to this very serious situation."

Russian President Vladimir Putin disputed responsibility for the incident, suggesting without evidence that the drone might have been a stray Ukrainian weapon. He told reporters he had only recently learned of the incident and requested that drone wreckage be handed over to Russia for an independent investigation.

The incident prompted condemnation from European leaders. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot denounced Russia's "irresponsible act," while European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Russia's "war of aggression has crossed yet another line" and pledged European solidarity with Romania.

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha characterized the incident as evidence that Russian aggression poses a threat to all of Europe and the broader region.

The Securities and Exchange Commission moved to repeal a rule requiring companies to report greenhouse gas emissions and climate risks. The agency proposed scrapping the Biden-era regulation that mandated climate change disclosures from publicly traded firms.

The rule had required companies to include information about their carbon emissions and how climate change might affect their business operations in regulatory filings. The proposed repeal represents a reversal of the previous administration's policy on corporate environmental transparency.

The SEC's action aligns with broader regulatory changes under the current administration. Wall Street's top regulator is now seeking to eliminate requirements that companies assess and disclose climate-related financial risks to investors.

The original climate disclosure rule was part of efforts to give investors more information about how environmental factors might impact company performance and valuations. Supporters argued the requirements helped shareholders make informed decisions about climate-related business risks.

The proposed repeal must go through a formal regulatory process before taking effect. The SEC will likely accept public comments on the proposal before making a final decision on whether to eliminate the reporting requirements.

Companies that had begun preparing climate disclosures under the rule now face uncertainty about future reporting obligations. The rule had affected publicly traded corporations across industries, from energy companies to financial institutions.

The move follows other regulatory rollbacks affecting environmental and corporate governance policies. Several Biden-era rules addressing climate and sustainability issues have faced revision or elimination under the new administration.

Beyond Star Wars, Lucas edited American Graffiti and contributed to other notable productions during a decade that transformed American filmmaking. Her work during this period established her as a significant figure in the industry.

The family's attorney described her as a brilliant storyteller and a trailblazer in film editing. Her contributions to Star Wars extended beyond technical editing to shaping the narrative structure that made the film resonate with audiences worldwide.

Lucas was married to George Lucas during the production of his early films, including American Graffiti and Star Wars. The couple later divorced, but her professional achievements stood independently as a major force in 1970s cinema.

She retired from film editing after her Oscar-winning work, stepping away from Hollywood at the height of her career. Lucas spent her later years in California, away from the film industry that she had helped reshape through her editing choices.

Her death removes one of the last remaining major creative forces behind the original Star Wars film. The movie, released in 1977, transformed both the science fiction genre and the broader film industry, with Lucas's editing playing a central role in its storytelling success.

The decision halts Trump's planned renovation of the performing arts venue, which was scheduled to close later this year so construction work could begin. The judge concluded that the Kennedy Center's board overstepped its legal authority by unilaterally agreeing to the name change and renovation plans.

The Kennedy Center operates as a federally chartered nonprofit institution and serves as the national cultural center. The facility hosts theatrical performances, concerts, and other cultural events in the nation's capital.

The court order represents a setback to the president's efforts to reshape the prominent Washington landmark. The ruling questions whether the board had the statutory power to make such significant changes to the facility without additional oversight or approval.

The two-week timeline for compliance means the president must act quickly to remove existing signage and branding bearing his name. The order does not address whether Trump can appeal the decision or seek to modify the renovation plans in other ways.

The Kennedy Center board has not publicly commented on how it will implement the court's directive or what impact the ruling will have on any other planned changes to the facility.
