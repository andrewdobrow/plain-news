8:00 AM ET

President Donald Trump may need to explicitly renounce his controversial Anti-Weaponization Fund before Republicans move forward with a stalled immigration enforcement bill. Trump administration officials are attempting to address the fury among congressional Republicans over the fund.

The fund, created following Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the IRS over leaked tax return documents, establishes nearly $1.8 billion to be distributed to alleged victims of what the administration calls "lawfare." The justice department will administer the fund through four commissioners appointed by Trump's attorney general, plus one commissioner selected in consultation with congressional leadership. Trump retains the ability to fire the commissioners, giving him ultimate control over the fund's operations.

The fund will have authority to issue formal apologies for alleged mistreatment of conservative political figures by previous administrations. Legal experts and congressional critics from both parties have attacked the fund as a corrupt scheme they describe as a slush fund for Trump allies. Some have noted that January 6 insurrectionists may be eligible recipients, with some reportedly planning to apply for grants.

The controversy highlights significant tensions within the Republican party over the administration's financial priorities and use of federal resources. Some Republicans are demanding clarity on the fund before proceeding with other legislative initiatives. The dispute has become a roadblock to advancing immigration enforcement legislation that party members otherwise support.

Critics have raised concerns about the fund's lack of transparency. The fund's work is not required to be made public, and reports to the attorney general regarding its conduct must remain confidential. This opacity has drawn additional scrutiny from lawmakers concerned about accountability.

The agreement establishing the fund requires the IRS to drop all audits of Trump and his family. The justice department said it "disagrees strongly" with a recent court decision halting the fund's establishment, signaling the administration intends to continue defending the arrangement through legal channels.

Legal scholars have characterized the fund as problematic on multiple levels. They argue that because Trump effectively controlled both sides of the lawsuit against the IRS, a genuine conflict did not exist. A federal judge had commissioned an independent legal review, which found "reason to believe that the president is, in fact, exercising his control over the defendants in this litigation." The agreement was reached shortly before the judge's deadline requiring the parties to explain how they were actually in conflict.

The fund's establishment is part of a broader pattern that critics say demonstrates widespread self-dealing and conflicts of interest within the Trump administration. Beyond this particular controversy, legal experts worry the fund sets a problematic precedent for future administrations regarding the use of federal resources for personal or political purposes.

The strikes targeted Kyiv, the central cities of Dnipro and Zaporizhzhia, and the eastern cities of Poltava and Kharkiv. The Russian arsenal included eight hypersonic Tsirkon missiles. The attack cut electricity to 140,000 residents, though utility workers restored power to 110,000 by morning.

A rescuer was killed in a follow-up strike on the same location. The city's mayor accused Russia of deliberately using cluster munitions in populated areas to maximize civilian casualties.

Ukraine's air defenses intercepted approximately half the missiles fired, but more than 30 struck civilian targets. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy responded to the assault by urgently requesting Patriot missiles from the United States. He described these air defense systems as vital tools for saving lives and characterized ballistic missiles as Moscow's "last major advantage on the battlefield."

Zelenskyy said on social media: "If Ukraine is not protected from ballistic and other missile strikes, these strikes will continue. Europe needs its own anti-ballistic missiles so that this war can finally end."

The Trump administration has not yet responded to these requests despite Zelenskyy's previous pleas. Ukraine claims it is running out of Patriot interceptors, the only air defense system capable of stopping fast-moving ballistic missiles. The U.S. had recently depleted its Patriot stockpile during operations against Iran.

Ukrainian officials attribute the escalation to Russia's deteriorating military position. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha stated: "Putin is a war criminal and loser who has no cards except terror. Moscow is losing on the battlefield. No number of missiles can change this."

The attack followed days of Russian threats against Kyiv. Zelenskyy had warned residents on Monday that intelligence indicated a major strike was being prepared. The combination of warnings and delayed execution appeared designed to create psychological pressure on civilians.

In recent weeks, Ukraine has conducted increasingly effective long-range drone campaigns against Russian oil refineries, ports, and critical supply corridors. Military analysts suggest Russia is attempting to strengthen its negotiating position through intensified civilian attacks despite mounting battlefield losses.

A federal jury has convicted Andrew Left, a well-known short-seller, of securities fraud charges. The verdict marks a significant moment for the investment community, particularly those who profit from betting that stock prices will decline.

Left built his reputation through Citron Research, a firm that published reports questioning the valuations and business practices of publicly traded companies. His analysis often sparked substantial movements in stock prices, attracting both supporters who agreed with his assessments and criticism from the companies he targeted.

Prosecutors charged Left with manipulating markets through his research reports and social media posts. The government argued that his public statements about companies he had shorted were designed to move stock prices in ways that benefited his positions. A jury deliberated before reaching the guilty verdict on the securities fraud charges.

The conviction carries serious potential consequences, including fines and prison time. A sentencing date has not yet been scheduled. Legal experts anticipate the case will influence how regulators and prosecutors define the boundary between legitimate critical research and illegal market manipulation.

The verdict has generated considerable concern within the short-selling community. Other investors who engage in this practice worry the conviction could establish a legal precedent that restricts their ability to publish critical research about companies. Short-selling involves borrowing shares, selling them immediately, and then purchasing them back at a lower price to profit from the difference. Practitioners in this field frequently publish detailed research explaining their negative views on target companies.

The case has drawn attention from financial analysts and legal experts who are monitoring whether the verdict leads to increased regulatory scrutiny of short-sellers' activities and publications. Many in the investment community are closely watching to see what precedent this conviction may set for their own work.

Citron Research gained prominence for its reports on various companies, sometimes making bold claims about their business models and financial health. Left's work became influential enough that his findings could trigger significant stock price movements, demonstrating the market impact his research could achieve.

The guilty verdict represents a notable moment in discussions about market manipulation and investor speech. As the legal process moves toward sentencing, observers across Wall Street are considering the implications for how short-sellers can operate and communicate their research findings to the public. The outcome may reshape how prosecutors and regulators approach cases involving investment research and stock price movements going forward.

Many players who played for Adelman spoke about how his confidence in their abilities and his mentorship shaped their development as athletes.

Before becoming a coach, Adelman played as a guard in the NBA. He moved into coaching after his playing career concluded and eventually worked his way up to become a head coach. He established himself as one of the most respected coaches in professional basketball through his sustained success and leadership.

Adelman's coaching approach focused on ball movement and proper spacing on the court, principles that have grown increasingly important in modern basketball. His strategic insights helped shape how the game is played today.

The basketball community has responded to his passing by sharing stories of his character and his impact as a coach. People who worked with him during his time coaching the Kings, Trail Blazers, and other teams have highlighted his humanity and the ways he influenced both the careers and personal lives of his players.

Adelman's career achievements place him among the most successful coaches in basketball history. His ability to sustain excellence across different organizations and his commitment to developing talent earned him respect throughout the sport. The coaches and players who worked under him credited his guidance as instrumental in their success both during and after their time on the court.

His death represents a significant loss for the basketball community, which has long recognized him as one of the game's greatest coaches and most influential figures in shaping modern basketball strategy and player development.
