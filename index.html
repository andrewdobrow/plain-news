A few minutes ago

A federal judge has ordered the removal of Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, ruling that the Washington DC venue cannot be renamed without an act of Congress.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper directed the Trump administration to remove all physical signage bearing Trump's name and eliminate any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials within 14 days. In his 94-page decision, the judge wrote that it was "crystal clear" the arts complex was named for the late President John F. Kennedy.

The ruling also blocks the Kennedy Center from closing for two years of planned renovations. The judge prevented the center from winding down programming and shutting down operations as the Trump administration had announced.

The decision came after Trump sought to add his name to the facility. The judge determined that only Congress has the authority to change the center's name, making the board's decision to rename it unlawful. The Kennedy Center was established as a federal memorial to President Kennedy, and that designation cannot be altered without congressional action.

Kennedy family members celebrated the ruling. Maria Shriver, JFK's niece, called it "an appropriate birthday present on my uncle's birthday." She noted that the judge found both Trump and the Kennedy Center Board had acted unlawfully. Kerry Kennedy, daughter of Robert F. Kennedy, wrote: "What a great way to celebrate you on your birthday, Uncle Jack!" Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who serves as Trump's health secretary, made no immediate comment.

Trump responded with a lengthy statement posted on social media hours after announcing he was entering the White House Situation Room regarding Iran. He attacked the judge, noting that Cooper had been nominated to the bench by Barack Obama. Trump also expressed anger at the 14-day deadline to remove his name from the Kennedy Center's facade and website.

Trump wrote that his handpicked board members "unanimously voted to add the name 'TRUMP' onto the former Kennedy Center," but the judge ruled they lacked the authority to do so. Trump said he was instructing the Department of Commerce to work with Congress on transferring the Kennedy Center back to legislative control, stating: "I have instructed the Department of Commerce to make all necessary arrangements with Congress to allow a full and complete transfer of this Institution, giving them the responsibility for its Operation, Maintenance, and Management."

The Kennedy Center has served as Washington's primary performing arts venue since its opening and operates as a national cultural center. The judge's decision emphasizes the facility's historical designation and its status as a living memorial to the late president.

The drone crashed into the roof of the apartment building on Friday, forcing the evacuation of several residents. Romanian military forces scrambled two F-16 fighter jets and a helicopter in response, while emergency services treated the injured and issued alerts to residents in affected areas.

The strike prompted immediate diplomatic responses from NATO and European leaders. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said the alliance is "ready to defend every inch" of its territory after speaking with Romanian President Nicusor Dan. "Russia's reckless behaviour is a danger to us all," Rutte stated.

Romanian Foreign Minister Oana Toiu said her country would formally communicate consequences to Russia and consider diplomatic repercussions. The foreign ministry described the incident as a "grave and irresponsible escalation" and a serious breach of international law. Romania's government announced it would request NATO to accelerate the transfer of anti-drone capabilities and suggested invoking Article 4 of the NATO treaty, which triggers formal discussions within the alliance about security threats.

Romanian President Dan convened an emergency meeting of the country's supreme council of national defence and instructed the foreign ministry to prepare a package of measures regarding Romania's relationship with Moscow. He said Romania would not accept the war being transferred to its citizens.

Russia denied responsibility for the strike. Vladimir Putin suggested without evidence that the drone might have been a stray Ukrainian weapon and called for the wreckage to be handed over for independent investigation. A Romanian military commander stated the strike was not "an attack from Russia against Romania," but emphasized that Russians should understand Russia poses a threat to regional security.

Galati is located on the Danube River, west of Romania's borders with Moldova and Ukraine. Romanian authorities said the drone was tracked by radar in Romanian airspace before impact.

International leaders swiftly condemned the incident. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyer said Russia's "war of aggression has crossed yet another line," while French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot denounced what he called an irresponsible act. Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the incident demonstrated Russia poses a threat to all of Europe.

The strike represents a significant escalation in how the Ukraine conflict affects neighboring countries. Drones from the war have previously entered Romanian airspace, but this marked the first occasion resulting in civilian injuries. The incident occurs amid broader concerns about Russian hybrid warfare operations across Europe.
