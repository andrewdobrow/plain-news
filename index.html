Field Marshal Asim Munir led a delegation to Tehran on Wednesday to convey messages from Washington while arranging a second round of ceasefire talks. Iran's Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said both sides have continued exchanging messages even after 21 hours of talks in Islamabad ended without agreement over the weekend. "During this visit, the views of both sides are likely to be discussed in detail," Baghaei said.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu suggested Wednesday that his country has not committed to any ceasefire. The escalating tensions extend beyond Lebanon. Iran's military leadership responded to the blockade with warnings. Major General Ali Abdollahi, leader of Iran's joint military command, said the military could halt trade in the Gulf region if the U.S. did not lift its port blockade. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated the administration was prepared to escalate financial pressure through secondary sanctions against countries and companies purchasing Iranian oil or holding Iranian assets.

Despite these tensions, both sides appear reluctant to return to full-scale conflict. Trump predicted a swift resolution, telling ABC News correspondent Jonathan Karl that the war could end either before the ceasefire expires or after a deal is reached.

Leavitt said a second round of negotiations would "very likely" be held in Islamabad, indicating the diplomatic process continues even as military and economic pressure intensifies.

Shockwaves from the explosion were felt across Florida's space coast, causing homes to shake in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach. Residents in South Carolina, hundreds of miles to the north, reported seeing a glow in the sky.

Bezos acknowledged the setback on social media, stating that all employees were accounted for and safe. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it," he wrote. "Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it." Emergency officials confirmed there was no threat from fumes or other hazards, though flames continued burning at the launchpad more than two hours after the incident. Blue Origin described the event as "an anomaly."

The explosion threatens NASA's ambitious timeline for lunar exploration. Just one day before the test failure, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of a 20 billion dollar lunar base. The New Glenn rocket was intended to launch landers to the Moon for NASA's Artemis Program, including vehicles that will transport astronauts to the lunar surface.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said in a statement that the agency would work with Blue Origin to investigate the explosion and assess the impact on near-term missions and the broader Artemis program. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he wrote.

This explosion marks the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin. Last month, a payload from the rocket ended up in the wrong orbit during a flight, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the vehicle. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared New Glenn to return to flight the previous week.

Blue Origin and SpaceX are both competing for NASA contracts under the Artemis program, which aims to return humans to the Moon for the first time since 1972 and establish a sustainable lunar presence. Elon Musk responded to the explosion on social media, noting that "rockets are hard."
