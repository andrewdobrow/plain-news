The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to begin negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, according to Vice President JD Vance and multiple news sources. However, President Trump has not yet approved the deal, and significant disagreements remain about key terms.

Iran has set three preconditions for success in the talks that began Thursday in Geneva. Tehran insists on the symbolic right to enrich uranium, the ability to dilute its stockpile of highly enriched uranium, and an agreement that excludes controls on its ballistic missile program. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said it would be a "big problem" if Iran did not negotiate over missiles, signaling potential disagreement on this point.

U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff and Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner have reportedly already accepted these principles in previous indirect talks. However, Trump could overturn these terms, which Iranian officials warn could lead to broader Middle East conflict.

The U.S. proposal asks Iran to limit uranium enrichment to below 5 percent purity, roughly the level accepted in the 2015 nuclear agreement. According to sources close to Iran's negotiation team, Iran was surprised by what it considered relatively favorable initial terms. The proposal requests that Iran agree to limit enrichment to 5 percent and convert the program to civilian use.

However, the U.S. offer does not include immediate sanctions relief or restored diplomatic ties. Iran would face continued economic restrictions while negotiations progress toward gradual sanctions relief and dialogue.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said before leaving for Geneva that achieving an agreement is possible but depends on prioritizing diplomacy. He emphasized that Iran will never develop nuclear weapons but will not abandon its right to peaceful nuclear technology.

The timing of negotiations has grown complicated by Trump's recent statements. In his State of the Union address, delivered hours before Araghchi's social media post reiterating Iran's commitment to avoiding nuclear weapons, Trump accused Iran of pursuing "sinister ambitions" and warned about its ballistic missiles threatening Europe.

The presence of Raphael Grossi, head of the UN nuclear watchdog, at the Geneva talks is considered significant by Iran. Grossi has legal authority to verify whether any access Iran offers for inspections meets international requirements.

Iran's negotiating team recognizes that allowing Trump to claim any agreement is better than the 2015 deal is essential for U.S. domestic politics. Grossi warned before the talks that a dangerous situation is developing amid the complete U.S. military buildup in the region, while stressing the U.S. wants quick negotiations rather than drawn-out discussions.

A Russian drone struck an apartment building in Romania near the Ukrainian border, injuring two civilians, Romanian officials said. The incident marks the first time civilians have been wounded by drones in Romania since the war in Ukraine began.

Romania is a member of NATO, and the security alliance condemned what it called Russia's recklessness. The strike sharply escalated tensions between the alliance and Moscow. Romanian officials said the drone hit the building in an area close to the ongoing conflict in neighboring Ukraine.

Romania's foreign minister said the country could invoke Article 4 of NATO's founding treaty. The clause allows member states to request consultations when they believe their territorial integrity, political independence, or security is threatened. It stops short of the collective defense provision in Article 5, which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all.

Drones from the war in Ukraine have posed a growing threat to Romania. Officials said such incidents had become increasingly common near the border, though this was the first to cause civilian casualties. The building was struck while the conflict continued across the border.

NATO members condemned the strike. The alliance criticized Russia for what it described as reckless behavior that endangered civilian lives in a member state. The incident occurred as tensions between NATO and Russia remain high over the war in Ukraine.

Romanian officials have not provided details about the extent of the injuries to the two civilians. The drone strike adds to a pattern of spillover from the war that has affected neighboring countries, particularly those sharing borders with Ukraine.

8:43 AM ET

A New Glenn rocket owned by Blue Origin exploded during a test at Cape Canaveral in Florida, dealing a significant blow to both the company and NASA's ambitious plans to establish a lunar base and return humans to the moon within the next two years.

The explosion occurred at approximately 9pm ET during a scheduled "hotfire" test, which involves igniting a rocket while it remains on the launchpad. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launch facility, with the orange glow visible from Fort Pierce, 185 kilometers to the south. The blast caused shockwaves that were felt across Florida's Atlantic coast and even reported by residents in South Carolina hundreds of miles away.

Blue Origin described the incident as an "anomaly" and confirmed that all employees were accounted for and safe. Company founder Jeff Bezos acknowledged the setback in a statement, saying "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The timing of the failure is particularly consequential for NASA's Artemis program. Just two days before the explosion, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won the contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. The New Glenn rocket was essential to this effort, designed to carry landers to the lunar surface that will eventually transport astronauts.

Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission scheduled for 2028, which would mark humanity's return to the moon for the first time since 1972. NASA administrator Jared Isaacman stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of timeline impacts following the explosion, noting that "spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult."

The explosion represents the latest in a series of challenges for Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from a New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit, resulting in the rocket being temporarily grounded by the Federal Aviation Administration. Thursday's test marked the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight.

Both Blue Origin and SpaceX have invested heavily in large facilities at Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions as part of NASA's lunar exploration efforts. Engineers will now need to analyze telemetry data, video footage, and physical evidence to understand what caused the explosion. The timeline for Blue Origin's next launch attempt, and by extension NASA's lunar base construction schedule, will depend on the findings of that investigation.

The San Antonio Spurs defeated the Oklahoma City Thunder 118-91 in Game 6 on Thursday, forcing a decisive seventh game in the Western Conference finals. Victor Wembanyama led the way with 28 points and 10 rebounds, delivering the dominant performance needed to level the series at 3-3.

Game 7 will take place Saturday in Oklahoma City, with the winner advancing to face the New York Knicks in the NBA Finals.

Wembanyama's impact on the series has been extraordinary. Through the first four games, he produced a 41-point, 24-rebound double-double in Game 1 that forced overtime when the Spurs were struggling offensively. He also hit a buzzer-beating three-pointer from 43 feet at the end of the second quarter in Game 4, a shot that helped spark a 21-point victory that tied the series.

The Thunder remain the deeper team overall, with their bench significantly outperforming San Antonio's reserves. Oklahoma City is the defending NBA champion with the best regular season record. However, the Spurs' starting five have narrowly outplayed the Thunder's starters, largely due to Wembanyama's two-way excellence.

Thunder center Isaiah Hartenstein has worked to contain Wembanyama by using physical defense that has limited the Frenchman's ability to operate in the paint. In Game 3, Wembanyama had only four rebounds as the Thunder's second unit dominated San Antonio's bench while resting their stars. That performance raised concerns about whether the Spurs could maintain competitiveness throughout the series.

Game 4 provided a different picture. Playing just 31 minutes, Wembanyama put up 33 points while maintaining his defensive presence. The Spurs enjoyed a healthy fourth-quarter lead, allowing him to rest on a stationary bike in the tunnel while still winning his minutes by 29 points.

The Spurs' path forward remains challenging. Crucial offensive creators De'Aaron Fox and Dylan Harper are dealing with injuries. While Devin Vassell and Stephon Castle provide solid two-way play, neither consistently takes over games. Stephon Castle has also struggled with turnovers throughout the series.

The disparity between Wembanyama and backup center Luke Kornet highlights how critical the young star is to San Antonio's chances. Kornet, who could start for other NBA teams, has been outscored significantly during his playing time against Oklahoma City.

Despite being underdogs given their youth, injuries, and the Thunder's overall quality, the Spurs believe Wembanyama's performances can carry them through. The series will be decided Saturday night in Oklahoma City, where one team will earn a trip to the NBA Finals.

A few minutes ago

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent delivered a speech at the Reagan National Economic Forum warning that decades of United States industrial policy have left the country strategically vulnerable. The speech, titled While America Slept, argued that policymakers have prioritized efficiency over resilience.

Bessent said this approach has hollowed out productive capacity in semiconductors, rare earths, and medicines. The Treasury Secretary's remarks come as the administration faces questions about supply chain security and domestic manufacturing capabilities.

The Reagan National Economic Forum provided a platform for Bessent to outline his concerns about the long-term effects of trade and manufacturing policies. His focus on strategic vulnerability suggests the administration may be considering changes to industrial policy.

The semiconductor industry, rare earth materials, and pharmaceutical manufacturing have all become focal points in recent policy discussions. Bessent's speech connects these sectors to broader questions about national economic security.

By framing the issue as a matter of resilience rather than just efficiency, Bessent appeared to be making the case for a shift in how the government approaches industrial policy. The speech did not detail specific policy proposals but laid out the rationale for why changes might be necessary.

The Treasury Secretary's remarks add to ongoing debates about the role of government in supporting domestic manufacturing. Previous administrations have also grappled with questions about how to maintain critical industrial capabilities while remaining competitive in global markets.

Bessent's speech represents one of the administration's most direct statements about the perceived risks of past policy choices. The focus on strategic vulnerability indicates the Treasury Department views these issues as matters of national importance rather than purely economic concerns.
