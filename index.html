Yesterday, 6:58 PM ET

The federal government has proposed new funding rules that would permit officials to cancel research grants at any time, according to documents describing the regulatory changes. The proposal would also make peer review optional for grant applications and introduce political staff screening of grants for forbidden topics.

The changes represent a shift in how federal research funding would be administered. Under current practice, research grants typically undergo peer review by scientific experts and include defined terms and conditions. The proposed rules would give administrators broader authority to terminate funding arrangements.

Political appointees would gain the ability to screen grant applications for subject matter deemed off limits. This review process would occur alongside or in place of traditional scientific peer review, which has historically served as the primary evaluation mechanism for research proposals.

The peer review system has traditionally involved scientists and subject matter experts assessing the merit and methodology of proposed research. Making this process optional would alter how grant applications are evaluated before funding decisions are made.

The proposal to allow grant cancellations at any time would affect ongoing research projects. Current grant agreements typically specify terms, timelines, and conditions under which funding can be withdrawn. Removing these constraints would give funding agencies more flexibility to end support for research already underway.

Researchers rely on multi-year grants to conduct studies, hire staff, and maintain laboratory operations. The ability to cancel grants without defined cause or process could affect planning and execution of long-term research projects.

The introduction of political staff into the grant screening process adds a layer of review focused on topic restrictions. This differs from scientific peer review, which evaluates research quality, methodology, and potential contributions to a field of study.

The proposed rules have not yet been finalized. Federal regulatory changes typically undergo a comment period before implementation, allowing affected parties and the public to submit feedback on proposed policies.

Yesterday, 9:54 PM ET

A chemical tank rupture at a paper mill in Longview, Washington has claimed nine lives, with two workers still unaccounted for. The disaster occurred Tuesday morning at the Nippon Dynawave Packaging Co facility when an industrial tank holding white liquor ruptured and released its contents across the site.

Recovery crews have retrieved the remains of seven victims so far. The operation shifted from rescue to recovery efforts after authorities determined there was no possibility of finding additional survivors. Matt Amos with the Longview fire department said the recovery process would be "slow, methodical and deliberate," with recovered victims undergoing decontamination before transport to the coroner.

White liquor is a caustic chemical mixture made primarily of sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide, used in the paper industry to break down wood for kraft paper production. The ruptured tank held approximately 600,000 gallons of the liquid. An additional 25,000 gallons remains in the damaged tank and is slowly leaking into surrounding areas.

The incident sent white liquor spilling into a drainage ditch and contaminating the Columbia River. Dead carp were found following the spill. Officials urged the public to avoid ditches and dikes while water testing continues, though they reported no threats to air quality or drinking water in the city of 40,000.

Among the victims were two brothers who worked together at the facility, a trivia champion, and an electrician known for helping farmer neighbors. One worker had arrived early to attend an ultrasound appointment with his partner.

Washington Governor Bob Ferguson stated authorities were "bracing ourselves for this being the deadliest industrial tragedy in modern Washington state history." The sprawling Longview plant employs about 1,000 people and manufactures materials for tissues, printing paper, cups, plates and cartons.

The U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board announced it was launching a federal investigation into the tank failure. The cause remains unknown. Safety complaints were filed against Nippon Dynawave in March and May, though authorities said both were unrelated to the current situation. The company has faced three separate health and safety violations since 2021, resulting in a $3,400 fine.

During a news conference, Brian Wood with Nippon Dynawave stated the company was focused on victims and cooperating with first responders. He pledged the company would "cooperate to the maximum extent" with investigators.

At a community vigil, residents gathered to pray and light candles as families waited for answers. Crystal Moldenhauer, a Longview resident with friends at the plant, expressed the community's uncertainty: "There's families that have been torn apart, and we don't know why."

The facility remains closed as investigations continue.

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Launch Complex 36 in Cape Canaveral, Florida on Thursday evening at 9pm ET. The massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad seconds after the scheduled "hotfire" test began. The explosion was visible across a wide area, with an orange sky reported in Fort Pierce, 115 miles to the south, and shockwaves felt by residents in nearby communities and as far north as South Carolina.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were safe and unaccounted for following the incident. The company described the explosion as "an anomaly" on social media. Jeff Bezos, the company's founder, acknowledged it was "a very rough day" but expressed commitment to investigating the cause and returning to flight operations.

The explosion carries significant implications for NASA's lunar program. Just two days before the test failure, NASA had announced that Blue Origin won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. The New Glenn rocket is the vehicle Blue Origin intends to use to launch lunar landers for NASA, including those that will carry astronauts to the lunar surface as part of the Artemis program.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the timeline for the Artemis program and lunar base construction following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," Isaacman wrote. NASA said it would work with Blue Origin to support a thorough investigation and assess the impact on near-term missions.

This explosion represents the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts. The company's third New Glenn flight in the previous month ended with a payload in the wrong orbit, resulting in a temporary FAA grounding. Thursday's static fire test was the first since the FAA cleared the rocket to resume flights the previous week.

Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which will land astronauts on the moon for the first time since 1972. The two companies are both developing lunar landers and have constructed large new facilities near Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions in partnership with NASA.

Flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the explosion. Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards. The cause of the explosion has not yet been determined, and the FAA has not indicated whether it will launch a new investigation into the incident.

Statistically, Djokovic's record in best-of-five matches after winning the first two sets stood at 301-1 before this loss.

"I didn't, really," Fonseca said when asked if he believed he could win from two sets down. "It's my first time stepping on court with him, he's a legend. I'm just really happy." He added that he was simply focused on hitting the ball hard and was not thinking about the magnitude of the occasion. Fonseca also credited the changing light as the match extended into evening, noting that the court slowed down, which helped his game. Fonseca acknowledged his mother's birthday and thanked Brazilian fans for their support throughout the match. Her collaborations with both George Lucas and Scorsese placed her at the center of some of the most important films of the decade.

Lucas retired from film editing in the early 1980s. She maintained a low public profile in the decades that followed, rarely giving interviews about her work or her time in the film industry. Her death removes one of the last surviving key creative figures from the original Star Wars production.

The Academy Award she received for Star Wars recognized her technical skill and creative vision. That Oscar win cemented her place in film history, even as she chose to step away from the industry that had brought her recognition.
