The military has not disclosed specific information about the suspected origin or destination of the drugs, the nationality of those killed, or whether any narcotics were actually recovered from the vessels. Details about the location of the latest strike remain limited.

The series of operations has raised questions about the scale and intensity of the campaign, particularly given the number of casualties in such a short timeframe. The military has not publicly disclosed the specific legal authority under which the attacks are being conducted or whether the targeted boats were armed.

The Pacific Ocean has long served as a major corridor for drug trafficking organizations attempting to move illegal substances between production areas and consumer markets. The U.S. has conducted maritime interdiction operations in international waters for years, though the lethal force campaign currently underway appears to represent a shift in tactics and scale compared to previous enforcement efforts.

A United Airlines flight traveling from Chicago to Minneapolis was diverted to Madison, Wisconsin, after a passenger made repeated attempts to enter the cockpit. The incident aboard flight 2005 prompted a hijacking alert and an emergency landing on Friday evening.

Crew members reported that the passenger tried to breach the cockpit multiple times during the flight. According to air traffic communications, a crew member told ground control: "I do not believe they ever cuffed him, but they were able to finally get control of him after multiple attempts to try to breach the cockpit. I believe at this point he is seated in a seat and flanked with law enforcement officers on either side."

The aircraft landed safely at Dane County Regional Airport in Madison, where local law enforcement detained the passenger. The FBI in Milwaukee was notified of the diversion and responded to the airport along with local police. An FBI spokesperson confirmed that "a subject was detained by the sheriff's office, and afterwards passengers resumed their flight."

The flight carried 147 passengers and six crew members. No injuries were reported during the incident. United Airlines issued a statement saying flight 2005 "landed safely in Madison to address a security concern with an unruly passenger."

After the passenger was removed and law enforcement secured the scene, the remaining passengers continued their journey to Minneapolis, landing early Saturday morning, according to flight tracking data.

The incident underscores the serious nature of cockpit security in modern aviation. Federal regulations strictly prohibit unauthorized access to aircraft cockpits, and attempts to breach cockpit doors carry significant criminal penalties. Since the September 11 terrorist attacks in 2001, hijackings have become extremely rare in the United States. The last major wave of aircraft hijackings occurred between 1968 and 1972, when more than 130 U.S. planes were hijacked. Many hijackers during that period demanded to be flown to Cuba.

Airlines maintain extensive training protocols for crew members to respond to security threats, and cockpit doors have been hardened specifically to prevent unauthorized entry. The crew's ability to manage the situation and maintain control of the aircraft demonstrates the effectiveness of these safety measures.

The diversion represented a significant disruption for the flight, though most passengers ultimately reached their destination. United Airlines expressed appreciation for the crew's handling of the security incident. The airline did not release additional details about the passenger's identity or potential motivations for the attempted breach. Authorities have not announced charges or provided further information about the investigation.

9:44 AM ET

NASA confirmed that a meteor exploded over the northeastern United States on Saturday afternoon, producing a sonic boom that startled thousands of residents across the Boston area. The fireball streaked across the sky at around 2:06 pm ET and detonated north of Cape Cod Bay.

The explosion created powerful booms that were heard widely throughout the region. Residents reported the unexpected noise shook their houses, and several people captured footage of the bright fireball on camera. The event was visible enough to register clearly in satellite imagery, lighting up the sky in broad daylight.

The initial confusion about what caused the disturbance prompted widespread speculation among residents before NASA provided confirmation. Many people took to social media to report what they had experienced, describing the unexpected shaking and noise that startled the region.

NASA's analysis determined that the sonic boom resulted from the meteor exploding as it entered Earth's atmosphere. When space rocks travel at extremely high speeds through the atmosphere, friction and pressure cause them to heat up dramatically and often break apart. This fragmentation creates the characteristic booming sound that can be heard over large distances.

The agency indicated that fragments from the meteor may have fallen into the water near Cape Cod Bay. NASA has not provided specific details about the exact size of the meteor or precise locations where any debris might have landed.

This type of meteoric event happens periodically but draws particular attention when it occurs over populated areas. The Boston area incident gained widespread notice because of how far the boom was heard and the initial confusion about its source.

According to NASA's website, most space rocks smaller than a football field will break apart in Earth's atmosphere. Though rare, these objects can occasionally explode in the air, as happened in this case over Massachusetts.

Sonic booms from meteors occur when space rocks enter the atmosphere at hypersonic speeds. The extreme conditions cause the rocks to heat intensely and fragment, releasing the energy that produces the audible boom heard across wide areas.

NASA regularly tracks and analyzes meteoric events as part of its ongoing monitoring of near-Earth objects. The agency uses various detection methods, including satellite data and eyewitness reports, to identify and study meteors that enter Earth's atmosphere.

The confirmation from NASA provided clarity about the natural phenomenon that had startled residents across the Boston metropolitan area. The event demonstrated how visible and impactful meteor explosions can be when they occur near population centers, even when the objects themselves are relatively small.
