A few minutes ago

The United States and Iran traded military fire on Thursday in the most serious test yet of their month-long ceasefire. The strikes threatened to unravel ongoing peace negotiations aimed at ending a three-month conflict between the two nations.

According to U.S. Central Command, Iranian forces launched multiple missiles, drones, and small boats at three American destroyers transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The military said it responded by targeting Iranian military facilities responsible for the attacks, including missile and drone launch sites and command centers. The U.S. military reported that no American assets were struck and that Iranian attackers were "completely destroyed."

Iran presented a different account of events. Military officials accused the U.S. of violating the ceasefire by targeting two ships in the strait and attacking civilian areas along the Iranian coast, including the island of Qeshm, home to about 150,000 people. Iran said its armed forces responded by attacking U.S. military vessels, claiming to have inflicted significant damage.

The exchange occurred as the two countries have been negotiating a potential agreement to end hostilities. Reports suggested the nations may have been close to a temporary halt to fighting, with a one-page memorandum being shared between Washington and Tehran through Pakistan. However, senior Iranian officials have recently rejected concessions, with some preferring to drag out negotiations closer to the U.S. midterm elections in November.

President Trump downplayed the significance of the strikes, describing them as a "love tap" during an interview with ABC News. He insisted the ceasefire remained intact despite the military exchanges and said a deal "might not happen, but it could happen any day." Trump also claimed that Iran wants the deal more than he does.

The strikes sent oil prices climbing, with Brent crude rising to around $101 a barrel. The volatility reflects concerns about the stability of global energy markets, particularly given the U.S. military's focus on keeping the Strait of Hormuz open through what Trump called "Project Freedom."

Regional diplomats offered varying assessments of the situation. Some believe Iran could overplay its hand by prolonging negotiations, as the current moment offers an opportunity to end the war and claim victory. Others suggest that if no agreement is reached, the U.S. could unilaterally end the conflict and leave Iran under economic sanctions.

Despite the latest military exchanges, neither side appears eager to return to full-scale conflict. U.S. Central Command stated it does not seek escalation but remains positioned to protect American forces. The strikes complicate diplomatic efforts, but both nations have indicated their willingness to continue pursuing a negotiated settlement.

World number one Jannik Sinner's bid to win a first French Open title and complete a career Grand Slam ended in the second round with a five-set defeat to Argentina's Juan Manuel Cerundolo on Thursday.

Sinner won the opening two sets comfortably, taking them 6-3 and 6-2. He appeared to be cruising toward victory when he built a 5-1 lead in the third set. However, the Italian's performance deteriorated dramatically as temperatures reached 33 degrees Celsius on the Paris court. He dropped three consecutive games in the third set and required medical treatment off the court before losing the next two sets and eventually falling 3-6, 2-6, 7-5, 6-1, 6-1.

The defeat represents a seismic shock in the men's tournament, where Sinner had entered as the clear favorite. The 24-year-old arrived at Roland Garros in exceptional form, riding a 29-match winning streak and having just become the second player ever to win every Masters 1000 title. He had achieved this remarkable feat by winning six consecutive titles at Masters level, with his record-breaking streak at such events standing at 34 matches.

Sinner was attempting to join an exclusive group in tennis history. By winning the French Open, he would have completed the career Grand Slam and followed Novak Djokovic as only the second player to capture all four major championships, nine Masters 1000 titles, the ATP Finals, and the Davis Cup.

Before the tournament, Daniil Medvedev assessed the challenge of facing Sinner, saying: "It's super tough. Any match that we saw even this year, because I think he lost only two, the only way to beat him, you need to be at your best for all four, five sets. You need to run, be strong, to serve well, to return well. Everything needs to be on the top level, because him, his game is everything at the top level."

The extreme heat conditions clearly played a significant role in Sinner's collapse. Having dominated the opening sets, his fitness deteriorated markedly as the match progressed and temperatures soared. The medical timeout he received during the contest sparked debate among observers about the demands placed on players in such brutal conditions.

Sinner's early exit opens up the men's draw considerably. His loss removes the dominant force that had overshadowed the tournament and gives other competitors a genuine opportunity to reach the later stages without facing the world number one. His career Grand Slam aspirations will have to wait at least another year, though his loss remains one of the biggest upsets of the tournament's early rounds.

"It is also due to the long period of detention that he says it was the biggest mistake of his life," Mair said outside the courtroom.

Beran A faces charges including terrorist offences and membership of a terrorist organization. He could receive up to 20 years in prison.

According to prosecutors, Beran A planned to target spectators gathering outside Vienna's Ernst Happel stadium, where up to 30,000 people gathered each night with another 65,000 inside the venue. He allegedly intended to use knives or homemade explosives to "kill as many people as possible." Authorities discovered bomb-making materials during a search of his apartment on August 7, 2024, the day before the concerts were scheduled to begin.

The suspect allegedly followed Islamic State video instructions to create a shrapnel bomb and produced a small amount of the explosive triacetone triperoxide. Prosecutors say he also illegally attempted to purchase weapons, including a machine gun and hand grenade. He networked with other extremist members to discuss acquiring weapons and making bombs.

The plot prompted Austrian authorities to cancel Swift's performances in August 2024, devastating fans who had traveled from around the globe for the Eras Tour. "The reason for the cancellations filled me with a new sense of fear, and a tremendous amount of guilt because so many people had planned on coming to those shows," Swift wrote in a statement posted to Instagram.

U.S. intelligence contributed to the decision to cancel the concerts. Fans who gathered in Vienna transformed the city into a gathering place for Swifties, trading friendship bracelets and singing together.

The trial also involves a Slovak national, Arda K, who faces charges related to planned attacks in the Middle East that never occurred. Both men allegedly planned simultaneous attacks across Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and the United Arab Emirates during Ramadan 2024. A third man, identified as Hasan E, is suspected of carrying out a knife attack in Mecca on March 11, 2024, and remains in custody in Saudi Arabia.

The trial is taking place in Wiener Neustadt, about an hour south of Vienna. Additional hearings are scheduled through May 21, with the possibility of more sessions if needed.
