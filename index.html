10:57 AM ET

Chinese intelligence services are using professional networking platforms like LinkedIn to identify and recruit Westerners with access to sensitive information, according to a new security advisory. The warning highlights how public job search sites have become tools for foreign espionage operations targeting individuals in government and private sector positions.

The advisory describes how operatives pose as recruiters or business contacts to build relationships with targets who have access to non-public information. These recruitment efforts often begin with seemingly legitimate job opportunities or professional networking requests, making them difficult for users to identify as potential security threats.

LinkedIn has become a particularly attractive platform for these operations because it provides detailed professional histories, security clearances, and employment information that help operatives identify valuable targets. The platform's business networking culture also makes initial contact appear routine rather than suspicious.

Security officials warn that these recruitment attempts represent a systematic effort to gather intelligence from Western countries. The operatives typically seek information about government operations, defense projects, corporate research, and other sensitive areas. Individuals with access to classified or proprietary information face particular risk.

The advisory encourages professionals to scrutinize unsolicited contact from unknown parties, especially those offering unusually generous opportunities or showing excessive interest in their work. Users should verify the identity of contacts making recruitment offers and report suspicious approaches to security officials. The warning represents part of broader efforts to counter foreign intelligence gathering through social and professional networks.

11:22 AM ET

John Bolton, former national security adviser under President Trump, has agreed to plead guilty to charges involving the mishandling of classified documents. The arrangement resolves a federal case that the Department of Justice filed against him in October 2025.

Bolton will enter a guilty plea to one count of illegal retention of sensitive national security documents. As part of the deal, he has agreed to pay a fine exceeding $2 million. Court records indicate he is scheduled to appear in court on June 26 to formally enter his new plea.

The charges stem from allegations that Bolton improperly retained classified material spanning from April 2018 through August 2025. Prosecutors say the mishandled documents included diaries kept on a computer in his Bethesda, Maryland home and in his Washington DC office. Materials recovered during an August raid contained briefings about weapons of mass destruction, intelligence on foreign adversaries' leadership, and foreign policy information.

Bolton's attorney, Abbe Lowell, has previously argued that the notes were memoir materials rather than officially classified documents and has characterized the prosecution as politically motivated. Lowell has built a legal practice defending individuals he views as targets of retaliatory actions.

Bolton served as Trump's national security adviser before leaving the administration and becoming one of the president's prominent critics. In 2020, he published a memoir titled "The Room Where It Happened," which offered a critical behind-the-scenes account of his time in the Trump administration. The Justice Department initially sought to block the book's publication on national security grounds through a civil complaint filed in June 2020.

The FBI began investigating Bolton during the Biden administration regarding both the memoir and a 2021 foreign hack of his personal email. His book included claims that Trump had discussed repealing presidential term limits, praised China's construction of concentration camps, and sought China's economic support to help him win a second election.

Bolton's prosecution represents part of what his legal team characterizes as a broader pattern. The Justice Department filed charges against multiple Trump critics in a matter of weeks beginning in October 2025. Other prosecutions of Trump's perceived adversaries, including cases against former FBI director James Comey and New York attorney general Letitia James, have faced legal obstacles and widespread accusations of political motivation.

Bolton's case came as Trump claimed he was unaware of the charges against his former adviser, though he described Bolton as a "bad guy." The guilty plea agreement marks a significant legal development for a figure who once held one of the most sensitive positions in the national security apparatus before his public break with the administration.
