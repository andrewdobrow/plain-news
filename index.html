Yesterday, 5:46 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed at least 65 confirmed cases, with 246 suspected cases reported so far, according to health officials. The outbreak is centered in Ituri province, a conflict-affected region that borders Uganda and South Sudan.

The World Health Organization has estimated the death rate at between 30 and 50 percent among confirmed cases. "It's huge. It means that up to five out of 10 people are likely to die," said Anaïs Legand from the WHO's high threat pathogens team.

Médecins Sans Frontières has described the spread of the virus as deeply alarming. The medical charity made this warning as the head of the World Health Organization arrived in the region to support containment efforts.

Uganda's health ministry has confirmed cases of the disease in the country. A 59-year-old man died in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC, marking the virus's spread across borders.

The outbreak poses particular challenges because Ituri province is a mining hub where thousands of people work in close proximity daily. The region is also affected by ongoing conflict between rebel groups. Displaced people are being forced into overcrowded camps to escape fighting, which makes controlling virus transmission significantly harder. These conditions create what experts describe as a perfect storm for disease spread.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has confirmed that the Ebola virus was detected in 13 of 20 samples tested. However, the detected strain is the Bundibugyo variant, for which there is no licensed vaccine. This differs from the more common Zaire strain found in previous outbreaks. Only two previous outbreaks of Bundibugyo have occurred, in 2007 and 2012.

Health workers in the region lack adequate equipment to contain the virus. Cases have been reported mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in the neighboring city of Bunia.

The Africa CDC director general, Dr Jean Kaseya, said the organization was convening an urgent meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies. "Given the high population movement between affected areas and neighbouring countries, rapid regional coordination is essential," he stated.

Aid organizations including MSF are rushing resources to the affected areas. Their response includes establishing treatment centers, distributing protective equipment to medical staff, and educating communities about infection prevention. International health authorities are working with Congolese officials to trace contacts of infected individuals and isolate new cases to prevent further transmission of the virus.

The explosion generated loud booms that were felt and heard across a wide geographic area. The sonic boom was so powerful that it rattled windows and startled people throughout the region.

Reports of the meteor came in from a broad area stretching from Delaware to Montreal, indicating the event was visible and audible across a substantial portion of the northeastern United States and into Canada. The geographic spread of sightings demonstrated the significant atmospheric disturbance created by the meteor's explosion.

Meteors explode when space rocks enter Earth's atmosphere at extremely high speeds. The friction and pressure from the atmosphere cause these objects to break apart. When they travel faster than the speed of sound, the resulting shock wave creates the sonic boom that residents hear and feel.

NASA explained that most space rocks smaller than a football field will break apart in Earth's atmosphere during entry. Though rare, some may explode in the air rather than reach the ground. According to scientific studies, approximately 17,000 meteorites fall to Earth annually.

The explosion over Massachusetts generated significant public attention, with numerous residents puzzled by the sudden boom and uncertain of its source until authorities confirmed the cause. Local media outlets reported extensively on the event as residents described their experiences and shared videos and photos of the incident.

Despite the dramatic nature of the explosion, no injuries or damage have been reported from the meteor event. Such atmospheric events occur periodically when meteoroids large enough to create significant disturbances enter Earth's airspace, though they remain relatively uncommon occurrences.

The incident highlights how visible and audible meteor explosions can be across large geographic areas when objects of sufficient size enter the atmosphere.
