President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, to serve as acting director of national intelligence. Pulte will retain his position overseeing the FHFA while taking on one of the nation's most sensitive security roles.

The appointment came days after Tulsi Gabbard departed from the intelligence director position. Trump announced the decision on his Truth Social platform, stating that "William has deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America."

Pulte, an heir to a home construction company fortune and major Republican donor, has no known background in national security or intelligence work. His prominence in the Trump administration stems from his role at the FHFA, the agency that regulates Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which together back approximately half of all U.S. mortgages.

During his tenure at the housing agency, Pulte has made public allegations against several of Trump's political opponents. He has accused Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook of mortgage fraud, charges Cook has denied. He also played a role in Trump's campaign against former Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell.

The dual appointment represents an extraordinary concentration of responsibility. The director of national intelligence coordinates work across 18 intelligence agencies and serves as the president's primary intelligence adviser. The position, created after the September 11 attacks to improve coordination across the intelligence community, has historically demanded exclusive focus given its scope and sensitivity.

Democratic Senator Mark Warner, vice-chairman of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, criticized the appointment. "Rather than selecting a respected national security professional capable of delivering independent judgments, the president has chosen an official who has demonstrated not just willingness but eagerness to use the authorities of government to pursue political retribution," Warner said.

Warner noted that Pulte had used his FHFA authority to pursue what he characterized as the president's grievances. "Americans have already seen Mr Pulte use the powers of his office at the Federal Housing Finance Agency to pursue the president's perceived political opponents," Warner stated.

A former high-ranking official described Pulte's handling of the Cook case as "very bizarre."

The appointment occurs as the administration manages multiple foreign policy challenges, including ongoing negotiations with Iran and questions surrounding Taiwan policy. Gabbard, during her time as intelligence director, faced marginalization in the role and appeared sidelined as Trump pursued aggressive foreign policy actions.

Pulte's appointment as acting intelligence director while continuing to lead the FHFA means he will simultaneously oversee housing finance policy and manage relationships with the nation's intelligence agencies and foreign intelligence services.

The Trump administration announced a 25% tariff on imports from Brazil, citing what it calls unfair trade practices. The proposal represents the latest in a series of tariff actions as the administration seeks to rebuild its trade enforcement agenda through Section 301 investigations.

Section 301 refers to a provision of the Trade Act of 1974 that allows the United States to investigate and respond to foreign trade practices deemed unfair or discriminatory. The administration has increasingly relied on this mechanism to justify tariffs and other trade restrictions on countries it believes are not trading fairly with American companies.

Brazil is a significant trading partner of the United States, with bilateral trade in goods totaling billions of dollars annually. The country exports a wide range of products to the United States, including steel, aircraft, agricultural goods, and machinery. A 25% tariff would substantially increase costs for American importers and could affect prices for consumers and businesses that rely on Brazilian products.

The tariff announcement comes as the administration has pursued an aggressive trade policy focused on reducing trade deficits and protecting domestic industries. Previous tariff actions have targeted countries including China, Canada, Mexico, and members of the European Union. Some of these tariffs have prompted retaliatory measures from trading partners, leading to disputes that have affected various sectors of the American economy.

The proposal will likely face scrutiny from American businesses that import Brazilian goods, as well as from Brazil itself, which may consider counter-measures. Trade groups representing importers and manufacturers have previously opposed broad tariff increases, arguing they raise costs for American companies and consumers.

This announcement arrives as the Trump administration faces ongoing disputes over its earlier tariff policies. The Supreme Court previously struck down some of Trump's tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, leading to a refund process for roughly 330,000 importers who paid more than $166 billion in fees. Federal agencies have now established a refund process allowing businesses to recover tariff costs through their original customs brokers, with refunds expected within 60 to 90 days.

Major shipping companies have responded to the tariff refund process by pledging to return funds to customers. FedEx and UPS have committed to sharing refunds, while DHL is doing the same. However, larger retailers including Amazon, Apple, and Costco have remained silent on whether they will pass refund money along to their customers.

The implementation timeline and any potential exemptions for the new Brazilian tariff have not been detailed in the announcement. Trade groups and business organizations are expected to respond with concerns about the economic impact on supply chains and consumer prices.

The United Nations has warned that a new El Niño weather pattern could start within weeks, adding heat to a planet already experiencing the effects of climate change. The natural phenomenon, which involves warming ocean temperatures in the Pacific, has historically contributed to increased global temperatures and extreme weather events.

El Niño occurs when warmer than average sea surface temperatures develop in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. These conditions alter atmospheric circulation patterns, affecting weather worldwide. The phenomenon typically appears every two to seven years and lasts nine to twelve months, though some episodes have persisted for longer periods.

The timing raises concerns among climate scientists because global temperatures have already risen due to greenhouse gas emissions. When El Niño conditions combine with underlying warming trends, the result is often record-breaking heat. Previous strong El Niño events have been associated with droughts, floods, and disruptions to agriculture and water supplies across multiple continents.

The UN characterization of this potential El Niño as possibly the strongest in decades suggests the upcoming episode could produce significant temperature increases. Strong El Niño events have historically driven global average temperatures upward by fractions of a degree Celsius, which can translate into tangible impacts on weather patterns, crop yields, and water availability in vulnerable regions.

The warning comes as countries continue efforts to reduce emissions and adapt to changing climate conditions. Scientists monitor ocean temperatures and atmospheric indicators to predict El Niño development and provide advance notice to governments and communities that may face drought, flooding, or other weather extremes. The weeks ahead will determine whether the predicted pattern materializes and how strong it becomes, shaping weather and climate outcomes for much of the next year.

The success of these lower-budget horror films contrasts with the massive spending typical of studio blockbusters. The scale of the attack was significant. Ukraine's air defences intercepted approximately half of the missiles fired, but more than 30 struck civilian targets. In Kyiv, six people died and 66 were injured, including three children. A 24-storey apartment block was hit, triggering a collapse. Other buildings caught fire, including a nine-storey block, and vehicles were damaged by falling debris. Power was cut for 140,000 residents, though utility workers later restored electricity to 110,000.

In Dnipro, at least 12 people were killed and 37 injured. Six people remained missing under the rubble, and a rescuer was killed in a second strike. The mayor accused Russia of deliberately using cluster munitions in residential areas to maximize civilian casualties. In Kharkiv, at least 14 people were injured and numerous homes and vehicles were damaged.

The attack prompted Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to appeal directly to Donald Trump for Patriot missiles. Zelenskyy has repeatedly stated that Ukraine is running out of Patriot interceptors, which are needed to defend against the ballistic and hypersonic missiles Russia is using. He described them as "vital tools" in protecting civilian lives and noted that ballistic missiles represent Moscow's "last major advantage on the battlefield."

The Trump administration has not responded to Zelenskyy's requests. The Patriot systems are in short supply globally after hundreds were used in the February conflict between the United States and Iran.

Ukrainian officials framed the strikes as evidence of Russian desperation. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha stated that Russia is losing on the battlefield and that "no number of missiles can change this." He accused Putin of using terror as his remaining option after Ukraine's recent successes in aerial campaigns, including strikes on Russian oil refineries and ports.

The assault came after Zelenskyy had warned residents to heed air raid alerts, indicating that intelligence suggested a major strike was being prepared. Rescue operations continued as residents sheltered in basements, corridors and metro stations throughout the affected cities.

11:00 AM ET

Two horror films produced by YouTube creators opened this past weekend to combined box office success, marking what industry observers describe as a fundamental shift in how films reach audiences. The results demonstrate that established online communities can translate directly into theatrical performance without traditional studio marketing infrastructure.

Backrooms, produced for approximately $10 million and distributed by A24, earned $81 million in its opening weekend. The film originated from a 4chan creepypasta, an internet horror story format that spread through online communities before being adapted into a feature film. Obsession, made for $750,000, brought in matching box office returns on its opening weekend.

Both films drew audiences primarily through their existing YouTube fan bases rather than conventional marketing campaigns. The production costs for both projects came in well below typical Hollywood studio budgets, which typically range from $50 million to over $200 million for major releases.

The results have prompted discussions across the entertainment industry about changing distribution patterns. Traditional studios spend tens of millions on marketing campaigns alongside their substantial production budgets. The success of these YouTube-originated projects suggests audiences will pay for theatrical experiences based on content they discovered online, regardless of promotional spending.

Industry analysts note the results could influence how studios evaluate projects and allocate resources. Both Backrooms and Obsession benefited from years of community building on YouTube before their theatrical releases, a timeline that differs significantly from traditional film development and marketing cycles. This established fanbase proved more valuable than expensive promotional campaigns in driving opening weekend attendance.

The shift reflects broader changes in how younger audiences discover and consume entertainment. Generation Z viewers increasingly find content through digital platforms rather than traditional media channels. These YouTube creators had already built loyal audiences eager to see their work on the big screen, eliminating the need for extensive studio marketing efforts.

The success of these lower-budget horror films contrasts with the massive spending typical of studio blockbusters. While major film productions allocate enormous budgets to both creation and promotion, the YouTube-originated films achieved comparable or superior opening weekend returns with minimal marketing investment.

The theatrical releases represent a natural progression for content creators who had already developed substantial followings online. Rather than starting from scratch with unknown projects, these filmmakers brought existing communities into theaters, bypassing the traditional path of building awareness through advertising and publicity campaigns.

Moving forward, studios may reconsider how they identify and develop projects. The box office performance of Backrooms and Obsession demonstrates that online popularity can serve as a reliable indicator of theatrical demand. This could lead to increased investment in adapting successful online content and recruiting digital creators with proven audience loyalty, fundamentally changing how the entertainment industry discovers and develops new films.
