Rescue crews have recovered six bodies from a paper mill in Longview, Washington, following a chemical tank rupture earlier this week. Nine workers were reported missing after the incident, with three individuals still unaccounted for and presumed dead.

The tank contained a chemical mixture used in paper manufacturing. The rupture has significantly complicated recovery and cleanup operations at the facility, particularly efforts to manage contaminated water at the site.

According to officials, the confirmed death toll has risen to eight as recovery efforts continue. Eight additional people were injured in the incident, including a firefighter who was responding to the emergency.

The chemical tank's collapse has made recovery operations challenging for emergency responders. Teams have been working to dilute contaminated water and manage the hazardous materials released during the rupture. The exact circumstances that led to the tank failure remain under investigation by authorities.

The incident represents a major industrial accident for the region. Emergency services mobilized quickly to respond to the initial reports, and recovery crews have been working systematically to locate missing workers and manage the site's hazardous conditions.

Local emergency management has coordinated response efforts involving multiple agencies. Officials have emphasized that recovery operations are ongoing as crews work to locate the three remaining missing individuals.

The identities of the recovered victims have not been released by authorities. Families of the missing workers have been notified as recovery and investigation efforts continue.

The paper mill facility has been the focus of extensive emergency response operations. Authorities have stated that they are not currently searching for additional missing persons beyond the three originally reported.

Community support has begun to mobilize for those affected by the disaster. Local organizations have been setting up assistance programs for victims' families, and community members have opportunities to contribute to relief efforts.

Investigators are working to determine what led to the tank rupture and are examining the facility's operations and maintenance protocols. The recovery process has required specialized equipment and expertise due to the hazardous materials involved.

Emergency responders have praised the coordination between different agencies in managing the response. The incident has highlighted the risks associated with chemical storage and handling in industrial facilities.

The site remains an active recovery and investigation zone. Authorities have indicated they will provide updates as the investigation progresses and as recovery operations conclude. The full scope of the damage and detailed findings regarding the incident's cause remain under review.

Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz has indicated that the government's ultimate goal is for large numbers of Palestinians to leave Gaza through what he called "voluntary migration," though human rights activists describe this as ethnic cleansing achieved by making living conditions intolerable.

The expansion to 70 percent control would compress Gaza's 2.2 million Palestinians into less than a third of their original territory. The area is already described as the world's single most overcrowded place. Israeli forces have systematically destroyed buildings in their controlled zone, leaving Palestinians with virtually no space to relocate.

According to UN briefings for humanitarian agencies, Israeli forces have been steadily expanding their positions throughout the ceasefire. In northern Jabalia, tanks have advanced daily with drones targeting any movement near the dividing line. Major League Baseball has submitted its opening proposal in collective bargaining negotiations with the players union, marking the first time since 1994 that the league has formally proposed a hard salary cap system. The offer also includes a $171 million salary floor that would require all teams to meet a minimum payroll threshold.

The proposal comes as MLB seeks to address what ownership describes as competitive balance issues within the league. Team owners have long contended that a salary cap would create a more level playing field between franchises in large markets and those in smaller markets with lower revenues.

Currently, MLB operates under a luxury tax system that penalizes teams for exceeding a certain payroll threshold rather than imposing a strict cap on spending. The introduction of a hard cap would represent a fundamental shift in how the league structures player compensation and team roster construction.

The $171 million salary floor would have significant implications for lower-spending franchises. Teams that currently operate with smaller payrolls would be forced to substantially increase their spending on player salaries to comply with the new requirement.

The last time MLB formally proposed a salary cap was in 1994, a move that triggered a work stoppage that resulted in the cancellation of 938 games and the World Series. That dispute stands as one of the most damaging periods in baseball history and remains a sensitive topic in labor negotiations.

The players union now possesses the proposal and will conduct a comprehensive review of all terms. Union representatives have not yet publicly responded to the offer or indicated their initial reaction to the salary cap proposal. Before the union can move forward with negotiations, player representatives must evaluate the complete proposal and develop their own counteroffer.

This opening position from ownership suggests that negotiations could extend over an extended period as both sides work toward reaching a new collective bargaining agreement. The proposal represents one of the most significant proposed changes to baseball's economic structure in decades.

The path forward will depend heavily on how players and their agents respond to the cap and floor system. If the union views the proposal as a serious starting point for negotiation, discussions could proceed with relative efficiency. However, given the contentious history surrounding salary caps in baseball, there remains the possibility that disagreements could escalate into another work stoppage.

Negotiations between MLB and the players union are expected to continue as both sides attempt to reach consensus on a new agreement before the current labor deal expires.

A few minutes ago

The United States and Iran are close to finalizing a memorandum of understanding that would extend their ceasefire by 60 days and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, though some disagreements remain unresolved.

Vice President JD Vance said Thursday that negotiators were "very close" to completing the agreement but still working through final details. "We're still going back and forth on nuclear stuff," Vance said, indicating that disputes over Tehran's nuclear program continue to be a sticking point.

According to US officials, negotiators have already reached agreement on the framework of a 60-day ceasefire extension. However, the deal still requires final approval from President Trump and Iran's leadership before it becomes official.

The tentative memorandum addresses multiple pressing issues. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping route that has been closed during the conflict, would reopen under the agreement. The deal would also establish a framework for broader negotiations on Tehran's nuclear program, a long-standing concern for the Trump administration.

Pakistan has been playing an active role in mediating talks between the two sides. Pakistan's army chief led a delegation to Tehran to convey messages from Washington, while Pakistan's prime minister traveled to Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Turkey to build regional support for a peace agreement.

Karoline Leavitt, the White House press secretary, said on Wednesday that Washington remained engaged in negotiations despite earlier denials of a formal extension request. She stated that a second round of negotiations would "very likely" be held in Islamabad and that the White House feels "good about the prospects of a deal."

The current ceasefire was set to expire on April 22. Both sides have been engaged in indirect talks aimed at extending the truce, with continued exchanges of messages between Washington and Tehran even after previous rounds of negotiations broke down.

Trump and his advisers have previously believed they were close to a final agreement at earlier stages of the conflict, though those negotiations ultimately stalled. A final comprehensive agreement addressing Trump's nuclear demands would require further intensive negotiations beyond the initial memorandum of understanding.

The three-month conflict has strained regional stability and disrupted global shipping routes. Reports from the region suggest both sides favor prolonging the truce, though significant work remains to resolve underlying disputes.

The tentative agreement represents the most substantial diplomatic breakthrough since the conflict began. However, nothing is considered official until formally announced by the White House. Both sides continue intensive discussions to finalize the remaining details before the ceasefire expires.
