President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, to serve as acting director of national intelligence, replacing Tulsi Gabbard who resigned last month. The appointment has drawn sharp criticism from both Democrats and Republicans, with concerns centered on Pulte's lack of relevant experience for the role.

Pulte, heir to a home construction company fortune, previously had no government experience before Trump appointed him to lead the FHFA, which oversees federal housing lenders Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. He has no known background in national security or intelligence matters. Despite this, Trump stated on his Truth Social platform that "William has deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America." Pulte will continue serving as FHFA director while taking on the intelligence role.

Since arriving at the FHFA, Pulte has used his position to make public allegations against Trump's political opponents. He has accused Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook of committing mortgage fraud, claims Cook has denied. This pattern of behavior has raised concerns among Republicans about potential weaponization of the intelligence apparatus.

Senate Republicans have expressed unease with the selection. John Thune, the Senate majority leader, told reporters that "we don't need a weaponized" national intelligence director. He noted that if Pulte is nominated for a permanent position, "he'll have to go through a confirmation process and hearings and everything else." Thune added: "We need professionals there."

As an acting appointee, Pulte can serve in the role without Senate confirmation. However, a permanent appointment would require Senate approval, a hurdle that may prove difficult given Republican reservations.

Gabbard's time in the position was tumultuous, with the former diplomat largely sidelined as Trump pursued aggressive policies toward Venezuela and Iran. According to two people briefed on the discussions, Trump had been asking cabinet members as early as April whether he should replace her.

Critics have highlighted Pulte's questionable handling of mortgage fraud allegations. One former high-ranking official described his approach in the Cook case as "very bizarre." A former Republican now working in media derided Pulte as a "nepo baby" with zero experience in military, intelligence, or government service.

The Pulte Family Charitable Foundation, which had ties to his family, publicly distanced itself from him in a 2023 statement, saying some of his communications had created confusion and emphasizing he does not represent their organization. The New York Times reported that Pulte no longer has any official connection to PulteGroup, having been pushed off the board in 2020.

The appointment comes as the intelligence community faces significant global security challenges, from counterterrorism operations to monitoring foreign adversaries.

5:55 PM ET

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced on Tuesday that the Trump administration is abandoning its $1.8 billion fund designed to compensate individuals who claimed to be victims of government investigations. During a House appropriations committee hearing, Blanche stated clearly, "We are not moving forward with the fund, period."

The fund faced mounting legal and political obstacles since its announcement two weeks earlier. A federal judge in Virginia temporarily blocked the administration from transferring money from the fund after the nonprofit Democracy Forward filed a lawsuit challenging it. The decision to scrap the program marks a significant retreat on a proposal that had generated intense controversy across both parties.

Blanche explained the administration's rationale for the fund, saying, "The reasons for the fund is something that President Trump talked about for a long time, which is the fact that there were a lot of people in this country who had their government weaponized against them." However, he emphasized that moving forward with the fund was no longer viable.

The proposal had drawn criticism from multiple directions. Republicans including Senate Majority Leader John Thune expressed concern about lack of oversight and the possibility of payments to participants in the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot. Mike Pence, Trump's first-term vice president, called the prospect of compensating rioters "deeply offensive."

Democrats had vowed to force votes blocking the fund and launched investigations into it. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called it "the most brazen act of self-dealing yet and one of the most corrupt schemes ever launched by a president." Several Democratic-led states, including California, New York, Illinois, and Connecticut, proposed 100 percent income taxes on any distributions from the fund.

The fund was established as part of a settlement in Trump's lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service over the leak of his tax returns. The secretive nature of the fund, combined with the lack of transparency about who would receive payments and how much they would get, fueled accusations of corruption.

Despite scrapping the fund, Blanche confirmed that one significant element of the settlement remains in place. The administration is maintaining an agreement that prohibits the IRS from auditing Trump, his family, and related entities. The Justice Department had not defended itself in the original lawsuit against Trump, raising accusations among critics about collusion between the administration and the agency it controls through appointees.

The cancellation removes a major flashpoint between the Trump administration and Congress, though questions about executive power and financial accountability continue to dominate discussions on Capitol Hill.

3:30 PM ET

The U.S. Treasury Department has imposed sanctions on Iran's largest digital asset exchange, accusing the platform of facilitating terror financing and helping the Iranian government bypass international sanctions. The action targets the exchange for its connections to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and its role in moving funds that support Iranian military and proxy operations.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that the United States seized approximately one billion dollars in Iranian cryptocurrency as part of the sanctions enforcement. The seizure represents one of the largest confiscations of digital assets tied to a foreign government and demonstrates the Treasury Department's intensifying focus on cryptocurrency platforms used to circumvent financial restrictions.

The sanctions prevent American individuals and companies from conducting transactions with the exchange and freeze any assets the platform holds within U.S. jurisdiction. Treasury officials described the exchange as a critical financial node for Iran, enabling the government to convert sanctioned funds into usable currency through digital asset trading. Officials stated that the platform processed transactions for entities previously designated for supporting terrorism and weapons proliferation.

Bessent characterized Iran as facing significant financial strain, citing the cryptocurrency seizure as evidence of the regime's reliance on alternative financial channels. According to Bessent's statements, Iran is "negotiating on fumes" regarding current discussions with the United States.

The sanctions action reflects growing U.S. government concern about cryptocurrency use by sanctioned governments and organizations. Digital assets provide a mechanism for moving money across borders without traditional banking infrastructure, making them attractive to countries isolated from the international financial system. The Treasury Department has expanded its capacity to track and seize cryptocurrency in recent years, working with blockchain analysis firms and international law enforcement to identify and freeze digital wallets connected to sanctioned entities.

This action comes amid ongoing tensions between the United States and Iran, including recent military exchanges near the Strait of Hormuz. The Treasury Department's sanctions announcement underscores the administration's strategy of maintaining economic pressure while pursuing diplomatic engagement. The timing reflects broader efforts to restrict Iran's financial capabilities across multiple channels.

Recent developments include military strikes near the Strait of Hormuz and apparent retaliatory actions by Iranian forces. These incidents highlight the tensions surrounding the waterway, which remains a critical shipping lane. Discussion regarding the strait's control and management has become a point of contention in ongoing negotiations between the two nations.

The cryptocurrency seizure represents a significant escalation in the Treasury's enforcement efforts against sanctioned entities using digital assets to evade restrictions. As the U.S. government continues developing tools to monitor blockchain transactions, cryptocurrency exchanges and digital asset platforms face increasing scrutiny from regulators worldwide.

Microsoft also announced Project Solara, a version of Android designed around agents rather than traditional applications, signaling a strategic shift in how the company envisions users interacting with their devices.

The announcement came during the Microsoft Build 2026 developer conference, where the company made several AI-focused reveals. Microsoft also unveiled new AI models intended to reduce its dependence on OpenAI and lower costs for developers. Additionally, Microsoft announced WSL 3, which brings near-native GPU and NPU passthrough capabilities to Windows, enabling local AI processing. The updates position Windows as what Microsoft calls a trusted platform for development in an era the company describes as AI-driven devices.

The Scout assistant is designed to remain active and accessible at all times, functioning as what Microsoft describes as an AI coworker. The internal documents indicating the company's goal to create user addiction raise questions about the boundaries between helpful automation and dependency-driven product design. Microsoft has not publicly addressed the language used in the internal materials.
