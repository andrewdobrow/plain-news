The first Windows personal computer powered by Nvidia processors will be unveiled next week, according to a report from Axios. The device will use Nvidia's new N1X laptop processors, developed in partnership with Microsoft and Arm.

Nvidia, Microsoft, and Arm have all posted cryptic messages on social media ahead of the announcement, which is expected to take place at Nvidia's GTC event. The messages appear to be coordinated teasers for the new Arm-powered Windows PC chip.

The development marks a significant shift in the PC market, where Intel and AMD have long dominated the processor space for Windows machines. Nvidia, best known for its graphics processing units, has been expanding its chip design capabilities beyond GPUs.

The new N1X processors represent Nvidia's entry into the market for central processing units designed for laptops running Microsoft's Windows operating system. The chips are built on Arm architecture, the same technology used in many smartphones and tablets.

Arm-based processors have been gaining traction in the PC market in recent years, offering potential advantages in power efficiency compared to traditional x86 processors. Apple successfully transitioned its Mac computers to Arm-based chips starting in 2020, demonstrating the viability of the architecture for laptop and desktop computing.

The timing of the announcement suggests Nvidia aims to establish itself as a major player in the PC processor market. The company's existing strengths in artificial intelligence and graphics processing could give its laptop chips distinctive capabilities, though specific performance details have not been disclosed.

Microsoft's involvement in the partnership is significant, as the company's support will be essential for ensuring Windows software runs smoothly on the new Nvidia processors. The collaboration between Nvidia, Microsoft, and Arm represents a coordinated effort to introduce a new option for Windows PC manufacturers and consumers.

5:46 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed at least 65 confirmed people, with 246 suspected cases reported across the conflict-affected Ituri province. The disease has also spread to neighboring Uganda, where a 59-year-old man died after traveling from the DRC.

The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, a particularly concerning development since no licensed vaccine currently exists for this variant. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed the virus in 13 of 20 samples tested by the DRC's national research laboratory. Previous Bundibugyo outbreaks occurred in 2007 and 2012.

Cases have been concentrated in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with additional suspected cases in neighboring Bunia. The World Health Organization estimates the death rate for confirmed cases at between 30 and 50 percent, which officials describe as extremely high.

Geographic challenges complicate containment efforts. Ituri province contains mining towns where population movement is constant, creating ideal conditions for disease spread. The region also shares borders with Uganda and South Sudan, raising concerns about transmission across international boundaries. The Africa CDC is coordinating urgent meetings with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, South Sudan, and the WHO to develop a regional response strategy.

Uganda's health ministry confirmed cases within its borders following the death of the traveler from Kampala. Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa CDC, emphasized that rapid coordination among neighboring countries is essential. He stated that the organization stands "in solidarity with the government and people of the Democratic Republic of the Congo as they respond to this outbreak."

The WHO chief visited the affected region to support containment efforts. Medical charity Médecins Sans Frontières described the situation as deeply alarming, particularly due to the rapid geographic spread of cases.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was identified there in 1976. However, the Bundibugyo strain's lack of an available vaccine distinguishes this outbreak from previous episodes. The country's healthcare system faces ongoing resource constraints that complicate epidemic response and treatment capacity.

Dr. Michael Head, a senior research fellow in global health at the University of Southampton, noted that the DRC likely experiences regular outbreaks due to multiple intersecting factors. These include close contact between humans and animal reservoirs, particularly bats, combined with population movement between rural and urban areas, tropical climate conditions, and extensive rainforest coverage.

Health workers and international organizations are establishing treatment centers and distributing protective equipment to staff. The situation remains fluid as authorities continue identifying and confirming additional cases across affected regions.

The task order represented confidence in Blue Origin's capabilities as a provider for critical government missions.

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed that all company employees were accounted for and safe. He described the day as "very rough" on social media but pledged to rebuild and return to flying. The company called the explosion an "anomaly" and said an investigation into the root cause was already underway.

The loss of this rocket now threatens NASA's ambitious timeline.

NASA announced plans on Tuesday for Blue Origin to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of a 20 billion dollar moon base. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that a full evaluation of the program timeline would be conducted following the explosion. This represents the latest setback for Blue Origin in its competition with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from the third flight of New Glenn ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to resume flights.

Blue Origin and SpaceX are both competing to support NASA's Artemis program, which aims to return humans to the moon. NASA plans to evaluate both companies' lunar landers during the Artemis III mission in 2027, which will determine which system ferries astronauts to the lunar surface during the subsequent Artemis IV mission planned for 2028.

Trump requests changes to Iran deal negotiated by his envoys

The request has triggered another round of negotiations between the parties that could extend several days. U.S. officials said Trump wants to finalize the deal but seeks to strengthen...

9:04 PM ET

President Trump asked for several amendments to an agreement his envoys reached with Iranian counterparts during a meeting on Friday, according to administration officials briefed on the matter.

The request has triggered another round of negotiations between the parties that could extend several days. U.S. officials said Trump wants to finalize the deal but seeks to strengthen certain provisions, particularly those involving Iran's nuclear material and program.

The tentative agreement would extend the current ceasefire and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a key shipping lane. The two sides reached the preliminary framework this week, though final approval from both Trump and Tehran remains pending.

Trump announced on Friday that he had spoken with leaders from multiple countries about the deal, including officials from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Bahrain, Turkey, and Pakistan. He also discussed the matter with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Trump stated on his social media platform that "an agreement has been largely negotiated" and that "final aspects and details" would be announced shortly.

According to reports, details of the proposed agreement include opening the Strait of Hormuz without tolls during a 60-day ceasefire extension, allowing Iran to sell oil freely, and holding negotiations on Iran's nuclear program over 30 to 60 days. In exchange, the United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports.

However, Iran's state media reported conflicting positions. Iran's Fars news agency, close to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, stated that Iran would retain control of the Strait of Hormuz, including determining passage routes, timing, and methods. The agency said Trump's assertion that an agreement was nearly final was "inconsistent with reality."

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif later congratulated Trump on his peace efforts and indicated that Pakistan hoped to host another round of talks between the U.S. and Iran very soon. Pakistan's Army Chief Syed Asim Munir has been a key figure in the negotiations, holding recent meetings in Tehran with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf.

The negotiations come as the U.S. military maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports. The diplomatic efforts involve multiple U.S. officials, including special envoy Steve Witkoff and adviser Jared Kushner, working to address Trump's specific concerns.

The ongoing discussions signal that while both sides have made progress toward an agreement, significant differences remain. Trump has indicated he will only accept a deal "where we get everything we want," and has continued threatening military strikes if negotiations fail.
