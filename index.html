Russian drone and missile attacks spanning multiple Ukrainian cities have killed at least ten people and injured dozens, authorities confirmed. The overnight bombardment targeted residential areas in Kyiv, Dnipro, Kharkiv, and Odesa, with apartment buildings suffering direct hits.

In Kyiv, at least four people were killed and 29 others wounded, according to Tymur Tkachenko, head of the capital's military administration. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported that two high-rise apartment buildings were struck, with concerns that people remain trapped beneath the rubble. Emergency crews worked through the morning conducting search and rescue operations at multiple damaged sites.

Dnipro experienced particularly severe impacts, with five people confirmed dead. Four bodies were recovered from the ruins of a destroyed house, and rescue workers continued searching for additional victims Saturday morning. The strikes sparked fires that spread across the city, causing partial destruction to several residential blocks, businesses, and private homes. An additional person was killed in a Russian strike in the same residential area later Saturday afternoon.

The coordinated assault involved more than 600 Russian drones, representing what Ukrainian authorities described as the largest attack in several days. Ukraine's air defenses repelled most of the incoming weapons, though significant damage still occurred. Drones also targeted Kharkiv and Odesa, where at least three people were injured.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy commented on the attacks through social media, stating: "The Russians' tactics have not changed: strike drones, cruise missiles and a significant amount of ballistics. Most of the targets are ordinary infrastructure in cities. Residential buildings, energy, and enterprises have been damaged."

The strikes came after Ukrainian officials had warned of a potential large-scale Russian assault. The attack reflects Russia's ongoing campaign deliberately targeting civilian infrastructure and population centers throughout the country.

Separately, Ukraine carried out some of its longest-distance drone strikes deep into Russian territory. In Russia's Belgorod region, a woman was killed and a man seriously injured in a Ukrainian drone strike.

The overnight bombardment followed a prisoner exchange on Friday, in which Russia and Ukraine exchanged 193 service members. These periodic exchanges were established as a condition of US-brokered negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv, though discussions have achieved no progress on key issues that would lead to ending Russia's invasion.

As rescue operations continued across affected regions, the full casualty count remained uncertain. Ukrainian emergency services mobilized across multiple cities to respond to the widespread destruction from the coordinated assault.

Appeals court rules Trump transgender military ban unconstitutional

Yesterday, 10:49 PM ET

A federal appeals court panel ruled Monday that the Trump administration unconstitutionally expelled active transgender service members under a policy implemented by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The divided decision restricts the ban while allowing the armed services to continue blocking transgender people from enlisting.

The three-judge panel from the US Court of Appeals for Washington DC found the expulsion of active-duty troops "arbitrary, and based on animus," according to the majority opinion. The ruling represents a significant legal challenge to one of the Trump administration's major military personnel policies.

Hegseth responded to the decision by signaling his intention to appeal to the Supreme Court, writing "see you at SCOTUS" in a statement. The defense secretary has defended the ban as necessary for military readiness, though critics argue it discriminates against service members based solely on gender identity.

The policy removed active-duty transgender troops from military service, reversing previous rules that allowed them to serve openly. Transgender service members had been permitted to serve openly under previous administrations before Hegseth reinstated restrictions. Advocacy groups representing transgender troops argued the ban disrupted military careers and forced out qualified personnel.

The split decision deals a blow to the Trump administration's anti-diversity agenda. The court found that while the government cannot force out active service members, it can continue preventing new transgender recruits from enlisting. This distinction leaves uncertainty about how the policy will ultimately function across the military.

The appeals court decision is expected to be challenged by the government, and the case is likely to reach the US Supreme Court. The justices have previously ruled on transgender rights cases, though not specifically addressing military service. A Supreme Court decision could take months or longer, leaving the status of affected service members in limbo during the appeal process.

The ruling addresses a fundamental constitutional question about whether the military can discriminate against a group of people based solely on gender identity. Those supporting the ban contend it affects military readiness and unit cohesion, while opponents argue there is no evidence supporting these claims and that the policy amounts to unconstitutional discrimination.

The case represents the latest legal battle over Trump administration policies involving LGBTQ rights and military service. Transgender troops have faced ongoing uncertainty about their place in the armed forces as administrations have changed positions on the issue multiple times in recent years.

The divided nature of the appeals court panel suggests the judiciary remains split on the constitutional questions at stake. With the case heading to the Supreme Court, the final outcome will likely have far-reaching implications for transgender military service and civil rights protections under the Constitution.
