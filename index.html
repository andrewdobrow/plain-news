Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps seized two ships in the waterway on Wednesday, accusing them of attempting to exit covertly. The Panama-flagged MSC Francesca and Liberia-flagged Epaminondas were brought to shore. The seizures represent the first time Iran has taken control of vessels since the war began on February 28. A UK-based maritime security monitor reported that one ship was damaged when approached by an Iranian gunboat.

The moves came after the U.S. fired on and seized an Iranian cargo vessel and boarded an Iranian oil tanker in the Indian Ocean, further escalating the standoff.

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliament speaker and lead negotiator, said on Wednesday that reopening the strait would be "impossible" while the U.S. and Israel continued breaching the ceasefire. He pointed to the U.S. naval blockade, describing it as "the hostage-taking of the world's economy."

The White House responded by doubling down on its pressure campaign. Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said President Trump was "satisfied" with the naval blockade and that Iran was in a weak position, losing approximately $500 million daily through economic strangulation.

Scheduled peace talks in Islamabad failed to materialize. Pakistan had prepared to host negotiations with a U.S. delegation led by Vice President JD Vance, but the talks never occurred. A Pakistani official told Reuters the cancellation was unexpected, noting that Iran had indicated willingness to participate but the U.S. delegation never left Washington.

The blockade has created a global economic crisis. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remain stranded in the Gulf. The International Maritime Organization has appealed for assistance for the trapped crews. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas during normal times.

The economic impact extends beyond the Middle East. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in emergency aid for households and farmers struggling with fuel costs and supply shortages.

This conflict represents a significant departure from Trump's previous rejection of Iran nuclear agreements. During his first term, he withdrew from the nuclear deal that limited Iran's enrichment program, a decision discouraged by Israel, Iran's regional adversary.

The situation remains volatile, with both sides entrenched in their positions and little progress toward resolving the underlying disputes over nuclear capabilities and regional security.

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida on Thursday night, dealing a significant blow to the company's heavy-lift rocket program and raising questions about NASA's ambitious lunar plans.

The explosion occurred at 9pm ET during a scheduled "hotfire" test at Launch Complex 36. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad, sending shockwaves across Florida's space coast. The blast was visible from more than 115 miles away in Fort Pierce, and residents in South Carolina reported seeing a glow in the sky. Flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the incident.

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed that all employees were safe and accounted for. He acknowledged it was "a very rough day" but expressed confidence the company would recover. Blue Origin described the incident as "an anomaly" without providing further details about the cause.

The timing of the explosion creates major complications for NASA's lunar program. Just two days before the blast, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won the contract to launch the first of three uncrewed lunar missions planned for this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA's goal is to return humans to the moon within two years and complete the base construction.

The New Glenn rocket was specifically selected to launch lunar landers for NASA, including the vehicles that will eventually carry astronauts to the lunar surface. Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide the lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission scheduled for 2028, which would mark humanity's first moon landing since 1972.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the timeline's impact following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," Isaacman stated on social media.

This represents the latest in a series of setbacks for the New Glenn program. Last month, a payload from the rocket's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the vehicle. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight.

The explosion adds pressure on Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts and attempts to establish itself as a major player in the commercial launch market. Both companies have built large facilities at Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions with NASA.

Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards from the explosion.

Federal judge blocks Trump administration fund for alleged government targeting victims

A federal judge in Virginia temporarily blocked the Trump administration from transferring money into a $1.776 billion fund designed to compensate people who claim they were wrongfully targeted by the government. U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema issued the order Friday, directing the Department of Justice to halt further action on the fund. The order prevents the government...

A few minutes ago

A federal judge in Virginia temporarily blocked the Trump administration from transferring money into a $1.776 billion fund designed to compensate people who claim they were wrongfully targeted by the government.

U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema issued the order Friday, directing the Department of Justice to halt further action on the fund. The order prevents the government from considering claims or disbursing any money while the legal challenge proceeds. Judge Brinkema, a Bill Clinton appointee, indicated the pause is necessary to ensure no funds are irreversibly transferred before she can rule on a motion to block the fund permanently.

The Trump administration created the fund to resolve a $10 billion lawsuit filed by Donald Trump and his sons over the leak of Trump's tax returns. The government declined to take preliminary steps to defend itself in the lawsuit, prompting accusations of improper coordination and corruption.

The administration has described the fund as a mechanism to compensate anyone who claims to be a victim of government weaponization. The fund would be controlled by four commissioners appointed by the U.S. attorney general and a fifth appointed in consultation with congressional leadership. All commissioners can be fired by Trump at will.

The fund has generated significant controversy. There are no restrictions on who can apply for compensation, raising concerns that January 6 rioters who assaulted police officers could receive payments. Additionally, the fund is not required to disclose how much money it pays to individual applicants or explain its reasoning, leading critics to characterize it as a slush fund.

The funding would come from the judgment fund, a perpetual government appropriation used to settle cases. Critics argue this approach allows Trump to circumvent Congress's power over government spending.

The temporary block came in a lawsuit filed by the watchdog group Democracy Forward seeking to block the fund. The suit includes claims from a former federal prosecutor who led efforts to prosecute January 6 protesters, a California professor acquitted of charges related to immigration agents, the Connecticut city of New Haven, Common Cause, and the National Abortion Federation. Police officers who defended the Capitol on January 6 have also filed a separate legal challenge to the fund in the District of Columbia.

Judge Brinkema's order preserves the current situation pending further legal arguments. The ruling does not address the merits of the underlying challenge to the fund but instead functions as a procedural measure to prevent irreversible actions while the court evaluates whether to extend or lift the temporary restrictions.

The Justice Department has not publicly commented on whether it will appeal the temporary block.
