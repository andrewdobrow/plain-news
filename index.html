President Trump has named Bill Pulte, the head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, to serve as acting director of national intelligence, replacing Tulsi Gabbard who recently left the role.

Pulte, heir to the PulteGroup home construction fortune, has no known experience in national security or intelligence matters. He currently oversees the Federal Housing Finance Agency, which regulates the federal housing lenders Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Trump stated that Pulte will retain his housing agency position while taking on the intelligence director role.

"William has deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America," Trump said on his Truth Social platform, though Pulte's background is primarily in real estate and housing finance rather than intelligence or national security.

The appointment has drawn sharp criticism from several quarters. Senate Majority Leader John Thune expressed concern about Pulte's suitability, telling reporters that "we need professionals there" and warning that Pulte would have "a lengthy road ahead of him" if Trump nominates him permanently, which would require Senate confirmation and hearings. Thune also cautioned against having a "weaponized" intelligence director.

Senator Cassidy stated publicly that Pulte lacks the competence required for the intelligence director role.

Critics have highlighted concerns about Pulte's tenure at the housing agency. Since taking over the Federal Housing Finance Agency, he has made extraordinary allegations against Trump's political opponents, accusing Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook and others of mortgage fraud. Cook has denied the allegations. One former high-ranking official described his handling of the Cook case as "very bizarre."

Pulte has also faced scrutiny for his family connections. Though often described as part of the PulteGroup empire due to his family ties, the New York Times reported that Pulte no longer holds any official position with the company, having been pushed off its board in 2020. The Pulte Family Charitable Foundation, which his aunt and father run, publicly distanced itself from him in 2023, stating that "Bill Pulte is not a representative of, nor has he served in any capacity" for the foundation.

The director of national intelligence oversees the entire U.S. intelligence community, coordinating the work of agencies including the CIA, NSA, and FBI. The position normally requires Senate confirmation, though acting directors can serve temporarily without it. The intelligence director is responsible for briefing the president on national security threats and managing relationships with foreign intelligence services.

Gabbard's departure from the role came after a turbulent tenure in which she appeared marginalized as Trump pursued aggressive foreign policy actions against Venezuela and Iran. According to multiple sources, Trump had been asking cabinet members as early as April about replacing her.

6:40 PM ET

Colorado enacted legislation requiring stricter scrutiny of ride-sharing companies Uber and Lyft. The law responds to findings that these companies received far more reports of sexual assault and misconduct than they had publicly disclosed.

The legislation cites a New York Times investigation that uncovered the discrepancy between the number of sexual assault and sexual misconduct reports Uber received and what the company shared with the public. The investigation revealed a pattern of underreporting that raised concerns about passenger safety and corporate transparency in the ride-sharing industry.

Under the new law, Colorado will impose additional regulatory requirements on ride-sharing platforms operating in the state. The measure aims to improve accountability and ensure companies provide accurate information about safety incidents to both regulators and the public. The law represents one of the most significant state-level regulatory actions targeting ride-sharing companies in recent years.

The ride-sharing industry has faced mounting pressure to address safety concerns, particularly regarding incidents of sexual assault and harassment involving drivers and passengers. Companies have implemented various safety features in recent years, including in-app emergency buttons and background checks for drivers, but critics argue these measures have not gone far enough.

Colorado's action could set a precedent for other states considering similar legislation. Ride-sharing companies operate in a patchwork of state and local regulations across the country, with safety standards varying significantly by jurisdiction. The new Colorado law adds to growing momentum for stronger oversight of the industry at the state level.

The Colorado legislation comes at a time when ride-sharing platforms face scrutiny on multiple fronts. Beyond safety concerns, drivers have also raised complaints about compensation and working conditions. As fuel prices have risen significantly in recent months, independent contractors working for these platforms have expressed frustration about inadequate support from their employers.

Drivers across the country have reported that rising fuel costs have substantially cut into their earnings without corresponding increases in pay from Uber and Lyft. The companies have responded by expanding discount programs and rewards through financial services products, though drivers have expressed skepticism about the effectiveness of these measures.

The Colorado law specifically addresses sexual assault and misconduct reporting, requiring companies to maintain higher transparency standards with both state regulators and the general public. By mandating stricter oversight, the state aims to hold ride-sharing platforms accountable for accurately disclosing safety incidents rather than minimizing or hiding them from public view.

As more states consider implementing similar regulations, the ride-sharing industry faces the prospect of increasingly fragmented regulatory requirements across different jurisdictions. Companies operating nationally will need to adapt their safety protocols and reporting procedures to comply with varying state standards. Colorado's approach reflects broader concerns about corporate accountability and passenger protection in the rapidly evolving gig economy.

5:37 PM ET

Microsoft introduced Scout, a new artificial intelligence assistant designed to function as a constant workplace companion for office workers. The software giant unveiled the product at its Build developer conference, positioning it as an autonomous agent that operates continuously rather than requiring manual activation.

Internal Microsoft documents reveal the company aims to make users dependent on the new tool. The documents state Microsoft wants to "make people addicted" to Scout, which represents a shift in how the company frames AI deployment in professional settings. The assistant is built on technology called OpenClaw and functions differently from traditional software by working autonomously rather than waiting for user commands.

Scout operates as what Microsoft calls a personal agent, designed to handle tasks typically managed by executive assistants. The system remains active constantly, monitoring workflows and responding to needs without requiring users to open specific applications or issue direct commands. Microsoft is also developing Project Solara, an Android-based operating system designed specifically for AI agents rather than traditional mobile applications.

The launch represents Microsoft's broader strategy to embed AI throughout its business products. The company also announced new AI models intended to reduce its dependence on OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT in which Microsoft holds a significant investment. The new models aim to lower costs for developers building AI-powered applications on Microsoft's platform.

Microsoft is testing a wearable AI device aimed at office workers alongside the Scout software. The hardware component would extend the AI assistant's reach beyond computer screens, though specific details about the wearable's capabilities remain limited. The dual approach of software and hardware reflects Microsoft's effort to compete in a market where multiple technology companies are racing to deploy AI tools that integrate into daily work routines.

Liverpool have agreed a deal in principle with Andoni Iraola to become their next head coach following the sacking of Arne Slot. The Spaniard has progressed quickly through negotiations with the club's sporting director Richard Hughes and is set to sign a two-year contract at Anfield.

Slot was dismissed with immediate effect after a troubled title defence that saw Liverpool suffer 19 defeats across all competitions. The season was overshadowed by the death of Diogo Jota. Although the club secured Champions League qualification on the final day, Liverpool's hierarchy decided a change in direction was necessary. In a statement, the owners acknowledged the difficulty of the decision but explained that "the team's trajectory is best addressed through a change of direction."

The club invested almost 450 million pounds in new signings last summer but saw significant regression this season. High-profile recruits, including Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak, failed to deliver the expected impact, while established players including Mohamed Salah and Ibrahima Konaté suffered severe drops in form. Problems at set pieces and in open play affected the team throughout the campaign.

Liverpool believe the team's playing style needs to evolve toward a more aggressive, front-foot approach to respond to changes within the Premier League. This tactical vision makes Iraola the clear frontrunner for the position. The Basque coach has built a reputation at Bournemouth for developing attractive, possession-based football since his appointment by Hughes, who now holds the role of sporting director at Liverpool.

Iraola is expected to bring his assistants Tommy Elphick and Shaun Cooper with him from Bournemouth. Both men are under contract at the south coast club, where they valued their experience of playing and coaching in England. However, Bournemouth want them to form part of incoming head coach Marco Rose's staff. The fitness coach Pablo de la Torre, the only staff member Iraola brought from Rayo Vallecano, is out of contract and could make the move.

Iraola faces competition for his signature from other clubs. Milan, Bayer Leverkusen and Crystal Palace have shown interest, but he has not committed to his next role so far.

Liverpool acknowledged that Slot's dismissal appears unfair considering his achievement in winning a record-equalling 20th league title in his first season. The club paid tribute to his handling of the difficult period following Jota's death, describing the compassion and humanity he showed. Slot responded to his departure by saying it had been "an amazing ride" and expressed his gratitude for winning the league.

The club aims to install a replacement quickly as they prepare for the new campaign.
