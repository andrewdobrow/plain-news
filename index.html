The House of Representatives passed a war powers resolution on Wednesday directing President Trump to end military hostilities with Iran, marking a rare bipartisan rebuke to the administration. The measure passed 215-208, with four Republicans joining Democrats in support.

The four Republicans who broke ranks were Thomas Massie of Kentucky, Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania, Warren Davidson of Ohio, and Tom Barrett of Michigan. The vote represented the first success after three previous attempts to pass similar legislation had failed.

War powers resolutions allow Congress to direct the withdrawal of U.S. armed forces from hostilities abroad without requiring presidential approval, though presidents have historically challenged their constitutional authority. The passage of this resolution signals growing unease among lawmakers about the scope and duration of military engagement with Iran.

Despite the symbolic victory for congressional oversight, the measure faces significant practical obstacles to becoming law. Democrats have been unable to advance a war powers resolution in the Senate, where Republican control has blocked similar efforts. Even if the Senate were to pass the measure, President Trump would likely veto it, making passage unlikely in both chambers.

The House vote capped a series of legislative setbacks for the Trump administration in Congress. The measure asserts Congress's constitutional authority over declarations of war and directs the president to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran. The four Republicans who crossed party lines reflected growing erosion in GOP support for the conflict.

The measure now moves to the Senate, where its fate remains uncertain. Republican leadership has demonstrated little appetite for challenging the president on foreign policy matters, making passage in that chamber unlikely. The House vote nonetheless marks a significant moment in the ongoing debate over executive war powers and congressional authority in matters of military conflict.

The bipartisan nature of the vote, though narrow, highlights rising concerns among lawmakers about the direction of U.S. military policy in the region. The resolution represents an assertion of congressional power at a time when some lawmakers have expressed frustration with executive authority over military decisions.

The passage comes as tensions over executive power have intensified across multiple policy areas. The House vote demonstrates that despite Trump's significant influence over the Republican Party, some members remain willing to challenge the administration on major national security decisions.

While the resolution's path forward in the Senate appears blocked, the House action sends a message about congressional sentiment regarding the Iran conflict. Whether additional pressure will mount for Senate action or whether the measure will ultimately be vetoed remains to be seen, but the vote underscores persistent legislative disagreement over the administration's foreign policy approach.

The spacecraft went silent after completing a normal orbit behind Mars. All systems appeared to be fun...

3:50 PM ET

NASA has officially concluded the MAVEN mission after losing contact with the spacecraft more than six months ago. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution orbiter stopped communicating with ground stations over a weekend and engineers were unable to restore the connection.

The spacecraft went silent after completing a normal orbit behind Mars. All systems appeared to be functioning properly before it passed behind the planet, according to NASA. When MAVEN reappeared from behind Mars, ground control received no signal. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly, but communication could not be reestablished.

MAVEN launched in 2013 and reached Mars orbit in 2014, beginning its study of the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with solar wind. Over more than a decade of operation, the orbiter collected crucial data about how Mars transformed from a potentially habitable world into the cold, dry planet it is today. Scientists determined that solar wind stripped away most of the Martian atmosphere over billions of years, carrying it into space and changing the planet's climate.

Beyond its primary research mission, MAVEN served an important support function for NASA's surface exploration efforts. The orbiter acted as a communication relay for the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers, helping transmit data from these exploration vehicles back to Earth.

NASA hosted a media call to announce the official end of the mission. The agency acknowledged the emotional impact of losing a spacecraft that had operated so long and contributed so much to Mars science. Scientists described the loss in personal terms, comparing it to losing a loved one.

The loss of MAVEN represents the end of one of NASA's most productive Mars missions. The research conducted by the orbiter provided essential insights into atmospheric loss processes that occur on planets throughout the solar system. Understanding how Mars lost its atmosphere has broader implications for studying planetary climates and the conditions necessary for habitability.

Despite the loss of MAVEN, NASA maintains an active presence at Mars. Two other spacecraft continue to operate in Mars orbit: the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, which launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, which launched in 2001. These missions continue gathering data about the red planet and supporting surface operations.

NASA has not announced plans for a direct replacement for MAVEN. However, the agency remains committed to studying the Martian atmosphere and climate history through future missions. The data already collected by MAVEN will continue to inform scientists' understanding of how planets lose their atmospheres and the solar wind's role in shaping planetary environments.

The director announced the decision through a public statement released Wednesday, instructing streaming services, television networks, and distribution partners to stop making the film publicly available.

In his statement, Wenders acknowledged that the young actor should have received better protection during the filming process. The decision marks a significant moment for the acclaimed filmmaker, who is known for directing Paris, Texas and Wings of Desire, among other celebrated works.

The withdrawal removes Wrong Move from all forms of distribution nearly five decades after its initial release. The film will no longer be accessible through theatrical screenings, home video releases, or streaming platforms where it previously appeared.

The decision reflects evolving cultural standards regarding the depiction of minors in cinema. In recent years, conversations within the film industry have intensified around content involving children and the responsibility that filmmakers and rights holders carry when reassessing older works. Similar discussions have emerged regarding other films from previous decades that included scenes now widely considered inappropriate or exploitative.

Kinski, who became a prominent actress during the 1970s and 1980s, was at the beginning of her career when Wrong Move was filmed. She has not made public statements about the withdrawal of the film.

Wenders' decision to voluntarily withdraw the work demonstrates an approach some filmmakers have taken to address problematic content in their own catalogs rather than waiting for external pressure or public controversy to force their hand. The director's foundation will oversee the removal of the film from distribution networks.

The withdrawal of Wrong Move represents one of the more visible examples of a filmmaker taking direct action regarding archival content that raises serious ethical concerns about child protection. As cultural attitudes continue to shift regarding the treatment and representation of minors in entertainment, other filmmakers and studios may face similar decisions about works in their collections.

The film had remained in distribution for nearly 50 years before this withdrawal, suggesting that awareness of the problematic scene may have increased in recent years as discussions around child safety in film production have become more prominent. Wenders' statement indicates a recognition that industry standards have changed significantly since the film's production in the mid-1970s.

A few minutes ago

Israel and Lebanon have agreed to implement a full ceasefire, contingent on Hezbollah halting attacks and withdrawing its operatives from the area south of the Litani River. The agreement was announced in a joint statement from the United States, Israel, and Lebanon.

Hezbollah has already indicated it would agree to a full ceasefire, but whether the Shia militia will accept the specific terms negotiated by Israel and the Lebanese government remains unclear. The Litani River, located in southern Lebanon, serves as a key geographic marker in the agreement, positioned approximately 18 miles north of the Israeli border.

The conditional nature of the ceasefire places significant responsibility on Hezbollah's compliance. The Lebanese government must ensure the militia withdraws its forces from southern Lebanon, while Israel has conditioned its participation on a complete cessation of attacks from Hezbollah. The United States brokered the agreement between the two nations.

However, the fragile ceasefire has already faced serious tests. Israel conducted a massive strike on Lebanon that killed more than 300 people, drawing widespread international condemnation. Israeli officials claimed the attacks specifically targeted Hezbollah members and command posts, though the strikes hit densely populated residential areas in central Beirut and other civilian centers without warning.

The timing of the strikes raised questions about Israel's commitment to the agreement. Hezbollah stated it had been "notified of a ceasefire" and had been "committed to it since this morning," according to Lebanese political sources. Despite this announcement, Israeli strikes continued, and by Thursday, Hezbollah and Israel were trading heavy fire again.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said the Israeli strikes violated the ceasefire agreement and would render negotiations meaningless. Senior Iranian officials warned of a response against Israel. The Soufan Center thinktank noted that the scale of Israel's strikes was likely to be viewed as escalatory regardless of ceasefire boundaries.

Some analysts suggested Israel may have been unhappy with the ceasefire negotiations. The Wall Street Journal reported that Israel was informed of the deal only at the last minute and "wasn't happy." This has raised concerns about Washington's ability to manage its relationship with Israel while pursuing de-escalation in the Middle East.

According to security experts, Israel's continued military operations in Lebanon could undermine the ceasefire overall and keep the United States trapped in a conflict it is seeking to exit. The Israeli Defense Forces' own assessment reportedly concludes that disarming or defeating Hezbollah is unrealistic despite the latest invasion and bombing campaign.

The ceasefire represents a potential end to hostilities between Israel and the Iran-backed militia, which operates from southern Lebanon. Whether the agreement takes effect depends on Hezbollah's acceptance of the withdrawal requirement. The joint statement did not specify enforcement mechanisms or timelines for implementation.
