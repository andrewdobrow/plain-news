The Florida Supreme Court has upheld a gas utility's rate increase, a decision that will affect customers across the state. The ruling came Thursday, marking the conclusion of a legal challenge to the rate hike that had been approved by state regulators.

The decision allows the utility to maintain higher rates that will impact monthly bills for natural gas customers throughout Florida. Gas utilities in the state are regulated by the Public Service Commission, which approves rate adjustments based on factors including infrastructure costs, maintenance expenses, and operational needs. When rate increases are approved, customers or consumer advocacy groups can challenge those decisions in court.

The Supreme Court's ruling means the rate increase will remain in effect for residential and commercial customers who rely on natural gas for heating, cooking, and other uses. Florida's natural gas customers span the entire state, with utilities serving millions of households and businesses. Rate decisions by the court have direct financial implications for families and companies managing energy costs.

The legal challenge to the rate hike had sought to overturn or modify the increase, arguing that the approved rates were excessive or unjustified. However, the state's highest court found sufficient basis to uphold the utility's rate structure as approved by regulators. This decision reinforces the authority of the Public Service Commission to set utility rates and limits the circumstances under which such determinations can be successfully challenged in Florida courts. Customers will continue to see the higher rates reflected in their monthly gas bills going forward.

Florida Property Tax Cut Proposal Could Raise Fees and Business Costs for Treasure Coast Residents

Yesterday, 9:02 PM ET

Florida lawmakers passed a property tax cut proposal Tuesday that would increase the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $250,000 over two years, with inflation adjustments beginning in 2029. The measure would also lower assessment caps on non-homesteaded properties from 10% to 5%. The bill still needs approval from 60% of voters in November.

Experts are divided on what the ripple effects could mean for Treasure Coast residents. Real estate attorney Steve Wallace, based in Boynton Beach, raised the possibility of new fees tied to local services. Wallace said there could potentially be non-ad valorem taxes assigned to the sheriff's office, fire rescue, and other county or local government services. He noted that fees may increase because counties and local governments will need to find other ways to generate revenue.

CPA Paul Miller suggested businesses could end up carrying more of the financial burden to offset lost revenue. Miller pointed to potential increases in annual fees on tax returns and other Florida business fees as ways to move the needle. Municipal attorney Raul Gastesi offered a different perspective, suggesting the measure could actually push home prices higher because home buyers would have more money at their disposal.

Danielle Blake, Chief of Residential Advocacy at MIAMI REALTORS + RWorld, was skeptical of that theory. Blake said there are many other factors that go into home prices besides property taxes, pointing to interest rates as a more significant barrier to sales. All four experts acknowledged there are significant unknowns about how counties, municipalities and markets might respond to decreasing property tax revenue. As with any ballot measure that passes, lawmakers would need to return to Tallahassee to work out the specifics in a bill to implement the changes.

Port St. Lucie woman sentenced to 3 years in prison for running illegal med spa out of backyard shed

Yesterday, 10:35 PM ET

Rosa Mena, 50, of Port St. Lucie has been sentenced to three years in prison and five years of probation after operating an illegal medical spa out of a shed in her backyard. The case began in May 2025 when a victim reported partial facial paralysis after receiving Botox injections at the makeshift facility on Northwest Coosa Drive, which Mena called Miracle Hand and Spa.

The victim told Port St. Lucie police she received 62 units of Botox during a session and had been referred to the business by coworkers who had previously used Mena's services. After the paralysis began, Mena refunded the $325 fee and asked the victim to return for vitamin injections to reverse the condition. When the victim requested proof of Mena's medical credentials, Mena provided an altered phlebotomy certificate that had actually expired in February 2024. Mena also told the victim she was a doctor from the Dominican Republic.

A multi-agency investigation involving Port St. Lucie Police, PSL Code Enforcement, the PSL Building Department, PSL Business Tax, and the Florida Department of Health led to a search warrant at Mena's residence in the 5000 block of Northwest Coosa Drive. Detectives discovered medical beds, fat sculpting machines, laser hair removal equipment, laser liposuction machines, and injection devices for fillers and Botox.

Mena was charged with three counts of aggravated battery causing bodily harm, three counts of practicing medicine without an active license, one count of fraud to obtain property valued at $300 or more, one count of fraud to obtain property under $20,000, and one count of using a two-way communication device to facilitate a felony. The case underscores the dangers of unlicensed medical procedures and the importance of verifying provider credentials before undergoing cosmetic treatments.
