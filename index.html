12:10 PM ET

The Securities and Exchange Commission proposed eliminating a regulation that would have required publicly traded companies to disclose climate-related risks. The rule would have mandated that all public companies report whether they faced significant risks from climate change and its effects.

The proposed reversal marks a shift in regulatory approach to corporate climate disclosures. The original regulation aimed to standardize how companies inform investors about climate-related financial risks.

The SEC has not yet provided details on the timeline for finalizing the proposal or the reasoning behind the decision to eliminate the disclosure requirement. Public companies would no longer need to report on climate risks if the proposal becomes final.

The regulation was part of broader efforts to increase transparency around environmental risks in financial markets. Investors and analysts had looked to such disclosures to assess how climate change might affect company operations and valuations.

Corporate reporting standards for environmental risks vary widely without a mandatory federal framework. Some companies voluntarily disclose climate-related information, while others provide minimal or no details about potential climate impacts on their business.

The proposed elimination applies to all publicly traded companies that would have been subject to the original rule. The SEC will likely accept public comments on the proposal before making a final determination.
