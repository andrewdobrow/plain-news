10:57 AM ET

Chinese Intelligence Officers Target Western Professionals on Job Platforms

Chinese military intelligence services are conducting an aggressive recruitment campaign on LinkedIn and other job platforms, targeting government and military personnel across Five Eyes nations, according to a joint security bulletin from intelligence agencies in the UK, US, Australia, Canada and New Zealand.

The operatives pose as human resources consultants or employees of private companies and thinktanks, posting advertisements for nonexistent positions such as foreign policy analysts or defence experts. After reviewing applicants' CVs to identify those with access to sensitive information, they conduct virtual interviews while concealing their true identities.

During these conversations, the recruiters question candidates about their government contacts and military knowledge. They then ask applicants to write trial reports on topics including China's international relations, defence capabilities, or trade policy. Once candidates have demonstrated their access to useful information, the conversation typically moves to encrypted messaging platforms where they are pressed for more sensitive material.

Those who provide information receive payment ranging from hundreds to several thousand dollars per report through various financial platforms including PayPal, Payoneer, Zelle, Skrill, Wise, Western Union, e-transfer services and cryptocurrency.

The security agencies warn that several categories of workers face particular risk. These include people holding security clearances in defence, foreign affairs and security sectors; military personnel stationed in the Indo-Pacific region; and individuals with indirect or peripheral access to government information, such as academics, journalists, thinktank employees and those working in defence and policy sectors.

The bulletin emphasises that even individuals without direct access to classified information remain targets. Details about government policy, military strategies and capabilities could compromise national security if disclosed, the agencies stated.

MI5 has previously warned that Chinese spies seeking to recruit Britons in sensitive positions have used LinkedIn, with reports suggesting that at least 20,000 Britons have been approached through such recruitment schemes.

The security agencies caution that anyone involved in unauthorised disclosure of information faces potential prosecution for espionage. They recommend that professionals contacted by unknown individuals on job platforms exercise caution, particularly when outreach appears focused on their access to specific information. Security experts suggest verifying job offers through independent research and remaining alert to requests seeking details about workplace security practices or sensitive projects.

A Chinese embassy spokesperson rejected the allegations as "entirely fabricated" and "malicious slander," characterising the Five Eyes intelligence gathering activities as "the real threat to peace-loving countries."

Former Trump Adviser John Bolton Reaches Plea Deal Over Classified Documents

John Bolton, the former national security adviser who became a vocal critic of Donald Trump after leaving his administration, has agreed to plead guilty to illegally retaining classified national security documents.

The Department of Justice filed charges against Bolton in October 2025 as part of a broader wave of prosecutions against Trump critics that occurred over several weeks. Bolton intends to plead guilty to one count of illegal retention of sensitive national security documents. As part of the agreement, he has also agreed to pay a fine exceeding $2 million.

Bolton served as national security adviser under Trump from April 2018 to September 2019 before departing amid disagreements over foreign policy matters, including the administration's approach to North Korea and Afghanistan. After leaving the White House, he became known for his public criticism of the former president.

Court records indicate that Bolton is scheduled to appear in court to enter his plea on June 26. Multiple news outlets, including CNN, the Associated Press, and the New York Times, reported the provisional plea agreement after sources familiar with the case confirmed the details.

Bolton's legal troubles extend beyond the current classified documents case. After leaving the Trump administration, he published a memoir that sparked a separate legal battle. The Justice Department sought to block publication, arguing that Bolton had not completed the required pre-publication review process for works by former officials with access to classified material. Despite government objections, the book was ultimately published, though legal proceedings continued at the time.

The plea agreement resolves the classified documents investigation that had been underway for an unspecified period. Federal charges related to mishandling classified information typically carry significant penalties, though plea agreements often result in reduced consequences in exchange for cooperation and admission of wrongdoing.

Trump claimed when the charges were announced that he was not aware of the prosecution, though he characterized Bolton as a "bad guy" at the time. Bolton's case adds to a growing list of high-level officials who have faced legal consequences for their handling of sensitive government documents.

Bolton has been a prominent foreign policy voice in conservative circles throughout his career, serving in various Republican administrations before his tenure under Trump. His departure from the White House and subsequent emergence as a Trump critic marked a notable shift in his public profile.

The formal guilty plea hearing will allow Bolton to officially enter his plea before a federal judge, completing the agreement he has reached with prosecutors. Details regarding sentencing recommendations and other specific terms of the plea deal were not immediately disclosed.

The confluence of major IPO filings underscores the rising financial stakes in emerging industries, particularly artificial intelligence and commercial space exploration. Musk's personal net worth stands to increase substantially through the IPO, given his significant stake in SpaceX. The offering marks a significant moment for the commercial space industry, which has attracted growing investment in recent years. CBS News announced major changes to 60 Minutes this week, including the termination of three correspondents and the appointment of new leadership, creating significant upheaval at the network's flagship news program.

The departures include veteran correspondent Scott Pelley, Sharyn Alfonsi, and Cecilia Vega. Additionally, Tanya Simon was removed from her role as the show's top producer, and executive editor Draggan Mihailovich parted ways with the program. These moves represent one of the most substantial shake-ups in the show's 52-year history as America's most-watched news program.

Nick Bilton, a former New York Times tech journalist, has been appointed as executive producer for the show's upcoming 59th season. Bilton stated in a memo to staff that the show must evolve to reflect how people now consume news. "Evolving or dying isn't a threat. It's simple math," he wrote, adding that his responsibility extends beyond technological transformation to maintaining public trust.

Bari Weiss, CBS News' editor-in-chief, praised the appointment on social media, calling Bilton "one of the most entrepreneurial and ambitious journalists working today."

However, the firings have triggered serious concerns among remaining staff members about the show's future direction and editorial independence. Vega released a statement suggesting political bias played a role in her termination, despite having almost a year remaining on her contract. She expressed concern about "efforts to insert political bias into our stories" and described what she called "censorship, both imposed and self-driven."

Alfonsi similarly claimed she was penalized for pushing back on criticism from Weiss regarding one of her reporting segments. She stated her contract was not renewed after she remained an at-will employee.

Pelley issued a public statement accusing network executives of instructing him "to inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. He emphasized that 60 Minutes has maintained its position as America's top-rated news program "because our beloved audience finds integrity, quality, and humanity in our stories."

The departures mean that of the seven full-time correspondents who anchored the show, only three remain: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Anderson Cooper announced in February that he was leaving the show to focus on his CNN responsibilities.

CBS News staffers are now questioning how the program will maintain its content and identity. The network has not provided details about hiring replacements or how it will fill the gap left by the departures. Bilton indicated he would meet with staff and return with a detailed plan within a month, though he has shared few specifics about planned changes beyond acknowledging the show must adapt to modern media consumption patterns.

Sen. Bill Cassidy of Louisiana directly blamed Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. for a resurgence in vaccine-preventable illnesses. Cassidy shared a New York Times article on social media platform X on Thursday reporting that hospitals are seeing more cases of illnesses that vaccines typically prevent.

The Louisiana senator, a physician by training, pointed to Kennedy's leadership at HHS as the cause of the uptick. Kennedy took office as HHS secretary under President Trump and has been a prominent skeptic of vaccine safety and efficacy for years before his appointment.

The timing of Cassidy's criticism follows a Senate Finance Committee hearing where Kennedy faced intense questioning from multiple senators about how the administration has responded to a measles outbreak and the spread of vaccine misinformation. The US recorded 2,288 measles cases last year, the highest number since the disease was declared eliminated 25 years ago. Another 1,748 cases have already been reported this year, raising concerns among public health experts.

During the hearing, Sen. Ron Wyden criticized Kennedy's messaging on vaccines, stating: "When it comes to vaccines, Robert Kennedy has used this once-in-a-lifetime platform to make parents doubt themselves and doubt their doctors." Wyden added that Kennedy had "ducked, bobbed and weaved without taking the responsibility of saying what needs to be said: vaccines save lives in America."

When pressed about the measles outbreak, Kennedy denied responsibility. "I had nothing to do with the measles outbreak here," he said. "We have limited our outbreak better than any country in the world."

Public health specialists have argued that Kennedy failed to strongly promote vaccination and instead highlighted unproven treatments while the virus spread across state lines.

However, Kennedy appeared to shift his position during the hearing. When asked directly whether the measles vaccine is vital to keeping American children healthy, Kennedy responded: "That's my position. We promote the measles vaccine." He also stated: "We advise every child to get the MMR."

Cassidy's public criticism represents a notable rebuke of a sitting cabinet secretary from a member of the president's own party. The Republican senator had supported Kennedy's confirmation after receiving assurances that federal vaccine guidelines would be upheld, assurances that Cassidy has suggested were not fully fulfilled.

The criticism comes as the CDC continues to face significant disruption under Kennedy's leadership. About 80 percent of senior leadership roles at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention remain unfilled, leaving no permanent leadership to guide policies affecting millions of Americans. According to current and former senior CDC officials, bureaucratic delays have slowed productivity in several areas, and disruptions in data collection have left the agency struggling to operate effectively.
