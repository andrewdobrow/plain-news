A few minutes ago

The Trump administration is abandoning its controversial $1.8 billion anti-weaponization fund, Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced during testimony before a House committee on Tuesday.

"We are not moving forward with the fund, period," Blanche told the House Appropriations Committee when questioned by Rep. Grace Meng. The decision comes after weeks of fierce backlash and legal challenges to the proposal.

The fund had been designed to compensate individuals who claimed they were wrongly investigated or prosecuted by federal agencies. Supporters within the Trump administration argued it would address what they characterized as the politicization of law enforcement, while critics questioned its legal basis and raised concerns about potential misuse of taxpayer funds.

Despite dropping the fund itself, Blanche indicated that the underlying concerns remain significant. "The reasons for the fund, I think, remain as important as they were before, but we are not moving forward with the fund," he said.

The Justice Department had faced substantial opposition to the proposal since its announcement. The plan generated criticism from members of both parties about eligibility criteria, oversight mechanisms, and the precedent it might establish. Legal experts called the initiative problematic, while Congress expressed skepticism about implementation and costs. A federal court had also issued a ruling halting the establishment of the fund, which the Justice Department indicated it would abide by, though officials said they disagreed strongly with the decision.

The abandonment of the fund represents a significant policy reversal for the administration. Congressional appropriators would have been required to authorize the spending, and the proposal had faced an uphill battle in securing approval.

Notably, while the administration is dropping the compensation fund, Blanche confirmed that another controversial measure will remain in place. The Justice Department is maintaining an agreement that prohibits the Internal Revenue Service from auditing President Trump, his family, and related entities.

The proposal had been under discussion for several weeks before the Justice Department formally announced it would abandon the initiative. No details were provided about what specifically prompted the reversal or whether the administration plans to pursue alternative measures to address the concerns that motivated the original proposal.

The decision reflects the intense scrutiny the fund faced from various quarters, including Democratic lawmakers who questioned whether it amounted to using taxpayer money to benefit the president's political allies. The controversy surrounding the proposal had made it increasingly difficult for the administration to move forward despite initial support from some Trump administration officials.

President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, to serve as acting director of national intelligence, replacing Tulsi Gabbard who recently exited the role. Pulte will continue leading the FHFA, which oversees federal housing lenders Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

The appointment has generated significant concern in Washington. Pulte has no background in national security or intelligence work. Before Trump appointed him to the FHFA, he had no government experience of any kind. His selection to lead the nation's intelligence community represents an unusual choice for a position traditionally filled by career intelligence professionals.

At the FHFA, Pulte has become known for making public allegations against Trump's political opponents, claiming they committed mortgage fraud. He has accused Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook of mortgage fraud, a charge she has denied. According to reporting, a former high-ranking official described his handling of the Cook case as "very bizarre."

Trump defended the appointment on his Truth Social platform, stating that "William has deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America." However, the selection has drawn skepticism from multiple observers and even some Republicans.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune expressed reservations about Pulte's potential permanent appointment to the role. "We don't need a weaponized director of national intelligence," Thune told reporters. He indicated that if Trump nominates Pulte for the position permanently, he would face a lengthy confirmation process and Senate hearings. Thune emphasized that the intelligence community needs "professionals" in leadership positions.

Critics have questioned whether Pulte might use his intelligence authority to target Trump's political adversaries, given his track record at the FHFA. Some observers have characterized Pulte as a "nepo baby" due to his family connections to PulteGroup, a major homebuilder founded by his grandfather.

Despite these family ties, Pulte no longer holds an official position with PulteGroup. He was pushed off the company's board in 2020. The Pulte Family Charitable Foundation, run by his aunt and father, has also publicly distanced itself from him. In a 2023 statement, the foundation said that "Bill Pulte is not a representative of, nor has he served in any capacity, for the PFCF," and noted that some of his public communications had created confusion between his activities and the foundation's work.

The appointment comes as Trump also signed an executive order seeking early access to new artificial intelligence models before their public release, part of a broader effort to strengthen national security oversight of emerging technologies. Pulte's role will require him to manage 18 intelligence agencies and advise the president on national security threats.

Microsoft announced Scout, an artificial intelligence assistant designed to operate as an autonomous agent that works continuously on behalf of users. The company introduced the tool at its Build developer conference, positioning it as a personal agent that remains active at all times.

Internal documents obtained by 404 Media reveal Microsoft's ambitions for the product. The documents state the company wants to make people addicted to Scout. The AI assistant is built on technology Microsoft calls OpenClaw and functions as an executive assistant that handles tasks without requiring constant user input.

The Scout announcement came alongside other AI product releases at the Build conference. Microsoft also introduced Project Solara, an Android operating system designed for AI agents rather than traditional applications. The company unveiled new AI models intended to reduce its dependence on OpenAI and lower costs for developers. A new framework called Adaptive Spec-driven Scoring for Evaluation and Regression Testing was released as open source software to help developers create AI behavior tests using text descriptions.

Microsoft's push into autonomous AI agents represents a shift from its earlier focus on applications. The company has positioned agents as the next generation of software interaction, where AI systems perform tasks independently rather than waiting for users to open and interact with separate apps. Scout fits into this strategy as a tool that operates in the background.

The Build conference presentations emphasized Windows as a platform for AI development. GitHub, owned by Microsoft, also announced a Copilot app described as an agent-native desktop experience. Microsoft has been investing heavily in AI infrastructure and partnerships, and the Scout announcement signals the company's intention to embed AI assistance directly into users' daily workflows.

The Cleveland Browns have traded star edge rusher Myles Garrett to the Los Angeles Rams in exchange for linebacker Jared Verse and three draft selections. General Manager Andrew Berry confirmed the blockbuster deal, stating he would not have seriously pursued the trade without Verse's inclusion.

Garrett, 30, is a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection. Since Cleveland selected him first overall in 2017, he has established himself as one of the league's premier defensive players. Across his tenure with the Browns, Garrett leads all players with 125.5 sacks, 413 pressures, and 149 tackles for loss. He capped the 2025 season by setting the single-season sacks record with 23, earning All-Pro honors despite the Browns finishing 5-12 and missing the playoffs for a second consecutive year.

Garrett had requested a trade in 2024 after the franchise posted a 3-14 record, expressing his desire to play for a championship contender. Despite these concerns, he signed a four-year extension worth 160 million dollars a month later, making him the highest-paid non-quarterback in the league at that time. The deal included 122.8 million in guaranteed money and an average annual value of 40 million dollars.

The contract situation changed when the Browns and Garrett agreed to modify payment terms in March, deferring option payments over the 2026-28 seasons. This restructuring enabled Berry to proceed with the trade after concluding the situation was best for the organization's long-term direction.

"As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" Berry said.

In return for Garrett, Cleveland received Verse, a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second-round selection, and a 2029 third-round choice. The 25-year-old Verse was Los Angeles's first-round pick in 2024 and earned Defensive Rookie of the Year accolades. He made the Pro Bowl this season with 12 career sacks and 22 tackles for loss.

For the Rams, the acquisition of Garrett pairs with quarterback Matthew Stafford, who won his first MVP award last season. This marks the first time an Associated Press NFL MVP and Defensive Player of the Year will be teammates. The Rams lost to the eventual champion Seattle Seahawks in last season's NFC Championship Game.

Cleveland simultaneously hired former Baltimore Ravens offensive coordinator Todd Monken to replace Kevin Stefanski as head coach following the disappointing season. Monken indicated he had not yet held a face-to-face meeting with Garrett before the trade was finalized.
