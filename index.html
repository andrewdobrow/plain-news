The cease-fire called for Hezbollah to halt its attacks and pull all fighters south of the Litani River, while Israel would end its military operations. The Lebanese government has been negotiating with Israel in an effort to reassert control over the country and disarm Hezbollah, but the group operates independently and maintains significant military capabilities.

Iran's foreign ministry said Lebanon must be an integral part of any ceasefire and final agreement.

Fighting continued on Thursday despite the ceasefire announcement. Israel carried out airstrikes in southern Lebanon and the Bekaa valley, killing four people. Hezbollah claimed it targeted Israeli soldiers in southern Lebanon. Israel's defense minister said his country had freedom of action to strike Beirut in response to attacks on Israeli communities.

The breakdown represents a significant setback for U.S. diplomatic efforts in the region. It highlights the complexity of negotiating peace when key parties are excluded from talks. The Lebanese government has limited ability to enforce terms that Hezbollah opposes, and the group functions as both a political party and an armed militia within Lebanon.

Israeli troops currently control more than 600 square kilometers of territory in southern Lebanon and have destroyed dozens of border villages. Lebanon and Israel have also agreed to establish pilot zones where Lebanese armed forces would take exclusive control of territory to exclude non-state actors. The failure of this ceasefire sets the stage for continued hostilities between Israel and Hezbollah along the Lebanese border.

A few minutes ago

Marjane Satrapi, the French-Iranian artist and graphic novelist whose memoir Persepolis transformed international understanding of Iran, has died at 56. Her family stated that she "died of sadness" following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away in April of the previous year. After his death, Satrapi posted on Instagram: "For I lost the love of my life."

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi grew up in Tehran with an engineer father and dress designer mother. As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and escape restrictions under the Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France, arriving in 1994 and becoming a French citizen in 2006.

Satrapi published Persepolis in 2000 as a comic book memoir that became an international publishing phenomenon. The work tells the story of a rebellious young girl navigating Iran after the shah's overthrow in 1979 and the establishment of the Islamic Republic. It follows her journey through the country's violence and ideological control before her departure to Europe at age 14. The memoir sold millions of copies worldwide and established Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors globally.

In a 2024 Guardian interview, Satrapi explained that Persepolis aimed to help Western readers recognize the humanity of Iranian people. She initially had modest expectations for the book. "With Persepolis, I didn't even think I'd find a publisher," she told El País in 2020. "I thought I'd make 50 photocopies for my friends to read."

Satrapi co-directed an animated film adaptation of Persepolis, which became an international success and earned her a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, making her the first woman nominated in that category. She went on to direct five feature films, including Radioactive in 2019, starring Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie.

Throughout her career, Satrapi described drawing as humanity's first language. "Drawing - it's the first language of human beings, before writing, before even talking, before words," she said in a 2012 interview. She emphasized that her work aimed to reassure young Iranians that the outside world heard and supported them.

After leaving comics for years, Satrapi returned to the medium in 2024 by coordinating Woman, Life, Freedom, a collaborative graphic work featuring 17 Iranian and international comic artists alongside academics. The book examined protest movements following the 2022 death in custody of Mahsa Amini, a young Kurdish-Iranian woman. Satrapi stated: "The only thing I can do is cultural work. This book is a message to the Iranian people to say, listen, you are not alone."

President Emmanuel Macron said Satrapi was "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale," while Margaret Atwood noted that Satrapi had lived the struggles she depicted, making her work increasingly relevant.

Her family stated that she "died of sadness" following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away in April of the previous year. After his death, Satrapi posted on Instagram: "For I lost the love of my life."

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi grew up in Tehran with an engineer father and dress designer mother. As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and escape restrictions under the Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France, arriving in 1994 and becoming a French citizen in 2006.

Satrapi published Persepolis in 2000 as a comic book memoir that became an international publishing phenomenon. The work tells the story of a rebellious young girl navigating Iran after the shah's overthrow in 1979 and the establishment of the Islamic Republic. It follows her journey through the country's violence and ideological control before her departure to Europe at age 14. The memoir sold millions of copies worldwide and established Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors globally.

In a 2024 Guardian interview, Satrapi explained that Persepolis aimed to help Western readers recognize the humanity of Iranian people. She initially had modest expectations for the book. "With Persepolis, I didn't even think I'd find a publisher," she told El País in 2020. "I thought I'd make 50 photocopies for my friends to read."

Satrapi co-directed an animated film adaptation of Persepolis, which became an international success and earned her a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, making her the first woman nominated in that category. She went on to direct five feature films, including Radioactive in 2019, starring Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie.

Throughout her career, Satrapi described drawing as humanity's first language. "Drawing - it's the first language of human beings, before writing, before even talking, before words," she said in a 2012 interview. She emphasized that her work aimed to reassure young Iranians that the outside world heard and supported them.

After leaving comics for years, Satrapi returned to the medium in 2024 by coordinating Woman, Life, Freedom, a collaborative graphic work featuring 17 Iranian and international comic artists alongside academics. The book examined protest movements following the 2022 death in custody of Mahsa Amini, a young Kurdish-Iranian woman. Satrapi stated: "The only thing I can do is cultural work. This book is a message to the Iranian people to say, listen, you are not alone."

President Emmanuel Macron said Satrapi was "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale," while Margaret Atwood noted that Satrapi had lived the struggles she depicted, making her work increasingly relevant.
