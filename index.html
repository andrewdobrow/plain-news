Yesterday, 10:49 PM ET

A federal appeals court has ruled that the Trump administration's policy expelling active transgender service members violates the Constitution. The three-judge panel issued a split decision that restricts the Defense Department's ability to discharge troops based on gender identity.

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Washington DC Circuit determined that removing service members already serving in the military when the ban took effect was unconstitutional. The court called the policy "arbitrary, and based on animus" in its majority opinion.

However, the ruling includes a significant limitation. While current transgender troops can remain in the military, the decision allows the armed services to continue blocking transgender individuals from enlisting. This split outcome means the ban's impact varies depending on military status at the time of implementation.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has signaled his intention to appeal the decision to the Supreme Court. In response to Monday's ruling, Hegseth stated "see you at SCOTUS," indicating the administration will challenge the appeals court's decision at the nation's highest court.

The policy, implemented last year under Hegseth's direction, represented a reversal of previous military guidelines that permitted transgender individuals to serve openly. The legal dispute centers on whether the government can discharge service members based on their transgender status.

The appeals court determined that expelling troops already on active duty exceeded the government's constitutional authority. The decision creates a legal barrier to some aspects of the administration's efforts to restrict transgender military service, though it does not address recruitment policies or individuals seeking to join the military for the first time.

Legal experts expect this case to ultimately reach the Supreme Court, as the appeals court ruling directly conflicts with the administration's policy objectives. The decision is likely to be challenged, setting up a potential high-profile legal battle over military personnel policy and the rights of transgender service members.

This case joins other ongoing legal challenges to Trump administration policies regarding gender identity in federal institutions. The ruling demonstrates continued judicial scrutiny of the administration's approach to transgender policy across government agencies.

The decision reflects a divided court on this issue. While the majority opinion restricted the ban's application to active service members, the split decision indicates disagreement among the judges about the broader constitutional questions at stake.

The fact that active troops can remain in service while new recruits can still be blocked creates a complex legal and practical situation. This middle ground outcome may intensify the administration's determination to pursue the case further, as it does not fully accomplish the stated goal of restricting transgender military service across all contexts.

"The Russians' tactics have not changed: strike drones, cruise missiles and a significant amount of ballistics," Zelenskyy wrote. The assault came after Russia and Ukraine completed a prisoner exchange on Friday, in which both sides exchanged 193 service members. During the same period, Ukraine conducted some of its longest-distance drone strikes deep inside Russian territory. A woman was killed and a man seriously injured in a Russian border region when struck by Ukrainian drones.

Rescue operations continued through Saturday as emergency crews worked to locate survivors trapped beneath collapsed structures. Authorities warned that the death toll could rise as rescue efforts progressed and damage assessments were completed across the affected cities.

3:54 AM ET

President Trump announced a proposal to impose a 25% tariff on imports from Brazil, citing what the administration characterizes as unfair trade practices. The tariff proposal represents the administration's continued effort to expand its trade enforcement agenda through Section 301 investigations, a legal mechanism that allows the US to respond to foreign trade barriers.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 authorizes the US Trade Representative to investigate and take action against foreign countries accused of violating trade agreements or engaging in practices that harm American commerce. The administration has used this provision repeatedly as the primary legal framework for implementing tariffs on trading partners. Brazil joins a growing list of countries subjected to these investigations as the White House pursues what it describes as reciprocal trade relationships.

The proposed tariff targets Brazilian imports broadly, though the administration has not specified which products or industries would face the highest impact. Brazil exports a wide range of goods to the United States, including agricultural products, steel, aircraft parts, and machinery. The two countries have had an evolving trade relationship, with the US importing approximately $40 billion worth of Brazilian goods annually in recent years.

Brazilian officials have not yet issued a formal response to the tariff proposal. Trade experts anticipate that Brazil may challenge the action through the World Trade Organization or consider retaliatory measures affecting American exports. US companies that rely on Brazilian imports may face higher costs if the tariff takes effect, potentially affecting prices for consumers and profit margins for businesses.

The timing of the announcement suggests the administration intends to maintain an aggressive posture on trade enforcement heading into the election season. The Section 301 process typically involves a period of investigation, public comment, and review before tariffs are formally implemented, meaning the proposal could take several months to become policy. The administration's approach continues to prioritize bilateral negotiations backed by the threat of tariffs over multilateral trade agreements.

The fundraising structure includes a $30 billion initial raise alongside the $10 billion from Berkshire Hathaway, plus a $40 billion flexible mechanism that can be deployed gradually over time. Berkshire's investment is particularly notable given its historical role providing capital to companies facing major challenges, such as its $5 billion investment in Goldman Sachs during the 2008 financial crisis.

The massive investment underscores the substantial computing resources required to develop and deploy advanced AI systems, particularly specialized graphics processing units and custom AI chips.

Analysts view the fundraising as evidence that the AI sector is entering a more capital-intensive phase. Matt Britzman, a senior equity analyst at Hargreaves Lansdown, noted that while $80 billion represents a significant sum, it comprises less than 2 percent of Alphabet's $4.6 trillion market valuation. He emphasized that the investment demonstrates Alphabet is spending from a position of strength rather than financial distress, pointing to strong demand for AI computing, accelerated Google Cloud growth, and resilience in the Search business.

However, the announcement also reflects concerns about whether the substantial spending on AI infrastructure will translate into meaningful financial returns. Jim Reid, a market strategist at Deutsche Bank, characterized the fundraising as a reminder of the "unprecedented scale of the AI spending boom," noting that AI capital expenditure financing is becoming increasingly important for markets.

Alphabet's move comes as other major AI companies prepare their own public market debuts. OpenAI and Elon Musk's xAI are also expected to pursue public listings this year.

Rick Adelman, the Hall of Fame basketball coach who led teams to more than 1,000 victories during a career spanning three decades, has died at age 79. The news was confirmed by multiple NBA organizations, including the Sacramento Kings and Portland Trail Blazers, two franchises where Adelman made his greatest impact.

Adelman coached in the NBA for 23 seasons, compiling 1,042 wins and guiding teams to 16 playoff appearances. He began his head coaching career with the Portland Trail Blazers in 1989, leading the team to the NBA Finals in 1990 and 1992. His tenure with the Trail Blazers established him as one of the league's elite coaches, known for his offensive systems and ability to maximize player talent.

He later found success with the Sacramento Kings from 1998 to 2006, building the franchise into a perennial contender during the early 2000s. The Kings became known for their fast-paced, high-scoring style under Adelman's direction. He also coached the Golden State Warriors, Houston Rockets, and Minnesota Timberwolves during his career. Adelman was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2021, recognition of his sustained excellence and influence on the game.

Adelman ranked among the winningest coaches in NBA history at the time of his retirement in 2014. His coaching tree influenced numerous assistants who went on to lead their own teams. Players who competed under Adelman frequently praised his basketball intelligence and calm demeanor, qualities that helped him navigate the pressures of coaching at the highest level.

The basketball community responded to news of his death with tributes highlighting his legacy. The Sacramento Kings released a statement honoring Adelman's contributions to the franchise and the sport. His career represented an era of NBA basketball defined by strategic innovation and competitive excellence, leaving a lasting mark on the league and the coaches and players he mentored throughout his time on the sidelines.
