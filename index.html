7:38 PM ET

The death toll from a chemical tank rupture at a paper mill in Longview, Washington, has risen to eight after recovery crews found the remains of six missing workers, authorities confirmed on Thursday.

Nine workers were initially reported missing following the incident at the Nippon Dynawave Packaging facility. Three individuals remain unaccounted for and are presumed dead. Eight other people sustained injuries in the disaster, including a firefighter who responded to the scene.

The tank contained approximately 600,000 gallons of white liquor, a chemical mixture made primarily of sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide. The substance is used in the paper industry to break down wood and produce kraft paper, which is commonly used for packaging, shopping bags, and other products. About 25,000 gallons of the liquid remained in the damaged tank and continued to leak slowly.

When the tank ruptured, white liquor spilled into a drainage ditch and contaminated the surrounding area. Officials confirmed that contamination had reached the Columbia River by Tuesday, and dead carp were discovered in the water. State ecology department officials urged the public to avoid ditches and dikes in the area while water testing continued. However, authorities said there were no issues related to air quality or drinking water in the city of Longview.

Recovery operations began Wednesday following a delay caused by concerns about the structural integrity of the damaged tank. Officials stated there was no hope of finding additional survivors and transitioned the operation from rescue to recovery. Matt Amos with the Longview fire department said the effort would be "slow, methodical and deliberate," with recovered victims undergoing decontamination before being transported to the coroner's office. Responders worked in chemical protective equipment while prioritizing safety.

Washington Governor Bob Ferguson said at a Wednesday news conference that authorities were "bracing ourselves for this being the deadliest industrial tragedy in modern Washington state history."

The U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board announced it was launching a federal investigation into the incident. The Washington state department of ecology and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency were overseeing operations to assess environmental impacts.

Brian Wood, representing Nippon Dynawave, a subsidiary of Japan-based Nippon Paper Group, said the company was focused on supporting victims and first responders. He pledged that the company would "cooperate to the maximum extent" with investigators.

Prior to the disaster, safety complaints had been filed against the facility in March and May. The state's labor and industries department said both complaints were unrelated to the current situation. Since 2021, the company had been fined $3,400 for three separate health and safety violations discovered by state inspectors.

The sprawling Longview plant employs approximately 1,000 people and produces material for tissues, printing paper, cups, plates, and cartons.

Lemieux built a memorable career as a fierce competitor known for his hard-hitting approach to the game. He won Stanley Cup championships with three different teams: the Montreal Canadiens, the New Jersey Devils twice, and the Colorado Avalanche. His physical style of play sometimes drew criticism from opponents, but his ability to perform in crucial moments earned him respect throughout the league.

The winger's most celebrated achievement came in 1995 when he won the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP while helping the New Jersey Devils capture their first Stanley Cup championship. Over his NHL career spanning from 1983 to 2003, Lemieux played 21 seasons and recorded 379 goals and 406 assists across 1,215 regular season games. In the postseason, he added 80 goals and 78 assists in 234 playoff appearances, establishing himself as one of hockey's premier playoff performers.

Lemieux's intensity on the ice was legendary. His fiery demeanor and willingness to engage in physical confrontations made him a polarizing figure throughout his playing days. Yet this same competitive fire translated into clutch performances when stakes were highest, particularly during playoff runs with his championship teams.

After retiring as a player, Lemieux transitioned into work as a player agent, continuing his involvement in professional hockey. His son Brendan Lemieux has gone on to play in the NHL, continuing the family's hockey legacy.

Throughout his career, Lemieux played for eight NHL franchises, including stints with the Coyotes, Stars, and Sharks in addition to his championship teams. This nomadic journey reflected both his value as a veteran presence and the way the league valued his playoff experience and ability to contribute to championship efforts.

The hockey community expressed heartfelt condolences following news of his death. Lemieux's passing adds to recent losses within professional sports and reminds the hockey world of the legacy he left across multiple franchises during championship runs spanning the 1980s through early 2000s.

His death also highlights the importance of mental health awareness and support systems for former athletes, as the sports community continues to grapple with the challenges faced by those who have left the spotlight after successful careers.

The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire by 60 days, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and establish a framework for talks on Tehran's nuclear program, according to US officials. Vice President JD Vance said on Thursday that the two countries were "very close" to finalizing a memorandum of understanding, though some sticking points remained.

"We're very close but not there yet," Vance said, adding that the US was still "going back and forth" with Iran on nuclear issues.

Negotiators have been working to finalize the terms of the agreement. US officials told the BBC that the framework of a ceasefire extension deal had been agreed, pending approval from President Trump and Iran's leadership. A second round of negotiations would "very likely" be held in Islamabad, according to White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt.

The tentative deal comes after weeks of indirect negotiations, with Pakistan playing a key role in mediation efforts. Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, led a delegation to Tehran on Wednesday to convey messages from Washington. Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif also launched a four-day regional tour to Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Turkey to coordinate support for peace efforts.

Leavitt said the White House remained "very much engaged" in negotiations and felt "good about the prospects of a deal." However, she emphasized that nothing was official until announced by the White House.

The proposed agreement would extend the ceasefire beyond its expiration on April 22 and launch further diplomatic efforts. The memorandum would create a framework for broader talks on Tehran's nuclear program and address concerns about Iran's nuclear capabilities. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments, would reopen under the terms being discussed.

News of the potential breakthrough led to falling oil prices on Thursday, reflecting market optimism about the deal.

However, complications remain on the ground. Sources in Tehran indicated that Iran demanded an end to Israeli attacks on Lebanon as a precondition for another round of negotiations with the US. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu signaled that Israel was continuing military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon, indicating a potential obstacle to broader regional peace.

The US military has also imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports following earlier negotiations. US warships turned back nine ships, including a Chinese-owned tanker, attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday.

Trump and his advisers have previously believed they were close to a deal several times during negotiations, only to see talks stall. The signing of the memorandum of understanding would represent the most significant diplomatic breakthrough since the war began.
