President Trump announced at a White House dinner that he plans to nominate Todd Blanche to serve as attorney general, according to a video of the event posted on social media by a White House aide.

Blanche has previously represented Trump in multiple legal matters and currently serves as deputy attorney general. If confirmed by the Senate, he would lead the Department of Justice and serve as the chief law enforcement officer of the federal government. The attorney general position ranks among the most powerful roles in the executive branch, with authority over federal prosecutions, civil rights enforcement, and antitrust matters. The Justice Department also oversees agencies including the FBI and the Bureau of Prisons.

The nomination requires Senate confirmation. The Senate Judiciary Committee will hold hearings to review Blanche's qualifications and record before the full Senate votes on his confirmation. Trump has not yet submitted the formal nomination paperwork to the Senate, but once submitted, the confirmation process will begin with committee review.

Blanche's potential confirmation comes amid controversy over his handling of documents related to Jeffrey Epstein. A lawsuit filed Monday by Katie Phang, an investigative journalist and legal analyst, alleges that Blanche engaged in a "brazen, shocking, and ongoing violation" of a law requiring the Department of Justice to release all documents related to the late sex offender.

Congress passed the Epstein Files Transparency Act in November with a December 19, 2025 deadline for releasing all documents. Phang's lawsuit claims that Blanche has missed statutory deadlines for document production, made improper or excessive redactions, failed to explain redactions as required by law, and withheld or retracted key documents after release. Blanche has argued that redactions were necessary to prevent the inadvertent identification of Epstein's victims, many of whom are minors, though he has acknowledged that "mistakes are inevitable."

Democratic lawmakers have criticized the process. Jamie Raskin, the Democratic ranking member of the House judiciary committee, told reporters in February that there were "tons of completely unnecessary redactions, in addition to the failure to redact the names of victims."

Phang wants the court to declare the Department of Justice in violation of the law and order the release of all required documents without unlawful redactions. The lawsuit also seeks the appointment of an independent special master from outside the department to oversee the agency's compliance with the transparency law.

The Justice Department's office of the inspector general announced last week that it was launching its own audit into the department's compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act, with a preliminary objective of evaluating the agency's processes for identifying, redacting, and releasing records.

Yesterday, 9:29 PM ET

NASA has ended its MAVEN Mars mission after losing contact with the spacecraft, which entered an unrecoverable state while orbiting the red planet. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission, launched in 2013, stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend after more than a decade of successful operations.

The spacecraft was functioning normally before passing behind Mars. When it reappeared from behind the planet, communications had been lost. "Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," NASA said in a statement. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly, but were unable to restore contact or recover the mission.

MAVEN arrived at Mars in 2014 and began studying the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with solar wind. The mission provided critical insights into how Mars lost most of its atmosphere over billions of years. Scientists determined that solar wind and radiation stripped away the planet's upper atmosphere, transforming Mars from a potentially warm, wet world capable of supporting liquid water into the cold, dry desert it is today.

Throughout its operational period, MAVEN made numerous discoveries about atmospheric escape processes and how solar wind interacts with Mars' magnetic field remnants. The spacecraft had far exceeded its original mission timeline, operating successfully for years beyond its planned lifespan.

Beyond its primary atmospheric research, MAVEN served as a communication relay for NASA's two Mars rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance. The loss of this relay capability removes an important tool for maintaining contact with the rovers on the Martian surface.

NASA maintains two other active spacecraft in Mars orbit. The Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, launched in 2001, continue to operate and study the planet. These missions will help fill some of the gap left by MAVEN's loss, though the spacecraft provided unique data about the upper atmosphere that will be difficult to replace.

The extensive data MAVEN collected during its operational lifetime will continue to inform scientific understanding of planetary atmospheres and climate evolution on Mars for years to come. Researchers plan to analyze the wealth of information gathered by the spacecraft to develop more complete models of how Mars' atmosphere changed over geological time.

MAVEN's conclusion marks the end of one of NASA's longer-running Mars orbital missions. The spacecraft's success in achieving and surpassing its scientific objectives demonstrated the importance of dedicated atmospheric research platforms in planetary science. The mission joins a growing list of retired Mars explorers that have collectively expanded humanity's knowledge of Earth's planetary neighbor.

NASA ends MAVEN Mars mission after spacecraft loses control

NASA has ended its MAVEN Mars mission after losing contact with the spacecraft, which entered an unrecoverable state while orbiting the red planet. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission, launched in 2013, st...

3:00 AM ET

NASA has ended its MAVEN Mars mission after losing contact with the spacecraft, which entered an unrecoverable state while orbiting the red planet. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission, launched in 2013, stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend after more than a decade of successful operations.

The spacecraft was functioning normally before passing behind Mars. When it reappeared from behind the planet, communications had been lost. "Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," NASA said in a statement. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly, but were unable to restore contact or recover the mission.

MAVEN arrived at Mars in 2014 and began studying the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with solar wind. The mission provided critical insights into how Mars lost most of its atmosphere over billions of years. Scientists determined that solar wind and radiation stripped away the planet's upper atmosphere, transforming Mars from a potentially warm, wet world capable of supporting liquid water into the cold, dry desert it is today.

Throughout its operational period, MAVEN made numerous discoveries about atmospheric escape processes and how solar wind interacts with Mars' magnetic field remnants. The spacecraft had far exceeded its original mission timeline, operating successfully for years beyond its planned lifespan.

Beyond its primary atmospheric research, MAVEN served as a communication relay for NASA's two Mars rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance. The loss of this relay capability removes an important tool for maintaining contact with the rovers on the Martian surface.

NASA maintains two other active spacecraft in Mars orbit. The Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, launched in 2001, continue to operate and study the planet. These missions will help fill some of the gap left by MAVEN's loss, though the spacecraft provided unique data about the upper atmosphere that will be difficult to replace.

The extensive data MAVEN collected during its operational lifetime will continue to inform scientific understanding of planetary atmospheres and climate evolution on Mars for years to come. Researchers plan to analyze the wealth of information gathered by the spacecraft to develop more complete models of how Mars' atmosphere changed over geological time.

MAVEN's conclusion marks the end of one of NASA's longer-running Mars orbital missions. The spacecraft's success in achieving and surpassing its scientific objectives demonstrated the importance of dedicated atmospheric research platforms in planetary science. The mission joins a growing list of retired Mars explorers that have collectively expanded humanity's knowledge of Earth's planetary neighbor.
