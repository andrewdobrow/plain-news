A New Glenn rocket operated by Blue Origin exploded on the launchpad at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night, destroying the launch facility and threatening NASA's ambitious timeline for lunar exploration. The massive fireball erupted seconds after the start of a scheduled static fire test at 9pm ET, with the orange glow visible more than 115 miles away in Fort Pierce.

The explosion comes just days after NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA's administrator Jared Isaacman emphasized that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of how the incident affects the timeline for the moon program.

"Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," Isaacman wrote on social media. "We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed that all employees were safe and uninjured. In a statement on social media, Bezos acknowledged it was "a very rough day" but expressed confidence the company would recover. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. We'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it," he said.

The New Glenn rocket is central to Blue Origin's role in NASA's Artemis program, which aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface for the first time since 1972. The rocket is intended to launch lunar landers to the moon, including the landers that will carry astronauts to the surface during the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028. Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for that mission.

The explosion represents the latest setback for Blue Origin as it attempts to compete with SpaceX for lucrative NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from the third flight of the New Glenn ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's static fire test was the first since the FAA cleared the vehicle to return to flight the previous week.

Shockwaves from the explosion were felt across Florida's Space Coast, with residents in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach reporting that their homes shook. Launch Complex 36, located at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, has been used for rocket launches since the 1960s. Blue Origin had been preparing the facility specifically for New Glenn operations.

Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards following the explosion. Flames were still burning at the launchpad more than two hours after the incident.

The federal government proposed new funding rules that would allow officials to cancel research grants at any time. The proposed changes also make peer review optional for grant applications and would require political staff to screen grants for forbidden topics.

The rules represent a shift in how federal research funding operates. For decades, peer review has been the standard process for evaluating scientific grant proposals, with experts in the field assessing the merit and feasibility of proposed research.

Under the proposed system, political appointees would gain authority to review grant applications before approval. The screening process would check for topics deemed off-limits by the administration. The nature of these forbidden topics was not specified in the available information.

The ability to cancel grants at any time marks a departure from current practice. Research grants typically run for fixed terms, with cancellation generally limited to cases of fraud or failure to meet stated objectives. The proposed rules would remove these constraints, giving officials broad discretion to terminate funding.

The optional peer review provision would eliminate the requirement that expert scientists evaluate grant proposals. Peer review has long been considered a safeguard against political interference in research, ensuring that funding decisions rest on scientific merit rather than ideology or politics.

Research institutions rely heavily on federal grants to fund scientific work. The government supports research across fields including medicine, physics, climate science, and technology development. Changes to grant procedures could affect how universities and research centers plan and conduct long-term studies.

The proposal comes as federal science agencies face broader changes to their operations and priorities. How the new rules would interact with existing grant commitments and ongoing research projects remains to be determined. The proposed rules would need to go through a formal comment period before taking effect.

5:36 PM ET

A federal judge in Washington, D.C. has ordered President Trump to remove his name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts within two weeks. The ruling also blocks the scheduled closure of the performing arts venue for planned renovations.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper signed the order on Friday, directing the Trump administration to take down all physical signage bearing Trump's name and remove any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials. The decision comes on what would have been former President John F. Kennedy's birthday.

In his 94-page ruling, Judge Cooper concluded that it was "crystal clear" that the arts complex was named for the late President Kennedy. The judge found that the Kennedy Center's board overstepped its legal authority by unilaterally renaming the facility after Trump without congressional approval.

"The Court has concluded that the Board overstepped its statutory bounds by unilaterally renaming the Kennedy Center after President Trump," Cooper wrote. He noted that in 1964, Congress deliberately rechristened the National Cultural Center as the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts.

The ruling represents a setback to Trump's renovation plans. The Kennedy Center had been scheduled to close later this year to begin a two-year renovation period. The court's injunction prevents the center from winding down its programming and closing for these renovations, at least for now.

Judge Cooper's decision addresses fundamental questions about the legal authority of the Kennedy Center's board to alter the identity and operations of the facility. The judge determined that only Congress has the power to change the center's official name.

Members of the Kennedy family responded positively to the ruling. JFK's niece Maria Shriver called it "an appropriate birthday present" on her uncle's birthday. "A federal judge ruled that President Trump and the Kennedy Center Board acted unlawfully in renaming the Kennedy Center," she wrote on social media. "The judge held that only Congress can change the Center's name and blocked the planned two-year closure."

Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Trump's health secretary and son of former Senator Robert Kennedy, made no immediate comment on the ruling. However, his sister Kerry Kennedy referenced the decision, writing that she would not need "that pickaxe after all." Joe Kennedy III, a grandson of former Senator Robert Kennedy and a former congressman, shared news of the ruling without comment.

Trump responded hours later with a lengthy statement attacking the judge's decision and criticizing Judge Cooper, who was nominated to the federal bench by former President Barack Obama. The president stated he has "no interest" in the Kennedy Center following the ruling.

9:00 PM ET

A federal judge in Washington, D.C. has ordered President Trump to remove his name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts within two weeks. The ruling also blocks the scheduled closure of the performing arts venue for planned renovations.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper signed the order on Friday, directing the Trump administration to take down all physical signage bearing Trump's name and remove any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials. The decision comes on what would have been former President John F. Kennedy's birthday.

In his 94-page ruling, Judge Cooper concluded that it was "crystal clear" that the arts complex was named for the late President Kennedy. The judge found that the Kennedy Center's board overstepped its legal authority by unilaterally renaming the facility after Trump without congressional approval.

"The Court has concluded that the Board overstepped its statutory bounds by unilaterally renaming the Kennedy Center after President Trump," Cooper wrote. He noted that in 1964, Congress deliberately rechristened the National Cultural Center as the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts.

The ruling represents a setback to Trump's renovation plans. The Kennedy Center had been scheduled to close later this year to begin a two-year renovation period. The court's injunction prevents the center from winding down its programming and closing for these renovations, at least for now.

Judge Cooper's decision addresses fundamental questions about the legal authority of the Kennedy Center's board to alter the identity and operations of the facility. The judge determined that only Congress has the power to change the center's official name.

Members of the Kennedy family responded positively to the ruling. JFK's niece Maria Shriver called it "an appropriate birthday present" on her uncle's birthday. "A federal judge ruled that President Trump and the Kennedy Center Board acted unlawfully in renaming the Kennedy Center," she wrote on social media. "The judge held that only Congress can change the Center's name and blocked the planned two-year closure."

Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Trump's health secretary and son of former Senator Robert Kennedy, made no immediate comment on the ruling. However, his sister Kerry Kennedy referenced the decision, writing that she would not need "that pickaxe after all." Joe Kennedy III, a grandson of former Senator Robert Kennedy and a former congressman, shared news of the ruling without comment.

Trump responded hours later with a lengthy statement attacking the judge's decision and criticizing Judge Cooper, who was nominated to the federal bench by former President Barack Obama. The president stated he has "no interest" in the Kennedy Center following the ruling.
