Israel launched a new wave of strikes targeting Iranian military infrastructure across Tehran shortly after Donald Trump announced a pause in American attacks on energy facilities. The Israeli military said it would continue operations according to government directives, even as the US administration signaled a shift in its negotiating approach.

Trump stated that Washington and Tehran had engaged in "very good and productive conversations" over the previous two days. He instructed the Department of Defense to postpone strikes against Iranian power plants and energy infrastructure for five days, pending the success of ongoing discussions. This announcement came approximately 40 minutes before the Israeli Defense Forces declared on social media that they had "just begun another wave of strikes targeting infrastructure of the Iranian terror regime across Tehran."

An Israeli military official told the Times of Israel that the IDF could not comment on the US president's announcement, describing it as a "political echelon matter." The official stated the military was "operating in accordance with the directives of Israel's political leadership and will continue to strike in Iran according to its plans until instructed otherwise." The IDF indicated that energy infrastructure would be spared from Israeli attacks, potentially aligning with Washington's new position.

According to Israeli military statements released Monday, the strikes targeted multiple military sites including an Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps air defence and ground forces headquarters, a Quds Force base and intelligence site, and a defence ministry missile production facility. An Al Jazeera Arabic correspondent in Tehran reported that explosions in the capital were of "unprecedented" size and volume.

The escalation came as Iran's Revolutionary Guards announced a new attack on Israeli targets. The military action underscores the fragile nature of ceasefire arrangements between the parties even as diplomatic efforts continue to resolve the three-month conflict.

The Israeli military claimed it had destroyed or rendered inoperable approximately 330 of Iran's estimated 470 ballistic missile launchers since the war began. More than half were destroyed through airstrikes, while others were disabled after entrances to underground storage sites were targeted. The military said it continues pursuing the remaining launchers as missile fire on Israel has decreased to roughly 10 per day from approximately 90 at the conflict's start.

Separately, Israel has maintained its military campaign against Hezbollah in Lebanon. Israeli strikes hit Beirut's southern suburbs on Monday, the first attack on the stronghold in several days. Lebanon's health ministry reported one death from the strikes.

Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for Israeli sovereignty over southern Lebanese territory, saying the military campaign "needs to end with a different reality entirely" and proposing the Litani River as Israel's new border. The statement signals potential territorial ambitions beyond immediate military objectives.

Yesterday, 8:45 PM ET

Federal prosecutors charged a Google engineer with insider trading after he made $1.2 million through bets placed on Polymarket, a prediction market platform.

According to the complaint, the engineer wagered over $2.7 million on outcomes related to Google's 2025 Year in Search campaign. The charges suggest the employee used non-public information about the company's planned announcements to place informed bets on the platform.

Polymarket allows users to bet on real-world events, including corporate announcements, political outcomes, and cultural trends. The platform has grown in popularity as a venue for prediction markets, where participants can profit from correctly forecasting future events.

The case represents one of the first major insider trading prosecutions involving prediction markets rather than traditional stock trading. Prosecutors allege the Google employee exploited his position at the company to gain access to confidential information about which search terms and topics would be featured in the annual Year in Search report.

The Year in Search campaign is released by Google each year, highlighting the most popular search queries and trending topics over the previous 12 months. The report typically generates significant media attention and public interest.

By placing large wagers on Polymarket before the information became public, the engineer allegedly profited from advance knowledge of the campaign's contents. The complaint indicates he risked more than $2.7 million across multiple bets to generate the $1.2 million in profits.

Insider trading laws prohibit individuals from trading securities or other instruments based on material non-public information. While prediction markets operate differently from stock markets, prosecutors appear to be applying similar legal standards to wagers that rely on confidential corporate information.

The charges highlight growing regulatory scrutiny of prediction markets and how insider information might be exploited on these platforms. As prediction markets expand beyond traditional betting on elections and sports, prosecutors are examining whether existing securities laws apply to these new forms of wagering on corporate and economic events.

Justice Department opens criminal investigation into Trump accuser E. Jean Carroll

The probe marks a significant turn in the yearslong legal b...

Yesterday, 10:32 PM ET

The Department of Justice has opened a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll, who accused President Trump of sexual assault, according to multiple reports.

CNN first reported the investigation, which focuses on whether Carroll committed perjury during a deposition as part of her two civil lawsuits against Trump. The probe marks a significant turn in the yearslong legal battle between Carroll and the president.

Carroll, a former magazine columnist, has accused Trump of sexually assaulting her in a New York department store dressing room in the mid-1990s. Trump has denied the allegations. Carroll sued Trump for defamation after he called her a liar and questioned her credibility. She later filed a second lawsuit against him under New York's Adult Survivors Act, which temporarily allowed victims of sexual assault to sue regardless of when the alleged abuse occurred.

The investigation centers on statements Carroll made under oath during depositions related to these civil cases. Perjury, the act of lying under oath, is a federal crime that can carry significant penalties including imprisonment.

The Justice Department's decision to investigate a prominent accuser of the president raises questions about the independence of federal law enforcement. Critics have long warned about potential conflicts of interest when the Justice Department investigates cases involving the president or his personal interests.

Carroll's legal team has not publicly commented on the investigation. The Justice Department declined to provide details about the scope or status of the probe.

The investigation comes as Trump has called for prosecutions of various political opponents and critics. During his campaign and presidency, Trump has repeatedly attacked Carroll and questioned her motives for bringing the lawsuits.

The civil cases between Trump and Carroll have proceeded through federal courts in New York. A jury previously found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation in one of the cases, awarding Carroll millions of dollars in damages. Trump has appealed that verdict.

Perjury investigations typically involve detailed review of sworn testimony, supporting documents, and other evidence to determine whether false statements were made knowingly and materially. Federal prosecutors must prove that any false statements were deliberate rather than mistaken or misremembered.

Insider trading laws prohibit individuals from trading securities or other instruments based on material non-public information.
