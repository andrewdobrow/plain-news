Three people died and five others were injured after a natural gas explosion triggered a massive fire at a Dallas apartment complex on Thursday. Nearly 100 firefighters responded to battle the blaze, which left the building collapsed and reduced to rubble.

The bodies of two adult women and one child were recovered from the debris, according to Dallas Fire and Rescue. Four additional people were hospitalized with injuries following the explosion.

Dallas Fire and Rescue Assistant Chief James Russ announced the confirmed deaths at a press conference. "The fire is contained, but our members are still working on the scene to do primary searches," Russ said. He noted that firefighters deployed a drone to survey the area for potential victims.

Witnesses described the force of the explosion as severe. Julie Jensen, who lived less than a block away, said she was watching television when the blast occurred. "I was sitting on my couch watching TV and stuff flew off our walls," she said. After hearing the noise and seeing smoke rise from the complex, she grabbed her family's cat and fled to a nearby parking lot.

Another resident, Terrance, told news outlets that he lost everything in the fire. "I lost my animals and everything," he said. "They probably burnt up, man. Two dogs, three cats. Getting off the bus coming home, and I've seen all this smoke. My wife says, 'That's our apartment complex.'"

The explosion occurred in the Oak Cliff area of Dallas. Multiple emergency response agencies deployed to the scene, with firefighters working through wreckage and debris. Some firefighters used long ladders to spray water on the smoking piles while others searched beneath burned lumber for possible survivors.

The blast was caused by natural gas, fire officials confirmed. The investigation into the exact circumstances and source of the explosion remained active as crews continued working at the site.

Dallas Mayor Eric Johnson responded to the disaster on social media. "I am closely monitoring the latest developments regarding the explosion in Oak Cliff," Johnson tweeted. "My prayers are with those injured, their families, and all those who have suffered loss from this tragic event."

The apartment complex sustained catastrophic damage from the force of the explosion. Emergency crews continued search and rescue operations as authorities worked to account for all residents and determine if additional people remained missing.

The incident affected numerous families living at the complex and residents in the surrounding area. Local officials coordinated the emergency response as rescue workers searched through the destroyed building.

A Russian drone struck an apartment building in the Romanian city of Galati, injuring two people, Romanian authorities said. The incident marks the first reported Russian attack on infrastructure in a NATO member state during the conflict with Ukraine.

Galati is located near the Romanian border with Ukraine. The drone hit a residential apartment building in the city. Two people sustained injuries in the strike, according to Romanian officials.

Romania is a member of NATO, which operates under a principle of collective defense. An attack on one member state is considered an attack on all members under Article 5 of the NATO treaty.

The strike comes as Russian forces continue military operations against Ukraine. Romania shares a border with Ukraine and has been a transit point for refugees fleeing the conflict. The country has also served as a logistical hub for Western military aid to Ukraine.

Russian drones have previously strayed into Romanian airspace during attacks on Ukrainian targets near the border. Romanian authorities have documented several incidents of drone fragments landing on Romanian territory. This incident represents the first time a Russian drone has directly struck a building with casualties on Romanian soil.

Romanian officials did not immediately provide details about the type of drone involved or whether they believe the strike was intentional or the result of a navigation error. The Romanian government has not yet issued a formal statement on how it plans to respond to the attack.

NATO headquarters had not released an official statement on the incident at the time of reporting. The alliance has previously said it would treat any attack on member territory seriously and consult with affected members to determine an appropriate response.

The two injured individuals were in the apartment building at the time of the strike. Romanian authorities did not release information about the severity of their injuries or their current condition.

10:21 PM ET

A New Glenn rocket operated by Blue Origin exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday evening, dealing a significant blow to Jeff Bezos' spaceflight company and NASA's lunar ambitions.

The explosion occurred at 9pm ET during a scheduled "hotfire" test at Kennedy Space Center. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad, with the orange glow visible from Fort Pierce, 115 miles to the south. Shockwaves from the blast were felt across Florida's space coast, with residents in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach reporting that their homes shook. People hundreds of miles away in South Carolina reported seeing the glow in the sky. Emergency officials confirmed there were no hazards from fumes or other releases.

Blue Origin said all employees were safe and accounted for. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" in an initial statement. Bezos acknowledged the setback on social media, tweeting that it was "a very rough day" but that the company would "rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying."

The explosion threatens NASA's timeline for returning humans to the moon. Just two days before the test failure, NASA had announced that Blue Origin won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions designed to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of how the explosion affects the Artemis program timeline and moon base construction plans.

New Glenn is Blue Origin's heavy-lift orbital launch vehicle, designed to compete with SpaceX's Falcon Heavy for satellite launches and government contracts. The rocket is named after John Glenn, the first American to orbit Earth. It was meant to serve as a central component in NASA's Artemis Program, which aims to land astronauts on the moon for the first time since 1972.

This explosion marks the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin. Last month, a payload from the third New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit, prompting the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the vehicle to return to flight the previous week.

The failure will likely delay Blue Origin's operational launch timeline. The company must investigate the root cause of the failure, implement necessary repairs, and conduct additional testing before attempting another launch. This process typically requires months, depending on the severity of the underlying issues.

SpaceX, Blue Origin's primary competitor for NASA contracts, is also involved in the Artemis program. The companies are competing to develop lunar landers for future missions planned through 2028.

Vice President Vance said the U.S. was very close to finalizing the ag...

Yesterday, 7:09 PM ET

The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire for 60 days and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to U.S. officials. The framework includes plans to restart talks about Iran's nuclear program, though final approval from both countries' leadership remains pending.

Vice President Vance said the U.S. was very close to finalizing the agreement but acknowledged that sticking points remained. The two sides continue negotiating what Vance described as "nuclear stuff," indicating disagreements persist over key details of any nuclear framework.

Under the proposed deal, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and allow its ports to resume normal operations. The waterway, which handles roughly a fifth of global oil traffic, has been a critical flashpoint throughout the three-month conflict. As part of the arrangement, Iran would clear mines it deployed in the strait and refrain from imposing tolls on passing ships.

In exchange, the U.S. would lift its blockade on Iranian ports that has been in place since April 13. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the deal could achieve a completely open Strait of Hormuz without tolls if successful. The agreement would also unfreeze some Iranian assets held in banks outside Iran.

The 60-day extension would halt hostilities on all fronts, including between Israel and Lebanon. This period would give negotiators additional time to work out details on a broader nuclear framework and address other outstanding issues between the nations.

However, important questions remain unresolved. A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Tehran had not agreed to hand over its highly enriched uranium stockpile, and that Iran's nuclear issue was not part of the preliminary agreement. Previous reports suggested Tehran might be willing to give up some of its stockpile, but no details have emerged about how such an arrangement would be implemented.

The potential deal also lacks mention of Iran's ballistic missile program or efforts to limit support for regional allies including Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Houthis in Yemen. These issues may be deferred to future negotiations or excluded entirely from current discussions.

The tentative agreement comes after months of military tensions between the countries. Both sides appear to recognize the mutual benefits of reducing immediate conflict while pursuing longer-term diplomatic solutions. Reopening the Strait of Hormuz would restore normal shipping operations and help stabilize global energy markets, which have faced uncertainty during the conflict.

Officials from both countries continue working to finalize the memorandum of understanding that would codify the extended ceasefire and create a structure for future discussions. While neither side appears interested in returning to all-out conflict, the persistence of disagreements suggests negotiations will remain challenging in coming weeks.
