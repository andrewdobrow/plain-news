Recovery crews have found six of the nine workers missing after an explosion at a paper mill in Longview, Washington, bringing the total confirmed death toll to eight. Three workers remain unaccounted for at the Nippon mill site.

The disaster occurred when a chemical tank ruptured at the facility. According to reports, eleven people are now presumed dead from the incident, while nine others were injured.

Search and recovery operations continue at the mill as crews work to locate the remaining three missing employees. The explosion site remains an active disaster zone as investigators work to determine the cause of the tank failure.

The Longview paper mill processes pulp and paper products using various chemicals for bleaching and processing operations. These chemicals are typically stored in large industrial containers. The specific chemical involved in the tank rupture has not been identified in available reports.

Families of the missing workers have been waiting for news as recovery efforts proceed. The recovered remains are being transported for identification. Authorities have not released the names of the victims.

The facility has suspended operations following the incident. Industrial accidents involving chemical processing equipment can result from equipment failure, pressure buildup, or structural weaknesses in storage containers.

Local emergency response teams and federal workplace safety investigators are examining the scene. The investigation will focus on the condition of the chemical tank, maintenance records, and safety protocols that were in place at the time of the explosion.

This marks one of the deadliest industrial accidents in Washington state in recent years. The paper industry relies on various chemicals for pulp processing and bleaching operations, which creates inherent hazards in facilities handling these materials.

The three workers who remain missing are still being sought by recovery teams working through the damaged sections of the mill. Recovery efforts continue as authorities work to complete the search of the disaster site.

The community of Longview, a timber and paper industry center along the Columbia River, has been affected by the scale of the tragedy. The incident has prompted significant response from multiple agencies coordinating recovery and investigation efforts.

Support services have been made available to families and coworkers of those killed in the explosion. Emergency services personnel have worked extensively at the site to recover remains and secure the area.

As the investigation proceeds, authorities will examine all factors that may have contributed to the tank rupture. The focus remains on understanding the equipment failure and determining whether any lapses in maintenance or safety protocols played a role in the disaster.

3:48 PM ET

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed the Israeli military to expand territorial control to 70 percent of the Gaza Strip, a move that threatens to collapse an already fragile ceasefire and worsen humanitarian conditions in the devastated territory.

Under an October ceasefire brokered by the United States, Israeli forces initially withdrew to a demarcation line that gave Israel control of 53 percent of Gaza. Since then, Israeli forces have steadily advanced westward into areas previously controlled by Hamas, establishing an expanding buffer zone where they claim the right to prevent entry and use force against perceived threats.

Speaking at a conference in an occupied West Bank settlement, Netanyahu outlined Israel's territorial goals. "We are currently squeezing Hamas. We now control 60 percent of the territory in the strip. You know, we were at 50, we moved to 60. My directive is to move to 70 percent," he said.

The expansion would violate the October ceasefire agreement, the UN Security Council resolution that endorsed it, and Donald Trump's 20-point peace plan. That plan established a temporary dividing line between Israeli and Hamas-controlled zones pending further negotiations and stated that no Palestinians would be forced to leave Gaza.

Defence Minister Israel Katz said the government's ultimate goal is for large numbers of Palestinians to leave Gaza through what he termed "voluntary migration." Human rights advocates describe this as ethnic cleansing achieved by making conditions intolerable.

Throughout the eight-month ceasefire, Israeli forces have killed more than 900 Palestinians through gunfire and airstrikes in areas under their control or nearby. Israeli-backed armed militias have taken an active role along the ceasefire line, advancing westward and ordering residents to evacuate their homes. One Gaza resident, Wael Nayef Abu al-Ajeen, described being forced out by militiamen who gave his family until 10 p.m. to leave their area near Deir al-Balah.

The expansion would force Gaza's 2.2 million surviving Palestinians into less than a third of their original territory. The area is already described as the single most overcrowded place on the planet, with every available space occupied by displaced families and improvised shelters.

Muhammad Shehada, a visiting fellow at the European Council on Foreign Relations, said the directive amounts to declaring Trump's peace framework "null and void." Nasser Khdour, a researcher at the Armed Conflict Location and Event Data Project, noted that militias are not only attacking Hamas but also forcibly displacing residents living near the ceasefire line.

The Israeli army declined to comment on Netanyahu's directive, referring requests to the political leadership. The timeline for implementing the expansion remains unclear.

Cloud providers are adjusting their systems as companies deploy AI agents for tasks ranging from data retrieval to automated decision-making. These agents operate continuously and make rapid-fire requests to web services and APIs.

The shift comes as businesses move AI capabilities from testing phases into everyday operations. Companies using AI agents need infrastructure that can handle automated processes running at machine speed rather than human pace.

The redesign efforts span multiple layers of internet infrastructure, from content delivery networks to server architecture. Providers are building systems optimized for the request patterns and data flows generated by automated agents rather than individual users clicking through websites.

This represents a fundamental change in how internet infrastructure is designed. For decades, systems were built around the assumption that humans would be the primary users, with design choices reflecting human browsing speeds and patterns.

The transition indicates that AI agents are becoming common enough in production environments to justify wholesale infrastructure changes by major cloud providers. The companies are investing in these redesigns as they anticipate continued growth in machine-to-machine internet traffic.

A 21-year-old Austrian man has been found guilty of planning a jihadist attack on Taylor Swift concertgoers in Vienna and sentenced to 15 years in prison. Beran A., identified according to Austrian privacy laws, pleaded guilty to terrorism-related charges connected to the foiled plot.

The defendant admitted to planning an assault on fans gathered outside Ernst Happel stadium during Swift's scheduled performances in August 2024. Authorities estimated up to 30,000 people would have been outside the venue each night, with another 65,000 inside. Prosecutors say Beran A. aimed to "kill as many people as possible" using knives and homemade explosives.

The plot was disrupted at the last minute, leading Austrian authorities to cancel Swift's three scheduled performances. Intelligence from the United States contributed to the decision to scrapped the concerts. When police searched Beran A.'s apartment on August 7, 2024, one day before the first scheduled show, they discovered bomb-making materials.

"Having our Vienna shows cancelled was devastating," Swift said in a statement posted to Instagram two weeks after the cancellation. "The reason for the cancellations filled me with a new sense of fear, and a tremendous amount of guilt because so many people had planned on coming to those shows."

Beran A. had allegedly networked with other Islamic State members before the planned attack. Prosecutors say the group discussed acquiring weapons and manufacturing bombs. Days before the concert, the defendant allegedly attempted to illegally purchase firearms, including a machine gun and hand grenade. He also reportedly viewed Islamic State instructional videos on making shrapnel bombs and produced a small quantity of an explosive compound.

Beyond the Swift concert plot, Beran A. is also accused of planning attacks in the Middle East alongside a Slovak national named Arda K. Prosecutors allege both men traveled to designated cities in March 2024 with intentions to carry out militant attacks. Beran A. traveled to Dubai while Arda K. went to Istanbul. A third man, Hasan E., is believed to have carried out a knife attack at Mecca's Grand Mosque on March 11, 2024, and remains in custody in Saudi Arabia.

Beran A.'s defense attorney, Anna Mair, stated that her client was remorseful. "Of course, he deeply regrets it all," Mair said outside court. "It is also due to the long period of detention that he says it was the biggest mistake of his life."

The case faced comparison to a 2017 suicide bombing at an Ariana Grande concert in Manchester, which killed 22 people. A Berlin court separately convicted a 16-year-old Syrian teenager of contributing to the Taylor Swift concert plot, sentencing him to an 18-month suspended sentence.

The deal also aims to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping passage closed by Iran early in the three-month conflict. President Donald Trump unilaterally announced the ceasef...

7:09 PM ET

The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire for 60 days and establish a framework for discussions on Iran's nuclear program, according to US sources. The deal also aims to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping passage closed by Iran early in the three-month conflict.

President Donald Trump unilaterally announced the ceasefire extension on Tuesday, stating he would hold off military operations until Iranian leaders submit a unified peace proposal. "I have therefore directed our Military to continue the Blockade and remain ready and able, and will therefore extend the Ceasefire until such time as their proposal is submitted," Trump wrote on Truth Social.

The move came after intense diplomatic efforts involving Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who has served as a principal mediator between the two countries. However, Iran's response has been mixed and cautious.

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and the regime's lead negotiator, dismissed Trump's ceasefire extension as "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike." His adviser, Mahdi Mohammadi, wrote on social media that "the losing side cannot dictate terms" and warned that continuing the US blockade would be met with military response.

The disagreement centers partly on the US Navy blockade of Iranian ports. Iran has demanded the blockade end before negotiations can resume, while Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have stated the blockade will continue. The ongoing blockade threatens to fill Iran's oil storage capacity within days, forcing wells to shut down.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has had severe global consequences. The waterway normally handles about a fifth of the world's oil and gas supplies. Fatih Birol, head of the International Energy Agency, described the combined impact of the conflict's effects on oil and Russia's war on gas supplies as "the biggest crisis in history" in global energy markets.

A first round of talks in Islamabad ended 10 days ago without agreement on reopening the strait. Meanwhile, tensions have escalated as the US seized Iranian cargo vessels and boarded an oil tanker previously sanctioned for smuggling Iranian crude.

Despite the tentative agreement's framework, significant disagreements remain. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei characterized recent US actions against vessels as "piracy at sea and state terrorism," questioning Washington's sincerity in negotiations.

The tentative nature of the agreement means final terms are still being worked out between negotiators. Neither the timeline for signing the memorandum nor the exact effective date of the ceasefire extension has been confirmed by US sources.
