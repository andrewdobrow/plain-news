8:08 PM ET

Berkshire Hathaway has acquired Taylor Morrison, a national homebuilder, in a transaction valued between $6.8 billion and $8.5 billion. The deal represents the first major takeover completed under the leadership of Greg Abel, who recently became CEO of the conglomerate.

Warren Buffett announced his retirement plans at Berkshire's annual shareholder meeting, stating he would step down as CEO and chair at the end of 2025. The 94-year-old billionaire, who has led the company since 1965, recommended that Abel succeed him in the top role. "I think the time has arrived where Greg should become the chief executive officer of the company at year end," Buffett said at the event in Omaha, Nebraska. He emphasized his confidence in Abel's capabilities, saying "The decision to keep every share is an economic decision because I think the prospects of Berkshire will be better under Greg's management than mine."

Abel, 62, has been designated as Buffett's successor since 2001 and brings 25 years of experience at Berkshire. As vice-chair, he currently oversees the company's non-insurance businesses, including fossil energy, chemical, real estate, and retail operations. The Taylor Morrison acquisition marks his first major deal as the company's new chief executive.

The valuation discrepancy between the $6.8 billion and $8.5 billion figures likely reflects different methodologies for calculating enterprise value versus equity value, or variations in how assumed debt is counted. The acquisition adds residential construction and land development operations to Berkshire's existing housing sector exposure through its building materials and home furnishings subsidiaries.

Taylor Morrison operates as a national homebuilder across multiple markets. The purchase strengthens Berkshire's position in the housing industry, an area where the company maintains long-term interest through various subsidiaries, including paint manufacturer Benjamin Moore and other building materials firms.

The timing of the acquisition comes as Buffett continues to lead the conglomerate during its leadership transition. In February, Berkshire reported record annual operating profit of $47.44 billion for 2024, representing a 27 percent increase. The company now operates 189 businesses spanning insurance, railroads, energy, manufacturing, and other sectors.

At the shareholder meeting, Buffett also addressed broader economic concerns, warning about potential consequences of trade policies and the importance of international commerce. He emphasized that maintaining positive global relationships benefits all parties involved in trade.

The Taylor Morrison deal reflects Berkshire's historical investment strategy of acquiring companies in sectors where it identifies long-term value and stable cash flows. The housing market has faced challenges from elevated mortgage rates in recent years, though homebuilders have adapted by offering rate buydowns and developing more affordable housing options.

For many in Lebanon, Nabatieh carries symbolic weight extending beyond its military value, having repeatedly been caught in the crossfire of Israeli campaigns throughout history.

Mohanad Hage Ali, a senior fellow at the Carnegie Middle East Center, warned of concerning possibilities. He suggested the level of destruction raises questions about whether Israel intends to deny the population's return or pursue settlement similar to actions in the West Bank. "Annexation is no longer a wild conspiracy theory," he said, referencing ministerial statements from Israeli officials.

Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam accused Israel of pursuing "a scorched-earth policy and collective punishment" through the destruction of towns and villages and forced displacement of inhabitants. He called for an immediate ceasefire and defended his government's engagement in US-brokered negotiations with Israel, describing them as "the least costly path for our country and our people."

The offensive has displaced more than 1.2 million people since March 2 through Israeli strikes and evacuation orders. A ceasefire nominally began on April 17 but has never been observed, with Israel and Hezbollah accusing each other of daily violations.

The escalation complicates ongoing US-Iran negotiations, as Tehran has indicated any deal must include an end to fighting in Lebanon. Observers suggest Israeli officials and military commanders want to inflict maximum damage on Hezbollah before potential diplomatic agreements impose new restrictions on their operations.

Dell has announced a new XPS 13 laptop designed to challenge Apple's entry-level MacBook Neo. The device will launch in July with an introductory student price of $599, though this promotional pricing runs only until September. After the back-to-school period ends, the laptop will cost $699 for general consumers.

The MacBook Neo, Apple's newest entry-level offering, starts at $599 and represents a significant shift in Apple's laptop strategy. It uses the A18 Pro chip from the iPhone 16 Pro rather than Apple's traditional M-series processors. The MacBook Neo features a 13-inch LCD screen with a resolution of 2408x1506 pixels, 8GB of RAM, and either 256GB or 512GB of storage. It weighs 1.23kg and measures 12.7mm thick.

Apple's MacBook Neo delivers strong performance for everyday tasks despite its lower specifications compared to other Mac models. The device manages approximately 13 hours of battery life during general use including browsing, note-taking, image editing, and email. The keyboard and trackpad maintain the quality standards of higher-end MacBooks, though the base model lacks Touch ID, which only comes with the more expensive 512GB storage option.

The MacBook Neo includes features that some budget laptops omit, such as a headphone jack, mechanical trackpad, and 1080p webcam. However, it has some limitations. The screen lacks True Tone technology and the port selection is restricted, with only one USB-C port supporting USB3 speeds. Neither USB-C port supports Thunderbolt, and the device cannot drive displays higher than 4K at 60Hz.

Dell's new XPS 13 positions itself as an alternative for budget-conscious laptop buyers. The company has traditionally positioned the XPS line as a premium product, but this model represents a significant price reduction aimed at capturing customers who might otherwise choose Apple's entry-level option.

Both laptops target similar buyers: those seeking a lightweight, well-built machine for everyday computing without the premium price tag of higher-end models. The MacBook Neo comes preloaded with macOS 26 Tahoe and includes Apple's suite of free applications such as Pages, Numbers, Keynote, and iMovie, giving users productivity and creative software from the start.

The competition between these two devices reflects broader changes in the laptop market, where manufacturers are increasingly focused on delivering capable machines at accessible price points. Dell's timing with the XPS 13 announcement suggests the company sees genuine market demand at this price level, particularly among students and young professionals evaluating their options during back-to-school season.

Wembanyama's supporting cast also delivered crucial performances. Julian Champagnie scored 20 points and made six three-pointers, while Dylan Harper provided a spark off the bench. Luke Kornet, Wembanyama's backup, made a key defensive play with a block in the fourth quarter as the Thunder mounted a comeback attempt.

The Thunder, defending NBA champions, struggled without guard Jalen Williams due to a hamstring injury. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, the reigning NBA MVP, led Oklahoma City with 35 points and nine assists, but no other Thunder player scored more than 20 points. As the game neared its end, the Thunder were forced to take desperate three-pointers from well beyond the arc. The Spurs sealed the win with a breakaway dunk.

Powell warns Federal Reserve credibility at risk in first remarks since leaving office

Former Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell has issued a stark warning that the central bank faces a credibility crisis in his first public comments since his term ended on May 15. Speaking while accepting the John F. Kennedy Profiles in Courage award on Sunday evening, Powell stated the Fed is undergoing a "stress test" with its credibility on the line. Though Powell did not e...

Yesterday, 8:31 PM ET

Former Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell has issued a stark warning that the central bank faces a credibility crisis in his first public comments since his term ended on May 15. Speaking while accepting the John F. Kennedy Profiles in Courage award on Sunday evening, Powell stated the Fed is undergoing a "stress test" with its credibility on the line.

Though Powell did not explicitly name the Trump administration in his remarks, the context made his concerns clear. The administration has taken several steps that challenge Fed independence, including launching a criminal investigation into the central bank's over-budget building renovation and attempting to remove Fed governor Lisa Cook.

Powell framed threats to Fed independence as dangers to an institution essential to American economic strength. The Federal Reserve has historically operated independently from political pressure to maintain public confidence in monetary policy decisions. This independence is considered fundamental to economic stability, allowing policymakers to base decisions on economic data rather than political considerations.

The timing of Powell's statement underscores his alarm about potential long-term damage to the Fed's reputation and effectiveness. Central banks worldwide depend on credibility to manage inflation expectations and preserve financial stability. Loss of public confidence in the Fed's independence could severely compromise its ability to fulfill its core mission.

Powell's decision to speak publicly immediately after leaving office is particularly significant. Though Fed leaders typically depart quietly after their terms conclude, Powell has announced he will remain on the Fed's board as a voting governor until the White House ends its scrutiny of the building renovation project. Powell characterized this scrutiny as "pretext" designed to pressure the central bank into lowering interest rates.

The former chair's warning comes as the Fed faces a transitional moment. Kevin Warsh, confirmed by the Senate on Wednesday in a 54-45 party-line vote, will take over as Fed chair on May 14. The confirmation was the most divisive in the position's history, with only one Democrat, Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, voting yes.

Warsh, a former Fed governor and Wall Street banker, has previously supported lower interest rates, echoing Trump's position. However, he will need to convince other members of the Fed's 12-member voting board to approve rate cuts. With inflation currently at 3.8%, making the case for lower rates could prove difficult.

At his Senate confirmation hearing, Warsh pledged to maintain Fed independence and remove politics from monetary policy. Yet his reluctance to answer whether Trump lost the 2020 election raised concerns among Democrats that he may prioritize political considerations over economic data.

Powell's warning reflects anxiety that the incoming administration may fundamentally alter how the central bank operates and makes decisions about interest rates and monetary policy.
