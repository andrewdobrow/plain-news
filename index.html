Iranian drones struck Kuwait's international airport, killing at least one person and wounding 63 others. The attack damaged a passenger terminal and forced the airport to suspend traffic and redirect incoming flights to alternative airports.

The strike represents a significant escalation in the Gulf region and marks the first deadly attack since a ceasefire took effect on April 8. Iran said the assault was retaliation for earlier US military action, including a strike on an Iranian oil tanker near the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on Iran's Qeshm Island.

The sequence of events began when US Central Command targeted an unladen tanker, the M/T Lexie, in international waters as it moved toward Iran's Kharg Island near Kuwait. US forces fired a Hellfire missile to disable the vessel's engine after the crew ignored repeated warnings over 24 hours. In response, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps attacked the US Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain with missiles and drones, a claim the US military denied. The Iranian military also launched three attack drones toward civilian ships in regional waters, which US forces said they shot down.

Kuwait's defense ministry called the airport attack "criminal Iranian aggression" causing significant material damage. The country summoned Iran's diplomatic representative to formally protest and ordered a reduction in Iranian embassy staff. Kuwait declared two Iranian diplomats persona non grata and gave them 24 hours to leave.

Iran's foreign ministry blamed Kuwait and Bahrain for the attacks, condemning what it described as the "colonialist use" of their territory and infrastructure by the United States to advance military plans against Iran. The statement held Kuwait and Bahrain's leaders "directly and unmistakably responsible" for the earlier strikes.

The attacks undermine ongoing diplomatic efforts between the United States and Iran. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio claimed during congressional testimony that a nuclear deal could be imminent, suggesting progress on aspects of Iran's nuclear program previously off limits for negotiation. However, Iran has indicated it would suspend peace talks in protest of the US actions.

Donald Trump said he is not seeking to escalate tensions and that talks with Iran continue. He claimed Iran is "pretty close" to signing a peace agreement and suggested it "could happen over the weekend." Trump also expressed interest in meeting Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned that Iran is "playing with fire" and suggested Trump could order a "full-scale return to military action" if negotiations fail. Netanyahu asserted that Israel and US forces remain ready for military operations.

The latest exchange of strikes highlights the fragility of ceasefire arrangements and the difficulty of achieving diplomatic breakthroughs amid continued military confrontation in the Middle East.

The deal, announced Monday on SpaceX's website, values SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion, setting the combined entity on course for a stock market valuation exceeding $1 trillion.

The merger creates what the companies described as "the most ambitious, vertically-integrated innovation engine on (and off) Earth, with AI, rockets, space-based internet, direct-to-mobile device communications and the world's foremost real-time information and free speech platform." Through the acquisition, SpaceX gains xAI's Grok chatbot and the social media platform X.

The consolidation reflects Musk's strategy to combine space technology with artificial intelligence infrastructure. Both companies cited plans for space-based datacenters powered by solar-powered satellites as central to the merger's rationale. According to the announcement, current AI advances depend on large terrestrial datacenters that consume enormous amounts of power and cooling. The companies stated that "global electricity demand for AI simply cannot be met with terrestrial solutions, even in the near term, without imposing hardship on communities and the environment" and that "in the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale."

The IPO is expected to occur in early summer, timed to coincide with a planetary alignment and Musk's birthday on June 28. Musk confirmed the deal minimally on X, responding with "yes" to posts referencing merger reports.

Both companies have experienced significant valuation growth recently. SpaceX revealed an expected value of $800 billion in a December letter to investors as it continues dominating satellite launches and securing contracts with the U.S. federal government. xAI raised $20 billion in a Series E funding round last month that valued the company at $230 billion, despite controversy surrounding its Grok chatbot over promotion of racist content and nonconsensual deepfake images.

The merger comes as Musk has increasingly intertwined his various business interests. xAI acquired the platform X in an all-stock transaction in early 2025, and Tesla announced plans last month to invest $2 billion in xAI.

The announcement provides Musk with positive momentum after Tesla released disappointing earnings showing declining revenues and a struggling car business. The news also comes days after the Department of Justice released files related to Jeffrey Epstein that showed Musk exchanging friendly emails with the disgraced financier. Musk called the email release a "distraction" and stated he was "well aware that some email correspondence with him could be misinterpreted and used by detractors to smear my name."

Yesterday, 9:29 PM ET

NASA has confirmed the end of its Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission after losing contact with the spacecraft. The orbiter stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend after operating normally before passing behind Mars. When it reappeared from behind the planet, the spacecraft remained silent.

"Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," NASA said in a statement. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly, but ground controllers were unable to restore contact or functionality. NASA declared MAVEN in an unrecoverable state after attempting to reestablish communications for six months.

MAVEN launched in 2013 and reached Mars the following year to begin its mission studying the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with the solar wind. During its more than a decade in orbit, the spacecraft collected extensive data about how Mars loses atmospheric gases to space. Scientists used MAVEN's findings to understand a fundamental question about the planet's history.

The research helped explain how Mars transformed from a world that may have been wet and warm to the cold and dry environment observed today. The sun itself played a crucial role in this transformation. Solar wind and radiation drove processes that stripped away most of the planet's atmosphere over billions of years, fundamentally changing Mars' climate and surface conditions.

Beyond its primary scientific mission, MAVEN served an important operational function. The spacecraft acted as a communications relay for NASA's two Mars rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance. These rovers have conducted extensive exploration of the planet and made numerous scientific discoveries that depend partly on MAVEN's relay capabilities.

The loss of MAVEN reduces NASA's fleet of active Mars orbiters but does not leave the agency without atmospheric monitoring capabilities. Two other NASA spacecraft continue to operate around Mars. Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, launched in 2001, remain active and continue studying the planet and supporting ongoing surface missions.

The mission significantly exceeded its original planned lifespan, continuing to transmit valuable scientific data well beyond initial expectations. MAVEN completed thousands of orbits around Mars during its operational period and made key discoveries about atmospheric escape processes.

Most NASA spacecraft missions at Mars end through planned decommissioning rather than sudden anomalies. The unexpected loss of MAVEN after so many years of successful operation marked an unusual conclusion to the long-running mission. Despite the loss, NASA continues to develop future Mars exploration missions as part of its broader planetary science program, maintaining its commitment to understanding the red planet and supporting ongoing surface exploration efforts.

NASA has confirmed the end of its Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission after losing contact with the spacecraft. The orbiter stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend after operating normally before passing behind Mars. When it reappeared from behind the planet, the spacecraft remained silent.

"Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," NASA said in a statement. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly, but ground controllers were unable to restore contact or functionality. NASA declared MAVEN in an unrecoverable state after attempting to reestablish communications for six months.

MAVEN launched in 2013 and reached Mars the following year to begin its mission studying the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with the solar wind. During its more than a decade in orbit, the spacecraft collected extensive data about how Mars loses atmospheric gases to space. Scientists used MAVEN's findings to understand a fundamental question about the planet's history.

The research helped explain how Mars transformed from a world that may have been wet and warm to the cold and dry environment observed today. The sun itself played a crucial role in this transformation. Solar wind and radiation drove processes that stripped away most of the planet's atmosphere over billions of years, fundamentally changing Mars' climate and surface conditions.

Beyond its primary scientific mission, MAVEN served an important operational function. The spacecraft acted as a communications relay for NASA's two Mars rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance. These rovers have conducted extensive exploration of the planet and made numerous scientific discoveries that depend partly on MAVEN's relay capabilities.

The loss of MAVEN reduces NASA's fleet of active Mars orbiters but does not leave the agency without atmospheric monitoring capabilities. Two other NASA spacecraft continue to operate around Mars. Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, launched in 2001, remain active and continue studying the planet and supporting ongoing surface missions.

The mission significantly exceeded its original planned lifespan, continuing to transmit valuable scientific data well beyond initial expectations. MAVEN completed thousands of orbits around Mars during its operational period and made key discoveries about atmospheric escape processes.

Most NASA spacecraft missions at Mars end through planned decommissioning rather than sudden anomalies. The unexpected loss of MAVEN after so many years of successful operation marked an unusual conclusion to the long-running mission. Despite the loss, NASA continues to develop future Mars exploration missions as part of its broader planetary science program, maintaining its commitment to understanding the red planet and supporting ongoing surface exploration efforts.
