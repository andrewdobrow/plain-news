Physicists at CERN's Large Hadron Collider observed a particle behaving in a way that existing theory did not predict, marking the strongest indication yet of physics beyond the Standard Model. The observation reached high statistical significance, meaning the behavior is unlikely to be a random fluctuation.

The Standard Model has guided particle physics for decades, describing the fundamental particles and forces that govern matter. This new anomaly suggests the model may be incomplete. The Large Hadron Collider, the world's most powerful particle accelerator located near Geneva, Switzerland, has been the site of major discoveries including the Higgs boson in 2012.

The exotic particle behavior detected at CERN could point to undiscovered forces or particles not accounted for in current physics. Scientists described the finding as having reached a threshold of statistical confidence that warrants serious attention from the physics community. Such anomalies, when confirmed through repeated observation, have historically led to fundamental revisions in scientific understanding.

The discovery comes from ongoing collision experiments where particles are accelerated to near light speed and smashed together, allowing physicists to observe the resulting debris and behavior. The specific particle involved displayed characteristics that deviate from theoretical predictions, opening questions about what additional physics might explain the observation.

Confirming whether this represents a genuine breakthrough or an experimental artifact will require additional data and analysis. CERN physicists will continue collision experiments to gather more observations of the phenomenon. If the anomaly persists under scrutiny, it could reshape fundamental physics and point the way toward a more complete theory of how the universe operates at its most basic level.

A few minutes ago

Former England manager and captain Kevin Keegan has revealed he has been diagnosed with cancer. The 74-year-old football legend was recently admitted to hospital for tests after experiencing ongoing abdominal symptoms, which confirmed the diagnosis of an undisclosed cancer type.

A statement from Keegan's family, shared through Newcastle United, said: "Kevin is grateful to the medical team for their intervention and ongoing care. During this difficult time the family are requesting privacy and will be making no further comment."

Keegan enjoyed one of English football's most celebrated playing careers. After helping Liverpool win three league titles along with the European Cup, two UEFA Cups and the FA Cup, he moved to Hamburg where he won the Bundesliga. His performances earned him back-to-back Ballon d'Or awards in 1978 and 1979, recognising him as European footballer of the year. Keegan won 63 caps for England, scoring 21 goals, and captained the national side 31 times. He also participated in the 1982 World Cup.

Following a period away from football spent largely playing golf in southern Spain, Keegan began his first managerial spell at Newcastle in 1992. He quickly secured the club's promotion to the top flight and built what became known as "The Entertainers" due to their thrilling attacking style of play. The team topped the Premier League table for much of the 1995-96 season but ultimately finished as runners-up as Sir Alex Ferguson's Manchester United overtook them in a memorable title race. Keegan resigned from Newcastle in January 1997.

He then managed Fulham before taking over as England manager in 1999, succeeding Glenn Hoddle. His tenure proved difficult, marked by disappointing performances at the 2000 European Championship. He resigned in autumn 2000 following a defeat to Germany in the final match played at Wembley Stadium before its reconstruction.

Keegan spent four years managing Manchester City from 2001 to 2005 before announcing his retirement. However, he returned to Newcastle for an emotional second spell in 2008. This period proved challenging due to tensions with owner Mike Ashley and director of football Dennis Wise, lasting only eight months before his departure. In 2009, an independent Premier League arbitration tribunal upheld Keegan's case for constructive dismissal and awarded him 2 million pounds in damages.

Throughout his career, Keegan earned various nicknames including "King Kev" and "Mighty Mouse". His impact on English football remains significant both for his achievements as a player competing at the highest levels of European football and his passionate approach to management.

Senator John Cornyn, a four-term incumbent and former Texas attorney general, faces a runoff challenge from state Attorney General Ken Paxton. Both candidates advanced to the runoff after neither secured enough votes in the primary to win outright. The winner will face Democrat James Talarico in the general election.

Cornyn represents what political analysts describe as the last remnants of the old-guard Republican establishment in Texas, tracing his political lineage to the Bush family era of pragmatic, business-focused conservatism. He has worked across party lines on bipartisan legislation and has been effective at fundraising for the party. However, this willingness to govern and compromise has made him vulnerable to challenges from the right. His 2022 participation in negotiating a bipartisan gun safety bill following the Uvalde school massacre particularly angered Trump's base, who viewed it as a betrayal.

Paxton, by contrast, pioneered the grievance-fueled, anti-establishment politics that would later define the Trump movement. Long before Trump entered politics, Paxton was pushing the boundaries of Republican orthodoxy through the Tea Party movement. As state attorney general, he launched investigations into abortion bans and transgender youth care issues, positioning himself as a national leader on the far right.

Yet Paxton arrives at the race carrying substantial political baggage. He was impeached in 2023 on corruption charges and acquitted in the Texas Senate. He faced felony securities fraud indictment but secured a dismissal through a pre-trial diversion agreement in 2024. His marriage of 38 years also ended in divorce last year.

Trump's endorsement of Paxton last week, describing him as "a true Maga warrior," has energized supporters in his hometown of McKinney. Voters like Jim Tubbesing cite Paxton's conservative credentials and policy positions, dismissing concerns about his legal troubles.

Political analysts suggest the race is fundamentally about style and direction rather than legislative substance. Both candidates would likely vote similarly on most legislation. Victory for Paxton would demonstrate Trump's continued influence over Republican politics, though his vulnerabilities could jeopardize the seat against the popular Democratic candidate.

Former England manager and captain Kevin Keegan has revealed he has been diagnosed with cancer. The 74-year-old football legend was recently admitted to hospital for tests after experiencing ongoing abdominal symptoms, which confirmed the diagnosis of an undisclosed cancer type.

A statement from Keegan's family, shared through Newcastle United, said: "Kevin is grateful to the medical team for their intervention and ongoing care. During this difficult time the family are requesting privacy and will be making no further comment."

Keegan enjoyed one of English football's most celebrated playing careers. After helping Liverpool win three league titles along with the European Cup, two UEFA Cups and the FA Cup, he moved to Hamburg where he won the Bundesliga. His performances earned him back-to-back Ballon d'Or awards in 1978 and 1979, recognising him as European footballer of the year. Keegan won 63 caps for England, scoring 21 goals, and captained the national side 31 times. He also participated in the 1982 World Cup.

Following a period away from football spent largely playing golf in southern Spain, Keegan began his first managerial spell at Newcastle in 1992. He quickly secured the club's promotion to the top flight and built what became known as "The Entertainers" due to their thrilling attacking style of play. The team topped the Premier League table for much of the 1995-96 season but ultimately finished as runners-up as Sir Alex Ferguson's Manchester United overtook them in a memorable title race. Keegan resigned from Newcastle in January 1997.

He then managed Fulham before taking over as England manager in 1999, succeeding Glenn Hoddle. His tenure proved difficult, marked by disappointing performances at the 2000 European Championship. He resigned in autumn 2000 following a defeat to Germany in the final match played at Wembley Stadium before its reconstruction.

Keegan spent four years managing Manchester City from 2001 to 2005 before announcing his retirement. However, he returned to Newcastle for an emotional second spell in 2008. This period proved challenging due to tensions with owner Mike Ashley and director of football Dennis Wise, lasting only eight months before his departure. In 2009, an independent Premier League arbitration tribunal upheld Keegan's case for constructive dismissal and awarded him 2 million pounds in damages.

Throughout his career, Keegan earned various nicknames including "King Kev" and "Mighty Mouse". His impact on English football remains significant both for his achievements as a player competing at the highest levels of European football and his passionate approach to management.

These challenges are exemplified by contentious primary battles within the party itself. In Texas, the Republican Senate race illustrates the deep tensions fracturing the GOP between its traditional establishment wing and a newer, more ideologically rigid faction aligned with former President Donald Trump.

Senator John Cornyn, a four-term incumbent and former Texas attorney general, faces a runoff challenge from state Attorney General Ken Paxton. Both candidates advanced to the runoff after neither secured enough votes in the primary to win outright. The winner will face Democrat James Talarico in the general election.

Cornyn represents what political analysts describe as the last remnants of the old-guard Republican establishment in Texas, tracing his political lineage to the Bush family era of pragmatic, business-focused conservatism. He has worked across party lines on bipartisan legislation and has been effective at fundraising for the party. However, this willingness to govern and compromise has made him vulnerable to challenges from the right. His 2022 participation in negotiating a bipartisan gun safety bill following the Uvalde school massacre particularly angered Trump's base, who viewed it as a betrayal.

Paxton, by contrast, pioneered the grievance-fueled, anti-establishment politics that would later define the Trump movement. Long before Trump entered politics, Paxton was pushing the boundaries of Republican orthodoxy through the Tea Party movement. As state attorney general, he launched investigations into abortion bans and transgender youth care issues, positioning himself as a national leader on the far right.

Yet Paxton arrives at the race carrying substantial political baggage. He was impeached in 2023 on corruption charges and acquitted in the Texas Senate. He faced felony securities fraud indictment but secured a dismissal through a pre-trial diversion agreement in 2024. His marriage of 38 years also ended in divorce last year.

Trump's endorsement of Paxton last week, describing him as "a true Maga warrior," has energized supporters in his hometown of McKinney. Voters like Jim Tubbesing cite Paxton's conservative credentials and policy positions, dismissing concerns about his legal troubles.

Political analysts suggest the race is fundamentally about style and direction rather than legislative substance. Both candidates would likely vote similarly on most legislation. Victory for Paxton would demonstrate Trump's continued influence over Republican politics, though his vulnerabilities could jeopardize the seat against the popular Democratic candidate.
