1:09 AM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral's Launch Complex 36 in Florida on Thursday evening at 9pm ET, destroying the launch pad in a massive fireball. The explosion was visible from miles away, with an orange sky reported in Fort Pierce, 115 miles to the south, and shockwaves felt across Florida's space coast and into South Carolina.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were safe and accounted for, with no personnel injured in the incident. The company described the explosion as "an anomaly" and has begun investigating the cause. Jeff Bezos, Blue Origin's founder, acknowledged the setback on social media, stating "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The explosion carries significant implications for NASA's lunar ambitions. The New Glenn is the rocket Blue Origin intends to use for launching lunar landers to the moon as part of NASA's Artemis program. Just days before the explosion, NASA had announced that Blue Origin won a contract to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of a 20 billion dollar moon base.

The incident threatens the timeline for NASA's goal to return humans to the moon within the next two years. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of mission timelines following the explosion, noting that "developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult." NASA said it would provide information about impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs as details become available.

This represents the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from New Glenn's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to ground the rocket temporarily. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the vehicle to return to flight.

Blue Origin and SpaceX are both competing for roles in the Artemis program. Artemis III, scheduled for 2027, will test both Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander and SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System to determine which will carry astronauts from the Orion capsule to the lunar surface during Artemis IV in 2028.

The explosion gives SpaceX a competitive advantage in the commercial space sector. SpaceX founder Elon Musk responded to the incident on social media, saying "Most unfortunate. Rockets are hard." The investigation into what caused the explosion will likely take several months, during which Blue Origin must assess damage to both the rocket and launch infrastructure before resuming operations.
