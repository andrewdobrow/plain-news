9:23 PM ET

The Trump administration has signaled it will stop work on an $1.8 billion fund designed to compensate individuals claiming they were targeted by federal agencies, following a court order and mounting Republican opposition.

The Justice Department announced it would pause the program to comply with a court ruling that halted the initiative. The fund had been created to provide financial compensation to people who believed they experienced political persecution by federal law enforcement or other government agencies. The announcement came after the administration communicated its retreat to GOP congressional leaders.

Though the Justice Department stated it "strongly disagrees" with the court's decision, the department said it would still abide by the ruling.

The fund emerged from a settlement in a case involving Trump and the Internal Revenue Service. The program drew intense criticism from multiple directions since its announcement two weeks earlier. More than a dozen Republican senators reportedly urged the administration to reconsider the initiative, expressing concerns about lack of oversight and the potential for payouts to individuals involved in the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot.

Democrats condemned the fund as unprecedented self-dealing. "This is just an attempt to clear the path for Senate Republicans to install President Trump's sock puppet," Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren said in response to related Justice Department actions, highlighting broader concerns about political independence in federal agencies.

Polling data showed that majorities of both Democrats and Republicans opposed the fund, indicating widespread public disapproval across party lines.

The program's supporters had argued it would provide accountability for what they characterized as weaponization of federal agencies and serve as a formal mechanism for individuals to seek redress for alleged political persecution. However, Republican concerns about the fund's structure and potential costs to taxpayers proved influential in shifting the administration's position.

The political resistance from within Trump's own party created a challenging dynamic for the administration on this signature proposal. The combination of judicial intervention through the court order and congressional Republican opposition proved decisive in the program's fate.

The decision represents a significant reversal for the administration on a key policy initiative. The retreat leaves unresolved the original concerns that motivated the fund's creation, raising questions about what alternative approaches the administration might pursue to address claims of government overreach and political targeting.

The Supreme Court continues to weigh related constitutional questions about presidential authority, with additional rulings expected by June.

The strikes represent a continuation of Russian military pressure on Ukrainian population centers since the invasion began. The attacks demonstrate the vulnerability of civilians in cities across the country, as Russian forces maintain their assault on infrastructure and residential areas. As rescue efforts continued, authorities expected the death toll to rise as workers uncovered more bodies in the rubble of destroyed structures.

The firings have sparked significant internal resistance from veteran journalists who regard 60 Minutes as a cornerstone of broadcast journalism. The program has aired for decades and built its reputation on investigative reporting and numerous awards.

Alfonsi issued a statement Wednesday saying the separation between editorial independence and corporate interests at CBS is being dismantled. "Journalists willing to challenge authority are being pushed aside in favor of those who will not," she said. She noted that a December 2025 segment about a prison in El Salvador that her team reported was shelved by Weiss.

Vega, who joined the show in 2023, suggested in her own statement that political bias influenced recent decisions at the network. "In recent months, my producing teams and I have experienced efforts to insert political bias into our stories," she said. She described instances where reporting teams hesitated to submit story pitches due to fears about internal consequences, characterizing this as censorship that threatens both the show and democracy. CBS News disputed Vega's characterization.

Former 60 Minutes executive producer Bill Owens also weighed in Monday evening, warning about the direction the show is taking under current leadership. He emphasized that CBS News and 60 Minutes should remain institutions where editorial integrity guides decisions, not partisan or ideological considerations.

Yesterday, 10:49 PM ET

A federal appeals court panel ruled Monday that the Trump administration unconstitutionally removed active-duty transgender service members under a policy implemented by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The divided decision prevents the military from expelling troops already serving based on their transgender status.

Hegseth responded to the ruling by signaling his intent to escalate the case to the Supreme Court, stating "see you at SCOTUS" in a statement. The decision represents a setback for the Trump administration's broader effort to restrict transgender participation in military service.

The three-judge panel of the US Court of Appeals for Washington DC found that expelling current service members based on their gender identity violates constitutional protections. However, the court was divided in its reasoning about why the ban was unconstitutional.

The ruling specifically addresses troops already serving in the military when the ban took effect. The decision does not address whether the military can prevent new transgender recruits from enlisting in the future, focusing instead on the status of those currently in uniform.

The case emerged after Hegseth implemented the transgender military ban last year as part of broader changes to military personnel policies under the Trump administration. This approach went further than previous restrictions by actively removing service members already in uniform, rather than simply preventing new recruits from joining.

Legal experts anticipate this case will likely reach the Supreme Court if the administration follows through on its appeal, potentially establishing national precedent on transgender military service. The outcome could significantly impact the Trump administration's anti-diversity agenda across multiple policy areas.

The dispute over transgender troops has extended across multiple presidential administrations. Trump's first term saw initial restrictions proposed and challenged in court. The current policy represents a more aggressive approach to removing existing service members.

The appeals court decision comes as the Trump administration escalates efforts to restrict transgender participation across multiple sectors. The Education Department is investigating one of the country's largest women's colleges over its admission of transgender women. The administration has also sought to end transgender participation in sports, filed lawsuits against states allowing such participation, and attempted to restrict access to gender-affirming medical care.

In its statement, the military indicated it would review the court's decision. The administration's response suggests it remains committed to challenging the ruling through the appeals process.

The case highlights ongoing constitutional questions about how federal policy addresses gender identity protections. As the case moves through the courts, it will likely influence debates about transgender rights in various public institutions and government policies.
