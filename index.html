Joe Negri, the jazz guitar virtuoso who appeared as Handyman Negri on Mister Rogers' Neighborhood for decades, died at 99, just days before his 100th birthday. The Pittsburgh musician became a fixture on the beloved children's program, where he played the friendly neighborhood handyman who shared his love of music and problem-solving with young viewers.

Negri's dual career as both a jazz performer and television personality spanned more than seven decades. His work on Mister Rogers' Neighborhood brought him into millions of American homes, where he appeared alongside host Fred Rogers in the show's Neighborhood of Make-Believe segments. The program, which aired from 1968 to 2001, used Negri's character to teach children about cooperation, creativity, and the value of skilled trades.

Beyond his television work, Negri built a distinguished reputation as a jazz guitarist. His musical career established him as a respected figure in the Pittsburgh jazz scene and beyond. He performed and recorded extensively throughout his life, maintaining an active presence in both live performance and studio work. His guitar playing combined technical precision with an accessible warmth that translated well to both concert halls and children's television.

Negri's connection to Pittsburgh ran deep. The city's cultural institutions celebrated his contributions to both music and children's programming. His appearances on Mister Rogers' Neighborhood were filmed in Pittsburgh, where the show originated. The program's impact on American culture has endured long after its final episode, with Negri's Handyman character remaining one of its most memorable supporting roles.

The musician's death comes as part of a broader reckoning with the legacy of Mister Rogers' Neighborhood and its cast. Negri was among the last surviving regular cast members from the show's original run. His nearly century-long life bridged multiple eras of American entertainment, from the early days of jazz to the golden age of public television and beyond.

Berkshire Hathaway has agreed to purchase Taylor Morrison, one of the largest homebuilders in the United States, in a deal valued at $8.5 billion. The acquisition marks a significant capital deployment for the conglomerate, which holds $397 billion in cash reserves, and represents the first major transaction under Greg Abel as he assumes leadership of the company.

Warren Buffett, the 94-year-old billionaire who has led Berkshire since 1965, recently announced his plan to step down at the end of 2025. At Berkshire's annual shareholder meeting, Buffett told thousands of investors that "the time has arrived where Greg should become the chief executive officer of the company at year end." Abel, 62, has served as vice chairman overseeing the company's non-insurance operations for more than two decades and was formally designated as Buffett's successor back in 2001.

The Taylor Morrison purchase demonstrates Abel's approach to deploying capital as he takes control of Berkshire's vast portfolio. Buffett stated that he believes "the prospects of Berkshire will be better under Greg's management than mine." The conglomerate has historically made substantial acquisitions across various industries, though the pace of major deals had slowed as the company's cash reserves grew significantly.

The housing sector provides the type of stable cash flows and tangible assets that align with Berkshire's longstanding investment philosophy. The acquisition expands the conglomerate's presence into residential construction and development, complementing its existing businesses in insurance, railroads, energy, and manufacturing.

The housing market has faced considerable challenges in recent years. The Federal Reserve's aggressive campaign to combat inflation through interest rate increases has raised borrowing costs, putting pressure on homebuilders and potential buyers. Construction costs have also remained elevated. Nevertheless, demand for new housing persists in many markets due to limited inventory of existing homes available for sale.

In February, Berkshire reported record annual operating profits for the third consecutive year, with earnings rising 27 percent to $47.44 billion in 2024. The company operates 189 businesses across diverse sectors.

At the shareholder meeting, Buffett also addressed broader economic concerns. He warned about the potential consequences of trade tariffs, stating that "there's no question that trade can be an act of war." He expressed concern that antagonistic trade policies could create global instability, saying the United States should focus on trading with other nations and allowing each country to focus on its comparative strengths.

The Taylor Morrison transaction is expected to close following regulatory approvals and fulfillment of standard closing conditions.

Nvidia announced its entry into the personal computer market with a new Arm-based chip designed to bring artificial intelligence capabilities directly to consumer devices. The chip debuts in laptops from Microsoft, Dell, and HP, marking a significant expansion for the company best known for its graphics cards and data center hardware.

The new hardware powers what Nvidia calls the DGX Station for Windows, described as a system capable of running trillion-parameter AI models on enterprise desks. Nvidia partnered with Microsoft to integrate the technology with Windows, with Microsoft unveiling its Surface Laptop Ultra as one of the first devices using the new chip. Dell and HP also announced laptops featuring the processor.

The announcement came at Computex 2026, where Nvidia outlined its RTX Spark roadmap extending through 2029. The company also introduced DLSS 4.5, a graphics technology featuring second-generation transformer-based ray reconstruction that will arrive in August for RTX 20, 30, 40, and 50 series graphics cards. Nvidia said more than 1,000 RTX games and applications are now available.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang discussed the company's AI products during the event, stating he uses Claude AI at work while his son runs AI agents at home to manage family tasks. The personal computer push represents Nvidia's effort to bring AI processing power beyond data centers and into consumer devices, competing in a market dominated by Intel and AMD.

The move positions Nvidia to capture a share of the PC market as manufacturers race to integrate AI features into everyday computing devices. The Arm-based architecture represents an alternative to the x86 processors that have powered most personal computers for decades, following a path similar to Apple's transition to its own Arm-based chips in recent years.

Yesterday, 8:31 PM ET

Jerome Powell has issued a stark warning about threats to Federal Reserve independence in his first public statements since stepping down as chair. Speaking while accepting the John F. Kennedy Profiles in Courage award on Sunday, Powell stated that the central bank faces a "stress test" and its credibility is on the line.

Though Powell did not directly name the Trump administration, his remarks clearly addressed efforts to undermine Fed independence. He pointed to several specific challenges, including a criminal investigation into the Federal Reserve's over-budget building renovation and attempts to remove Fed governor Lisa Cook from her position. Powell framed these actions as threats to an institution essential to American economic strength.

Powell's tenure as Fed chair ended on May 15, following a tumultuous period marked by significant economic pressures. He steered the central bank through pandemic-related disruptions and subsequently managed inflation through interest rate increases, attempting to cool prices without triggering widespread job losses. Throughout his leadership, Powell emphasized that the Federal Reserve's independence from political pressure remained vital for sound monetary policy.

The Federal Reserve operates as an independent institution, designed to make decisions based on economic conditions rather than partisan considerations. Powell's warning suggests he views recent political actions as fundamentally threatening this structure. His decision to use the Profiles in Courage platform to defend institutional independence underscores his concern about the central bank's future autonomy.

The award, given by the John F. Kennedy Library Foundation, recognizes public officials who demonstrate political courage in their service. Powell's selection and his choice to address Fed independence reflects broader concerns about government interference in the central bank's operations.

Powell's departure from the Fed chair role coincided with significant political changes. Kevin Warsh was confirmed by the Senate on Wednesday in one of the most contentious Fed chair confirmation votes in history, winning 54-45 along largely party lines. Democratic Senator John Fetterman from Pennsylvania was the sole Democrat to support Warsh's nomination. Warsh will assume the chairmanship on May 14, taking the helm as the Fed faces pressure from the Trump administration to lower interest rates despite rising inflation.

Before Powell stepped down, he announced plans to remain on the Fed's board as a voting governor. Powell stated he would continue in this role until the White House ends its scrutiny of the Fed's headquarters renovation project. He characterized this scrutiny as "pretext" being used to pressure the central bank toward lowering rates.

Warsh, an Ivy League economist and former Wall Street banker, previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. During his earlier tenure, he was known as an inflation hawk, advocating for higher rates to combat rising prices.
