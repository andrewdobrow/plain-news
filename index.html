Yesterday, 9:54 PM ET

A chemical tank rupture at a paper mill in Longview, Washington has claimed nine lives, with two workers still missing and presumed dead. Recovery crews continued operations at the Nippon Dynawave Packaging Co facility on Friday after finding additional remains.

The disaster occurred when an industrial tank holding approximately 600,000 gallons of white liquor ruptured on Tuesday morning. White liquor is a caustic chemical mixture containing sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide, used in the kraft paper manufacturing process to break down wood fibers.

"We're bracing ourselves for this being the deadliest industrial tragedy in modern Washington state history," said Washington Governor Bob Ferguson at a news conference Wednesday.

Recovery operations began Wednesday following delays caused by concerns about the structural integrity of the damaged tank. Authorities transitioned from rescue efforts to recovery, indicating no hope of finding additional survivors. Matt Amos with the Longview fire department said operations would proceed "slow, methodical and deliberate," with recovered victims undergoing decontamination before being transported to the coroner's office. Seven workers remained hospitalized with severe burns from exposure to the chemical.

The U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board announced a federal investigation into the incident. The cause of the tank failure remained under investigation, with authorities saying there was no immediate threat to the surrounding community of about 40,000 residents.

The spillage from the rupture flowed into drainage ditches and contaminated the nearby Columbia River. Dead carp were discovered following the incident. About 25,000 gallons of white liquor continued to slowly leak from the tank. The Washington Department of Ecology and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency oversaw operations to determine environmental impacts. Authorities warned the public to avoid ditches and dikes while testing continued, though they reported no air quality or drinking water concerns in Longview.

The sprawling Nippon Dynawave facility employs approximately 1,000 people and produces materials for tissues, printing paper, cups, plates and cartons. The plant sits along the Columbia River adjacent to other timber, paper and chemical businesses in this mill town.

Brian Wood, representing Nippon Dynawave, a subsidiary of Japan-based Nippon Paper Group, said the company was focused on the victims and would cooperate fully with the investigation. "These are our people," he stated during a Wednesday news conference.

Safety complaints had been filed against the company in March and May, though the state's labor and industries department said both were unrelated to the current situation. Since the start of 2021, the company had been fined 3,400 dollars for three separate health and safety violations.

At a community vigil Tuesday night, dozens of residents gathered to pray and light candles. Many remained waiting for answers about missing coworkers and friends. "There's families that have been torn apart, and we don't know why," said Longview resident Crystal Moldenhauer.

1:09 AM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on the launchpad at Cape Canaveral in Florida during a scheduled test, dealing a significant setback to Jeff Bezos's spaceflight company and potentially jeopardizing NASA's ambitious lunar plans.

The explosion occurred at Launch Complex 36 during a "hotfire" test at 9 p.m. ET on Thursday. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad seconds after the test began. The blast was visible from miles away, with an orange sky reported in Fort Pierce, 115 miles south of the launch site, and a glow observed by residents in South Carolina hundreds of miles north. Shockwaves were felt across Florida's space coast, shaking homes in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were safe and accounted for, with no injuries reported. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" and said it was already investigating the cause. Bezos acknowledged in a social media post that it was "a very rough day" but pledged to rebuild and return to flight operations.

The explosion threatens several major projects. NASA had just announced on Tuesday that Blue Origin won the contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a 20 billion dollar moon base. The New Glenn rocket is the vehicle Blue Origin intends to use for lunar landers, including those that will carry astronauts to the moon's surface.

The incident also affects NASA's Artemis program. The space agency is planning Artemis III for 2027 to test both Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander and SpaceX's competing lunar landing system. Both companies are competing for the contract to provide the lander for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which would mark the first crewed lunar landing since 1972.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of project timelines following the explosion and work with Blue Origin to support a thorough investigation and assess mission impacts.

This test marked the first static fire test since the Federal Aviation Administration cleared the New Glenn rocket to resume flights following a previous mishap last month, when a payload from the rocket's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit.

The explosion comes as Blue Origin faces intensifying competition with SpaceX for government contracts and commercial launch services. SpaceX has maintained a steady launch schedule and continues to expand operations, including developing vehicles for NASA missions.

Launch Complex 36 has a long history supporting American spaceflight and will require repairs before it can support future launch attempts.

Lucas died following a battle with metastatic cancer. Lucasfilm posted a statement mourning her death.

She was one of three editors to share an Academy Award for editing the original Star Wars film. Lucas also edited other notable films from the 1970s, including Taxi Driver.

Lucas was formerly married to Star Wars creator George Lucas. Her editing work helped shape some of the most influential films of the decade.

Lucasfilm described itself as deeply saddened by her death in its official statement released Friday. The studio acknowledged her contributions to the original Star Wars film, which became one of the most successful and culturally significant movies in cinema history.

The editing of Star Wars proved crucial to the film's success. Lucas worked alongside fellow editors Paul Hirsch and Richard Chew on the project. The three shared the Oscar for Best Film Editing at the 50th Academy Awards in 1978.

Her work on Taxi Driver, directed by Martin Scorsese and starring Robert De Niro, also earned critical acclaim. The film became one of the defining works of 1970s American cinema.

Lucas spent her final years in Rancho Mirage, a desert city in Riverside County, California. She had been battling metastatic cancer before her death on Wednesday.

The film industry has lost one of its pioneering editors from an era that transformed Hollywood filmmaking. Her technical skill and creative vision helped create two of the most enduring films from that period.

Friday's strike represents the third attack this week on suspected drug smuggling vessels. An earlier strike earlier in the week killed 11 people, making it one of the deadliest attacks this year. Since September, U.S. strikes on suspected drug-smuggling boats have killed at least 200 people.

These operations reflect an escalation under the Trump administration, which has increased U.S. military presence in the region to intercept drug traffickers. The strikes mark a shift from traditional interdiction methods, which typically involve boarding and seizing vessels suspected of smuggling narcotics.

However, the legality of these operations has drawn growing scrutiny from lawmakers and legal experts. Critics argue the Pentagon is conducting extrajudicial killings and abusing its power with life-or-death consequences.

"Under both U.S. and international law, it is flagrantly illegal to use the military to kill civilians suspected only of crimes," said Jeffrey Stein, a staff attorney with the American Civil Liberties Union, and Christopher Anders, director of the Democracy and Technology Division at the ACLU, in a December statement. "Civilians, including those suspected of smuggling drugs, are not lawful targets."

The debate over strike policy has also affected military leadership. Gen. Francis L. Donovan took over U.S. Southern Command after Admiral Alvin Holsey abruptly retired, reportedly over disagreements about the strike policy. Southern Command oversees U.S. military operations in Latin America and the Caribbean, conducting counter-narcotics missions as part of its regional security mandate.

The eastern Pacific corridor serves as a major transit route for drug smuggling operations attempting to move narcotics northward through Central America and Mexico. Southern Command has not provided information about the nationalities of the three men killed, whether any drugs were recovered from the vessel, or additional details about the specific terrorist organizations allegedly operating the boat.
