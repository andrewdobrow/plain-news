The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire, though the arrangement requires final approval from President Trump before taking effect.

Trump announced the extension unilaterally on Tuesday through his Truth Social platform. The decision came after he had earlier suggested military action was imminent, telling CNBC that the US military was "raring to go" and that he did not want to extend the ceasefire, believing the US held a strong negotiating position.

Trump said the extension would continue until Iranian leaders submit a unified proposal for peace. He cited the "fractured" state of Iran's government and stated he was acting upon requests from Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir. "I have therefore directed our Military to continue the Blockade and, in all other respects, remain ready and able, and will therefore extend the Ceasefire until such time as their proposal is submitted," Trump wrote.

However, the announcement drew sharp criticism from Iran's negotiating team. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and the Islamic regime's lead negotiator, rejected the ceasefire extension. His personal adviser, Mahdi Mohammadi, dismissed it as "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike" and stated that "the losing side cannot dictate terms." Ghalibaf himself said Iran would not accept "negotiations under the shadow of threats" and accused Trump of seeking to turn the negotiating table into a "table of surrender."

The US Navy blockade of Iranian ports remains a major obstacle to restarting negotiations. Iran has demanded the US end the blockade before talks can resume, but Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent both warned on Tuesday that the blockade will continue. Bessent warned that Iranian oil storage facilities would soon become full, forcing wells to shut down.

A first round of talks in Islamabad ten days earlier ended without agreement on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway Iran closed in the early days of the conflict. That closure has reduced global oil and gas supplies by roughly a fifth and prompted international concern about economic consequences.

Pakistan has continued efforts to mediate between the two sides. Prime Minister Sharif thanked Trump for the ceasefire extension, saying Pakistan would "continue its earnest efforts for a negotiated settlement."

The conflicting statements from Washington and Tehran underscore the deep distrust between the two countries. While some US officials characterize negotiations as progressing, Iranian leadership remains skeptical of American intentions and has suggested preparing for military escalation rather than compromise.

A 21-year-old Austrian man was sentenced to 15 years in prison for planning a jihadist attack on Taylor Swift's concerts in Vienna, which were ultimately canceled in August 2024. The defendant, identified only as Beran A. under Austrian privacy laws, pleaded guilty to terrorism-related charges.

Beran A. admitted to planning an attack targeting concertgoers at Ernst Happel Stadium, where Swift was scheduled to perform three nights. Authorities estimate up to 30,000 people gathered outside the venue nightly, with another 65,000 inside. According to prosecutors, the defendant allegedly intended to use knives or homemade explosives to "kill as many people as possible."

The plot was discovered during a police search of his apartment on August 7, 2024, the day before the concerts were scheduled to begin. Authorities found bomb-making materials at the residence. Investigators determined that Beran A. had studied Islamic State video instructions on constructing shrapnel bombs and had produced a small amount of triacetone triperoxide explosive. He also allegedly attempted to illegally purchase weapons, including a machine gun and hand grenade.

Swift's team canceled all three scheduled performances following the threat. The U.S. provided intelligence that contributed to the decision. In a statement posted to Instagram two weeks later, Swift said the cancellation filled her with "a new sense of fear, and a tremendous amount of guilt because so many people had planned on coming to those shows."

The case extends beyond the Vienna plot. Beran A. and two other men, school friends identified as Arda K. and Hasan E., allegedly planned simultaneous attacks in multiple Middle Eastern cities during Ramadan 2024. While all three traveled to their designated locations, only Hasan E. is believed to have carried out an attack, stabbing a security guard at Mecca's Grand Mosque on March 11, 2024. He remains in custody in Saudi Arabia.

Prosecutors said Beran A. shared Islamic State propaganda through messaging services and networked with other extremist members. According to prosecutors, he swore allegiance to the militant group and discussed acquiring weapons and manufacturing explosives with associates.

Beran A.'s defense attorney, Anna Mair, said her client expressed deep remorse for his actions. "It is also due to the long period of detention that he says it was the biggest mistake of his life," Mair stated outside court.

A Berlin court previously convicted a Syrian teenager of contributing to the Vienna plot, sentencing him to 18 months suspended imprisonment. The attack plot drew comparisons to the 2017 Manchester bombing at an Ariana Grande concert, which killed 22 people.
