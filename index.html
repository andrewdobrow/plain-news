Judge orders Trump's name removed from Kennedy Center

1:33 AM ET

A federal judge has ordered the removal of Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, ruling that the prestigious Washington D.C. venue cannot be renamed without congressional action.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper directed the Trump administration to remove all physical signage bearing Trump's name and eliminate any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials within 14 days. In his 94-page opinion, Cooper wrote that the Kennedy Center's federal statute makes clear the institution is named for President John F. Kennedy and cannot bear any other formal name without congressional approval.

"Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it," Cooper stated. He found that the Kennedy Center's board overstepped its authority when it voted to rename the facility after Trump.

The ruling also temporarily blocked the center from closing this summer for proposed renovations. Trump had announced a two-year closure to allow for extensive upgrades. Cooper criticized the board's decision to support the closure, saying it was based on insufficient information and neglected the center's statutory obligations and potential negative impacts on programming and memorial functions. He described the decision as "ill-informed and seemingly preordained."

The lawsuit was brought by Joyce Beatty, an Ohio Democratic U.S. representative who serves on the Kennedy Center's board by virtue of her congressional position. Beatty welcomed the ruling, stating that the decision "rightly affirms that this administration's efforts to rename and close the Center have no basis in law." She added that the Kennedy Center belongs to the American people, not to Trump.

Trump responded by posting on his Truth Social platform that he would work with Congress on transferring ownership of the Kennedy Center to them. He stated he had instructed the Department of Commerce to make arrangements with Congress for a complete transfer of the institution. Trump said he has no interest in continuing with the Kennedy Center "unless I am free to do what I do better than anyone else, bring this Institution back, physically, financially, and artistically."

Trump appointed himself chair of the Kennedy Center's board in February after taking office, calling it a "take-over." He purged the existing board and replaced its members with his own appointees. The reconstituted board voted in December to add Trump's name to the facility.

The renaming effort sparked significant backlash. Artists and performing groups canceled bookings in protest, including the Tony award-winning musical Hamilton. The National Symphony Orchestra's executive director left to take a position elsewhere. Richard Grenell, a Trump appointee who played a key role in pushing to overhaul the institution, stepped down as president in March.

The Kennedy Center is a federally-funded venue that has long enjoyed bipartisan support as one of America's leading arts institutions.

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on the launchpad at Cape Canaveral in Florida during a scheduled test, threatening NASA's plans to build a lunar base and return humans to the moon within the next two years.

The explosion occurred at Launch Complex 36 during a "hotfire" test at 9 p.m. ET on Thursday. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad seconds after the test began. The blast was visible across Florida's space coast, with an orange sky seen as far south as Fort Pierce, 115 miles away. Shockwaves were felt in nearby communities, and residents in South Carolina hundreds of miles north reported seeing a glow in the sky.

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed that all company employees were safe and uninjured. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" but has not yet released details about the cause or the extent of damage to the launch complex.

The New Glenn rocket is critical to Blue Origin's space ambitions. The company had just won a NASA contract on Tuesday to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion lunar base. Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission scheduled for 2028, which will mark humanity's first return to the moon since 1972.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the timeline's impact following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he stated, adding that NASA would work with its partners to investigate the incident and assess impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs.

Bezos acknowledged the setback in a social media post, saying "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it." He added, "Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The explosion represents another challenge for Blue Origin as it attempts to compete with SpaceX. The company has faced recent setbacks, including a payload that ended up in the wrong orbit during a flight last month, resulting in a temporary FAA grounding. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight the previous week.

Elon Musk, whose SpaceX has dominated the commercial space industry, responded to the explosion on social media, stating simply that rockets are difficult. Both companies have constructed major facilities near Cape Canaveral to support NASA's Artemis program, which aims to land astronauts on the lunar surface in the coming years.

Federal research grants typically fund work at universities, research institutions, and laboratories across the country. These grants support projects ranging from basic science to applied research in fields including medicine, engineering, and environmental science.

The proposed rules apply to federal funding mechanisms used by agencies that support scientific research. Implementation would require final approval after a regulatory review process.
