Defense Secretary Blocks Promotions for Eight Navy Officers

The removals represent an unusual intervention by the defense secretary in personnel decisions th...

Yesterday, 9:01 PM ET

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has removed eight senior Navy officers from a promotion list, preventing their advancement to higher ranks. The affected group includes women and Black service members who had already received approval through the standard military promotion process.

The removals represent an unusual intervention by the defense secretary in personnel decisions that typically move through established military channels. According to the Wall Street Journal, Hegseth personally directed the action to strike these officers from the promotion list.

The eight officers had successfully completed the rigorous vetting process that precedes flag officer promotions. This process normally includes performance evaluations, peer reviews, and congressional notification before final approval. While the defense secretary has the authority to alter promotion lists, such direct interventions remain relatively uncommon in military practice.

The Pentagon has not publicly explained the specific reasons for removing these officers from consideration. The decision affects personnel who were positioned for senior leadership roles within the Navy. Flag officer promotions carry significant weight because they place individuals in command of major naval operations and lead strategic planning efforts across the service.

The blocked promotions have drawn scrutiny partly because the officers affected include both female and Black naval personnel. This comes during a period when military diversity and advancement practices have become subjects of broader public discussion. The Navy and other military branches have worked to expand opportunities for women and minority officers in senior leadership positions.

Critics have questioned whether the removals were based on merit or other considerations. Some have connected the decision to broader personnel shifts within the Pentagon leadership. The Navy secretary position recently changed hands, with Hung Cao, a former Navy officer and Republican political candidate, named as acting secretary following the departure of his predecessor.

The affected officers have not been publicly identified by name in official announcements. The specific ranks these service members were seeking have also remained undisclosed. This lack of transparency has contributed to questions about the rationale behind the personnel action.

The situation reflects broader turbulence within the Defense Department in recent weeks. Multiple high-ranking officials have departed from their positions, and Republican senators have privately warned that additional departures may be forthcoming. Some lawmakers have indicated concern about the pace and nature of personnel changes occurring across the administration.

The flag officer promotion process represents a critical juncture in military careers. These positions determine who will lead major commands and influence military strategy and operations. The removal of already-approved officers from promotion lists raises questions about the decision-making process and the standards being applied to senior military personnel advancement.

Yesterday, 11:40 PM ET

A large-scale Russian drone and missile assault across Ukraine killed at least seven people overnight and injured at least 34, according to local authorities. The strikes lasted throughout the night and affected multiple cities, with Dnipro experiencing the heaviest casualties.

Five people died in Dnipro, with four bodies found in the ruins of a destroyed house on Saturday morning. Workers continued searching through debris as rescue operations proceeded. Another person was killed in a separate strike in the same residential area on Saturday afternoon. The bombardment started fires across the city and partially destroyed several apartment blocks, businesses, and a private house.

Oleksandr Hanzha, the Dnipropetrovsk regional head, reported that Russian forces targeted Dnipro and other communities "practically all night." At least three additional people were injured in strikes on the cities of Odesa and Kharkiv, where drones also struck targets.

The overnight assault represented one of the largest attacks in several days. Ukrainian authorities reported repelling most of more than 600 Russian drones launched during the attack. Despite air defense efforts, some projectiles reached their targets in populated areas.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the Russian tactics on social media, stating that strikes involved drones, cruise missiles, and a significant amount of ballistic weapons. He noted that most targets were civilian infrastructure in cities, with residential buildings, energy facilities, and enterprises suffering damage.

The attack came as Ukraine conducted some of its longest-distance drone strikes deep inside Russian territory. In the Russian border region of Belgorod, a woman was killed and a man seriously injured by a Ukrainian drone strike.

The overnight bombardment followed a prisoner exchange on Friday between Russia and Ukraine, in which both sides exchanged 193 service members. These periodic exchanges have been part of US-brokered negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv aimed at ending the conflict. However, those talks have made no progress on key issues, preventing any resolution to Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

The assault underscores the ongoing threat posed to Ukrainian civilians more than two years into the conflict. Emergency services responded to multiple impact sites simultaneously, with rescue efforts hampered by extensive structural damage to residential complexes. The full extent of casualties and damage continued to emerge as rescue crews searched through debris across the affected cities.

Yesterday, 4:58 PM ET

Moderna has secured $50 million in funding to accelerate development of an mRNA vaccine targeting the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola during an active outbreak. The investment reflects growing recognition of mRNA technology's potential to rapidly respond to emerging infectious disease threats.

The Bundibugyo strain is one of several known variants of the Ebola virus, which causes hemorrhagic fever in humans and primates. Ebola outbreaks have historically occurred in Central and West Africa, with case fatality rates ranging from 25% to 90% depending on the strain and quality of medical care available.

Moderna's mRNA platform delivers genetic instructions that prompt cells to produce proteins triggering an immune response. The company successfully used this same approach to develop COVID-19 vaccines in record time, demonstrating that mRNA technology can accelerate vaccine creation compared to traditional methods. Scientists can rapidly adapt vaccines to target different pathogens by modifying the genetic sequence in the mRNA.

Traditional vaccine development typically requires years or decades to complete, but mRNA vaccines can potentially be designed and manufactured within months. This speed advantage becomes critical during active outbreaks when delays directly translate to additional infections and deaths.

The $50 million allocation specifically targets urgent acceleration of vaccine development, indicating that health officials consider the current outbreak a significant public health threat requiring rapid response. Moderna has not yet announced a timeline for clinical trials or when the Bundibugyo vaccine might become available.

The company has been expanding its infectious disease vaccine pipeline beyond COVID-19. Current projects include vaccines for influenza, respiratory syncytial virus, and other pathogens. This latest Ebola funding represents part of Moderna's broader effort to apply its mRNA platform to multiple disease threats.

The investment comes during a period of notable developments in mRNA vaccine applications. A recent study found that an mRNA vaccine is highly effective at preventing melanoma recurrence when combined with an immunotherapy drug called Keytruda. Meanwhile, the FDA recently agreed to review Moderna's application for an mRNA-based flu vaccine after initially declining to consider it.

The World Health Organization has called for community cooperation in containing Ebola outbreaks, particularly in the Democratic Republic of Congo, where some residents have protested medical protocols for handling victims' bodies.

The $50 million Ebola vaccine funding reflects confidence in mRNA technology as a platform for responding to emerging infectious disease threats. As Moderna pursues approvals for multiple mRNA vaccines across different disease areas, the company continues advancing its position as a leader in this rapidly expanding field of vaccine development.

Yesterday, 10:14 PM ET

The Cleveland Browns traded star edge rusher Myles Garrett to the Los Angeles Rams on Monday in exchange for linebacker Jared Verse and three draft picks. The transaction represents one of the biggest defensive trades in NFL history.

Garrett, 30, is a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection. Since the Browns selected him first overall in 2017, he has established himself among the league's elite defensive players. He holds the record for most sacks (125.5), pressures (413), and tackles for loss (149) among all players since his draft year. In 2025, Garrett set the single-season sacks record with 23.

Garrett had requested a trade from the Browns in 2024 after the team finished with a 3-14 record. He expressed a desire to play for a contender with a realistic championship opportunity. Despite this request, he signed a four-year, $160 million extension with Cleveland that made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in league history at that time. The deal included $122.8 million in guaranteed money and averaged $40 million annually.

The Browns finished the 2025 season with a 5-12 record and missed the playoffs for the second consecutive year, though Garrett earned All-Pro recognition. Following the disappointing season, the team fired head coach Kevin Stefanski and replaced him with former Baltimore Ravens offensive coordinator Todd Monken.

General manager Andrew Berry negotiated a deal after the Browns and Garrett agreed to modify his contract by deferring option payments over the 2026-28 seasons. A payment of approximately $10 million that was originally due on March 28 was moved to near the start of the regular season.

"As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" Berry said after announcing the trade.

The Browns received Verse, a 2027 first-round selection, a 2028 second-round pick, and a 2029 third-round selection in the deal.

The Rams, who lost to the eventual champion Seattle Seahawks in the NFC championship game last season, have a history of pursuing blockbuster trades. In 2021, they acquired quarterback Matthew Stafford from the Detroit Lions, and Stafford led the team to a Super Bowl victory in his first season. That championship game was played at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, which will also host Super Bowl LXI next February.

Verse, 25, was a first-round draft pick by the Rams in 2024 and earned Defensive Rookie of the Year honors. He was selected to the Pro Bowl this past season with 12 career sacks and 22 tackles for loss.
