The United States military has assembled sufficient forces in the Middle East to launch strikes against Iran as soon as this weekend, according to multiple news outlets citing unnamed sources. However, President Trump has not yet made a final decision on whether to proceed with an attack.

Trump has repeatedly demanded that Iran cease its nuclear program and has warned he intends to use force if no agreement is reached. The New York Times, CBS News and CNN all reported that the US military has positioned enough air and naval resources in the region to carry out strikes in the coming days. Reuters reported a slightly different timeline, noting that top US national security advisers were told during a Wednesday White House meeting that all deployed forces should be in place by mid-March.

Iran is expected to submit a written proposal addressing its standoff with the US following talks held in Geneva on Tuesday. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said during a Wednesday press conference that "Iran would be very wise to make a deal with President Trump." She acknowledged "a little bit of progress" from the Geneva talks but stressed that the two sides remain "very far apart on some issues."

Trump met Wednesday with Steve Witkoff, a real estate developer serving as an envoy, and Jared Kushner, his son-in-law, who are leading indirect talks with Iran. According to journalist Barak Ravid reporting on Israeli television, US sources described the Geneva talks with Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi as a "nothingburger" and indicated the Pentagon was preparing for a joint Israeli-US offensive that could last weeks.

The military buildup is substantial. The USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier group is already deployed in the region, while the USS Gerald Ford is en route to the Middle East. The Pentagon has also positioned dozens of refueling tankers and more than 50 additional fighter jets in the region. At the same time, CBS News reported that the Pentagon is moving some personnel out of the Middle East to minimize potential casualties from Iranian counterattacks.

Israel is also preparing for the possibility of joining strikes with the US, according to the New York Times.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov warned against military action, telling Saudi Arabia's Al-Arabiya television that "the consequences are not good" of any new US strike on Iran. "No one wants an increase in tension. Everyone understands this is playing with fire," Lavrov said, calling on the US to allow Iran to pursue a peaceful nuclear program.

In June, the US struck key nuclear enrichment sites in Iran, while Israeli fighter jets struck dozens of targets across the country, including a long-range missile site in Yazd.

Yesterday, 10:47 PM ET

The Australian government filed a lawsuit against US manufacturing giant 3M over contamination from chemicals in firefighting foam used at defense sites across the country. The case seeks A$2 billion in damages, making it the largest lawsuit ever brought by the Australian government.

The lawsuit centers on so-called forever chemicals in firefighting foam that contaminated military installations. These chemicals, which persist in the environment and do not break down naturally, have been linked to contamination at multiple defense facilities.

3M manufactures a wide range of industrial and consumer products. The company has faced growing scrutiny worldwide over the use of these persistent chemicals in its products, particularly in firefighting foams used by military and emergency services.

The Australian government's legal action represents an escalation in efforts to hold manufacturers accountable for environmental contamination. The chemicals at issue are known as PFAS, or per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, which have been found in water supplies near military bases and firefighting training sites.

The scale of the lawsuit reflects both the extent of contamination across Australian defense sites and the cost of remediation efforts. Forever chemicals earned their nickname because they do not degrade in the environment, leading to long-term contamination issues that can affect groundwater and soil for decades.

3M has faced similar legal challenges in other countries over PFAS contamination. The company previously reached settlements with US water utilities and has set aside billions of dollars to address PFAS-related liabilities. The Australian case adds to mounting global pressure on manufacturers of products containing these chemicals.

The lawsuit seeks compensation for cleanup costs and damages related to the contamination at defense installations. Australian defense sites used firefighting foams containing PFAS for training exercises and emergency response over many years before the full environmental impact of these chemicals became widely understood.

Yesterday, 10:32 PM ET

The Department of Justice has opened a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll, who accused President Trump of sexual assault, according to multiple reports.

CNN first reported that the investigation focuses on whether Carroll committed perjury during a deposition as part of her two civil cases against Trump. The probe marks a significant escalation in the ongoing legal battles between Carroll and the president.

Carroll won two civil verdicts against Trump in federal court. In May 2023, a jury found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation, awarding Carroll $5 million. A separate jury in January 2024 awarded her an additional $83.3 million for defamation after Trump continued to deny her allegations.

The criminal investigation centers on statements Carroll made under oath during depositions related to those civil cases. Perjury charges require prosecutors to prove that a witness knowingly made false statements while under oath about material facts in a case.

Carroll first publicly accused Trump of sexually assaulting her in a Bergdorf Goodman dressing room in the mid-1990s when she published her memoir in 2019. Trump has consistently denied the allegations, claiming he never met Carroll and that she fabricated the story.

The timing of the Justice Department investigation has raised questions given the administration's relationship with the case. Trump previously sued the IRS and has been involved in various legal disputes with federal agencies.

Legal experts note that perjury prosecutions are relatively rare and require a high burden of proof. Prosecutors must demonstrate not only that statements were false, but that the witness knew they were false when making them and that the false statements were material to the proceedings.

The investigation comes as Carroll's legal team has not yet commented publicly on the probe. The Justice Department also has not released additional details about the scope or timeline of the investigation.

Carroll's civil cases against Trump were notable for being among the first successful legal actions brought against him by an accuser. The substantial damage awards reflected the jury's findings that Trump's public statements about Carroll were defamatory and made with malice.

Researchers achieve perfect random number generation using quantum mechanics

Scientists demonstrated true randomness for the first time using quantum computing systems, a breakthrough with implications for encryption and data security.

Yesterday, 5:48 PM ET

Researchers successfully generated perfectly random numbers using quantum mechanics, marking the first time true randomness has been achieved experimentally. The breakthrough was published in Nature and represents a significant advance in quantum computing applications.

The ability to create genuinely random numbers has major implications for cryptography and secure communications. Traditional computer systems can only produce pseudo-random numbers based on algorithms, which are theoretically predictable. Quantum randomness relies on the fundamental unpredictability of quantum mechanical processes. The research team's method amplifies naturally occurring quantum randomness to produce numbers that are provably random rather than merely appearing random.
