The Trump administration is abandoning a proposed $1.8 billion fund designed to compensate people who claim they were targeted by federal law enforcement, Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced this week during a House appropriations committee hearing.

"We are not moving forward with the fund, period," Blanche said, though he acknowledged that the underlying concerns remain valid. "The reasons for the fund, I think, remain as important as they were before, but we are not moving forward with the fund."

The fund, which the administration had framed as an "anti-weaponization" initiative, faced immediate legal challenges after its announcement. A federal judge issued a temporary block on payouts, and Republican lawmakers voiced significant opposition to the proposal despite their general alignment with the Trump administration on other matters.

The symbolic figure of $1.776 billion was a reference to American independence, and the fund would have provided compensation to individuals who claimed they were investigated or prosecuted for political reasons. However, legal experts and government watchdog groups questioned whether federal money could be appropriated for such payments without congressional approval.

Multiple courts also raised concerns about the legal basis for the payments and the process by which recipients would have been selected. These legal obstacles, combined with internal Republican resistance, proved decisive in the administration's decision to withdraw the proposal.

The collapse of the fund represents a rare instance of the administration backing down from a major policy initiative in the face of opposition from its own party. The decision closes a chapter on one of the administration's more contentious domestic policy proposals.

However, the administration is maintaining a separate protection that emerged alongside the fund's controversy. The IRS will still be prohibited from auditing Donald Trump, his family, and related entities. This audit immunity agreement remains in place even as the broader compensation fund is being scrapped.

Blanche emphasized that while the administration is abandoning the fund itself, it continues to view weaponization of federal agencies as a serious concern that motivated the proposal. The Justice Department has not indicated whether it will pursue alternative approaches to address claims of politically motivated investigations or prosecutions.

The decision also comes as the administration pursues other legal initiatives. The DEA recently announced it would hold a new administrative hearing on cannabis rescheduling, reflecting the broader scope of legal and regulatory changes the Trump administration has attempted to implement during this term.

SpaceX Merger with xAI Ahead of Public Offering

SpaceX has acquired Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company xAI in a major merger that combines the aerospace firm with AI technology, satellite communications, and social media operations. The deal valued SpaceX at approximately $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion, creating a combined entity positioned for a stock market listing expected to value the business well above $1 trillion.

The companies announced the merger on Monday through a statement on SpaceX's website, describing the combined operation as "the most ambitious, vertically-integrated innovation engine on (and off) Earth, with AI, rockets, space-based internet, direct-to-mobile device communications and the world's foremost real-time information and free speech platform."

The merger gives SpaceX access to xAI's Grok chatbot and the social media platform X. According to the announcement, the deal was motivated partly by Musk's plans to establish datacenters and solar-powered satellites in space to support artificial intelligence infrastructure. The companies stated that current AI advances depend heavily on large terrestrial datacenters requiring enormous amounts of power and cooling. "Global electricity demand for AI simply cannot be met with terrestrial solutions, even in the near term, without imposing hardship on communities and the environment," the announcement said. "In the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale."

The public offering is expected to occur in early summer, timed to coincide with a planetary alignment and Musk's birthday on June 28. Musk, who turns 55 that day, wrote "yes" on X in response to merger reports, offering apparent confirmation of the deal.

Both companies have received substantial valuations recently. SpaceX sent a letter to investors in December revealing an expected value of $800 billion. xAI announced a $20 billion Series E fundraise last month from major investors, valuing the artificial intelligence company at $230 billion. Tesla also revealed plans last month to invest $2 billion in xAI.

The merger represents the latest example of Musk consolidating his business interests. xAI acquired the platform X in an all-stock transaction earlier in 2025. SpaceX continues to dominate commercial satellite launches and maintains extensive contracts with federal agencies. The company operates the Starship launch system and provides satellite internet service through its Starlink division.

News of the merger came as a positive development for Musk following Tesla's recent earnings report showing declining revenues and struggles in its car business. A Department of Justice release of files related to financier Jeffrey Epstein also showed Musk exchanging friendly emails with the disgraced figure and making plans to visit his private island. Musk called the email release a "distraction" and claimed the correspondence "could be misinterpreted and used by detractors to smear my name."

He sang "Beauty and the Beast" with Celine Dion for the 1991 animated film and "A Whole New World" with Regina Belle for 1992's "Aladdin." Both songs won Grammy Awards and became enduring standards in popular music.

Beyond his Disney work, Bryson built a distinguished career in R&B and soul music. His hits included "I'm So Into You," "Let the Feeling Flow," and other ballads that earned him recognition as a leading voice in romantic music. He won multiple Grammy Awards throughout his career and was known for his smooth tenor voice and emotional delivery.

Bryson's music spanned decades and reached audiences across generations. The Disney collaborations introduced his voice to millions of younger listeners while his R&B catalog established him as a major figure in soul music. His work appeared in films, on radio, and became staples at weddings and romantic events worldwide.

The singer's influence extended beyond commercial success. He was regarded as a master of the ballad form and influenced countless artists in R&B and pop music. His ability to convey emotion through song made him a sought-after collaborator, and his Disney performances remain among the most recognizable movie songs ever recorded.

Rep. Ashley Hinson won the Republican nomination for Iowa's Senate seat, defeating former state Rep. Jim Carlin in the primary. Hinson, who has represented Iowa's northeastern congressional district in the House since 2021, previously worked as a reporter and state legislator.

The Republican primary took place as Iowa voters cast ballots in heated contests that could influence the balance of power in Congress. A state that Republicans have dominated for the past decade, Iowa has emerged as a competitive battleground where Democrats believe they can make gains in multiple races.

Hinson will face Democrat Josh Turek in the November general election. Turek won his party's primary after receiving millions of dollars in outside spending and endorsements from top Democratic leaders. Turek is a state representative and two-time Paralympic gold medalist who represents a district that voted for Donald Trump in recent elections.

In the Democratic primary, Turek defeated state Senator Zach Wahls in a contentious race that revealed divisions within the party. Wahls positioned himself as an outsider willing to challenge party leadership, including US Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer. Wahls accused Turek of being the choice of the Democratic establishment, pointing to more than five million dollars in spending by VoteVets, a political action committee that has received funding from Schumer's campaign.

Turek responded by arguing he was the stronger candidate for general election success, citing his ability to win office in a conservative-leaning area of western Iowa. Wahls, by contrast, represented a liberal district in eastern Iowa.

The race is for the seat held by Sen. Joni Ernst, a Republican who is retiring. Donald Trump endorsed Hinson for the nomination, backing her bid to succeed Ernst.

Iowa's elections extend beyond the Senate race. Democrats are hopeful about winning three of the state's four House seats, which could be significant for their efforts to regain control of the chamber. In the first congressional district in southeastern Iowa, Republican incumbent Mariannette Miller-Meeks won her primary against rightwing challenger David Pautsch. She will face Democrat Christina Bohannan, a University of Iowa law professor, in a rematch of their 2024 contest, which Miller-Meeks won by approximately 800 votes.

In the third district around Des Moines, Republican incumbent Zach Nunn and Democrat Sarah Trone Garriott, a state senator, emerged from their respective primaries and will face each other in the fall.

Across the state, Iowa Republicans have maintained strength in statewide races, though Democrats see openings to compete in previously safe GOP seats. The November elections will test whether the party can translate primary success into general election victories.
