10:14 PM ET

Federal prosecutors in New York charged a longtime Google employee with insider trading after he allegedly used confidential company information to place bets on Polymarket, a prediction market platform. The engineer made $1.2 million from the trades, according to the charges.

The case represents a new application of insider trading laws to prediction markets, which allow users to bet on the outcomes of real-world events. Prosecutors allege the Google employee accessed internal data through his position at the company and used that information to gain an unfair advantage when placing bets on the platform.

Polymarket has grown in popularity as a platform where users can wager on everything from election outcomes to corporate announcements. The markets are designed to aggregate information and predict future events, but they rely on participants trading based on publicly available information rather than confidential data.

The charges indicate that federal authorities view the use of insider information on prediction markets the same way they treat traditional securities trading. Insider trading laws prohibit individuals from trading based on material, nonpublic information that could affect the value of securities or, in this case, the outcome of prediction market contracts.

The Google engineer worked at the company for several years before the alleged trades took place. Prosecutors say he violated his duty to keep internal company information confidential by using it for personal financial gain.

The case could set a precedent for how prediction markets are regulated and how insider trading laws apply to new forms of financial platforms. As these markets have grown, questions about their oversight and potential for abuse have increased.

The employee faces federal charges that could result in significant fines and prison time if convicted. Google has not publicly commented on the case or the employee's current status with the company.

8:20 PM ET

The U.S. military struck an Iranian ground control station and shot down four Iranian attack drones in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, according to U.S. officials. The military also attacked an Iranian drone-launching unit on the ground.

U.S. Central Command said in a statement that its forces shot down four Iranian one-way attack drones that posed a threat in the area. A senior U.S. official said the military struck a ground control station in Bandar Abbas, a port city on Iran's southern coast, that was preparing to launch a fifth drone.

The incident marks the second such military clash in 48 hours between the two countries. Iran's military fired the four drones at a commercial ship in the Strait of Hormuz before U.S. forces intercepted them, according to a senior U.S. official speaking on condition of anonymity.

The strikes occurred as the United States and Iran are engaged in negotiations to end their ongoing conflict. President Trump had indicated over the weekend that an agreement was close, but his remarks at a Cabinet meeting on Wednesday differed from those earlier statements.

During the Cabinet meeting, Trump maintained what officials described as maximum demands on Iran, signaling a harder line than his weekend comments suggested. The president did not elaborate on the specific terms being discussed in the negotiations.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical shipping channel through which a significant portion of global oil supplies pass. Attacks on commercial vessels in the waterway have raised concerns about freedom of navigation and energy security.

U.S. Central Command has not released additional details about the commercial ship targeted by the Iranian drones or whether it sustained any damage. The military characterized the Iranian drones as posing a threat to vessels in the area.

The timing of the military action complicates the diplomatic effort to reach an agreement between Washington and Tehran. Neither the White House nor Iranian officials have publicly commented on how the latest clash might affect the ongoing peace talks.
