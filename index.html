A meteor exploded off the coast of Massachusetts, creating a blast equivalent to 300 tons of TNT and causing a loud boom heard across the region. The explosion occurred as the meteorite entered Earth's atmosphere above Cape Cod Bay. Residents reported sightings from Delaware to Montreal, and footage captured the meteor's descent.

NASA stated that fragments from the meteorite may have landed in Cape Cod Bay and could potentially be recovered. The agency is analyzing the trajectory and impact data to determine the precise location where pieces might be found. The blast was powerful enough to generate reports of the explosion across multiple states and into Canada.

Meteors entering Earth's atmosphere at high speeds often create visible fireballs and sonic booms as they disintegrate from friction and pressure. The energy released during atmospheric entry can produce explosions comparable to conventional explosives, measured in tons of TNT equivalent. This particular meteor's 300-ton blast places it among the more substantial recent atmospheric entries detected over populated areas.

The event drew widespread attention due to its visibility across such a large geographic area and the intensity of the sonic boom. Witnesses described seeing a bright flash in the sky followed by a thunderous noise. Video footage from news stations showed the meteor's luminous trail as it descended toward the ocean. The explosion occurred over water, preventing any damage to populated areas or infrastructure.

Meteor events of this magnitude, while not exceptionally rare, serve as reminders of the constant influx of space debris entering Earth's atmosphere. Most meteors burn up completely before reaching the surface, but larger fragments occasionally survive to become meteorites. If pieces are recovered from Cape Cod Bay, they could provide scientists with material for studying the composition and origin of the meteor. NASA continues to monitor for additional reports and is coordinating with local authorities regarding potential recovery efforts.
