Yesterday, 10:49 PM ET

A federal appeals court has ruled that the Trump administration acted unconstitutionally by expelling active transgender service members under a policy implemented by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The divided three-judge panel decision from the US Court of Appeals for Washington DC restricts the administration's ability to discharge troops currently serving in the military based on their transgender status.

The court's majority opinion found the ban to be "arbitrary, and based on animus," according to reporting on the decision. While the ruling prevents the military from removing existing transgender service members, the court allowed the armed services to continue blocking new transgender individuals from enlisting.

Hegseth responded to the decision by signaling his intention to appeal to the Supreme Court. On Monday, the defense secretary wrote on social media that he would "see you at SCOTUS," indicating his commitment to challenging the appeals court ruling.

The policy permits the Pentagon to remove transgender troops from active service. Legal experts and observers expect the government to challenge the appeals court decision, with the case likely to eventually reach the Supreme Court.

The ruling affects service members currently serving in uniform who identify as transgender. The court's decision limits the Pentagon's ability to discharge these individuals while legal proceedings continue, though the administration has made clear it will pursue further appeals to defend the policy.

This case represents part of a broader Trump administration effort targeting transgender people across multiple government sectors. The Department of Education's Office of Civil Rights announced an investigation into Smith College, one of the country's largest women's colleges, over its admittance of transgender women. The department stated the college could be violating federal law by "allowing biological males into women's intimate spaces," including dormitories, bathrooms, locker rooms, and sports teams.

The education department argued that Title IX's exemption for single-sex colleges applies only to "biological sex difference, not subjective gender identity." Kimberly Richey, assistant secretary for civil rights, stated that "an all-women's college loses all meaning if it is admitting biological males."

Smith College's admission policy allows for "any applicants who self-identify as women," including "cis, trans and nonbinary women." The college affirmed this policy in 2015 after studying the issue and consulting with students and alumni, noting that "concepts of female identity have evolved" since the college's founding in 1875.

The investigation follows a complaint filed by Defending Education, a rightwing group that targets schools over diversity policies. The administration has pursued multiple actions against transgender people, including filing lawsuits against states allowing transgender sports participation and seeking to ban access to gender-affirming medical care. Trump also banned transgender people from military service and required passports to match biological sex at birth.

Russia launched a massive two-day assault on Ukrainian cities with hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles, killing at least 13 people and leaving others trapped beneath rubble. The attacks hit major urban centers including Kyiv, Dnipro, Kharkiv, Poltava and Zaporizhzhia.

The overnight strikes began at 3am Thursday with what Ukraine's air force described as 56 missiles of various types and nearly 700 drones. Ukrainian air defenses destroyed or suppressed 40 missiles and 602 drones, but many projectiles still reached their targets. The assault followed heavy daytime raids on Wednesday that killed at least 14 people and injured more than 80 others.

Kyiv bore the brunt of the attack. Five people were confirmed killed in the capital overnight, with 40 injured including two children. Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko reported that a large apartment block collapsed in the city's Darnytskyi district, destroying 18 apartments. Rescue teams pulled 11 people from the rubble, though authorities said more than 10 people were still believed missing. Water and power supplies were disrupted in eastern parts of the city.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the strikes represented "one of the longest and most massive Russian attacks against Ukraine." He stated the raids appeared designed to overwhelm air defense systems through sheer volume and intensity of weapons. Zelenskyy called for a "just response" and ordered Ukraine's armed forces to prepare retaliation options.

Wednesday's attacks included "double-tap" strikes that targeted first responders sent to initial strike sites. Strikes also hit two dozen locations associated with Ukraine's railway system and other critical infrastructure. Russian drones separately struck a UN vehicle in the southern city of Kherson.

The assault dealt a significant blow to recent claims about imminent peace negotiations. U.S. President Donald Trump had suggested a deal between Russia and Ukraine was close, citing remarks by Russian President Vladimir Putin that the war might be approaching an end. The scale and timing of these attacks appeared to undermine those assertions.

The intensity of recent Russian bombardment prompted concerns that Moscow was deliberately attempting to overwhelm Ukrainian air defenses through sustained attacks with large numbers of drones and missiles. Ukraine's intelligence assessed Russia aimed to cause "as much grief and pain as possible" during the campaign.

The strikes came as Hungary summoned Russia's ambassador in response to attacks near the Hungarian border, marking a shift in Budapest's stance following the recent election of Péter Magyar as prime minister, ending years of warmer relations between Hungary and Moscow under his predecessor.

Trump Proposes 25% Tariff on Brazilian Imports

A few minutes ago

The Trump administration announced a proposal to impose a 25% tariff on imports from Brazil, citing unfair trade practices as justification. The move represents the latest step in the administration's effort to expand its tariff program through Section 301 investigations, a trade mechanism that allows the US to retaliate against foreign countries for practices deemed harmful to American commerce.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 grants the US Trade Representative authority to investigate and respond to foreign trade barriers. The administration has increasingly relied on this tool to impose tariffs on trading partners, arguing that current trade relationships disadvantage American workers and businesses. Brazil joins a growing list of countries facing tariff threats under this framework.

Brazil is a major trading partner of the United States, with significant exports in agricultural products, steel, and manufactured goods. A 25% tariff would substantially increase costs for American companies importing Brazilian products and could prompt retaliatory measures from Brazil.

The announcement did not specify which Brazilian products would face tariffs or provide a timeline for implementation. Trade proposals under Section 301 typically undergo a period of public comment and review before taking effect. The Brazilian government has not yet issued a formal response to the tariff proposal.

This latest tariff proposal comes as the Trump administration continues reshaping its trade agenda following previous legal challenges. Earlier tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act were struck down by the Supreme Court, though the federal government has since established a refund process for importers who paid roughly 166 billion dollars in tariff fees. The refund process, which began in late April, requires importers to work with their original customs brokers to file claims through the government's Ace Secure Data Portal.

Industry observers expect the refund process to take 60 to 90 days, though businesses must factor in additional fees and potential tax implications when calculating returns. Some major shipping companies like FedEx and UPS have pledged to return tariff refunds to customers, while larger retailers including Amazon and Apple have remained silent on whether they will pass refunds along to consumers.

The new Brazil tariff plan adds to uncertainty in international trade markets at a time when businesses are already navigating supply chain disruptions and inflation concerns. Trade groups representing importers and manufacturers will likely weigh in during the comment period, particularly those dependent on Brazilian goods. The proposal underscores the administration's continued focus on using tariffs to reshape trade relationships, even as questions persist about the legal framework and long-term economic consequences of such policies.

Rick Adelman, a Hall of Fame basketball coach who led teams to more than 1,000 wins across three decades in the NBA, has died at age 79. The longtime coach spent his career with multiple franchises including the Portland Trail Blazers, Sacramento Kings, Houston Rockets, and Minnesota Timberwolves.

Adelman compiled 1,042 wins during his NBA coaching career, ranking him among the winningest coaches in league history. He guided the Trail Blazers to two NBA Finals appearances in 1990 and 1992, and later transformed the Sacramento Kings into perennial contenders during the early 2000s. His Kings teams became known for their fast-paced, ball-movement offense that influenced modern NBA play.

The coach was inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame in recognition of his contributions to the sport. Beyond his win total, Adelman earned respect throughout the league for his offensive schemes and player development. He coached numerous All-Star players and helped shape the careers of stars across multiple eras of professional basketball.

Adelman began his NBA journey as a player before transitioning to coaching, where he found his greatest success. He served as head coach for the Trail Blazers from 1989 to 1994, the Golden State Warriors briefly, the Kings from 1998 to 2006, the Rockets from 2007 to 2011, and the Timberwolves from 2011 to 2014. His coaching philosophy emphasized teamwork and offensive efficiency, principles that influenced a generation of coaches who followed.

Tributes from across the basketball community followed news of his death. The Sacramento Kings released an official statement honoring Adelman, and multiple NBA organizations acknowledged his impact on the sport. His legacy extends beyond statistics to his influence on how modern basketball is played and coached at the highest level.

The timing of Owens' statement coincides with the non-renewal of veteran correspondent Sharyn Alfonsi's contract. Alfonsi, who spent nearly twenty years at CBS News and more than a decade at 60 Minutes, accused the network of penalizing her for "refusing to sanitize factually accurate reporting."

Alfonsi's departure followed an editorial dispute over a segment about a prison in El Salvador. CBS News editor-in-chief Bari Weiss shelved the story, which Alfonsi said was factually accurate. Alfonsi claimed the network's silence in response to her representation's attempts to establish a path forward made her situation clear.

In a statement, Alfonsi warned that CBS management may "hide behind corporate euphemisms like 'modernization' and 'restructuring'" to explain her departure. She said the decision "sends a chilling message to the entire newsroom" and represents "a deliberate choice to penalize a journalist for refusing to sanitize factually accurate reporting."

Alfonsi criticized the network for choosing "access journalism over accountability and protecting power rather than scrutinizing it." She expressed concern that "the wall between editorial independence and corporate interest at CBS is being methodically torn down."

The upheaval extends beyond Alfonsi. Veteran correspondent Scott Pelley reportedly confronted CBS News management in a heated meeting, taking direct aim at Weiss over decisions to fire the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents including Alfonsi and Cecilia Vega.

Anderson Cooper, another veteran correspondent, also opted to leave the show. In comments tied to his departure, Cooper emphasized that "the independence of 60 Minutes has been critical." He expressed hope that "60 Minutes remains 60 Minutes" while acknowledging that programs can evolve.

The program has been a staple of American broadcast journalism for decades, known for investigative reporting and in-depth interviews. The changes to its leadership and correspondent lineup represent a significant shift for one of television's most established news programs.

CBS News has not publicly responded to the criticism from former and current staff members. The network faces the challenge of managing its restructuring while addressing concerns about maintaining the editorial standards that made 60 Minutes one of the most-watched news broadcasts in the country.
