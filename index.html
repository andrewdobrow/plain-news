President Donald Trump has reiterated his demands on Iran and threatened additional military strikes, signaling that despite ongoing diplomatic efforts, military options remain viable. The president's public statements have created uncertainty about whether negotiations will succeed or escalate into further conflict.

Trump set an ultimatum for Iran to comply with his conditions, warning of severe consequences if a deal was not reached by a specific deadline. He threatened to destroy Iran's power stations and bridges, stating that "a whole civilization will die tonight" if Tehran did not capitulate. The White House later clarified that the U.S. was not considering nuclear weapons after Vice President JD Vance's comments raised concerns about undisclosed military capabilities.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards responded to Trump's threats by signaling willingness to escalate, threatening to retaliate "beyond the region" and potentially target oil and gas facilities in the Gulf. Such action could destabilize global energy markets and trigger international economic consequences.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif publicly requested that Trump delay his deadline by two weeks to allow diplomatic efforts to continue. Sharif characterized the negotiations as "progressing steadily, strongly and powerfully," though he provided no specific updates on substantive progress. A senior Iranian official indicated Tehran was reviewing a ceasefire proposal "positively," though Iran remained unwilling to surrender its main leverage: control over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil and gas shipments.

Iran presented its own ten-point plan demanding long-term security guarantees, which Trump rejected as insufficient. Iran's leadership, including President Masoud Pezeshkian and First Vice President Mohammad Reza Aref, declared their nation was prepared for all possible outcomes and would defend itself if attacked.

At the United Nations, Iran's representative accused Trump of inciting war crimes and potentially genocide. UN Secretary-General António Guterres reminded all parties that attacking civilian infrastructure violates international law. Pope Leo condemned Trump's threats as "truly unacceptable" and called on world leaders to pursue peaceful resolution.

Trump's approach has alternated between aggressive demands and suggestions of flexibility, creating ambiguity about Washington's actual negotiating position and true red lines. He previously stated that a deal would include reopening the Strait of Hormuz but offered no additional details about agreement terms.

The president insists that November's midterm elections will not pressure him into accepting unfavorable terms, asserting instead that Iran is "negotiating on fumes." His willingness to maintain maximum demands while threatening force, combined with simultaneous diplomatic engagement, characterizes his Iran strategy.

Officers in the chain of command face obligations under U.S. and international law to refuse unlawful orders, though it remained unclear whether anyone in Trump's administration would intervene to prevent potentially illegal military action. Whether Trump's approach yields a lasting agreement or further escalation continues to hang in the balance.

6:32 PM ET

The Trump administration is developing plans to stop processing international flights at airports in cities that restrict cooperation with federal immigration authorities, according to recent statements from the Department of Homeland Security.

Acting DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin announced during a Fox News interview on Tuesday that the administration is "drawing up plans" to withdraw immigration processing services from airports in sanctuary cities. These jurisdictions have policies that limit local police cooperation with Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents.

The threat comes in response to escalating tensions at a detention facility in Newark, New Jersey. For five days, hundreds of immigrants detained at the privately-operated Delaney Hall ICE facility have participated in a hunger and work strike, demanding improved conditions, better medical care, and faster processing of their immigration cases. On Tuesday evening, immigration officials deployed pepper spray and Tasers against protesters outside the facility.

Mullin questioned why the federal government should process international flights into Newark Liberty International Airport while the city limits ICE operations. "If they're going to not allow us to go out and arrest the 'worst of the worst,' then why are we processing international flights into the airport there?" Mullin stated during the Fox interview. He added that officials are preparing plans to stop processing flights at airports in "sanctuary cities, where the local radical left Democrats aren't allowing us to do our job."

Customs and Border Protection and the Transportation Security Administration, which handle immigration processing and security at airports, are part of the Department of Homeland Security alongside ICE.

The confrontation intensified after Senator Andy Kim was pepper-sprayed by ICE officers on Monday while attending the protests. Mullin subsequently accused Kim and other Democratic politicians of "spreading smears" about ICE and engaging in political stunts.

On Wednesday, the hunger strike continued inside the facility. Congressman Adriano Espaillat conducted an oversight visit to the detention center, describing serious conditions inside. "The food conditions are horrible. We feel that they're not getting medical services. We feel that they're overcrowded, and they're denied their fundamental rights," Espaillat said, pledging to continue fighting to close the facility.

Newark Mayor Ras Baraka has called on the governor to empower the state attorney general to investigate conditions at Delaney Hall. Senator Andy Kim responded to Mullin's criticism, stating the situation was never about personal politics.

The administration has not specified which airports would be affected or provided a timeline for implementation. However, major international airports operate in several cities with sanctuary policies, including New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Chicago. The proposal represents a significant escalation in efforts to pressure sanctuary jurisdictions to change their immigration enforcement policies.

The timeline for processing such a large volume of refund claims presents logistical challenges for customs operations, though the court's order suggests patience may be wearing thin.

CPL Productions, the independent company that produces the show for Channel 4, said the woman did not initially inform them about this threat, describing it instead as a passing comment when reported later. CPL stated it took immediate action once the woman indicated she felt unsafe.

The second woman alleged she told both Channel 4 and CPL before her episodes aired that she had been raped by her on-screen husband, yet the episodes were broadcast regardless. CPL's lawyers said the woman had previously confirmed to them that all sexual activity was consensual before making the rape allegation. They maintain they followed welfare protocols and took her concerns seriously.

Channel 4 stated it received "serious allegations" in April against several past contributors, which were denied by those involved. The broadcaster said it removed all episodes from its streaming service and linear channels on Monday afternoon, along with the show's social media accounts.

Channel 4 said it operates under "comprehensive and robust welfare protocols," including background checks, a code of conduct, daily check-ins with participants, and a specialist welfare team. The channel's new chief executive, Priya Dogra, launched an external review of contributor welfare last month to examine how claims were handled and whether protocols need updating.

"I want to express my sympathy to contributors who have clearly been distressed after taking part," Dogra said. She added that based on information available at the time, Channel 4 believed it "acted quickly, appropriately, sensitively and with wellbeing front and centre."

CPL's legal representatives argued that the company's welfare protocols represent "gold standard" practice and that it responded appropriately in all cases discussed. The allegations represent the latest scrutiny faced by reality television production in the UK, raising questions about participant safety and duty of care across the industry.
