"An F-35C fighter jet from Abraham Lincoln shot down the Iranian drone in self-defence and to protect the aircraft carrier and personnel on board," Hawkins said. No U.S. service members were harmed or equipment damaged.

Also on Tuesday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards harassed a U.S.-flagged merchant vessel in the Strait of Hormuz. Two IRGC boats and an Iranian Mohajer drone approached the M/V Stena Imperative at high speeds and threatened to board the tanker, according to military officials.

These military confrontations come as diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran have stalled. However, there are signs negotiations may resume. The White House confirmed that Trump envoy Steve Witkoff plans to hold talks with Iranian officials later in the week.

The escalation represents the latest in a series of military exchanges in the Middle East involving American and Iranian forces. U.S. Central Command oversees military operations across the Middle East and Central Asia, including forces stationed in Kuwait, Bahrain, and other regional bases that came under threat from the Iranian attacks. 8:14 PM ET

Kevin Warsh officially became chair of the Federal Reserve on May 14, taking control of one of the most influential positions in the U.S. government at a moment of intense scrutiny over the central bank's independence. The Senate confirmed Warsh in a 54-45 vote split largely along party lines, with Democrat John Fetterman of Pennsylvania voting with Republicans. This marked the most divisive Fed chair confirmation in history.

Warsh announced his first personnel decisions by appointing two conservative policy veterans as interim advisers. Paul Winfree, who contributed to Project 2025 (a conservative policy document), and another adviser named Heil join the Fed leadership. The appointments signal a significant shift in the ideological direction of the central bank under the new administration.

The 54-year-old economist and former Wall Street banker previously served as a Federal Reserve governor from 2006 to 2011, when he earned a reputation as an "inflation hawk" for advocating higher interest rates to combat rising prices. He left the Fed board partly due to disagreements over the central bank's post-financial crisis stimulus policies. Trump ultimately chose Jerome Powell for the top Fed job in 2018, a decision Trump now calls "a really big mistake."

Warsh takes the helm as the Fed faces pressure from the Trump administration to lower interest rates. Inflation currently stands at 3.8%, complicating any push for rate cuts. The Fed controls interest rates, which determine borrowing costs. Higher rates cool spending and reduce prices but risk increasing unemployment. Lower rates can stimulate the economy but may push prices higher.

At his Senate confirmation hearing, Warsh pledged to maintain Fed independence and "take politics out of monetary policy and monetary policy out of politics." However, he refused to answer whether Trump lost the 2020 election, raising concerns among Democrats. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate banking committee, criticized his nomination, saying Trump selected Warsh to be his "sock puppet" to control interest rates. She warned that "Warsh's confirmation equals another step in Trump's attempt to take over the Fed."

Fetterman, the lone Democrat supporting Warsh, said he believes the new chair will be "transparent and responsive to Congress and the public" and praised Warsh's promise to maintain Fed independence in setting rates.

The appointments of conservative policy experts represent a departure from recent Fed staffing patterns. Former Fed Chair Jerome Powell, whose term ended when Warsh took over, announced he would remain on the Fed board as a voting governor, citing ongoing White House scrutiny of the central bank's headquarters renovations. Powell later called this scrutiny "pretext" for pressure to lower rates and warned that political interference could permanently damage public trust in the Fed.

Microsoft has released Scout, an AI assistant that operates continuously to help users manage work tasks and information. The company describes Scout as a personal agent that remains active in the background, drawing comparisons to an executive assistant.

Internal documents obtained by 404 Media reveal that Microsoft aims to make users dependent on the new tool. The documents state the company wants to "make people addicted" to Scout, according to the reporting. Microsoft has positioned Scout as an always-on assistant that can handle various workplace functions.

The launch represents Microsoft's latest effort to integrate AI capabilities into productivity workflows. Scout builds on the company's existing AI investments, including its partnership with OpenAI. The assistant is designed to work alongside users rather than requiring them to switch between applications or manually trigger functions.

Microsoft announced Scout at its Build developer conference, where the company also introduced several other AI-focused initiatives. The company unveiled new AI models intended to reduce its reliance on OpenAI and lower costs for developers. Microsoft also presented Work IQ APIs and demonstrated Linux tools for Windows developers, along with an RTX Spark desktop for development work.

The Scout launch coincides with Microsoft testing a wearable AI device aimed at office workers, according to BBC reporting. The combination of software and potential hardware products indicates Microsoft is pursuing multiple approaches to embedding AI assistants into daily work routines. The company has framed these developments as part of a new era of AI-driven devices and services at its annual developer conference.
