President Trump announced that a peace agreement with Iran "has been largely negotiated" after conversations with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies and Israeli leadership. However, the announcement came with significant complications, as Trump's own negotiating team and Iran have presented conflicting accounts of what has actually been agreed.

Trump stated on his social media platform that "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" remain under discussion and will be announced shortly. He claimed the Strait of Hormuz would be opened as part of the deal and indicated that an agreement had been "largely negotiated" between the United States, Iran and various other countries.

However, Iran's Fars news agency, affiliated with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, rejected Trump's characterization. The agency stated that "the management of the Strait, determining the route, time, method of passage, and issuing permits will continue to be the monopoly and discretion of the Islamic Republic of Iran," directly contradicting Trump's assertions about the strait's status in any agreement. The agency said Trump's claims were "inconsistent with reality."

Multiple sources have outlined potential deal terms. According to reports, the framework would include opening the strait with no tolls during a 60-day ceasefire extension, allowing Iran to freely sell oil and initiating negotiations on its nuclear program. The United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports in exchange. Three senior Iranian officials told the New York Times the agreement could release 25 billion dollars in frozen Iranian assets and result in nuclear negotiations within 30 to 60 days.

Trump met with his negotiating team, including special envoy Steve Witkoff and adviser Jared Kushner, to discuss the latest proposals. He also conducted calls with leaders from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Bahrain, Pakistan and Turkey.

The uncertainty surrounding the deal has drawn criticism from Republican hawks. Mike Pompeo, who served as CIA director and secretary of state during Trump's first term, denounced the reported terms as too similar to the 2015 nuclear deal negotiated by the Obama administration, which Trump withdrew from in 2018.

However, Robert Malley, one of Obama's negotiators, responded that while the approach differed from what his team would have taken, he would accept the current deal if it ended the war, stopped loss of life and prevented economic damage.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif later said Pakistan hopes to host another round of talks between the United States and Iran "very soon," describing recent diplomatic calls as "very useful and productive."

The negotiations remain fluid with no confirmed timeline for finalization. Trump has threatened to resume striking Iran if a deal meeting American demands is not reached.

5:46 PM ET

More than 245 suspected cases of Ebola have been reported in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with confirmed deaths reaching at least 65 people, according to health officials. The outbreak is concentrated in Ituri province, a conflict-affected region that borders Uganda and South Sudan, raising concerns about potential spread across borders.

The strain identified in this outbreak is the Bundibugyo virus, detected in laboratory samples tested by the DRC's national research laboratory. This represents a significant concern because no licensed vaccine currently exists for the Bundibugyo strain, unlike the more commonly seen Zaire strain of Ebola that has caused previous outbreaks in the region. Only two prior outbreaks of Bundibugyo virus have occurred, in 2007 and 2012.

The World Health Organization has estimated the death rate for this outbreak at between 30 and 50 percent based on confirmed cases. Anaïs Legand from the WHO's high threat pathogens team described this rate as "huge," meaning that up to five out of every ten infected people are likely to die.

The geographical challenges facing containment efforts are substantial. Ituri province contains numerous mining towns with constant population movement, making disease control particularly difficult. Cases have been reported mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in the neighboring city of Bunia.

Uganda's health ministry has already confirmed cases in the country, with a 59-year-old man dying from Ebola in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC. This development prompted urgent regional coordination efforts. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention convened an emergency meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies.

Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa CDC, emphasized the importance of rapid coordination given the high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries. According to experts, multiple factors contribute to recurring Ebola outbreaks in the region, including close human contact with animal reservoirs such as bats and possibly primates, movement between rural and urban environments, the tropical climate, and extensive rainforest coverage.

The World Health Organization's director general visited the hardest-hit areas to assess the situation and support containment efforts. Medical charities including Doctors Without Borders have deployed teams to assist health workers facing severe resource shortages and inadequate protective equipment. The organizations have described the virus's spread as deeply alarming, highlighting the significant gap between the outbreak's scale and available resources.

The DRC has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976, though this outbreak presents unique challenges due to the Bundibugyo strain and regional instability.

5:06 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on the launchpad at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida on Thursday night during a static fire test, destroying the launch facility and raising serious questions about NASA's timeline for returning humans to the moon.

The explosion occurred at 9pm ET at Launch Complex 36, creating a massive fireball visible from miles away. An orange sky was reported in Fort Pierce, 185 kilometers to the south, and shockwaves were felt by residents across Florida's space coast and even in South Carolina hundreds of miles north. Flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the incident.

Blue Origin described the event as an "anomaly" and confirmed that all employees were safe and uninjured. Company founder Jeff Bezos acknowledged the setback on social media, stating "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The timing of the explosion is particularly significant for NASA's ambitions. The agency had announced just the day before that Blue Origin won a contract to launch the first of three planned missions to begin construction of a 20 billion dollar lunar base. The New Glenn rocket is intended to carry landers to the moon as part of NASA's broader effort to establish a permanent lunar presence and return astronauts to the surface for the first time since 1972.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of its timeline following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he wrote. "We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

The explosion marks another setback for Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for lucrative NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from the third flight of New Glenn ended up in the wrong orbit, and the rocket was temporarily grounded by the Federal Aviation Administration before receiving clearance to return to flight just last week. Thursday's static fire test was the first such test since that clearance.

The incident also impacts competition for upcoming missions. Blue Origin is scheduled to test its Blue Moon lander for NASA's Artemis III mission planned for 2027, competing against SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System to determine which company will ferry astronauts from orbit to the lunar surface.

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk responded to the explosion on social media, noting simply, "Rockets are hard."

Nvidia will introduce its first processor designed for Windows PCs next week, according to Axios. The chip represents Nvidia's entry into a market long dominated by Intel and AMD.

The processor is expected to debut during Nvidia's GTC event. Microsoft and Arm have also posted cryptic messages on social media ahead of the announcement, suggesting collaboration on the new hardware.

The chip, referred to as the N1X in some reports, is designed for laptop computers. It would mark the first time Nvidia has produced a central processing unit for Windows machines, though the company already makes graphics processors widely used in PCs.

Nvidia, Microsoft, and Arm have all teased the upcoming announcement without revealing specific details. The processor appears to use Arm architecture rather than the x86 design traditional in Windows computers.

The move could reshape the PC market. Nvidia is already the dominant supplier of chips for artificial intelligence applications, and its entry into the broader PC processor space would create a third major competitor alongside Intel and AMD.

Windows PCs powered by Arm chips have existed before, but they have struggled to gain significant market share. Previous efforts faced compatibility issues with software designed for x86 processors. Whether Nvidia's approach will overcome these challenges remains to be seen.

The announcement comes as demand for AI-capable hardware continues to grow. Nvidia's expertise in AI chip design could give its PC processors an advantage in handling machine learning tasks.

Neither Nvidia nor Microsoft has officially confirmed the product details or launch date. The companies have limited their public statements to social media posts that hint at a major announcement without providing specifics.
