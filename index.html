Secretary of State Marco Rubio appeared before the Senate Foreign Relations Committee on Tuesday to detail U.S. demands in ongoing negotiations with Iran, marking his first public testimony since the Trump administration launched the war in February. His appearance comes as Iran has announced it is halting peace talks and moving to fully close the Strait of Hormuz, intensifying the regional conflict.

Rubio claimed that Iran has agreed to negotiate aspects of its nuclear program that officials refused to discuss a month earlier. "There is the prospect before us, which could happen today, it could happen tomorrow, it could happen next week - that for the first time, certainly in my memory, they have agreed to negotiate aspects of their nuclear program that just a month ago they said they would not," he told senators.

The secretary's optimism contrasts sharply with Iran's announcement that it would stop exchanging messages with Washington through intermediaries, citing Israel's military operations in Lebanon as a ceasefire violation. President Trump later insisted that conversations "have been going on continuously" and denied reports that officials had stopped speaking.

Rubio outlined a two-phase framework for negotiations. He stated that reopening the Strait of Hormuz serves as a precondition for talks, not a bargaining chip. Iran must declare it is not charging tolls, assist in removing mines it placed there, and commit not to fire on ships. Only after these conditions are met would phase two discussions begin, which would require Iran to dispose of its highly enriched uranium stockpile and accept "severe and long-term limitations, or cancellation of enrichment activity."

When questioned by Democratic Senator Chris Murphy about offering sanctions relief to reopen the waterway, Rubio ruled out that approach. "Any sanctions relief is condition-based," he said, tied to Iran's nuclear program. "That's not been discussed, that's not been offered."

Regarding Iran's military capabilities, Rubio suggested that the country's missile program has been "substantially degraded" and its drone-building capability "eroded." He claimed there is "no Iranian navy," stating it "lies at the bottom of the ocean." However, he acknowledged that Iran still possesses "a lot of drones" because they are relatively easy to manufacture.

These claims about Iran's military destruction have been contested by independent analysis. The New York Times reported in May that Iran retained roughly 70 percent of its prewar missile stockpile, though over 85 percent of its ballistic missile, drone and naval defense industrial base has been damaged or destroyed.

Rubio noted that a U.S. counter-blockade targeting all ships seeking Iranian ports is costing Tehran "hundreds of millions of dollars a day" in lost oil revenue. This blockade followed Iran's failure to honor an April ceasefire agreement that included a commitment to reopen the strait.

The attack targeted five major cities: Kyiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Poltava, and Kharkiv. According to Ukraine's air force, Russia deployed 73 missiles and 656 drones, including eight hypersonic Tsirkon missiles. The strikes hit residential buildings, energy infrastructure, and businesses across multiple locations.

In Dnipro, five people were killed as Russian forces hit the city throughout the night, according to regional officials. Four bodies were discovered in the ruins of a destroyed house, with rescue workers continuing to search for additional victims in the rubble. At least 34 people were injured in the overnight strikes, which authorities said lasted for hours. Fires broke out across the city, partly destroying several apartment blocks and private residences.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the pattern of attacks on social media, stating: "The Russians' tactics have not changed: strike drones, cruise missiles and a significant amount of ballistics. Most of the targets are ordinary infrastructure in cities. Residential buildings, energy, and enterprises have been damaged."

In response to the escalating violence, Zelenskyy asked President Donald Trump to send Patriot missiles to Ukraine to help defend against future attacks.

Authorities reported that Ukrainian air defenses repelled the majority of the incoming drones, which represented one of the largest assault waves in several days. Additional casualties were reported in Odesa and Kharkiv, with at least three people injured in those cities.

The overnight assault followed a prisoner exchange between Russia and Ukraine on Friday, in which both sides swapped 193 service members. These exchanges were negotiated as part of US-brokered talks between Moscow and Kyiv, though negotiations have produced no progress on ending Russia's invasion.

Ukraine also conducted long-range drone strikes deep inside Russian territory. A woman was killed and a man seriously injured in a drone strike in the Russian border region of Belgorod.

The repeated strikes on Ukrainian cities come as Russia faces mounting battlefield losses and casualties in the conflict. Military analysts suggest Moscow is using intensified bombing campaigns as leverage for potential future negotiations, hoping to strengthen its position at the bargaining table. The combination of advance warnings about strikes and the subsequent delays before attacks appeared designed to create psychological pressure on Ukrainian civilians, observers said.

Rescue operations continued as workers searched through debris at multiple sites across the country, with the death toll expected to rise as recovery efforts proceeded.

President Trump has named Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, as acting director of national intelligence. The appointment came days after Tulsi Gabbard announced her departure from the role.

Pulte, whose grandfather founded the PulteGroup homebuilder, previously had no government experience before Trump appointed him to lead the FHFA, which oversees the federal housing lenders Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. He will remain in his housing finance position while taking on the intelligence role temporarily as acting director, meaning he can serve without Senate confirmation.

The appointment has generated significant alarm among national security professionals and lawmakers. Critics have emphasized that Pulte lacks any background in national security or intelligence work, despite the position's critical importance in coordinating the work of agencies including the CIA, NSA, and FBI.

As head of the FHFA, Pulte has attracted scrutiny for using his authority to investigate Trump's political opponents. He has publicly accused targets of the president, including Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook, of committing mortgage fraud. Cook has denied wrongdoing. A former high-ranking official described his handling of the Cook case as "very bizarre."

Democratic Senator Mark Warner, vice-chair of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, criticized the selection. "Rather than selecting a respected national security professional capable of delivering independent judgments, the president has chosen an official who has demonstrated not just willingness but eagerness to use the authorities of government to pursue political retribution," Warner said. He argued that Trump was "not looking for an intelligence leader who will follow the facts or speak truth to power, but rather someone who will be willing to shape intelligence around the president's wishes."

Even Senate Republicans expressed concerns about the appointment. Senate Majority Leader John Thune said "we don't need a weaponized" national intelligence director and indicated Pulte would face a "lengthy road ahead" if nominated for a permanent position requiring Senate confirmation. Thune stressed the need for "professionals" in the role rather than someone focused on political aims.

Other Republican senators on the intelligence committee voiced skepticism. Susan Collins said she did not know Pulte's background, while James Lankford stated he was unaware of any intelligence experience and emphasized that the role "should be a neutral."

Trump justified the appointment on Truth Social, saying Pulte has "deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America."

The director of national intelligence position was created after the September 11 attacks to improve coordination among intelligence agencies. The role requires synthesizing intelligence reports across government and advising the president on classified national security threats. The position has traditionally been filled by individuals with extensive backgrounds in intelligence, military service, or national security policy.

Dashlane serves millions of users who rely on the service to stor...

11:40 AM ET

Password management company Dashlane has disclosed that attackers gained unauthorized access to customer accounts and downloaded their password vaults. According to the company, hackers were able to circumvent its two-factor authentication system through brute-force attacks, granting them entry to customer data.

Dashlane serves millions of users who rely on the service to store sensitive information including login credentials and payment details within encrypted vaults. The company has not specified how many customers were affected by the breach.

Password managers function as centralized repositories for authentication information, making them attractive targets for cybercriminals. When a password manager is successfully compromised, it can expose all stored credentials at once. However, in this case, the vaults that attackers downloaded contain encrypted passwords rather than plaintext versions. This means the actual passwords remain protected by encryption, and their security depends on the strength of each user's master password.

The attack exploited Dashlane's two-factor authentication system through brute-force methods. Two-factor authentication adds a second verification layer beyond passwords, typically requiring users to provide additional confirmation such as a code transmitted to their phone or generated by an authenticator application. In brute-force attacks, perpetrators systematically test numerous possible combinations until discovering one that provides access.

Dashlane has not revealed specific details about the vulnerability that enabled the brute-force attack to succeed. The company also has not disclosed what actions it has implemented to prevent future similar incidents. Additionally, Dashlane has not announced whether it is directing affected customers to change their master passwords or implement other protective measures.

Customers whose vaults were downloaded face potential risk of password exposure if attackers can successfully crack the encryption protecting their master password. This represents a significant security concern because a compromised master password would unlock access to all stored credentials within a customer's vault.

The disclosure raises questions about the security practices at major password management providers and the effectiveness of their authentication systems. Password managers have become increasingly popular as tools for managing the growing number of online accounts that individuals must maintain, but breaches highlight the critical importance of choosing services with robust security measures.

Users affected by this breach should consider changing their master passwords and reviewing their accounts for any unauthorized activity. They may also want to monitor their financial accounts and credit reports for signs of fraud. For those concerned about their security, this incident underscores the importance of using strong, unique master passwords that would be difficult for attackers to crack through brute-force methods.

Dashlane and the affected customers remain in a situation where the full scope and impact of the breach continue to unfold as the company conducts its investigation.

Cleveland Browns general manager Andrew Berry said he would not have seriously pursued trading star defensive end Myles Garrett to the Los Angeles Rams unless the package included edge rusher Jared Verse.

Garrett, a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection, has been one of the league's most dominant defenders since Cleveland selected him first overall in 2017. He holds the most sacks (125.5), pressures (413) and tackles for loss (149) among all players since that draft. In 2025, he set the single-season sacks record with 23.

The trade sent Garrett to Los Angeles in exchange for Verse, a 2027 first-round selection, a 2028 second-round pick and a 2029 third-round selection. Verse, 25, was a first-round pick by the Rams in 2024 and earned Defensive Rookie of the Year honors. He has recorded 12 career sacks and 22 tackles for loss while earning Pro Bowl selection this past season.

Berry explained the thinking behind the blockbuster deal after it was announced. "As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" he said.

Garrett had requested a trade from the Browns in 2024 after they finished 3-14, expressing a desire to play for a championship contender. The following month, he signed a four-year, $160 million extension with Cleveland that made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in the league at the time. The deal included $122.8 million guaranteed and an average annual salary of $40 million.

The Browns again missed the playoffs in 2025 with a 5-12 record. Berry was able to facilitate the trade after Garrett and the team agreed to modify his contract in March, deferring option payments over the 2026-28 seasons.

The Rams, fresh off losing in last season's NFC championship game to the eventual Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks, are known for aggressive moves. In 2021, they acquired Matthew Stafford from Detroit and won the Super Bowl in his first season with the team.

Garrett's move to Los Angeles reunites him with Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford, who won the AP NFL MVP award last season. This marks the first time a reigning MVP and Defensive Player of the Year will be teammates.

The trade reflects different philosophies for both franchises. The Rams continue their win-now approach by acquiring a proven superstar, while the Browns pivot toward a younger roster build centered around Verse as their defensive anchor for years to come.

2:00 PM ET

Cleveland Browns general manager Andrew Berry said he would not have seriously pursued trading star defensive end Myles Garrett to the Los Angeles Rams unless the package included edge rusher Jared Verse.

Garrett, a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection, has been one of the league's most dominant defenders since Cleveland selected him first overall in 2017. He holds the most sacks (125.5), pressures (413) and tackles for loss (149) among all players since that draft. In 2025, he set the single-season sacks record with 23.

The trade sent Garrett to Los Angeles in exchange for Verse, a 2027 first-round selection, a 2028 second-round pick and a 2029 third-round selection. Verse, 25, was a first-round pick by the Rams in 2024 and earned Defensive Rookie of the Year honors. He has recorded 12 career sacks and 22 tackles for loss while earning Pro Bowl selection this past season.

Berry explained the thinking behind the blockbuster deal after it was announced. "As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" he said.

Garrett had requested a trade from the Browns in 2024 after they finished 3-14, expressing a desire to play for a championship contender. The following month, he signed a four-year, $160 million extension with Cleveland that made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in the league at the time. The deal included $122.8 million guaranteed and an average annual salary of $40 million.

The Browns again missed the playoffs in 2025 with a 5-12 record. Berry was able to facilitate the trade after Garrett and the team agreed to modify his contract in March, deferring option payments over the 2026-28 seasons.

The Rams, fresh off losing in last season's NFC championship game to the eventual Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks, are known for aggressive moves. In 2021, they acquired Matthew Stafford from Detroit and won the Super Bowl in his first season with the team.

Garrett's move to Los Angeles reunites him with Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford, who won the AP NFL MVP award last season. This marks the first time a reigning MVP and Defensive Player of the Year will be teammates.

The trade reflects different philosophies for both franchises. The Rams continue their win-now approach by acquiring a proven superstar, while the Browns pivot toward a younger roster build centered around Verse as their defensive anchor for years to come.
