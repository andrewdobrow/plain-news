A few minutes ago

U.S. and Iranian negotiators have reached a tentative agreement to extend a ceasefire for 60 days and begin new nuclear talks, according to U.S. officials. The deal now awaits final approval from President Trump.

Trump unilaterally announced the ceasefire extension on Tuesday through his Truth Social platform after hours of escalating rhetoric in which he said he "expected to be bombing." The extension came after requests from Pakistani leaders to hold military action while Iranian negotiators work toward a unified proposal.

"Based on the fact that the Government of Iran is seriously fractured, not unexpectedly so and, upon the request of Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, of Pakistan, we have been asked to hold our Attack on the Country of Iran until such time as their leaders and representatives can come up with a unified proposal," Trump wrote.

The agreement would extend the existing ceasefire for two months and create a framework for renewed negotiations on Iran's nuclear program. The tentative deal represents a potential diplomatic breakthrough after years of heightened tensions between Washington and Tehran.

However, Iran has sent conflicting signals about the negotiations. Tehran's state television broadcast a message confirming that no Iranian delegation had visited Islamabad for talks. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the speaker of Iran's parliament and the regime's lead negotiator, dismissed the ceasefire extension as "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike." His personal adviser Mahdi Mohammadi said on social media that Iran must take military initiative, stating "the losing side cannot dictate terms."

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who has served as principal mediator, thanked Trump and said Pakistan would continue efforts for a negotiated settlement.

A major obstacle remains the ongoing U.S. Navy blockade of Iranian ports. Iran has insisted the blockade must end before negotiations resume. However, Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent both warned on Tuesday that the blockade will continue. Bessent stated that Iranian oil storage on Kharg Island will be full within days, threatening to shut in fragile Iranian wells.

U.S. forces have escalated pressure through the blockade by seizing Iranian cargo vessels and boarding oil tankers previously sanctioned for smuggling Iranian crude oil. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei characterized these actions as "piracy at sea and state terrorism."

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz by Iran, announced early in the conflict, has cut off supply of approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and gas. The International Energy Agency's chief called the combined impact of the conflict on energy markets "the biggest crisis in history."

The White House has not issued a public statement on whether Trump will approve the tentative deal.

Anthropic has become the world's most valuable artificial intelligence startup after raising $65 billion in Series H funding at a post-money valuation of $965 billion. The funding round, led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia Capital, eclipses OpenAI's previous valuation of $852 billion, marking a significant shift in the competitive landscape of the AI industry.

The company's rapid ascent reflects the widespread adoption of Claude, its AI chatbot, particularly among large enterprise customers. The release of powerful coding assistants in recent months has strengthened Anthropic's position as a dominant player in the global AI market. Krishna Rao, the company's chief financial officer, stated that "Claude is increasingly indispensable to our growing global community of customers, and we work tirelessly to make tools like Claude Code and Cowork more helpful, more powerful, and more adaptable to their needs."

Anthropic's strategy has diverged from some competitors in several key ways. The company has focused heavily on enterprise and coding services rather than consumer-facing products. Additionally, Anthropic has positioned itself as prioritizing AI safety and responsible development. One of its co-founders attended a recent papal encyclical addressing dangers associated with AI technology. The company has also maintained legal disputes with the Pentagon, refusing to remove safeguards from Claude that would allow its use for mass domestic surveillance or lethal autonomous weapons systems.

This valuation milestone places Anthropic within striking distance of the $1 trillion threshold, a benchmark historically associated only with publicly traded technology giants. The company joins an extremely small group of private enterprises approaching such valuations.

The funding round underscores the enormous capital flows continuing into the AI sector despite public concerns about the technology. Anthropic's valuation surge follows OpenAI's $122 billion funding round in March, which valued that company at $852 billion. OpenAI is reportedly planning to pursue a $1 trillion initial public offering later this year.

Anthropic has also emerged as an influential force in policy discussions surrounding AI regulation. The company has advocated for increased government oversight of artificial intelligence technology, positioning itself differently from other industry leaders who favor lighter regulatory frameworks.

The deal demonstrates investor confidence in Anthropic's business model and technological capabilities. The company's focus on enterprise applications, combined with its public commitment to safety-conscious AI development, appears to have resonated with major capital providers. As competition intensifies among AI companies seeking to develop and commercialize advanced systems, Anthropic's latest valuation reflects the broader trend of substantial investment flowing toward leading AI firms.

Lemieux's playing style combined physical aggression with clutch performance in critical moments. His ferocious approach sometimes drew criticism from opponents and overshadowed his considerable skills, but his ability to elevate his game during playoff runs made him one of the sport's most decorated players.

During his NHL career, Lemieux won Stanley Cups with three different franchises. He captured championships with the Montreal Canadiens, the New Jersey Devils twice, and the Colorado Avalanche. His four championship rings place him among an elite group of players who achieved this level of success across multiple organizations.

Lemieux earned the Conn Smythe Trophy, awarded to the playoff MVP, in 1995. His reputation as an agitator who thrived under pressure made him a valuable asset during championship runs, though his competitive fire sometimes created friction with other players.

The discovery of Lemieux's death came through his family. His son found him at the family's furniture store business, which had become his focus following his playing career. Beyond his time on the ice, Lemieux later worked as a player agent.

Just days before his death, Lemieux had participated in a ceremonial role at the Eastern Conference finals, carrying the torch as part of the playoff festivities. This appearance stood in sharp contrast to the tragic news that followed shortly after.

The hockey community has expressed shock and sadness at Lemieux's passing. His contributions to the sport spanned more than two decades across multiple teams, establishing him as a respected and accomplished figure throughout the league.

Lemieux entered professional hockey with a distinctive style that made him memorable to fans and opponents alike. His nicknames reflected his personality and approach to the game, capturing both his intensity and his willingness to engage in confrontation. Yet beneath the physical play lay a player who understood how to perform when championships were on the line.

The loss of Lemieux represents a significant moment for the NHL community. His four Stanley Cup victories, achieved across different eras and teams, demonstrated a career of sustained excellence at hockey's highest level. The circumstances of his death have prompted reflection within the sports world about mental health and the pressures faced by athletes both during and after their playing careers.
