The United States carried out strikes against Iranian radar installations as military tensions intensified around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments. Iran and the US both confirmed they conducted fresh strikes in the region, while Kuwait condemned what it described as repeated Iranian missile and drone attacks on its territory.

The military exchange marks a significant escalation in the ongoing conflict between the two nations in one of the world's most strategically important maritime corridors. The Strait of Hormuz serves as a chokepoint for roughly one-fifth of global oil supply, making any military activity in the area a potential threat to energy markets and international shipping.

Kuwait's public condemnation of Iranian attacks signals growing concern among Gulf nations about the expanding scope of the confrontation. The country reported being targeted by both missiles and drones in what it characterized as a pattern of aggression. Kuwait has historically maintained a delicate diplomatic balance in the region, making its public criticism of Iran particularly noteworthy.

The US targeting of radar sites appears designed to degrade Iran's ability to track and target vessels or aircraft moving through the strait. Such installations are critical components of air defense and anti-ship missile systems. Iran has previously threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz during periods of heightened tension with Washington, though it has not attempted to do so.

The latest military actions come at a time when diplomatic efforts between the two countries face significant obstacles. The confrontation raises questions about freedom of navigation in international waters and the potential for miscalculation that could draw additional nations into the conflict. Regional allies of both nations are monitoring the situation closely as the strikes represent the most direct military engagement between US and Iranian forces in the area in recent memory.

Berkshire Hathaway has agreed to buy homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $6.8 billion in cash, marking the first significant acquisition by new CEO Greg Abel. The deal values Taylor Morrison at approximately $8.5 billion when including assumed debt.

This transaction represents a notable shift in strategy for Berkshire, which has spent recent years accumulating cash rather than making major purchases. The company is deploying roughly 2 percent of its $397 billion cash reserves to enter the residential construction market at substantial scale.

Abel, 62, took over as chief executive following Warren Buffett's announcement that he will retire at the end of 2025. Buffett, who has led Berkshire since 1965, revealed his retirement plans at the company's annual shareholder meeting in Omaha, Nebraska. He designated Abel as his successor, saying he believes "the prospects of Berkshire will be better under Greg's management than mine." Abel was unaware of the announcement until Buffett made it public.

Abel has served as Berkshire's vice chair and currently oversees most of the company's investments. He has been the designated successor since 2001 and is a 25-year veteran of the conglomerate. Before becoming CEO, he managed all of Berkshire's non-insurance businesses, including fossil energy, chemical, real estate, and retail operations.

Taylor Morrison operates as one of the larger homebuilders in the United States, constructing residential properties across multiple markets. The acquisition expands Berkshire's existing presence in residential real estate. The company already owns Clayton Homes, which manufactures modular and manufactured housing. The Taylor Morrison purchase moves Berkshire into traditional site-built home construction.

The acquisition signals confidence in the housing market's long-term prospects, despite challenges facing the industry. Builders have contended with elevated mortgage rates and rising construction costs. Abel's decision to spend a significant portion of Berkshire's cash pile indicates the company sees value in homebuilding at current valuations.

This deal ends a prolonged period of acquisition inactivity for Berkshire, which has focused primarily on stock buybacks and cash accumulation in recent years. The transaction is expected to close following regulatory approvals and standard closing conditions.

At 94 years old, Buffett remains deeply involved in company operations and strategic thinking. He has stated he has "no intention, zero, of selling one share of Berkshire Hathaway" and plans to donate 99.5 percent of his wealth to charity when he dies. The Taylor Morrison acquisition represents the kind of large-scale opportunity Berkshire has been seeking to deploy its substantial cash reserves.

CERN detects particle behavior that defies current physics theory

Yesterday, 11:32 PM ET

Physicists at CERN's Large Hadron Collider observed a particle behaving in a way that contradicts existing theoretical predictions, marking the strongest indication yet of physics beyond the Standard Model. The discovery, made at the world's largest particle accelerator, represents a significant anomaly in how particles are expected to behave according to current understanding.

The Standard Model has been the foundation of particle physics for decades, describing the fundamental particles and forces that govern the universe. While the model has been remarkably successful at predicting particle behavior, physicists have long suspected it is incomplete. The model does not account for dark matter, dark energy, or gravity at the quantum level, leading researchers to search for evidence of new physics.

The observed anomaly at CERN reached high statistical significance, meaning the unusual behavior is unlikely to be a random fluctuation in the data. When particles collide at extreme energies inside the Large Hadron Collider, physicists measure how the resulting particles decay and interact. The exotic particle detected in these experiments deviated from predicted patterns in ways that cannot be explained by known physics.

CERN has not yet disclosed the specific particle involved or the precise nature of the anomaly. The Large Hadron Collider, located beneath the border of France and Switzerland, accelerates protons to nearly the speed of light before colliding them. These collisions recreate conditions similar to those that existed fractions of a second after the Big Bang, allowing scientists to study fundamental particles and forces.

If confirmed through additional experiments, this discovery could require physicists to expand or revise the Standard Model. Such a breakthrough would represent one of the most significant advances in particle physics since the discovery of the Higgs boson at CERN in 2012. Researchers will continue analyzing data and conducting further tests to verify the finding and determine what new physics might explain the observed behavior.

The Trump administration says the existing rules have led to discrimination against white people. The EEOC enforces federal laws prohibiting em...

5:00 AM ET

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission is seeking to overturn employment discrimination rules established decades ago, marking a significant shift in how the federal government approaches civil rights enforcement in the workplace. The Trump administration says the existing rules have led to discrimination against white people.

The EEOC enforces federal laws prohibiting employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, and genetic information. The agency's current effort targets regulations created to tackle workplace discrimination, though the administration frames the changes as necessary to address what it characterizes as reverse discrimination.

The move represents one of several actions by the commission to reshape civil rights enforcement. Critics argue the changes could weaken protections that have been in place for workers facing discrimination, while supporters of the changes contend the existing framework has created unfair outcomes.

The EEOC's actions come as the Trump administration pursues broader changes to civil rights policy across multiple federal agencies. The commission operates as an independent federal agency, though its leadership is appointed by the president and confirmed by the Senate.

The proposed changes would mark a departure from longstanding practices in how the federal government investigates and remedies workplace discrimination complaints. The full scope of which specific rules the EEOC plans to overturn and the timeline for implementing changes remains under development as the agency proceeds with its regulatory review process.

Berkshire Hathaway buys homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $6.8 billion

6:00 AM ET

Berkshire Hathaway has agreed to buy homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $6.8 billion in cash, marking the first significant acquisition by new CEO Greg Abel. The deal values Taylor Morrison at approximately $8.5 billion when including assumed debt.

This transaction represents a notable shift in strategy for Berkshire, which has spent recent years accumulating cash rather than making major purchases. The company is deploying roughly 2 percent of its $397 billion cash reserves to enter the residential construction market at substantial scale.

Abel, 62, took over as chief executive following Warren Buffett's announcement that he will retire at the end of 2025. Buffett, who has led Berkshire since 1965, revealed his retirement plans at the company's annual shareholder meeting in Omaha, Nebraska. He designated Abel as his successor, saying he believes "the prospects of Berkshire will be better under Greg's management than mine." Abel was unaware of the announcement until Buffett made it public.

Abel has served as Berkshire's vice chair and currently oversees most of the company's investments. He has been the designated successor since 2001 and is a 25-year veteran of the conglomerate. Before becoming CEO, he managed all of Berkshire's non-insurance businesses, including fossil energy, chemical, real estate, and retail operations.

Taylor Morrison operates as one of the larger homebuilders in the United States, constructing residential properties across multiple markets. The acquisition expands Berkshire's existing presence in residential real estate. The company already owns Clayton Homes, which manufactures modular and manufactured housing. The Taylor Morrison purchase moves Berkshire into traditional site-built home construction.

The acquisition signals confidence in the housing market's long-term prospects, despite challenges facing the industry. Builders have contended with elevated mortgage rates and rising construction costs. Abel's decision to spend a significant portion of Berkshire's cash pile indicates the company sees value in homebuilding at current valuations.

This deal ends a prolonged period of acquisition inactivity for Berkshire, which has focused primarily on stock buybacks and cash accumulation in recent years. The transaction is expected to close following regulatory approvals and standard closing conditions.

At 94 years old, Buffett remains deeply involved in company operations and strategic thinking. He has stated he has "no intention, zero, of selling one share of Berkshire Hathaway" and plans to donate 99.5 percent of his wealth to charity when he dies. The Taylor Morrison acquisition represents the kind of large-scale opportunity Berkshire has been seeking to deploy its substantial cash reserves.
