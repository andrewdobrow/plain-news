A few minutes ago

The U.S. military carried out strikes on military targets in southern Iran, marking the second such attack in three days as a fragile cease-fire remains under threat.

The strikes targeted a military site in Bandar Abbas, a strategic port city in southern Iran, according to U.S. military officials. American forces also shot down four Iranian one-way attack drones and hit a drone ground-control station. A U.S. official said the attacks were conducted in self-defense.

Bandar Abbas sits along the Strait of Hormuz, a vital waterway for global oil shipments. The renewed military action sent oil prices higher as markets responded to fears of disruption in the strategic passage.

The strikes come as diplomatic efforts to establish a cease-fire hang in the balance. The U.S. and Iran have been engaged in negotiations, though the latest military exchange threatens to derail those talks. President Trump said Iran was "negotiating on fumes," suggesting the country's bargaining position has weakened.

The four attack drones destroyed by U.S. forces were launched by Iran before being intercepted. Officials characterized the drones and the ground-control station as imminent threats that justified the defensive strikes.

This marks an escalation in military tensions between the two countries over recent days. The previous U.S. strikes occurred just three days earlier, and officials have indicated that remaining military targets in Iran present more challenging and complicated scenarios for potential future action.

The cease-fire, described as fragile by multiple sources, now faces increased uncertainty following this second round of strikes. The military actions occurred even as diplomatic channels remained open between Washington and Tehran.

The situation in the Strait of Hormuz remains a key concern for international observers. Any prolonged disruption to shipping through the narrow passage could have significant implications for global energy markets and supply chains that depend on Middle Eastern oil exports.

The appearance requirement puts direct pressure on the administration to demonstrate concrete progress on refunds. Courts have broad authority to enforce their orders, including through contempt findings or additional remedial measures if compliance remains lacking.

The customs chief will need to provide the court with detailed information about refund status, including how many claims have been processed, how much money has been returned, and what obstacles have slowed the process. The hearing represents a significant escalation in judicial oversight of the refund effort.

8:45 PM ET

Federal authorities charged a Google engineer with insider trading after he made $1.2 million betting on Polymarket. The engineer placed wagers totaling over $2.7 million on outcomes related to Google's 2025 Year in Search campaign, according to the complaint.

The charges involve bets the engineer placed using knowledge he gained through his position at the company. Polymarket is a prediction market platform where users bet on the outcomes of real-world events. The platform has grown in popularity for wagering on everything from election results to corporate announcements.

The case marks a new frontier in insider trading enforcement, extending traditional securities law to prediction markets. Prosecutors allege the engineer used confidential information about Google's annual Year in Search campaign to place profitable bets on related outcomes before the information became public.

The Year in Search campaign is Google's annual roundup of the most popular search queries and trends. The timing and content of the campaign could provide betting advantages to someone with advance knowledge of the results or announcement details.

The engineer risked a total of $2.7 million across multiple wagers, ultimately profiting $1.2 million from the trades. The complaint does not specify how many individual bets were placed or over what time period.

Insider trading laws traditionally apply to securities like stocks and bonds, but prosecutors have increasingly applied them to other markets where material nonpublic information could provide an unfair advantage. Prediction markets have faced regulatory scrutiny over whether they constitute illegal gambling or unregulated securities trading.

Polymarket operates using cryptocurrency and has attracted millions in trading volume on high-profile events. The platform faced regulatory action from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission in 2022 but continued operating with restrictions on U.S. users.

The charges could set precedent for how insider trading laws apply to prediction markets and what types of corporate information are protected. Google has not commented on the case or the engineer's employment status.

Crystal Palace secured their first major European trophy by defeating Rayo Vallecano 1-0 in the Conference League final in Leipzig. Jean-Philippe Mateta scored the decisive goal in the second half, delivering Oliver Glasner a perfect send-off in his last match as manager.

The victory proved especially significant for Palace, who had been denied a place in this season's Europa League despite winning the FA Cup last year. UEFA had ruled that John Textor held a controlling interest in both Palace and Lyon, breaching multi-club ownership regulations. By winning the Conference League, Palace have secured the Europa League spot that should have been theirs.

Glasner's departure marks the end of a remarkable 12-month period in which he has won three trophies with the club. Along with the Conference League title, he guided Palace to both the FA Cup and Community Shield. The Austrian also won the Europa League with Eintracht Frankfurt in 2022, cementing his status as one of the club's greatest managers.

Before the final, Glasner addressed his players after they held a farewell party for him. He explained his hope that winning would allow them to experience European football at the level they deserved. "The best thing would be winning tomorrow because then the players would be in the Europa League next year and then we get what we should have got this year," Glasner said. He expressed his wish to watch them compete in the Europa League next season, confident they could succeed there too.

Club captain Dean Henderson praised Glasner's impact, describing his departure as "a huge miss." The goalkeeper noted that the manager had strengthened the connection between the team and supporters since arriving at the club. More than 15,000 Palace supporters traveled to Leipzig to witness the historic occasion, with thousands more watching on a big screen at Selhurst Park.

Midfielder Adam Wharton was among those who trained before the match, though Glasner kept his team selection uncertain. Wharton's exclusion from England's World Cup squad by manager Thomas Tuchel had drawn attention, but he was available for selection for this crucial fixture.

Rayo Vallecano, managed by Iñigo Pérez, operated on a significantly smaller budget than Palace but competed earnestly throughout. The Spanish side, who finished eighth in La Liga, were unable to find an equalizer as Palace's defense remained solid.

Palace now face the challenge of appointing Glasner's successor. The club is hopeful of securing Andoni Iraola, the outgoing Bournemouth manager, though he has attracted interest from Milan and Bayer Leverkusen. Pierre Sage, who led Lens to second place in Ligue 1 this season, represents an alternative option.

U.S. forces then attacked the Iranian drone-launching unit on the ground in Bandar Abbas, a port city on Iran...

8:20 PM ET

The U.S. military shot down four Iranian attack drones and struck a ground control station in Bandar Abbas on Wednesday, according to U.S. Central Command. The Iranian drones had targeted a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz before American forces destroyed them. U.S. forces then attacked the Iranian drone-launching unit on the ground in Bandar Abbas, a port city on Iran's southern coast.

"These actions were measured, purely defensive, and intended to maintain the ceasefire," Central Command said in a statement about the military response.

This represents the second military clash between U.S. and Iranian forces within 48 hours. The timing complicates ongoing diplomatic efforts between the two nations. Earlier in the week, U.S. military forces also shot down an Iranian Shahed-139 drone that approached the Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier in the Arabian Sea. An F-35C fighter jet conducted that operation in self-defense to protect the carrier and its personnel, according to Navy Captain Tim Hawkins at Central Command.

In a separate incident on Tuesday, the Iranian Revolutionary Guards harassed a U.S.-flagged merchant vessel in the Strait of Hormuz. Two IRGC boats and an Iranian Mohajer drone approached the M/V Stena Imperative at high speeds and threatened to seize the tanker, Hawkins said.

The military escalations stand in sharp contrast to diplomatic movements. Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian announced that he had instructed the country's foreign minister to pursue negotiations with the United States. This marked a significant shift for the reformist leader and suggested support from Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei for talks, which the cleric had previously rejected.

A Trump envoy plans to hold talks with Iranian officials this week despite the drone incident, according to White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt. However, Iran's foreign ministry said consultations were still underway about the meeting location, with reports indicating Tehran preferred Oman over Turkey and wanted discussions limited to nuclear-related issues.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical waterway for global oil shipments, making any military activity in the region significant for international commerce. The narrow passage between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman has repeatedly served as a flashpoint for U.S.-Iran tensions.

The continued military exchanges raise questions about the viability of peace negotiations. President Trump's recent statements at a Cabinet meeting suggested he was maintaining maximum demands on Iran, differing from his weekend comments that an agreement was close to completion. Trump has also accused Iran of attempting to delay negotiations until November's midterm elections in hopes of securing better terms.

Neither side has publicly detailed specific terms under discussion or provided clear indications of how close the parties are to reaching an agreement.
