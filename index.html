U.S. officials say they are closing in on an arrangement with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, though President Trump has not yet approved the emerging framework. The agreement could extend the current cease-fire and lead to more substantial negotiations between the two countries.

The framework would allow both nations to continue discussions on nuclear issues and other matters while the strait remains open. U.S. officials described the arrangement as a potential foundation for broader talks, though they emphasized that Trump's signature remains necessary before any deal takes effect.

The Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel for global oil supplies, has been at the center of tensions during the conflict between the United States and Iran. The proposed arrangement represents the most significant diplomatic progress since the cease-fire began.

However, recent developments suggest the negotiations remain fragile. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said reopening the strait would be "impossible" while the U.S. and Israel continue what he called "flagrant" breaches of the ceasefire, including a U.S. naval blockade. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps seized two ships attempting to cross the strait, the first seizures since the conflict began. The U.S. had previously fired on and seized an Iranian cargo vessel and boarded an Iranian oil tanker.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has triggered a global economic crisis. Approximately 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied gas passed through the waterway during peacetime. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remain stranded, according to the International Maritime Organization. Countries dependent on Gulf oil have faced severe shortages of fuel and raw materials. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in additional aid for households and farmers.

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt stated that Trump was "satisfied" with the naval blockade and believed Iran was in a "very weak position." She said the U.S. was "completely strangling their economy through this blockade, they're losing 500 million a day."

Planned peace talks in Islamabad failed to materialize. A Pakistani official said the country had prepared extensively for negotiations, with the U.S. delegation led by Vice President JD Vance expected to participate. However, Iran never publicly accepted the invitation and the U.S. delegation never left Washington.

The proposed framework focuses specifically on reopening the strait while leaving more complex issues for future negotiations. The timing of any final decision remains uncertain, as both Trump and Iran's leadership must weigh domestic and strategic considerations.

Neither side appears interested in returning to full-scale conflict, according to observers, despite recent military exchanges and the ongoing blockade standoff.

US and Iran agree to tentative ceasefire extension and Strait of Hormuz opening

The deal requires approval from President Trump before it can take effect. The agreement establishes a 60-day memorandum of understanding that would create a framework for broader nuclear program negotiat...

1:52 PM ET

The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire for two months and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to White House confirmation. The deal requires approval from President Trump before it can take effect.

The agreement establishes a 60-day memorandum of understanding that would create a framework for broader nuclear program negotiations between the two nations. This follows a two-week conditional ceasefire deal agreed to on Tuesday evening, which came after last-minute diplomatic intervention by Pakistan. The ceasefire prevented military action that had been threatened before a Trump deadline.

The Strait of Hormuz represents a critical waterway for global oil shipping and has been a significant point of tension between the countries. Opening this passage could have major implications for energy markets and international trade. Reports of the potential breakthrough have already influenced global oil prices, which fell following the announcement of the extended ceasefire talks.

The opening of the waterway addresses widespread concerns about disruptions to shipping routes that transport a substantial portion of the world's oil supply. The arrangement also comes amid energy cost concerns in other countries, with energy bills set to rise for millions as the impact of the Iran conflict continues to affect global markets.

The ceasefire extension aims to maintain regional stability while both nations pursue diplomatic discussions. The memorandum provides a structured path for continued dialogue over the coming two months, giving negotiators time to work toward longer-term solutions.

However, the agreement faces complications. Iran released two different versions of a 10-point plan intended as the basis for negotiations. The Farsi version included language about "acceptance of enrichment" for Iran's nuclear program, which was absent from the English translation shared with journalists. This discrepancy created uncertainty around the ceasefire process.

Trump has characterized Iran's 10-point plan as a "workable basis on which to negotiate," suggesting openness to the diplomatic path forward.

It is important to note that Israel has confirmed its backing for the US ceasefire with Iran, though Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated that the agreement does not cover Israeli military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon.

The tentative nature of the agreement means negotiations could still face obstacles before implementation. Presidential approval represents a required step before the arrangement can take effect. Whether this ceasefire extension leads to sustained peace depends on implementation and follow-through from both governments over the coming weeks and months.
