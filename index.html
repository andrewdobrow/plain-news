The shootout loss denied Arsenal the chance to complete a domestic and European double, a feat that would have cemented their status among the greatest modern teams. Instead, they must reflect on narrowly falling short in the competition's showpiece event.

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has resulted in 65 confirmed deaths, with 246 suspected cases reported so far in Ituri province. Health officials and international organizations are expressing deep concern about the speed at which the virus is spreading.

The outbreak is concentrated mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in the neighboring city of Bunia. Ituri province, which borders Uganda and South Sudan, has presented particular challenges for disease control efforts. The region is home to mining towns where significant population movement occurs regularly, making it difficult for health workers to track and contain cases.

Uganda has confirmed its own Ebola outbreak, with a 59-year-old man dying in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC. This cross-border spread has prompted urgent international coordination efforts.

A concerning aspect of this outbreak is the specific strain involved. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed that the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola has been detected in samples from the affected areas. Unlike the more common Zaire strain, for which vaccines exist, there is currently no licensed vaccine for Bundibugyo. Only two previous outbreaks of this strain have been documented, in 2007 and 2012.

The World Health Organization has estimated the death rate at between 30 percent and 50 percent based on confirmed cases. WHO officials have called for cooperation among neighboring countries and international partners to prevent further spread. The organization's director general emphasized the importance of rapid regional coordination given the high movement of people between affected areas and neighboring countries.

The Africa CDC has convened an urgent meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies, to coordinate response efforts.

Health workers in the region face significant obstacles. The DRC's medical infrastructure has limited resources to respond to the growing number of cases. International aid organizations, including Médecins Sans Frontières, have mobilized resources to support local health teams with equipment and expertise.

Experts point to several factors that make Ebola outbreaks common in the DRC. Close human contact with animal reservoirs, likely bats or primates, combined with population movement between rural and urban areas, creates conditions favorable for the virus's spread. The tropical climate and high rainforest coverage also contribute to these regular outbreaks.

The DRC has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976, making it crucial for health authorities to work quickly to contain this latest outbreak before it expands further into neighboring communities.

SoftBank's commitment to building up AI data center capacity in France comes as demand for computing power to support artificial intelligence applications continues to grow globally.

The investment will fund construction of multiple data centers dedicated to AI workloads. These facilities require significant energy and cooling infrastructure to support the intensive computational demands of training and running large AI models.

France has actively courted major technology investments in recent years, positioning itself as a favorable destination for AI research and infrastructure within Europe. The country has offered tax incentives and regulatory support to attract companies developing AI technologies.

The scale of the planned investment underscores the massive capital requirements for building modern AI infrastructure. Data centers that support AI training and deployment need specialized hardware, including advanced chips, along with reliable power supplies and network connectivity.

The announcement adds to growing competition among countries and regions to establish themselves as AI technology leaders. The United States and China have dominated AI infrastructure development, but European nations have sought to build their own capabilities to avoid dependence on foreign technology providers.

Construction timelines and specific locations for the data centers were not disclosed in the initial announcement. The investment will unfold over multiple years as SoftBank develops the facilities to support AI computing workloads across Europe.
