10:14 PM ET

Federal prosecutors in New York charged a longtime Google employee with insider trading after he allegedly used confidential company information to make $1.2 million in bets on Polymarket, a prediction market platform.

The engineer worked at Google and allegedly accessed internal data that gave him an advantage when placing bets on the platform. Prosecutors say he violated insider trading laws by using this non-public information for personal financial gain.

Polymarket allows users to bet on the outcomes of future events, from political elections to economic indicators. The platform has grown in popularity as a way to gauge public sentiment on various topics, but it has also attracted scrutiny over potential manipulation and misuse.

The case marks a notable application of insider trading law beyond traditional stock markets. While insider trading typically involves securities like stocks and bonds, prosecutors are applying similar legal principles to prediction markets where information asymmetry can create unfair advantages.

The charges did not specify what type of internal Google data the employee allegedly accessed or how that information translated into profitable bets on Polymarket. The engineer's position at Google and the duration of the alleged trading activity were also not detailed in available information about the charges.

Google has not publicly commented on the charges against its employee. The company maintains strict policies around the use of confidential information and regularly trains employees on legal and ethical obligations regarding proprietary data.

The case could set precedent for how insider trading laws apply to emerging financial platforms and prediction markets. Legal experts have debated whether traditional securities laws should extend to betting platforms, particularly as these markets grow in size and influence.

If convicted, the engineer could face significant fines and potential prison time. Federal insider trading charges typically carry maximum sentences of 20 years in prison, though actual sentences vary widely based on the specifics of each case and cooperation with authorities.

The US military shot down four Iranian attack drones and struck a ground control station in Bandar Abbas that was preparing to launch a fifth drone, a US official said Wednesday. The incident occurred in the Strait of Hormuz when Iranian forces targeted a commercial ship with one-way attack drones.

US Central Command confirmed its forces intercepted the drones that posed a threat in the strait. Bandar Abbas is a port city on Iran's southern coast. A senior US official characterized this as the second such clash in 48 hours.

The military action took place as the US and Iran are engaged in negotiations to end their ongoing conflict. President Donald Trump addressed the situation during a Cabinet meeting Wednesday, though his remarks differed from weekend statements suggesting an agreement was near. Trump maintained what officials described as maximum demands on Iran during the talks.

The strikes represent a continuation of military tensions even as diplomatic efforts proceed. The US attacked the Iranian drone-launching unit on the ground before it could deploy additional weapons, according to the senior official who spoke on condition of anonymity.

The Strait of Hormuz serves as a critical waterway for global shipping and energy transport. Iranian drone attacks on commercial vessels in the area have occurred repeatedly during the broader conflict between the two nations. US forces maintain a regular presence in the region to protect shipping lanes and respond to threats against civilian maritime traffic.

Neither government has provided details about the substance of ongoing peace negotiations or a timeline for potential agreement. The continued military exchanges raise questions about the progress of diplomatic talks aimed at resolving the conflict.
