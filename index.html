President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, as acting director of national intelligence, replacing Tulsi Gabbard following her recent departure from the role.

Gabbard announced her resignation in a letter to the president, stating she would leave her position on June 30. She cited her husband's recent diagnosis with a rare form of bone cancer as the reason for stepping down from public service. A spokesperson for Gabbard's office denied reports that she had been forced out, calling such claims "100% false." However, Reuters reported that the White House had pressured her to resign, citing sources familiar with the matter.

Trump issued a statement on his Truth Social platform saying Gabbard "has done an incredible job, and we will miss her." He indicated that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would initially serve as acting director before announcing Pulte's appointment days later.

Gabbard's tenure had been marked by significant challenges. Despite her efforts to align herself with Trump's agenda, including pledges to root out politicization in intelligence agencies and her embrace of election denial claims, she became increasingly sidelined by the president. Trump excluded her from key national security discussions regarding Iran and Venezuela, according to people familiar with her office. She was an unconventional choice for the role from the start, lacking an intelligence background and bringing foreign policy views that sometimes diverged from Trump's positions on military intervention abroad.

Pulte's appointment to lead the nation's intelligence apparatus represents an unusual selection for a position that typically requires extensive national security or intelligence experience. As an heir to a home construction company fortune, Pulte brings no background in intelligence or national security matters to the role.

The selection has drawn immediate criticism. Senate Majority Leader John Thune stated that the country does not need a weaponized director of national intelligence. Reports indicate that Pulte has previously demonstrated a willingness to pursue individuals perceived as enemies by the president.

The director of national intelligence serves as the principal advisor to the president on intelligence matters and oversees coordination among 18 intelligence organizations, including the CIA and NSA. The position was created after the September 11 attacks to improve information sharing among agencies. The acting designation means Pulte will serve without Senate confirmation, at least temporarily.

The intelligence community has faced ongoing scrutiny regarding its independence from political influence, with concerns from both parties about whether assessments are shaped by objective analysis or partisan considerations.

Dashlane, a prominent password management service, disclosed that attackers gained unauthorized access to customer accounts and obtained encrypted password vaults. The company revealed that hackers used brute-force techniques to circumvent its two-factor authentication system, enabling them to bypass a critical security layer meant to protect accounts when passwords are compromised.

Password managers maintain encrypted copies of user passwords, credit card numbers, and other confidential data. Although these vaults are encrypted and require a master password to unlock, security researchers have cautioned that stolen vaults could potentially be cracked offline if users selected weak master passwords.

The company has not specified the number of affected customers or disclosed when the breach took place. The incident highlights ongoing concerns about the effectiveness of two-factor authentication as a defense mechanism against unauthorized access. Brute-force attacks work by systematically attempting many password or security code combinations in rapid succession until one succeeds. The specific method the attackers employed to defeat Dashlane's two-factor system remains unexplained.

Dashlane is not alone in experiencing security issues. The company joins a growing roster of password managers that have encountered breaches or security incidents in recent years. This comes at a time when millions of people depend on such services to handle dozens or hundreds of online accounts.

Security researchers continue to recommend password managers as a safer option than reusing the same password across multiple websites. However, experts advise users to activate every available security feature and select strong master passwords to minimize risk. They emphasize that while password manager breaches are concerning, the alternative of password reuse presents even greater dangers.

Dashlane has not yet announced plans to contact affected customers directly or described what measures it will implement to prevent future attacks. When password manager breaches occur, users typically need to change their master passwords and examine their stored credentials for any evidence of misuse.

The breach raises significant questions about password manager security at a time when these services have become essential tools for managing digital life. While the companies that provide them generally maintain strong security practices, incidents like this demonstrate that even services specifically designed to protect sensitive information remain vulnerable to sophisticated attacks.

Users who maintain accounts with Dashlane may want to take precautions by changing their master passwords and monitoring their online accounts for suspicious activity. Security experts recommend that anyone using a password manager ensure their master password is both lengthy and complex, combining uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters to resist brute-force attempts.

Rick Adelman, the Hall of Fame basketball coach who won more than 1,000 games during a nearly 30-year career in the NBA, has died at age 79.

Adelman ranks among the most successful coaches in NBA history, finishing his career with 1,042 wins across stints with the Portland Trail Blazers, Golden State Warriors, Sacramento Kings, Houston Rockets, and Minnesota Timberwolves. His win total places him eighth on the NBA's all-time coaching victories list. He coached from 1988 to 2014, leading teams to the playoffs in 16 of his 23 seasons at the helm.

Known for his offensive creativity and ability to maximize player talent, Adelman guided the Trail Blazers to two NBA Finals appearances in 1990 and 1992. He later transformed the Sacramento Kings into one of the most exciting teams of the early 2000s, leading them to the Western Conference Finals in 2002. His teams were characterized by fluid ball movement and high-scoring offenses that emphasized teamwork over individual dominance.

Adelman was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2021, a recognition that came seven years after his retirement from coaching. Despite never winning an NBA championship, his sustained excellence and influence on modern offensive basketball earned him widespread respect throughout the sport. He was named NBA Coach of the Year in 1989 during his second season with Portland.

Adelman's coaching philosophy influenced a generation of coaches and players who valued team-oriented play. His legacy extends beyond his win total to the style of basketball he championed, which emphasized passing, spacing, and player movement principles that remain central to the modern game.

Throughout his career, Adelman demonstrated an ability to succeed with different rosters and organizations. Whether working with established stars or rebuilding teams, he consistently produced winning basketball. His approach to the game prioritized unselfish play and efficient offensive execution, principles that shaped how teams approach basketball strategy today.

The basketball community widely recognized Adelman's contributions to the sport. His influence could be seen in how subsequent coaches and teams adopted similar offensive philosophies, focusing on ball movement and creating scoring opportunities through spacing and player positioning rather than isolation plays.

Adelman's death marks the loss of one of basketball's most influential figures. His Hall of Fame career and the enduring impact of his coaching methods ensure his place among the sport's all-time greats. The principles he established and promoted continue to shape how basketball is played at the highest levels of professional competition.

His win total places him eighth on the NBA's all-time coaching victories list. He coached from 1988 to 2014, leading teams to the playoffs in 16 of his 23 seasons at the helm.

Known for his offensive creativity and ability to maximize player talent, Adelman guided the Trail Blazers to two NBA Finals appearances in 1990 and 1992. He later transformed the Sacramento Kings into one of the most exciting teams of the early 2000s, leading them to the Western Conference Finals in 2002. His teams were characterized by fluid ball movement and high-scoring offenses that emphasized teamwork over individual dominance.

Adelman was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2021, a recognition that came seven years after his retirement from coaching. Despite never winning an NBA championship, his sustained excellence and influence on modern offensive basketball earned him widespread respect throughout the sport. He was named NBA Coach of the Year in 1989 during his second season with Portland.

Adelman's coaching philosophy influenced a generation of coaches and players who valued team-oriented play. His legacy extends beyond his win total to the style of basketball he championed, which emphasized passing, spacing, and player movement principles that remain central to the modern game.

Throughout his career, Adelman demonstrated an ability to succeed with different rosters and organizations. Whether working with established stars or rebuilding teams, he consistently produced winning basketball. His approach to the game prioritized unselfish play and efficient offensive execution, principles that shaped how teams approach basketball strategy today.

The basketball community widely recognized Adelman's contributions to the sport. His influence could be seen in how subsequent coaches and teams adopted similar offensive philosophies, focusing on ball movement and creating scoring opportunities through spacing and player positioning rather than isolation plays.

Adelman's death marks the loss of one of basketball's most influential figures. His Hall of Fame career and the enduring impact of his coaching methods ensure his place among the sport's all-time greats. The principles he established and promoted continue to shape how basketball is played at the highest levels of professional competition.
