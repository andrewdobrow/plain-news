A federal judge has ordered that President Trump's name be removed from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts and blocked the venue from closing for planned renovations.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper ruled Friday that the performing arts center's board exceeded its authority when it voted to rename the institution. In his 94-page decision, Cooper wrote that it was "crystal clear" the arts complex has been named for the late President John F. Kennedy since 1964, when Congress officially redesignated it as the "John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts."

The ruling sided with Rep. Joyce Beatty, a Democrat from Ohio and an ex officio member of the center's board, who challenged the renaming. Cooper concluded the board lacked the statutory power to make such a change without broader authorization.

"The Court has concluded that the Board overstepped its statutory bounds by unilaterally renaming the Kennedy Center after President Trump," Cooper wrote in his decision.

The order requires the Trump administration to remove all signage bearing Trump's name from the building and to eliminate any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials within 14 days. The decision takes effect immediately.

The ruling also blocks the Kennedy Center's planned closure later this year. The venue was scheduled to shut down so extensive renovations could begin, with programming expected to wind down over a two-year construction period.

The decision represents a significant setback to the administration's renovation plans for the performing arts venue. Trump had pushed for the changes as part of his broader vision for the facility.

The Kennedy Center, located in Washington, D.C., has functioned as the nation's cultural center since its opening. The building was designated as a living memorial to President Kennedy, who championed the arts during his administration.

Beatty brought the legal challenge in her capacity as a board member, arguing that renaming the institution violated the center's founding charter and federal law. The judge agreed with this assessment.

The order prevents any further steps toward closing the facility or advancing renovation plans under the current proposal. However, the ruling does not prevent Congress from potentially authorizing the renovation project or approving a different approach to the facility's future.

The decision comes after months of litigation surrounding the center's status and the administration's authority to make changes to the institution. Judge Cooper had previously signaled concerns about the board's actions during earlier proceedings in the case.

Romanian officials determined that the drone was likely struck by Ukrainian air defenses over Ukraine, which altered its trajectory before it entered Romanian airspace. The impact hit the roof of the residential building in Galati, which sits on the Danube River west of the borders with Moldova and Ukraine.

The incident prompted swift action from Romania's government. She described the incident as a "grave and irresponsible escalation" and a serious breach of international law.

Romania requested that NATO accelerate the transfer of anti-drone capabilities to the country. The government also indicated it would invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty, which triggers formal discussions within the alliance about threats to a member's security.

General Gheorghe Maxim, a stand-in commander for Romania's armed forces, clarified that the strike was not "an attack from Russia against Romania," but emphasized that Romanians should understand Russia poses a threat to regional security.

Russia denied responsibility for the incident. President Vladimir Putin suggested the drone wreckage should be handed over to Russia for an independent investigation, claiming he had only recently learned of the incident.

The European Union and France also condemned the strike. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Russia's "war of aggression has crossed yet another line" and pledged full solidarity with Romania. France's foreign minister called it an irresponsible act by Russia.

Ukraine's foreign minister said the incident demonstrated that Russian aggression poses a threat to the entire European region. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has been pressing the United States to provide additional Patriot air defense missiles to counter Russian ballistic missile attacks, as Russia intensifies its campaign against Ukraine.

The Romanian military scrambled two F-16 fighter jets and a helicopter in response to the incident.

Oil prices dropped sharply as investors grew increasingly optimistic about a potential cease-fire extension between the United States and Iran. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, fell 6 percent to $97.43 a barrel on Monday, marking the lowest level in two weeks as traders responded to signs that a peace deal could soon be finalized.

The decline reflects market expectations that resolving tensions in the Middle East could stabilize energy supplies and ease inflation concerns. However, significant obstacles remain. While negotiators have established a framework for an agreement, the two countries still disagree on critical issues, including Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, a vital waterway for global petroleum flows. An Iranian government spokesperson cautioned that an agreement was "not imminent."

Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, urged caution about the market's optimism. "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down. Therefore, the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting," he told Reuters.

Stock markets responded positively to the diplomatic developments, with Japan's Nikkei rising nearly 3 percent and the pan-European Stoxx 600 index climbing 1 percent. The movements suggest traders are cautiously balancing hopes for de-escalation against lingering uncertainty about whether any agreement will hold or what its terms might include.

Even if negotiations succeed, analysts warn that returning to normal energy flows will take considerable time. Infrastructure in Qatar and other regions suffered damage during the conflict, and repairs could take months. Current shipping data shows some progress, with two tankers carrying liquefied natural gas departing the strait on Monday and a supertanker carrying Iraqi crude leaving the Gulf after being stranded for nearly three months.

Giovanni Staunovo, a UBS analyst, emphasized that physical oil flows remain the key factor to monitor. "We continue to believe that the key factors for the oil market to watch should be the physical oil flows, and so far flows through the strait remain restricted," he said.

Despite Monday's decline, oil prices remain substantially higher than before the conflict escalated, when Brent crude traded around $70 a barrel. Barclays recently forecasted an average price of $100 per barrel for the year, though analysts note risks could push prices higher if talks stall.

Beyond energy markets, the potential agreement has broader economic implications. Higher oil prices have driven global inflation concerns and shifted expectations away from interest rate cuts toward rate increases. The dollar dipped 0.3 percent against major currencies on Monday, while gold climbed 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce. Stephen Innes, an independent analyst, noted that "treasury futures rallied, gold climbed and equity futures pushed higher as investors started pricing the possibility that the world's most dangerous energy choke point may soon reopen to something resembling normal flow."

2:46 PM ET

Authorities dismantled a botnet comprising more than 17 million devices, marking one of the largest takedowns of its kind. The operation targeted infrastructure used to control the massive network of compromised computers and other internet-connected devices.

The botnet was tied to a Russia-based residential proxy network, according to reports. Residential proxy networks route internet traffic through compromised home devices, often without the knowledge of the device owners. This allows malicious actors to mask their online activities by making them appear to originate from legitimate residential IP addresses.

Botnets of this scale are typically used for various criminal activities, including distributed denial-of-service attacks, credential stuffing, web scraping, and ad fraud. The use of residential devices makes detection and prevention more challenging, as traffic appears to come from ordinary home internet connections rather than data centers or known malicious sources.

The sheer size of this botnet, with more than 17 million compromised devices, represents a significant threat to internet security. Each infected device can be remotely controlled to carry out coordinated attacks or other malicious activities. Device owners often remain unaware their equipment has been compromised until they notice performance issues or receive notifications from their internet service providers.

The dismantling operation likely involved coordination between multiple law enforcement agencies and cybersecurity organizations. Such takedowns typically require identifying and seizing command-and-control servers that manage the botnet, as well as working with internet service providers to notify affected users and help clean infected devices.

The connection to a Russia-based operation follows a pattern of large-scale cybercrime infrastructure originating from or operating through Russian networks. Residential proxy networks have become increasingly popular among cybercriminals because they provide a veneer of legitimacy to malicious traffic and are harder to block than traditional proxy services.

4:12 PM ET

Novak Djokovic's pursuit of a record 25th Grand Slam singles title came to an end in the third round of the French Open when Brazilian teenager Joao Fonseca mounted a remarkable comeback to claim victory in five sets.

Fonseca, 19, won 4-6, 4-6, 6-3, 7-5, 7-5 after nearly five hours of play on Court Philippe-Chatrier. The match lasted four hours and 53 minutes, with Fonseca refusing to surrender despite Djokovic's commanding two-set advantage. The 39-year-old Serbian champion, who holds 24 Grand Slam titles, could not maintain his early dominance on the red clay courts of Roland Garros.

The physical and mental toll of the match became evident as Djokovic struggled in the later stages. After four hours of intense competition, the champion's body showed signs of fatigue. Djokovic's two-set lead gradually unraveled as Fonseca's aggressive play and powerful serving took control of the contest. The Brazilian teenager demonstrated remarkable composure under pressure, winning three consecutive sets to complete an unexpected upset.

Fonseca's comeback ranks among the most impressive in recent Grand Slam history. This was only his second five-set match ever, yet he showed no signs of fatigue or mental weakness as the match progressed. His serving proved particularly effective in the final sets, with the teenager hitting multiple aces when it mattered most. When asked how he believed he could win from two sets down, Fonseca responded: "I didn't, really. It's my first time stepping on court with [Djokovic], he's a legend... I'm just really happy."

The teenager explained that as daylight faded during the match, the court conditions slowed down, which aided his comeback. "I wasn't thinking about [the occasion], I was just trying to hit the ball as hard as I could," Fonseca said. He also credited his powerful serving, noting: "I felt like John Isner [hitting three aces] – I had no idea I could hit them like this."

Fonseca's victory becomes only the second time in tennis history that a player has defeated Djokovic after trailing two sets to nothing. The only previous instance came against Jürgen Melzer at the 2010 French Open, when Fonseca was just three years old.

With this breakthrough win, Fonseca joins a growing generation of young players challenging tennis's established dominance. The teenager's next opponent will be either Casper Ruud or Tommy Paul in the fourth round.

Djokovic's early exit eliminates a tournament favorite and extends his wait for what would be a historic 25th major singles title, breaking his current tie with Margaret Court for the most Grand Slam singles championships in tennis history.

The botnet was tied to a Russia-based residential proxy network, according to reports. Residential proxy networks route internet traffic through compromised home devices, often without the knowledge of the device owners. This allows malicious actors to mask their online activities by making them appear to originate from legitimate residential IP addresses.

Botnets of this scale are typically used for various criminal activities, including distributed denial-of-service attacks, credential stuffing, web scraping, and ad fraud. The use of residential devices makes detection and prevention more challenging, as traffic appears to come from ordinary home internet connections rather than data centers or known malicious sources.

The sheer size of this botnet, with more than 17 million compromised devices, represents a significant threat to internet security. Each infected device can be remotely controlled to carry out coordinated attacks or other malicious activities. Device owners often remain unaware their equipment has been compromised until they notice performance issues or receive notifications from their internet service providers.

The dismantling operation likely involved coordination between multiple law enforcement agencies and cybersecurity organizations. Such takedowns typically require identifying and seizing command-and-control servers that manage the botnet, as well as working with internet service providers to notify affected users and help clean infected devices.

The connection to a Russia-based operation follows a pattern of large-scale cybercrime infrastructure originating from or operating through Russian networks. Residential proxy networks have become increasingly popular among cybercriminals because they provide a veneer of legitimacy to malicious traffic and are harder to block than traditional proxy services.
