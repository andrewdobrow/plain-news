US officials told the BBC that the framework of a ceasefire extension deal had been agreed, pending final sign-off from both governments. Trump has shared the draft agreement with Israel and other allies.

The arrangement would set the stage for extending the ceasefire and beginning more substantive negotiations between the two countries, including nuclear talks. Officials described the plan as a preliminary agreement with terms focused on the Strait of Hormuz, though sticking points remain over the waterway's reopening.

The diplomatic progress comes as the ceasefire faces tests. US Central Command stated that Iran launched what they called an egregious ceasefire violation. Kuwait said its air defenses intercepted hostile missile and drone attacks, which US officials attributed to Iranian aggression.

Neither side appears interested in returning to full conflict despite the recent exchange of strikes. The ceasefire extension framework could create space for nuclear discussions to begin, according to US officials involved in the negotiations.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical shipping lane for global oil transport. It carries between 10 and 25 percent of the world's oil and gas supplies. Its closure or restriction during the conflict has affected international energy markets and supply chains. A continued blockage could push 9 million people worldwide into food insecurity, according to World Bank predictions, while also driving unsustainable increases in oil and food prices.

The international community has mobilized around reopening the waterway. More than 40 countries participated in a virtual summit focused on diplomatic and economic measures to pressure Iran into reopening the strait. UK Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper said coordinated action was needed to counter Iran's closure of the shipping route, which is affecting nations worldwide through rising petrol prices, mortgage rates, jet fuel costs, fertilizer supplies to Africa, and gas access to Asia.

Countries have explored options including establishing a humanitarian shipping corridor to ensure fertilizer reaches vulnerable nations. A separate military discussion is scheduled to examine whether it may be possible to clear sea mines and rescue trapped ships in the waterway.

US officials emphasized that no final deal exists until both Trump and Iranian leaders approve the framework. The timing of any formal announcement remains uncertain as negotiators work through remaining disagreements on implementation details. However, reports of a potential breakthrough have already prompted global oil prices to fall.

While the current internet was built to serve human users browsing websites and streaming content, the emerging infrastructure prioritizes machine-to-machine communication as AI agents perform tasks autonomously.

Cloud providers are implementing new architectures to support AI agents that make decisions, retrieve information, and interact with services without human intervention. These agents generate different traffic patterns than traditional users, requiring infrastructure optimized for programmatic requests rather than user interface interactions.

The redesign affects core internet infrastructure including content delivery networks, load balancing systems, and data centers. Companies are adapting their systems to handle the computational demands and communication patterns specific to AI agent operations.

AI agents are moving beyond limited experiments into widespread production use across industries. Businesses are deploying agents to automate customer service, data analysis, and decision-making processes. This shift is driving infrastructure companies to prioritize machine traffic over human traffic in their planning.

The infrastructure changes represent one of the largest technical transformations of the internet since its creation. Cloud providers are making these adjustments now to prepare for a future where most internet activity originates from automated systems rather than people.

The transition affects how data centers allocate resources, how networks route traffic, and how services authenticate and respond to requests. Infrastructure companies are building new capabilities specifically designed for the requirements of AI systems operating at scale.

The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire by 60 days, according to US officials. The deal includes reopening the Strait of Hormuz and establishing a framework for negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, though final approval from both President Trump and Iran's leadership is still pending.

Vice President JD Vance said the two sides were "very close" to a deal but "not there yet." US officials told the BBC that the framework for the ceasefire extension had been agreed, pending leadership approval.

The proposed 60-day extension would provide time for broader talks on Iran's nuclear program to begin. The framework established in the agreement is intended to guide these discussions, though specific details remain undisclosed.

The Strait of Hormuz has been a critical focal point in the conflict. Iran closed the waterway in the early days of fighting, cutting access to around a fifth of the world's oil and gas supply. Reopening it under this deal would restore this vital shipping channel.

However, significant obstacles remain. Iran has demanded that the United States end its blockade of Iranian ports before resuming negotiations. Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have indicated the blockade will continue. In a meeting with his national security team, Trump decided to maintain the pressure on Iran through the ongoing blockade.

Trump announced the ceasefire extension unilaterally on his Truth Social platform, stating he had directed the military to "continue the Blockade" while remaining ready for action. This announcement drew sharp criticism from Iranian negotiators.

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and the Islamic regime's lead negotiator, accused Trump of seeking to turn negotiations into a "table of surrender." His adviser called the ceasefire extension "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike" and warned that "the time for Iran to take the initiative has come."

Senior figures in Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps expressed anger at Trump's statements, which they viewed as proclaiming victory and claiming Iran was surrendering on nuclear issues. Their displeasure led to Iran reclosing the Strait of Hormuz a day after the foreign minister had announced it open.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who has served as the principal mediator, thanked Trump and pledged to continue efforts toward "a negotiated settlement."

A first round of talks in Islamabad ended 10 days ago with no agreement on the strait's future. The closure threatens global energy markets, with the International Energy Agency head calling the combined effects of this conflict and Russia's war with Ukraine "the biggest crisis in history" for global energy supplies.

Both sides appear reluctant to return to full-scale conflict, yet deep disagreements persist over negotiating terms and preconditions for talks.

Major cloud infrastructure companies are rebuilding internet systems to handle traffic generated by artificial intelligence agents rather than humans. AWS, Cloudflare, and other providers are redesigning their cloud infrastructure as AI agents transition from experimental tools to production systems.

The shift reflects a fundamental change in how internet traffic is created and consumed. While the current internet was built to serve human users browsing websites and streaming content, the emerging infrastructure prioritizes machine-to-machine communication as AI agents perform tasks autonomously.

Cloud providers are implementing new architectures to support AI agents that make decisions, retrieve information, and interact with services without human intervention. These agents generate different traffic patterns than traditional users, requiring infrastructure optimized for programmatic requests rather than user interface interactions.

The redesign affects core internet infrastructure including content delivery networks, load balancing systems, and data centers. Companies are adapting their systems to handle the computational demands and communication patterns specific to AI agent operations.

AI agents are moving beyond limited experiments into widespread production use across industries. Businesses are deploying agents to automate customer service, data analysis, and decision-making processes. This shift is driving infrastructure companies to prioritize machine traffic over human traffic in their planning.

The infrastructure changes represent one of the largest technical transformations of the internet since its creation. Cloud providers are making these adjustments now to prepare for a future where most internet activity originates from automated systems rather than people.

The transition affects how data centers allocate resources, how networks route traffic, and how services authenticate and respond to requests. Infrastructure companies are building new capabilities specifically designed for the requirements of AI systems operating at scale.
