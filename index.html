The death toll has risen to two following a tank rupture at a paper mill in Longview, Washington, with nine workers still missing and presumed dead. The Longview fire department confirmed Wednesday that one person who was hospitalized after the Tuesday morning disaster at Nippon Dynawave Packaging Co has died. Authorities stated there is no hope of finding additional survivors.

The incident began when an industrial tank ruptured and released white liquor, a highly destructive chemical mixture used in the paper industry. The chemical spilled into a drainage ditch as recovery efforts were delayed due to concerns about the structural integrity of the damaged tank.

The tank had been holding approximately 900,000 gallons of liquid made mostly of sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide. Fire Chief Scott Goldstein said the tank still contained about 90,000 gallons of the volatile chemical. Officials said they would only work during daylight hours due to the dangers posed by the unstable tank.

"An inspection revealed the remaining product is a significantly smaller volume, allowing emergency responders to develop a plan to move forward to remove it," the fire department said. Crews must stabilize the tank before bodies of the missing workers can be recovered, as it risked collapsing further and leaking more caustic liquid.

Nippon Dynawave Packaging Co, a subsidiary of Japan-based Nippon Paper Group, employs about 1,000 people at the sprawling Longview facility. The plant produces material for tissues, printing paper, cups, plates and cartons. It sits along the Columbia River near other timber, paper and chemical businesses.

Officials said there is no threat to the community, a city of approximately 40,000 people with long ties to the paper and lumber industries. There are no issues related to air quality or drinking water in Longview, though authorities urged the public to avoid ditches and dikes while the area undergoes water testing. The Washington state department of ecology and the US Environmental Protection Agency are overseeing operations to determine environmental impacts.

Safety complaints were filed against Nippon Dynawave in March and May, though the state's labor and industries department said both were unrelated to the current situation. The company has been fined 3,400 dollars for three separate health and safety violations found by inspectors since the start of 2021.

At a community vigil Tuesday night, dozens of people gathered to pray, light candles and embrace loved ones. Crystal Moldenhauer, a Longview resident, said friends at the plant remained unaccounted for. "We're all still waiting for answers. There's families that have been torn apart, and we don't know why," she said.

2:18 PM ET

Uganda has closed its border with the Democratic Republic of Congo in response to a spreading Ebola outbreak that has reached the capital city of Kampala.

The outbreak is centered in Ituri province in eastern Congo, where health officials have reported 904 suspected cases and 119 suspected deaths as of the latest updates. Confirmed cases in Kampala now number seven, including a 59-year-old man who died in a hospital after traveling from the DRC. The arrival of cases in Uganda's densely populated capital represents a significant escalation of the public health emergency.

The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, a rare variant for which no licensed vaccine currently exists. Previous outbreaks of this strain occurred in 2007 and 2012. The DRC's national research laboratory confirmed the Ebola virus in 13 of 20 samples tested.

The situation is complicated by multiple challenging factors. Ituri province contains mining towns where people constantly move in and out, complicating disease control efforts. Additionally, ongoing armed conflict in the region is hampering response operations. The World Health Organization's director general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, described the crisis as a "catastrophic collision of disease and conflict," noting that fighting prevents health teams from reaching affected communities and delivering critical interventions.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with body fluids such as blood or vomit from infected people, or contact with dead bodies during funeral preparations. The virus is severe, with a high fatality rate.

Cases have been reported mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in neighboring Bunia. The high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries has prompted urgent coordination efforts. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has convened an emergency meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies.

Uganda's health ministry stated that the country has robust disease surveillance capabilities built from previous outbreak experiences. Health workers are tracing contacts of confirmed cases and working to identify additional infections while ensuring adequate supplies of protective equipment and treatment facilities.

The US Embassy in Kampala issued a health alert in response to the outbreak. The Trump administration is also establishing a quarantine and treatment center in Kenya for Americans affected by exposure in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

This outbreak adds to a series of public health challenges facing the region, where years of armed conflict have weakened healthcare infrastructure and displaced large populations. The DRC has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976.

3:12 PM ET

More Americans are going hungry now than during the height of the pandemic, according to a new report from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The report documents what researchers describe as a remarkable increase in households skipping meals due to rising food costs.

The findings show food insecurity has worsened significantly even as other pandemic-era economic disruptions have eased. Low-income households face the sharpest increases in what the New York Fed characterizes as a K-shaped economy, where different income groups experience diverging economic conditions.

The report arrives as consumer pessimism grows around food affordability. Rising food costs have forced more families to reduce meals or go without food entirely, reversing improvements seen as pandemic aid programs ended.

The New York Fed analysis warns that the increase in households unable to afford adequate food represents a significant shift in economic security for many Americans. The report tracks food insecurity rates that now exceed levels recorded during 2020 and 2021, when unemployment spiked and supply chains faced widespread disruption.

Researchers point to persistent inflation in food prices as a primary driver of increased hunger. While overall economic indicators have improved since the pandemic, the cost of groceries has remained elevated, creating sustained pressure on household budgets.

The data suggests that recovery from pandemic-era economic shocks has been uneven. Higher-income households have largely returned to pre-pandemic food security levels, while lower-income families face worsening conditions. This divergence illustrates broader economic inequality trends that have accelerated in recent years.

The Federal Reserve report provides new evidence that basic needs like food remain out of reach for a growing number of American households. The findings come as policymakers debate the adequacy of existing food assistance programs and whether additional intervention is needed to address rising hunger rates.

The increase in food insecurity affects millions of Americans who must now choose between meals and other essential expenses like housing, utilities, and medical care. The report indicates this trade-off has become more common as food prices have climbed faster than wages for many workers.

A Trump administration official asked Centers for Disease Control and Prevention staff to volunteer to screen travelers at airports as part of the new protocol. The policy repres...

2:52 PM ET

The Trump administration plans to prevent American citizens exposed to Ebola from returning to the United States and will instead relocate them to Kenya, according to sources familiar with the policy.

A Trump administration official asked Centers for Disease Control and Prevention staff to volunteer to screen travelers at airports as part of the new protocol. The policy represents a significant departure from previous US approaches to managing potential disease exposure among American citizens abroad.

The decision affects Americans who have been exposed to Ebola, a viral hemorrhagic fever that can be fatal. The disease spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Previous administrations have typically allowed exposed Americans to return home for monitoring and potential treatment at specialized medical facilities.

The relocation plan to Kenya raises questions about medical care access and legal authority to prevent citizens from entering their own country. Kenya has not publicly commented on hosting Americans exposed to the virus.

The CDC, which typically leads the federal response to infectious disease threats, will play a role in implementing the airport screening component of the policy. The agency has extensive experience with traveler screening programs, having deployed similar measures during previous Ebola outbreaks in West Africa.

The Trump official's request for CDC volunteers suggests the administration is moving quickly to operationalize the policy, though implementation details remain unclear. The screening would likely focus on travelers arriving from regions experiencing Ebola outbreaks.

Public health experts have previously debated the effectiveness of travel restrictions and mandatory relocation policies during disease outbreaks. Supporters argue such measures protect the domestic population from potential transmission, while critics contend they may discourage individuals from reporting exposures and can complicate medical response efforts.

The announcement comes as global health authorities continue to monitor Ebola activity in affected regions. The World Health Organization and CDC typically coordinate international responses to outbreaks, providing technical assistance and medical expertise to countries managing cases.

No details were provided about the duration of the Kenya relocation, medical monitoring protocols for relocated Americans, or the legal framework supporting the entry ban for exposed citizens.

Barcelona have agreed a £69.3 million transfer with Newcastle United to sign England forward Anthony Gordon. The deal was finalized on Wednesday night, with Gordon due to undergo a medical at Barcelona on Thursday.

Gordon was keen to join the La Liga champions and quickly agreed personal terms once Barcelona's interest became clear. Bayern Munich had been considered favorites for the winger's signature and made a late bid on Wednesday, but it was rejected. Liverpool had also previously attempted to sign Gordon but that move fell through in 2024, a period Gordon later acknowledged left him unsettled.

The agreement allows Gordon to double his wages to approximately £300,000 per week. Both clubs intend to complete all formalities by the weekend, though the transfer window does not officially open until June 15. The move should be rubber-stamped once Gordon fulfills his international obligations.

Gordon is scheduled to join the England squad in Florida to prepare for the World Cup, so the timing of the deal means he can travel as planned on Monday. The winger's contract at Newcastle runs until 2030.

Barcelona manager Hansi Flick values Gordon's versatility across the forward line. While his best position is on the left wing, Gordon proved capable of playing across the front during his time at Newcastle. This season he scored 17 goals in all competitions for the club, finishing as their top scorer. In the Champions League, Gordon registered 10 goals, making him joint-third in the competition's scoring charts behind Harry Kane and Kylian Mbappé.

However, Gordon's form proved inconsistent during Newcastle's underwhelming Premier League campaign, which saw the club finish twelfth. He sometimes showed reluctance to operate on the right and occasionally played as a center-forward. Despite this, his performances in European competition were notably strong.

At Barcelona, Gordon will face competition from established attacking options including Lamine Yamal and Raphinha on the flanks. Barcelona also retain the option of signing Manchester United's Marcus Rashford on loan permanently for £26 million.

For Newcastle, the substantial fee provides resources to rebuild following a disappointing season. Manager Eddie Howe avoided the extended transfer saga that characterized last summer's Alexander Isak situation, which only concluded on the deadline day when the striker joined Liverpool.

Gordon was absent from Newcastle's final six matches, initially cited as a minor hip injury before becoming an unused substitute in the closing fixtures. His departure marks the end of his spell at St James' Park, which began in January 2023 after joining from Everton.

Kenneth Iwamasa, Matthew Perry's live-in personal assistant, was sentenced to 41 months in federal prison on Wednesday for his role in the actor's fatal ketamine overdose in October 2023.

The 61-year-old Iwamasa pleaded guilty to distributing ketamine that resulted in death or serious bodily injury. He had injected Perry with ketamine multiple times without any medical training, including on the day the Friends actor was found dead in a hot tub at his Los Angeles home. The sentence matched what federal prosecutors had requested.

Iwamasa's sentencing concludes the criminal investigation into five people authorities say were involved in Perry's overdose death. Other defendants received varying sentences. Jasveen Sangha, a drug dealer known as the "Ketamine queen," who supplied the fatal dose, was sentenced to 15 years in prison. Erik Fleming, a drug addiction counselor who served as a middleman in the sale, faces two years behind bars. Dr. Salvador Plasencia, who continued supplying ketamine to Iwamasa even after Perry had a severe adverse reaction more than two weeks before his death, was sentenced to 30 months. Dr. Mark Chavez, who sold ketamine to Perry, avoided prison time and instead received eight months of home detention plus three years of supervised release.

Iwamasa had worked as Perry's personal assistant from 2022 to 2023 and had known the actor for over two decades. Perry, who had publicly discussed his struggles with opioid addiction, asked Iwamasa to help him obtain ketamine in fall 2023. Although Perry had been receiving ketamine infusion therapy at a California clinic to treat anxiety and depression, he sought additional doses from outside sources.

Prosecutors revealed that Iwamasa paid Dr. Plasencia at least $55,000 to purchase ketamine on several occasions between September and October 2023. Court documents show that in the three days before Perry's death, Iwamasa injected the actor with six to eight shots of ketamine daily. Authorities also found that Iwamasa had discovered Perry unresponsive at least twice that month.

Some Hollywood figures have expressed sympathy for Iwamasa, characterizing the relationship between celebrity and assistant as involving a significant power imbalance. His attorneys argued that he could not simply refuse Perry's demands.

However, Perry's family condemned Iwamasa's actions in letters to the judge. Perry's mother, Suzanne Morrison, said the family had trusted Iwamasa to help keep Perry sober. His sister, Madeline Morrison, recalled observing Iwamasa behave in an unsettled manner after Perry's death, repeatedly volunteering his version of events as if being interviewed rather than mourning a friend.

US to quarantine American Ebola patients in Kenya

The Trump administration announced plans Wednesday to establish a quarantine and treatment facility in Kenya for Americans exposed to Ebola, rather than evacuating them back to the United States. A White House official confirmed the facility would allow Americans to quickly leave the Democratic Republic of the Congo and quarantine without the risks of lengthy transport home. T...

A few minutes ago

The Trump administration announced plans Wednesday to establish a quarantine and treatment facility in Kenya for Americans exposed to Ebola, rather than evacuating them back to the United States.

A White House official confirmed the facility would allow Americans to quickly leave the Democratic Republic of the Congo and quarantine without the risks of lengthy transport home. The center will provide high-quality care for those exposed to the virus and treat Americans who contract Ebola, including critical care when needed. The official noted that individual cases would be evaluated for possible forward transport for more advanced care as appropriate.

The official did not specify whether additional transport would be to the United States or Europe, where some Americans have previously been treated. The statement also left unclear whether Americans could choose to return to the US instead of going to Kenya.

The announcement comes as the Trump administration has implemented travel restrictions related to Ebola exposure. The US has banned green card holders who recently traveled to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, and South Sudan from returning home, with other recent travelers to these countries also barred from entry.

The shift in policy has drawn criticism from public health experts. Jennifer Nuzzo, an epidemiologist and director of the Pandemic Center at Brown University School of Public Health, expressed concern about the approach. "It is shocking to me that the administration is looking to prevent Americans from coming home to receive the proven world-class care that our taxpayer-funded biocontainment and treatment units are equipped to provide," she said. "There are profound ethical concerns with this approach."

Nuzzo warned that inadequate quarantine plans could amplify virus spread and deter people from disclosing exposure, driving cases underground and increasing transmission risks.

Jeremy Konyndyk, president of Refugees International and former leader of the 2014-2015 USAID Ebola response, raised additional concerns about recruitment. He said the policy effectively tells American health workers that if they become sick while responding to the outbreak, they cannot return home.

"It disincentivizes people from going," Konyndyk said, noting that during the 2014 outbreak, officials specifically avoided travel bans because they understood such measures would undermine containment efforts.

Experts emphasize that transportation risks are extremely low for people without symptoms, and the US has extensive experience evacuating individuals exposed to or testing positive for Ebola and other contagious pathogens. The approach represents a significant departure from previous US protocols for handling potential Ebola cases among American citizens working abroad.

The 61-year-old Iwamasa pleaded guilty to distributing ketamine that resulted in death or serious bodily injury. He had injected Perry with ketamine multiple times without any medical training, including on the day the Friends actor was found dead in a hot tub at his Los Angeles home. The sentence matched what federal prosecutors had requested.

Iwamasa's sentencing concludes the criminal investigation into five people authorities say were involved in Perry's overdose death. Other defendants received varying sentences. Jasveen Sangha, a drug dealer known as the "Ketamine queen," who supplied the fatal dose, was sentenced to 15 years in prison. Erik Fleming, a drug addiction counselor who served as a middleman in the sale, faces two years behind bars. Dr. Salvador Plasencia, who continued supplying ketamine to Iwamasa even after Perry had a severe adverse reaction more than two weeks before his death, was sentenced to 30 months. Dr. Mark Chavez, who sold ketamine to Perry, avoided prison time and instead received eight months of home detention plus three years of supervised release.

Iwamasa had worked as Perry's personal assistant from 2022 to 2023 and had known the actor for over two decades. Perry, who had publicly discussed his struggles with opioid addiction, asked Iwamasa to help him obtain ketamine in fall 2023. Although Perry had been receiving ketamine infusion therapy at a California clinic to treat anxiety and depression, he sought additional doses from outside sources.

Prosecutors revealed that Iwamasa paid Dr. Plasencia at least $55,000 to purchase ketamine on several occasions between September and October 2023. Court documents show that in the three days before Perry's death, Iwamasa injected the actor with six to eight shots of ketamine daily. Authorities also found that Iwamasa had discovered Perry unresponsive at least twice that month.

Some Hollywood figures have expressed sympathy for Iwamasa, characterizing the relationship between celebrity and assistant as involving a significant power imbalance. His attorneys argued that he could not simply refuse Perry's demands.

However, Perry's family condemned Iwamasa's actions in letters to the judge. Perry's mother, Suzanne Morrison, said the family had trusted Iwamasa to help keep Perry sober. His sister, Madeline Morrison, recalled observing Iwamasa behave in an unsettled manner after Perry's death, repeatedly volunteering his version of events as if being interviewed rather than mourning a friend.

Uganda Closes Border With Congo as Ebola Outbreak Spreads

Uganda has closed its border with the Democratic Republic of Congo in response to a spreading Ebola outbreak that has reached the capital city of Kampala. The outbreak is centered in Ituri province in eastern Congo, whe...

5:00 PM ET

Uganda has closed its border with the Democratic Republic of Congo in response to a spreading Ebola outbreak that has reached the capital city of Kampala.

The outbreak is centered in Ituri province in eastern Congo, where health officials have reported 904 suspected cases and 119 suspected deaths as of the latest updates. Confirmed cases in Kampala now number seven, including a 59-year-old man who died in a hospital after traveling from the DRC. The arrival of cases in Uganda's densely populated capital represents a significant escalation of the public health emergency.

The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, a rare variant for which no licensed vaccine currently exists. Previous outbreaks of this strain occurred in 2007 and 2012. The DRC's national research laboratory confirmed the Ebola virus in 13 of 20 samples tested.

The situation is complicated by multiple challenging factors. Ituri province contains mining towns where people constantly move in and out, complicating disease control efforts. Additionally, ongoing armed conflict in the region is hampering response operations. The World Health Organization's director general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, described the crisis as a "catastrophic collision of disease and conflict," noting that fighting prevents health teams from reaching affected communities and delivering critical interventions.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with body fluids such as blood or vomit from infected people, or contact with dead bodies during funeral preparations. The virus is severe, with a high fatality rate.

Cases have been reported mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in neighboring Bunia. The high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries has prompted urgent coordination efforts. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has convened an emergency meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies.

Uganda's health ministry stated that the country has robust disease surveillance capabilities built from previous outbreak experiences. Health workers are tracing contacts of confirmed cases and working to identify additional infections while ensuring adequate supplies of protective equipment and treatment facilities.

The US Embassy in Kampala issued a health alert in response to the outbreak. The Trump administration is also establishing a quarantine and treatment center in Kenya for Americans affected by exposure in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

This outbreak adds to a series of public health challenges facing the region, where years of armed conflict have weakened healthcare infrastructure and displaced large populations. The DRC has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976.
