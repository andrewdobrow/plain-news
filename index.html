A few minutes ago

A federal judge in Miami has reopened Donald Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service, agreeing to examine a $1.8 billion settlement that the Trump administration reached with the Justice Department. The settlement established what Trump has termed an "anti-weaponization" fund.

Judge Kathleen Williams reopened the case after receiving a motion from a third party arguing that the settlement "is a product of collusion and is itself a fraud on the court." The lawsuit was originally brought by Trump and his sons following the leak of their personal and business tax returns by a former contractor.

The Trump administration has characterized the settlement as compensation for what it describes as improper government targeting. The fund's stated purpose is to address instances of what Trump calls weaponization of government agencies.

The settlement has generated significant opposition and controversy since its announcement earlier this month. The backlash has exposed divisions within the Republican Party, with some party members publicly questioning whether the agreement serves the public interest and whether the legal basis for such a large settlement is sound.

The judge's decision to reopen the case and examine whether the settlement constitutes fraud suggests judicial concern about the terms of the agreement. Legal observers note that judicial review of executive branch settlements, while not routine, can occur when questions arise about the propriety of such arrangements.

Trump originally filed the lawsuit as part of a broader dispute with the IRS over tax matters. The Justice Department then negotiated and reached the $1.8 billion settlement with the administration, which established the controversial fund.

The reopening of the case means active litigation will resume as the judge examines the circumstances and details surrounding the settlement agreement. The judge has ordered Trump to address what court filings describe as serious allegations in the case.

The settlement has lacked substantial detail in its public disclosures, which has contributed to concerns raised by critics and the third-party motion filed with the court. These gaps in transparency have fueled questions about how the fund will be administered and whether it constitutes an appropriate use of government resources.

The case now returns to active litigation status. However, no timeline has been announced for how long the judge's review will take or when she might issue a ruling on the fraud allegations and other matters under examination.

The situation reflects broader tensions within the Republican Party regarding executive power, government spending, and the administration's legal strategy. The dispute over the settlement's propriety has divided party members on whether such an agreement represents sound governance or an inappropriate use of taxpayer funds.

A severe Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has claimed at least 65 confirmed deaths, with health officials reporting 246 suspected cases in Ituri province. The World Health Organization estimates the death rate between 30 and 50 percent among confirmed cases, describing it as extremely high.

The outbreak is concentrated in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with additional suspected cases reported in the neighboring city of Bunia. Ituri province, which borders Uganda and South Sudan, has become the center of the crisis. The region's status as a hub for mining towns creates particular challenges, as constant movement of people through these areas complicates disease control efforts.

The strain identified in the outbreak is Bundibugyo Ebola, which differs from the more commonly seen Zaire strain. Critically, no licensed vaccine currently exists for Bundibugyo, though vaccines are available for other Ebola types. The Africa Centres for Disease Control detected the virus in 13 of 20 samples tested by the DRC's national research laboratory.

Uganda has already confirmed cases within its borders. A 59-year-old man died in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC, marking the disease's spread across international boundaries. Officials at the Africa CDC have expressed serious concern about further regional transmission given the high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries.

Medical charity Médecins Sans Frontières has described the virus spread as deeply alarming. Health workers in the region face significant obstacles, including limited equipment and resources as they attempt to contain the outbreak. The WHO chief visited the most affected areas to support containment efforts.

Ebola is a severe viral illness spread through direct contact with body fluids, including blood and vomit from infected people, as well as contact with dead bodies during funeral preparations. Symptoms include fever, severe headache, and bleeding.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976. Experts point to multiple factors contributing to these recurring crises. Close contact between humans and animal reservoirs, likely bats or primates, creates transmission opportunities. Additional risk factors include movement between rural and urban areas, tropical climate conditions, and extensive rainforest coverage.

The Africa CDC has convened an urgent meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, South Sudan, the World Health Organization, and pharmaceutical companies to coordinate response efforts. The outbreak represents a major public health crisis requiring sustained international coordination and resources to prevent further spread across the region and beyond.

Despite the sales downturn, automakers continue to generate profits, according to a NewsNation report. The industry has managed to maintain profitability even as fewer Americans can afford new vehicles, suggesting pricing strategies have compensated for lower sales volumes.

USA Today examined why over one million drivers are declining to purchase new cars. The trend has transformed new car ownership from a routine purchase into an increasingly distant prospect for many American households.

The sustained nature of the decline marks a significant shift in the automotive market. Eight consecutive months of falling sales represents the longest downturn in recent years, indicating structural changes in consumer purchasing power rather than temporary market fluctuations.

Yahoo Finance attributed the collapse partly to weakening demand for electric vehicles specifically, though the sales decline extends across all vehicle categories. The dual pressure of high vehicle prices and broader economic squeeze has left many potential buyers unable to justify new car purchases.

The researcher, who goes by Nightmare-Eclipse, published six zero-day exploits publicly. Microsoft responded by condemning what it called uncoordinated disclosures and warning of potential criminal investigation. The company has since involved law enforcement in the matter.

Zero-day vulnerabilities are software flaws unknown to vendors, making them especially dangerous since no patches exist when discovered. Industry standard practice calls for coordinated disclosure, where researchers privately notify companies of vulnerabilities and allow time for patch development before public release.

Microsoft's decision to escalate beyond typical responses has drawn criticism from portions of the cybersecurity community. Security researchers have described the situation as humiliating for Nightmare-Eclipse. In response to Microsoft's legal threats, the researcher pledged what they called a "bone shattering drop," suggesting additional vulnerability information may be released, though specifics remain unclear.

Some posts from Nightmare-Eclipse suggest they may be a disgruntled former Microsoft employee. Their public confrontation with the company highlights ongoing tension between security researchers and software vendors. Researchers sometimes argue that public disclosure pressures companies to fix problems faster, while vendors maintain that private reporting protects users by preventing exploitation during patch development.

Microsoft has not publicly specified what legal violations it believes occurred. The company's involvement of law enforcement represents a significant escalation beyond cease-and-desist letters or public statements typically used in disclosure disputes.

The six zero-day vulnerabilities affect Microsoft products, though specific details about which software was impacted and the severity of the flaws have not been disclosed. Whether Microsoft has since patched any of the disclosed vulnerabilities remains unclear.

Kevin Beaumont, a cybersecurity researcher, flagged the situation as particularly notable for how Microsoft handled the disclosure dispute. The case underscores fundamental disagreements within the technology sector about responsible vulnerability handling.

This dispute comes amid broader debates about cybersecurity responsibility. The tension between independent researchers and major software companies reflects competing priorities: vendors prioritize controlled patching processes while researchers sometimes believe public pressure accelerates fixes and protects users by raising visibility around threats.

Microsoft's approach differs markedly from how the industry typically handles these conflicts. Most companies work to resolve disclosure disputes through negotiation or legal channels without involving law enforcement, making Microsoft's decision to call authorities a notable departure from standard practice.

The outcome of this dispute could influence how technology companies respond to future uncoordinated disclosures and may reshape the relationship between independent security researchers and major vendors.

3:52 PM ET

U.S. Central Command announced on Saturday that it prevented another vessel from docking at an Iranian port as part of its ongoing naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

The military disabled a ship flying under a Gambian flag on Friday after the vessel ignored repeated warnings, according to a Centcom statement. The action represents the latest enforcement of restrictions the U.S. has placed on Iranian ports and the strategic waterway.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical shipping channel for global oil supplies. The U.S. blockade reflects the strategic importance of the waterway to international commerce and energy markets.

Centcom did not provide specific details about the extent of damage to the disabled vessel or information about the condition of its crew. The military statement only confirmed that the ship failed to comply with warnings before U.S. forces took action.

The incident occurs amid a broader confrontation in the region with multiple environmental consequences. The U.S. has sunk several Iranian vessels at the start of military operations, while Iran has used drones and missiles to strike container ships and oil tankers in an effort to police its own blockade of the strait.

One significant casualty has been the Shahid Bagheri, an Iranian Navy vessel described as a converted container ship equipped with a short runway for launching drones. The ship was struck by U.S. warplanes on March 6 and subsequently grounded in shallow waters in the Khuran strait. The vessel has been leaking heavy fuel oil in Iranian territorial waters, creating an environmental hazard.

Satellite analysis indicates the oil slick has traveled westward toward the Hara biosphere reserve, the largest mangrove forest on the Gulf shoreline. As of March 18, the oil had moved approximately 16 miles southwest toward the ecologically sensitive area. Rainfall on March 27 appeared to accelerate the spill's movement further.

The potential impact on the Hara region could be significant. The area serves as critical habitat for migrating birds, critically endangered turtles, and numerous species of fish and crustaceans. Coastal fishing communities in the region depend almost entirely on the sea for their livelihoods.

The environmental damage extends beyond this single spill. Multiple incidents have occurred in the Gulf, including three small spills off the coasts of Iraq and Kuwait and another in the strait from a sunken container ship. A U.S. torpedo attack on the Iranian navy ship Dena off Sri Lanka also caused a spill that was handled by Sri Lankan authorities.

Environmental analysts warn the situation could deteriorate. With ongoing military strikes targeting ships in the region, the risk of more significant environmental disasters remains high.
