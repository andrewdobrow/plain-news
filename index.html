President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, a housing official, to serve as acting director of national intelligence, replacing Tulsi Gabbard in the role. Multiple news organizations including CNBC, CNN, NBC News, Axios, and Bloomberg confirmed the appointment.

The director of national intelligence oversees 18 intelligence agencies including the CIA, NSA, and FBI. This position was established after the September 11 attacks to coordinate intelligence gathering and sharing across federal agencies. The director serves as the principal intelligence advisor to the president and the National Security Council.

Pulte's appointment marks another shift in Trump's national security team. Gabbard, a former Democratic congresswoman from Hawaii who later joined the Republican Party, had been serving in the intelligence leadership role. The reasons for her departure were not specified in available reports.

The acting designation allows Pulte to serve in the position temporarily without requiring Senate confirmation. Permanent directors of national intelligence must be approved by the Senate. Trump has frequently used acting appointments to staff key positions, a strategy that enables faster personnel changes but can raise questions about long-term leadership stability in sensitive national security roles.

According to the reports, Pulte has been characterized as a top housing official and regulatory figure. His background in housing administration represents a different career path than is typical for intelligence leadership roles. Some outlets described him as a "controversial" pick for the position, suggesting his appointment may draw scrutiny from lawmakers and analysts.

The transition in the intelligence directorship comes as Trump continues reorganizing his administration following his election. The use of acting appointments has been a consistent feature of his staffing approach, allowing him to place individuals in leadership positions quickly while avoiding the Senate confirmation process that can be time-consuming and contentious.

Gabbard's departure from the intelligence directorship follows her previous role as director of the Defense Intelligence Agency during Trump's first term. Her transition to a different position or her reasons for leaving the intelligence directorship have not been detailed in the available reports.

The appointment of Pulte to oversee the nation's intelligence agencies represents a significant personnel change in an area of government with substantial national security implications. As the intelligence community continues to address evolving threats and challenges, questions may arise about the experience and background of leadership overseeing 18 separate intelligence agencies and coordinating their operations across the government.

Russian forces launched a massive bombardment against Ukraine, killing at least 18 people and injuring dozens more across multiple cities. The assault involved 73 missiles and 656 drones, including eight hypersonic Tsirkon missiles, according to Ukraine's air force. The primary targets were Kyiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Poltava, and Kharkiv.

The attack came after Russia spent a week issuing repeated warnings of a major offensive. This delay between threats and actual strikes appeared designed to impose psychological pressure on Ukrainian civilians. Residents sheltered in basements, corridors, and metro stations as loud explosions rocked cities and black smoke filled the air.

In Kyiv, six people died and 66 were injured, including three children. A missile struck a 24-story apartment building, causing it to collapse. Other buildings caught fire, including a nine-story block. In one district, cars burned from falling missile debris, and fires broke out near a kindergarten. Power was cut for 140,000 residents, though utility workers later restored electricity to 110,000 people.

The destruction was particularly severe in Dnipro, where at least 12 people were killed and 37 injured. A four-story residential building collapsed, burying a three-year-old boy. Six people remained missing under the rubble, and a rescue worker was killed in a secondary strike. The city's mayor accused Russia of deliberately using cluster munitions in populated areas to maximize civilian casualties, posting images of shattered roads and damaged homes.

Ukraine's air defenses intercepted roughly half of the incoming missiles, but more than 30 struck civilian targets. Kharkiv saw at least 14 injured, with residential homes and vehicles damaged across the city.

The bombardment prompted President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to appeal directly to Donald Trump for Patriot missile systems. Zelenskyy emphasized that Ukraine is running out of Patriot interceptors, which are the only air defense systems capable of stopping fast-moving ballistic missiles. He described Patriots as a "vital tool" for saving lives and noted that ballistic missiles represent Moscow's "last major advantage on the battlefield." The Trump administration has not yet responded to these requests.

Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, characterized the strikes as evidence that Russia is losing. He stated that Moscow has no remaining options except terror and that battlefield losses mean no amount of missiles can change the outcome. He called Putin "a war criminal and loser."

The attacks underscore the intensity of the conflict as both sides face mounting pressure. Ukraine has recently waged an increasingly successful aerial campaign with long-range drones, striking Russian oil refineries, ports, and supply corridors. The Russian strikes appear aimed at strengthening Moscow's negotiating position as international pressure builds for talks to end the war.

A few minutes ago

The Trump administration announced a proposal to impose a 25% tariff on imports from Brazil, citing what it describes as unfair trade practices. The tariff plan represents the administration's latest attempt to advance its trade agenda through Section 301 investigations, a mechanism that allows the U.S. government to investigate and respond to foreign trade practices it considers harmful to American interests.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 gives the U.S. Trade Representative authority to investigate complaints about foreign trade barriers and unfair practices. The administration has increasingly relied on these investigations to justify tariffs and other trade measures. The proposed 25% rate would apply broadly to Brazilian goods entering the United States.

Brazil ranks as a major U.S. trading partner, with significant exports including agricultural products, steel, and manufactured goods. A 25% tariff would substantially increase costs for American companies that import Brazilian materials and products, potentially affecting supply chains across multiple industries. The measure could also strain diplomatic relations between the two largest economies in the Americas.

The proposal comes as the administration works to rebuild its tariff framework after earlier trade measures faced legal challenges and diplomatic pushback. Previous tariff programs under Section 301 have targeted China and other countries, with mixed economic results. Critics of broad tariff policies argue they raise costs for American businesses and consumers, while supporters contend they protect domestic industries and provide leverage in trade negotiations.

This proposed tariff reflects a broader pattern of trade measures that have already shaped business operations across the country. Hundreds of thousands of importers paid substantial tariff fees under previous Trump administration policies, leading to significant refund claims once the Supreme Court struck down those measures. The refund process that followed demonstrated how tariff policies can create complex cascading effects throughout supply chains and business operations.

The tariff proposal will likely face a review period during which businesses and other stakeholders can submit comments. Brazil's government has not yet publicly responded to the announcement. The final implementation timeline and any potential exemptions remain to be determined as the investigation proceeds.

For American companies relying on Brazilian imports, the proposed 25% tariff represents a significant concern. Businesses that previously paid tariffs under other administration policies understand the potential financial impact of such measures. The refund processes that followed earlier tariffs illustrated both the complexities involved and the substantial amounts of money at stake for importers.

The administration's continued reliance on Section 301 investigations signals that tariff-based trade policy will remain a central component of its economic strategy. Whether this approach successfully addresses the administration's trade concerns or creates additional economic friction remains uncertain as the investigation into Brazilian trade practices proceeds.

7:08 AM ET

Nvidia and Microsoft have unveiled a new generation of Windows personal computers built around artificial intelligence capabilities. The partnership marks what Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang calls a reinvention of the PC for the age of personal AI, arriving after three years of collaboration between the two companies.

The centerpiece of the announcement is Nvidia's RTX Spark chip, a superchip combining a microprocessor and graphics processor. Developed with assistance from Taiwan's MediaTek, the chip is designed to run AI agents locally on devices rather than relying on cloud computing. This approach would allow AI agents to navigate computers autonomously, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interactions as the primary way users control their machines.

Huang described the development as reinventing the PC for the first time in 40 years. The chip will power computers from major manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus, and HP, all running Microsoft Windows. Despite the powerful capabilities, Nvidia says computers using the chip will remain thin and light.

The move represents a significant expansion for Nvidia beyond its dominant position in data center AI semiconductors. The company is also developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents and already being adopted by companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX. Together, these products demonstrate Nvidia's growing focus on PC and CPU products alongside its existing graphics card business.

Industry analysts view the launch as strategically important but potentially a longer-term opportunity. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the RTX Spark moment to major technological shifts like the iPhone and ChatGPT, predicting that agentic AI personal computers will become commonplace in homes within coming years. Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, noted that while the move reinforces Huang's vision of PCs evolving into hyperintelligent digital co-workers, investors likely view it as a longer-term growth opportunity rather than an immediate earnings driver.

The announcement has intensified competition in consumer AI chips. Intel plans to ship its own AI chip later this year with cheaper memory and cooling technology, introducing a graphics processing unit codenamed Crescent Island. The company also faces competition from Apple and Qualcomm.

When asked about concerns that AI might eliminate software engineering jobs, Huang dismissed the notion. "This is complete nonsense," he said, arguing that AI increases worker productivity and leads to more software engineers being hired rather than fewer.

The launch comes as major technology companies race to integrate AI into consumer products. The success of Nvidia and Microsoft's platform will depend on whether consumers and businesses find genuine value in running AI applications locally on their machines compared to existing cloud-based alternatives.
