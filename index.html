Sources in Tehran indicated that Iran has demanded an end to Israeli attacks on Lebanon as a condition for continuing negotiations with the U.S. This demand reflects concerns about broader regional stability beyond U.S.-Iran relations alone.

Meanwhile, the situation remains volatile on other fronts. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu indicated he has not committed to a ceasefire with Lebanon, stating that Israeli forces would continue striking Hezbollah in its stronghold. Israeli military leadership vowed to eliminate Hezbollah's presence south of the Litani River, about 30 kilometers from the Israeli border.

The U.S. has implemented a naval blockade of Iranian ports, with American warships turning back nine ships attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's military leadership responded by warning it could halt regional trade if the blockade was not lifted.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent indicated the administration was prepared to escalate financial pressure on Iran through secondary sanctions against countries and companies dealing with Iranian oil or financial assets.

Trump has consistently predicted a swift resolution to the conflict, telling reporters the war could potentially end before the ceasefire expires, either through withdrawal after inflicting significant military damage or through a formal agreement with Tehran.

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a ground test at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night, destroying the launch pad and creating a massive fireball visible from over 100 miles away. The explosion occurred at 9 p.m. ET during a scheduled static fire test, where the rocket remains on the pad while engines are ignited.

The incident represents a significant setback for Jeff Bezos' spaceflight company as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Blue Origin had just won a NASA contract on Tuesday to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to support construction of a $20 billion moon base.

The New Glenn is a heavy-lift launch vehicle designed to carry payloads into orbit and was named after John Glenn, the first American to orbit Earth. Blue Origin intends to use this rocket to launch lunar landers for NASA's Artemis program, including landers that will transport astronauts to the moon's surface.

The explosion jeopardized NASA's ambitious timeline to establish a lunar base and return humans to the moon within the next two years. NASA administrator Jared Isaacman stated that a full evaluation of the program timeline would be conducted following the incident. He emphasized that developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult and pledged to work with partners on a thorough investigation.

"Spaceflight is unforgiving," Isaacman wrote. "We will provide information on any impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs as it becomes available."

Bezos confirmed that all Blue Origin employees were safe and uninjured. In a social media post, he described it as a "very rough day" but expressed determination to rebuild and resume operations. The company described the explosion as an anomaly, though the root cause remained unknown immediately after the incident.

The blast was felt across Florida's space coast, with shockwaves reaching nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach. Residents in South Carolina, hundreds of miles away, reported seeing a glow in the sky. Flames continued burning at the launch pad more than two hours after the explosion. Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards.

This latest setback follows previous challenges for Blue Origin. The company experienced a payload failure during a recent flight when cargo ended up in the wrong orbit, leading to a temporary FAA grounding. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to resume operations.

The incident comes as Blue Origin and SpaceX compete for NASA's Artemis program contracts. Both companies are building large facilities near Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions. Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander is scheduled to be tested during Artemis III in 2027, competing against SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System to determine which will ferry astronauts to the lunar surface during Artemis IV.
