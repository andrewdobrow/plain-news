The medical charity Médecins Sans Frontières has described the spread as deeply alarming. The organization is among several aid agencies working to support health workers who lack adequate equipment to respond to the crisis.

The outbreak centers on Ituri province, a conflict-affected area that shares borders with Uganda and South Sudan. The province presents particular challenges for disease control due to its role as a mining hub where thousands of people work in close proximity daily. Uganda's health ministry confirmed the virus has already spread beyond the DRC, reporting that a 59-year-old man died in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the affected region.

The WHO estimates the death rate at between 30 and 50 percent among confirmed cases. As one WHO official stated, "It's huge. It means that up to five out of 10 people are likely to die."

A significant concern is the strain of Ebola detected in the outbreak. The DRC's national research laboratory identified the Bundibugyo strain in 13 of 20 samples tested. Unlike the more common Zaire strain, for which vaccines exist, there is no licensed vaccine for Bundibugyo. Only two previous outbreaks of this strain have been recorded, in 2007 and 2012.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids, including blood and vomit from infected people, as well as through contact with dead bodies during funeral preparations. The virus causes severe illness with high fatality rates.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention have expressed concern about further spread, particularly given the high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries. The organization has convened an urgent regional meeting with authorities from the DRC, Uganda and South Sudan, along with the WHO and pharmaceutical companies.

The DRC has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified there in 1976. According to global health experts, multiple factors contribute to these recurring outbreaks. Close contact with animal reservoirs, likely bats or primates, combined with movement between rural and urban areas, tropical climate conditions, and extensive rainforest coverage create conditions that facilitate transmission.

One Dead, One Injured in Washington Skydiving Accident

A skydiving accident in Washington state resulted in one death and one injury after two jumpers collided midair during a group jump, authorities said this week.

The Adams County Sheriff's Office responded Sunday evening to a call reporting two skydivers needing medical attention at West Plains Skydiving near Longview. Upon arrival, deputies learned that a midair collision had occurred during a scheduled group jump, leaving one skydiver dead and another injured.

The two skydivers involved were identified as Randy Hubbs of the Kennewick area and Nicole Klein of the Colville area. According to preliminary findings from the investigation, the collision happened during the descent phase of the jump.

Following the impact, Hubbs became incapacitated and lost control of his parachute canopy. Staff members at the drop zone observed and tracked Hubbs from approximately 500 feet above ground level as he drifted north away from the designated landing zone. Witnesses reported that he appeared unresponsive with his head and arms down before disappearing beyond a hill to the northeast.

Klein sustained injuries that required additional medical treatment at a facility.

West Plains Skydiving provided details about how the collision occurred. According to the company's statement, at approximately 1,000 feet above the ground, the two parachutes came into contact with neither jumper aware of the other's position.

Weather conditions were not considered a contributing factor in the incident, according to the sheriff's office. A total of 11 individuals participated in the jump operation.

Both skydivers had extensive experience. Hubbs had completed more than 800 previous jumps, while Klein had completed approximately 900 jumps.

In a statement, West Plains Skydiving expressed grief over the loss. "The Skydive West Plains community is mourning the loss of a friend who has been part of the drop zone family for nearly a decade," the company said. The statement added that the team's thoughts were with Klein and her family as she recovers from her injuries.

The Adams County Sheriff's Office said investigators were continuing to look into the circumstances surrounding the accident. In a statement released Monday, the sheriff's office offered condolences to those affected by the incident.

The investigation remains ongoing as authorities work to determine the exact sequence of events that led to the collision.

A meteor exploded over northeastern Massachusetts and southeastern New Hampshire on Saturday afternoon, creating a powerful sonic boom that was heard across a wide region, according to Nasa.

The fireball broke up just after 2pm local time at an altitude of 40 miles while traveling at 75,000 miles per hour. The explosion produced a blast equivalent to 300 tons of TNT, Nasa's deputy news chief Jennifer Dooren confirmed.

The sonic boom was detected across a vast geographic area. Witnesses reported hearing and feeling the explosion from multiple states, with reports coming from as far away as Delaware and extending north into parts of Canada. The meteor's path took it over populated areas of the northeastern United States, making it visible and audible to hundreds of people across the region.

The loud noise startled residents throughout New England who were uncertain about its cause. Many turned to social media and contacted local authorities to report the unusual phenomenon. Some described the experience as terrifying, with the sudden sound jolting people awake or interrupting their daily activities.

Meteors entering Earth's atmosphere at extremely high speeds create intense friction with air molecules, generating tremendous heat. When a meteor breaks apart in the atmosphere, it produces shock waves that reach the ground as sonic booms. These sounds can travel considerable distances depending on atmospheric conditions and the size of the meteor.

No confirmed reports of debris reaching the ground or causing damage have been released. Most meteors burn up completely in the atmosphere before any fragments can reach the surface. When fragments do survive atmospheric entry, they typically fall across remote areas where they go undiscovered.

Nasa noted that while the meteor was significant in size and speed, such events occur periodically around the world. Most space objects are small enough to disintegrate harmlessly above the planet. According to scientific research, approximately 17,000 meteorites fall to Earth annually, though most are small and undetected.

Authorities have not issued any warnings or alerts related to the meteor. The phenomenon, while startling to those who experienced it, posed no threat to public safety. The event served as a reminder that Earth regularly encounters space debris as it travels through the solar system. Nasa confirmed the first visibility of the meteor occurred above the region before it fragmented during its descent through the atmosphere.

The match went to a shootout after regulation and extra time could not separate the two sides. Arsenal defender Gabriel missed the decisive penalty kick in the shootout that determined the outcome.

For Arsenal, the loss represented a missed opportunity to complete what would have been a historic season. The club had won the Premier League title for the first time in 22 years just days before the Champions League final. Arsenal has never won the European competition and had reached the final only once before, in 2006, when they lost to Barcelona.

Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta had expressed confidence heading into the match. He emphasized that winning the Premier League had only increased the squad's hunger for more trophies. Arteta told his players they were capable of winning the Champions League after their recent domestic success.

"The ambition is bigger," Arteta said before the match. "We have one, and now we want the second one." He highlighted the team's desire and clarity of purpose, saying he was confident they would be close to winning.

Arsenal forward Bukayo Saka, who had scored in last season's semifinal loss to PSG, expressed belief in the team's chances. He noted that winning the Premier League after finishing second three times in a row had given the players confidence going into the final.

Saka also revealed that Thierry Henry, who was part of Arsenal's team that lost to Barcelona in 2006, had reached out during the week to offer encouragement. Saka said winning the Champions League would have completed a perfect season for the club.

PSG came into the final as the defending champions after winning the competition for the first time the previous year. The French club had defeated Chelsea, Liverpool, and Bayern Munich in the knockout stages on their path to Budapest.

The match marked Arsenal's 63rd game of the season, more than any other team from the top five European leagues. PSG had played 56 matches heading into the final.

The result sparked celebrations in Paris, though the festivities led to significant unrest. Paris police deployed thousands of officers and arrested more than 130 people as crowds gathered around PSG's stadium. Police used teargas to control the crowds, and footage showed brief skirmishes as supporters fired fireworks.
