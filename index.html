NASA has concluded its MAVEN mission after losing contact with the spacecraft that has orbited Mars for more than a decade. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution orbiter stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend, according to the space agency.

The spacecraft was functioning normally before passing behind Mars, but when it reappeared, NASA received no signal. "Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," NASA said in a statement. "The spacecraft and operations teams are investigating the anomaly to address the situation."

Launched in 2013, MAVEN reached Mars in 2014 and began studying the planet's upper atmosphere and its interaction with solar wind. The mission provided crucial insights into how Mars transformed from a potentially habitable world with liquid water to the cold, dry desert it is today. Scientists determined that solar wind and radiation stripped away much of the Martian atmosphere over billions of years.

The orbiter carried eight scientific instruments designed to measure the composition and structure of Mars' upper atmosphere. Its observations have contributed significantly to understanding atmospheric loss on planets, with implications for identifying habitable worlds beyond our solar system.

MAVEN also served as a communications relay for NASA's rovers on the Martian surface. The spacecraft maintained connections between Earth and NASA's two active rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance, which have made numerous scientific discoveries during their time on Mars.

The loss of MAVEN reduces NASA's orbital presence around Mars but does not eliminate it. Two other spacecraft remain operational in Martian orbit: the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey, launched in 2001. These spacecraft continue to support scientific research and rover communications.

MAVEN exceeded its original two-year mission timeline by operating successfully for more than a decade. The extended mission allowed scientists to gather substantially more data about the Martian atmosphere than initially planned, making the orbiter one of NASA's longest-serving Mars missions.

NASA has not disclosed what caused the spacecraft to stop responding or detailed any recovery attempts made before declaring the mission concluded. The space agency indicated that more information would be shared once the investigation progresses and the agency completes its assessment of what happened to MAVEN during its fateful pass behind the red planet.

Marjane Satrapi, the French-Iranian artist and graphic novelist best known for her groundbreaking memoir Persepolis, has died at age 56. Her family stated that she "died of sadness" following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away on April 8 of the previous year.

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi was raised in Tehran by her father, an engineer, and her mother, a dress designer. As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and escape the restrictions of Iran's Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France in 1994 and became a French citizen in 2006.

Satrapi gained international acclaim with Persepolis, a graphic novel memoir published in 2000. The work tells the story of a rebellious young girl navigating Iran's upheaval following the 1979 revolution and the establishment of the Islamic Republic, before being sent alone to Europe at age 14. The memoir became an international publishing phenomenon, selling millions of copies and establishing Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors globally.

In a 2020 interview, Satrapi reflected on her unlikely success, saying she initially had little expectation the work would reach publication. "With Persepolis, I didn't even think I'd find a publisher. I thought I'd make 50 photocopies for my friends to read," she told El País.

The 2007 animated film adaptation, which Satrapi co-directed with Vincent Paronnaud, became an international success. It earned Satrapi a place in Oscar history as the first woman nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Satrapi later directed five feature films, including the 2019 biopic Radioactive, starring Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie. In recent years, she returned to comics, coordinating Woman, Life, Freedom in 2024, a collaborative graphic work bringing together 17 Iranian and international artists to examine the protest movement following the death of Mahsa Amini in 2022.

Discussing her choice of medium, Satrapi once explained that drawing represented humanity's first language, preceding writing and speech. Throughout her career, she emphasized that her work aimed to reassure young Iranians they were heard and supported by the outside world.

President Emmanuel Macron paid tribute to Satrapi, describing her as "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale." Her work challenged Western assumptions about Iranian society and culture, earning widespread recognition for its ability to convey complex historical and personal experiences through illustration and narrative.

The House of Representatives voted 215 to 208 to advance a war powers resolution that would force President Trump to seek congressional approval or withdraw U.S. forces from the ongoing conflict with Iran. The measure passed with support from all Democrats and four Republicans who broke ranks with their party.

The four Republican dissidents were Thomas Massie of Kentucky, Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania, Warren Davidson of Ohio, and Tom Barrett of Michigan. The resolution now moves to the Senate for consideration.

Trump responded swiftly to the House action on Thursday, dismissing the vote as meaningless and criticizing the timing. He characterized the four Republicans who supported the measure as "bad Republicans" and questioned their patriotism, asking "who would do such an unpatriotic thing." The president stated that the vote came at a critical moment in his final negotiations with Iran and argued that the Republicans "know where the negotiations stand."

The vote represents the fourth congressional effort to constrain Trump's war powers regarding the Iran conflict. The timing is significant because the War Powers Resolution of 1973 requires presidents to seek congressional approval to continue military operations after 90 days of hostilities. The Iran war has now exceeded that threshold, though the White House has contested this requirement by citing a temporary ceasefire that took effect on April 8, despite multiple breaches by the United States, Israel, and Iran.

While the resolution is largely symbolic and faces an uncertain path in the Senate, it demonstrates rare fracturing within Republican ranks on military matters. The four Republican defections underscore growing congressional concern about the scope and duration of the Iran operation.

The House action comes as the Trump administration simultaneously pursues broader Middle East negotiations. The administration announced an agreement between Israel and Lebanon to implement a ceasefire, though the deal carries significant conditions. The ceasefire is contingent on complete cessation of fire from the Iran-aligned Hezbollah armed group and the evacuation of all its fighters from south of the Litani River in southern Lebanon.

However, the ceasefire agreement has proven fragile. Despite the announced deal, Israel carried out drone strikes in southern Lebanon on Thursday morning, striking what the military characterized as Hezbollah infrastructure near Tyre. The military acknowledged that a hospital was "affected incidentally" and accused Hezbollah of occupying the facility.

Reports indicate that three hospitals in southern Lebanon have been attacked by Israeli forces within a week, resulting in more than 150 wounded and nine deaths. Analysts and human rights experts have suggested the attacks on healthcare facilities aimed to degrade living conditions in southern Lebanon.

Marjane Satrapi, Oscar-Nominated Persepolis Creator, Dies at 56

10:00 AM ET

Marjane Satrapi, the French-Iranian artist and graphic novelist best known for her groundbreaking memoir Persepolis, has died at age 56. Her family stated that she "died of sadness" following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away on April 8 of the previous year.

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi was raised in Tehran by her father, an engineer, and her mother, a dress designer. As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and escape the restrictions of Iran's Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France in 1994 and became a French citizen in 2006.

Satrapi gained international acclaim with Persepolis, a graphic novel memoir published in 2000. The work tells the story of a rebellious young girl navigating Iran's upheaval following the 1979 revolution and the establishment of the Islamic Republic, before being sent alone to Europe at age 14. The memoir became an international publishing phenomenon, selling millions of copies and establishing Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors globally.

In a 2020 interview, Satrapi reflected on her unlikely success, saying she initially had little expectation the work would reach publication. "With Persepolis, I didn't even think I'd find a publisher. I thought I'd make 50 photocopies for my friends to read," she told El País.

The 2007 animated film adaptation, which Satrapi co-directed with Vincent Paronnaud, became an international success. It earned Satrapi a place in Oscar history as the first woman nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Satrapi later directed five feature films, including the 2019 biopic Radioactive, starring Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie. In recent years, she returned to comics, coordinating Woman, Life, Freedom in 2024, a collaborative graphic work bringing together 17 Iranian and international artists to examine the protest movement following the death of Mahsa Amini in 2022.

Discussing her choice of medium, Satrapi once explained that drawing represented humanity's first language, preceding writing and speech. Throughout her career, she emphasized that her work aimed to reassure young Iranians they were heard and supported by the outside world.

President Emmanuel Macron paid tribute to Satrapi, describing her as "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale." Her work challenged Western assumptions about Iranian society and culture, earning widespread recognition for its ability to convey complex historical and personal experiences through illustration and narrative.
