The United States and Iran have reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire for 60 days, though the deal still requires final approval from President Trump and Iranian leadership.

Trump announced the extension unilaterally on Tuesday through his Truth Social platform, stating he would hold off on military action until Iranian negotiators submitted a unified peace proposal. "Based on the fact that the Government of Iran is seriously fractured," Trump wrote, he had directed the military to continue the blockade while remaining ready to attack. He said he would extend the ceasefire until Iran's leaders submitted their proposal and discussions concluded.

Vice President JD Vance had indicated the two countries were "very close" to a deal but "not there yet," with an expected trip to Islamabad being put on hold during volatile negotiations.

The framework reportedly includes provisions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway Iran closed in the early days of conflict, cutting off roughly one-fifth of global oil and gas supplies. A draft deal also references a $300 billion investment plan, though details remain limited.

However, Iran responded coolly to Trump's announcement. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the speaker of Iran's parliament and the regime's lead negotiator, dismissed the ceasefire extension as "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike." His adviser, Mahdi Mohammadi, wrote on social media that "the losing side cannot dictate terms" and suggested military response might follow if the blockade continued.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has created serious economic pressure. Fatih Birol, head of the International Energy Agency, called the conflict's combined impact on global energy supplies "the biggest crisis in history" in energy markets.

The U.S. Navy blockade of Iranian ports remains a major obstacle to restarting talks. Iran has demanded the U.S. end the blockade before negotiations can resume, but Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent both warned the blockade would continue. Bessent stated that Iranian oil storage would soon be full and wells would shut down due to the pressure campaign.

The first round of talks in Islamabad 10 days earlier ended without agreement on the strait's future. Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who has served as principal mediator, thanked Trump for the ceasefire extension and pledged to continue efforts toward a negotiated settlement.

U.S. forces have escalated pressure on Iran by seizing Iranian cargo vessels and boarding oil tankers suspected of smuggling Iranian crude. Iran's foreign ministry called these actions "piracy at sea and state terrorism" and questioned American commitment to genuine negotiations.

Both governments must give final approval before the agreement takes effect. The 60-day window could provide time for further diplomatic efforts, though fierce disagreement among Iran's leadership over how to respond to U.S. pressure remains unresolved.

Yesterday, 11:02 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on a launch pad at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night during a static fire test. The massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad at Kennedy Space Center seconds after the scheduled "hotfire" test began at 9pm ET. The explosion was visible across Florida's space coast, with an orange sky reported as far south as Fort Pierce, 115 miles away. Shockwaves from the blast were felt in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach, and residents as far north as South Carolina reported seeing a glow in the sky.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were accounted for and safe, with no injuries reported. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" on social media. Jeff Bezos, Blue Origin's founder, tweeted that it was "a very rough day" but expressed determination to recover. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it. We'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it," he said.

The explosion represents a significant setback for Blue Origin's ambitions. Just two days before the test failure, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. The New Glenn rocket was central to these plans, designed to carry landers that would place astronauts on the lunar surface as part of the Artemis program.

The incident jeopardizes NASA's timeline for lunar exploration. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the Artemis program's timeline following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he wrote. "We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

This explosion marks the latest in a series of challenges for Blue Origin. The company was temporarily grounded by the Federal Aviation Administration last month after a payload from a New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to resume operations.

Blue Origin is competing with SpaceX for contracts to support NASA's Artemis missions, which aim to return humans to the moon for the first time since 1972. The company has invested heavily in new facilities at Cape Canaveral to support both crewed and cargo missions. The explosion underscores the difficulty of developing heavy-lift launch capability and the competitive pressure both companies face in the commercial space industry.

His regular season statistics included 379 goals and 786 points across 1,215 games. In the playoffs, where he truly distinguished himself, he recorded 80 goals and 158 points in 234 games.

Lemieux's championship wins came with three different organizations. He captured Stanley Cups with the Montreal Canadiens, won twice with the New Jersey Devils, and once with the Colorado Avalanche. His success across multiple teams during the 1990s and early 2000s made him a central figure in those organizations' dominance.

Known as a fiery winger and agitator throughout his playing days, Lemieux's competitive spirit and sharp wit created deep connections with hockey fans. He earned a reputation for his ability to elevate his game during high-pressure situations, making him a valuable asset during championship runs.

After his playing career ended, Lemieux transitioned into the business side of hockey, working as a player agent.

The hockey community mourned his passing, recognizing the significant mark he left on the sport. His contributions during an era when the Devils and Avalanche emerged as dominant franchises remain notable.
