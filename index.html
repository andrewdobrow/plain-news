President Trump announced nearly $700 million in funding for the coal industry during a White House ceremony where he received a trophy from the Washington Coal Club, an advocacy group with ties to the coal industry. Trump described the move as historic action to bring down energy prices and the cost of living for Americans.

The funding package includes $175 million from the Department of Energy allocated to six projects designed to modernize, retrofit and extend the life of coal-fired power plants serving rural and remote communities in West Virginia, Ohio, North Carolina and Kentucky. The remainder comes from wartime authority powers that Trump has directed toward securing long-term power purchase agreements between the Defense Department and coal plants.

At the ceremony, James Grech, CEO of Peabody Energy, the largest coal company in the US, presented Trump with a bronze trophy depicting a coal miner. "We stand here today representing the thousands of coal miners across the country to express our deep gratitude to you, sir, for the actions you have taken to support our industry," Grech said. More than a dozen coal executives and miners attended the event, along with several Republican lawmakers and cabinet members including EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin and Interior Secretary Doug Burgum.

Trump signed an executive order directing the Defense Department to purchase power from coal plants for military installations and other mission-critical facilities. "We're going to be buying a lot of coal through the military now," Trump said. "We're lifting up our hard-working American miners like nobody has ever done before."

This announcement represents the latest effort by the Trump administration to revive the US coal industry. In September, the White House announced it would open 13.1 million acres of public land to coal mining and provide $625 million for coal-fired power plants.

Coal has declined significantly as an energy source in the United States over recent decades. According to the Energy Information Administration, US coal production in 2023 was less than half its 2008 level, and coal plants generated only about 15 percent of US electricity in 2024, down from roughly 50 percent in 2000. Coal is considered the most polluting and costly fossil fuel.

Trump has repeatedly expressed support for coal as an energy source throughout his presidency. The award ceremony adds to a series of honors and gifts the president has received from business leaders and officials since the start of his second term, including recognition from FIFA and gifts from Swiss billionaires and Apple's CEO.

Marjane Satrapi, the French-Iranian artist and graphic novelist whose memoir Persepolis reshaped how the world understood Iran, has died at age 56. According to her family, she died of sadness following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away in April of last year.

Satrapi's groundbreaking work told the story of her own childhood and adolescence in Iran during the Islamic Revolution and its aftermath. Published in 2000, Persepolis became an international publishing phenomenon, selling millions of copies and establishing Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors in the world. The graphic memoir followed a rebellious young girl navigating the upheaval after the Shah's overthrow in 1979 and the establishment of the Islamic Republic, before being sent alone to Europe at age 14.

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi was raised in Tehran by her father, an engineer, and her mother, a dress designer. When she was a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to pursue her education, hoping to protect her from the restrictions of the Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France, arriving in 1994 and becoming a French citizen in 2006.

In interviews, Satrapi explained that her purpose in creating Persepolis was to help Western readers recognize the humanity of Iranian people. She also wanted to reassure young Iranians that they were being heard and supported by the outside world. When she first created the work, she was an arts student in Strasbourg with limited professional comics experience and never expected it would find a publisher.

The animated film adaptation of Persepolis became an international success, and Satrapi made history as the first woman nominated for the Academy Award for best animated feature. She went on to direct five feature films, including Radioactive in 2019, which starred Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie.

After stepping away from comics for years, Satrapi returned to the medium in 2024 with Woman, Life, Freedom, a collaborative graphic work that brought together 17 Iranian and international comic artists, academics, and researchers. The book examined the protest movement that emerged after the death of Mahsa Amini, a Kurdish-Iranian woman who died in custody in 2022 for allegedly failing to comply with Iran's mandatory headscarf rules.

Throughout her life, Satrapi remained a vocal opponent of Iran's clerical establishment. She believed in the power of drawing as a universal language, stating that it was humanity's first language, preceding even speech.

World leaders and cultural figures paid tribute to her legacy, with President Emmanuel Macron calling her "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale."

Helion, a fusion energy company supported by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, has secured $465 million in funding to construct a power plant for Microsoft. The company is targeting 2028 for completion, working to fulfill a commercial agreement with the technology company to supply electricity generated through fusion.

Fusion energy remains largely theoretical in commercial applications, though it has been pursued as a clean energy solution for decades. Helion's approach involves heating plasma to extreme temperatures and using magnetic fields to compress it until fusion reactions occur. Unlike traditional nuclear fission, which splits atoms and generates long-lived radioactive waste, fusion combines atoms and produces no greenhouse gas emissions or hazardous byproducts.

The investment reflects growing interest in fusion technology from major tech companies seeking reliable clean energy sources. Data centers and artificial intelligence infrastructure require enormous amounts of electricity, driving companies to explore alternatives to conventional power generation. Microsoft's commitment to purchase power from Helion represents one of the first major commercial agreements for fusion-generated electricity.

The 2028 deadline is aggressive for an industry that has experienced repeated setbacks. Helion has built several prototype machines and announced achieving key technical milestones, but has not yet demonstrated net energy gain, the point at which a fusion reaction produces more energy than is consumed to create it. Other fusion companies and government-funded projects worldwide are pursuing different technological approaches on varying timelines.

The funding will support the power plant's construction and further technology development. Altman has been instrumental in backing Helion, viewing fusion as essential infrastructure for future technological progress. The company's ability to meet its 2028 deadline will serve as a crucial test case for whether private fusion ventures can deliver on their promises to transition the technology from research laboratories to commercial power generation.

Helion operates within a broader landscape of fusion energy investment. Multiple private companies and government programs are racing to commercialize the technology, recognizing its potential to address energy demands while producing minimal environmental impact. The sector has attracted billions in funding from technology leaders and investors confident that fusion will play a central role in powering next-generation infrastructure.

Success would represent a major breakthrough for the energy industry and could reshape how data centers and other power-intensive operations obtain electricity. However, the field faces significant technical challenges, and skeptics question whether fusion can be produced at commercial scale within the timelines companies have announced. Helion's progress toward its 2028 goal will provide important evidence about whether the private fusion sector can deliver on its ambitious promises.

As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and shield her from the restrictions of the Islamic Republic. Satrapi later co-directed an animated film adaptation of Persepolis, which became an international success and made her the first woman nominated for an Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. Discussing her choice of medium, she explained in a 2012 interview that "drawing is the first language of human beings, before writing, before even talking, before words."

Satrapi described the purpose of her work as reassuring young Iranians they were heard and supported globally. She told the Guardian in 2024 that Persepolis aimed to make Western readers recognize the humanity of Iranian people, showing "they're actually human beings like us."

Tributes poured in from French political and cultural figures. President Emmanuel Macron called her "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale." Satrapi's work continues to be taught in schools and universities worldwide, introducing new generations to the complexities of life in Iran and the immigrant experience.
