This level of scrutiny indicates the judge is taking the fraud allegations seriously enough to warrant a thorough examination of the settlement agreement.

The reopening of this lawsuit and the demand for answers about the settlement adds another layer to Trump's legal challenges. The explosion occurred at 9pm ET during the scheduled hotfire test at Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 36. A massive fireball engu...

Yesterday, 6:47 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on its launch pad at Cape Canaveral in Florida during a static fire test on Thursday evening, destroying the launch facility and potentially derailing NASA's ambitious plans to return humans to the Moon.

The explosion occurred at 9pm ET during the scheduled hotfire test at Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 36. A massive fireball engulfed the launchpad, and the blast was so powerful that residents reported feeling shockwaves across Florida's space coast. An orange sky was visible as far south as Fort Pierce, roughly 115 miles away, while people in South Carolina hundreds of miles north reported seeing the glow from the explosion.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were safe and uninjured. The company described the incident as "an anomaly" but has not yet disclosed the root cause or provided a revised timeline for its programs.

The timing of the explosion creates significant challenges for NASA's Artemis program. Just one day before the test, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned lunar missions this year to begin construction of NASA's $20 billion Moon base. The New Glenn rocket is central to Blue Origin's strategy to support these missions, as it is the vehicle the company intends to use to launch landers to the Moon, including those that will carry astronauts to the lunar surface.

Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which would mark the first crewed lunar landing since 1972. Artemis III, scheduled for 2027, is set to test both Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander and SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System to determine which will transport astronauts from the Orion capsule to the lunar surface.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the timeline following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he wrote on social media. NASA pledged to work with partners to support a thorough investigation and assess impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs.

This explosion represents the latest setback for Blue Origin as it attempts to establish itself as a reliable government contractor. Last month, a payload from the New Glenn's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit, and the FAA temporarily grounded the rocket. The company had only recently been cleared to return to flight operations before Thursday's test.

The Federal Aviation Administration has not yet indicated whether the explosion will trigger another investigation. The extent of the damage to the launch facility and the findings of Blue Origin's investigation will determine how significantly the company's participation in NASA's lunar missions will be delayed.

Under current practices, peer review by subject matte...

Yesterday, 6:58 PM ET

The federal government has proposed new rules for research funding that would allow grants to be terminated at any time and make peer review optional. Political staff would gain authority to screen grant applications for forbidden topics.

The proposed changes represent a major shift in how federal research funding operates. Under current practices, peer review by subject matter experts forms the foundation of grant approval processes. The new rules would make this practice discretionary rather than required.

Political appointees would review grant applications to identify research on topics deemed off limits, though the proposal does not specify which subjects would fall under restrictions. This screening process would occur alongside or instead of traditional scientific evaluation.

The cancellation provision would give funding agencies power to end grants after they have been awarded and research has begun. Existing grant agreements typically include specific terms and timelines that provide stability for research projects, which often span multiple years.

Research institutions and scientists rely on grant funding to conduct studies, pay staff, and maintain laboratories. Federal agencies including the National Institutes of Health and National Science Foundation award billions of dollars in competitive grants each year. These funds support work ranging from basic science to applied research in medicine, technology, and other fields.

The proposal applies to future grant awards. How it would interact with existing grant agreements remains unaddressed in the available information about the rules.

The shift toward optional peer review and expanded political oversight marks a departure from long standing practices in federal research funding. Scientific organizations have traditionally advocated for peer review as a way to ensure research quality and prevent political interference in scientific decision making.

The proposed rules do not indicate when they might take effect or what process would govern grant cancellations if the changes are adopted. Federal agencies typically accept public comment on proposed rule changes before finalizing them.

Lucas was one of three editors to share an Academy Award for editing the original Star Wars film. Her work helped define the visual rhythm and pacing of George Lucas's space epic, which became one of the most influential films in cinema history.

Beyond Star Wars, Lucas edited several iconic films of the 1970s, including Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver. Her editorial contributions during this period helped establish her as one of the era's most respected film editors.

Lucas was formerly married to George Lucas, the creator and director of Star Wars. The couple divorced after the completion of the original trilogy.

Lucasfilm's statement acknowledged her contributions to the studio's history and expressed condolences following her death. The statement noted that the company was deeply saddened by the loss.

Lucas died in Rancho Mirage, a desert city in Riverside County, California, where she had been living. Her death followed an extended battle with metastatic cancer, a form of cancer that spreads from its original site to other parts of the body.

The film industry has lost one of its pioneering editors whose work on Star Wars and other films of the 1970s left a lasting impact on cinema. Her editorial vision helped create some of the most memorable sequences in American film history.

When he spoke to me, that was not something we talked about," Simmons said.

Simmons expressed uncertainty about the necessity of the conflict. "I just certainly hope the decisions being made are necessary," he said he told Hegseth.

Other family members have also questioned the war. Stephan Douglas, a cousin of one of the fallen service members, said the conflict was unnecessary. "We didn't need to be in this war," Douglas stated.
