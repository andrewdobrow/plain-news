A Russian drone struck a residential apartment building in Galati, Romania on Friday, injuring two people and triggering a fire, according to Romania's defense ministry. The incident marks a direct hit on a NATO member's territory during Russian military operations targeting Ukrainian infrastructure across the border.

Romanian authorities tracked the drone on radar as it entered Romanian airspace before crashing onto the roof of the apartment block. The impact sparked a fire that emergency responders contained. The two injured residents sustained cuts requiring medical treatment, and several other residents were evacuated from the building as a precaution.

Romania's defense ministry condemned the strike as an "irresponsible escalation" by Moscow. The country's military scrambled F-16 fighter jets in response to the incident, though the drone had already impacted the building by the time they were deployed.

The drone was part of a broader Russian overnight attack on Ukrainian targets, particularly port facilities across the Danube River that forms the border between Romania and Ukraine. These ports serve as critical infrastructure for grain exports from Ukraine. Russian forces have repeatedly targeted these facilities as part of their campaign against Ukrainian infrastructure and supply lines.

While debris from previous Russian strikes on Ukrainian positions has crossed into Romanian territory, direct hits on residential buildings represent a more serious incident. The strike underscores the risks faced by Romania as a NATO member bordering an active conflict zone.

Romania joined NATO in 2004 and has become an increasingly important member of the alliance's eastern flank since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The country borders Ukraine to the north and has hosted expanded NATO military presence over the past two years. Romania has also served as a crucial transit route for military and humanitarian aid flowing to Ukraine.

Under NATO's founding treaty, an armed attack against one member is considered an attack against all members. Article 5 of the NATO charter, which commits members to collective defense, has been invoked only once in the alliance's 75-year history, following the September 11, 2001 attacks on the United States.

The incident comes as Russian forces continue sustained attacks across Ukrainian territory. Russia has not released any statement regarding the Galati strike. Ukrainian officials have previously acknowledged incidents where Russian electronic jamming has diverted Ukrainian drones off course into neighboring NATO territory, though they have emphasized that such diversions are unintentional consequences of Russian interference rather than deliberate actions by Kyiv.

Romanian officials confirmed through military analysis that the drone responsible for the Galati strike was of Russian origin.

Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin suffered a major setback when its New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida on Thursday night. The explosion destroyed the launchpad at Kennedy Space Center seconds after the test began at 9 p.m. ET, creating a massive fireball visible from across Florida's space coast and even farther north.

The explosion jeopardizes NASA's ambitious timeline to build a lunar base and return humans to the moon. New Glenn was central to Blue Origin's role in NASA's Artemis Program, which just two days prior had selected Blue Origin to conduct the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of the space agency's $20 billion moon base. The rocket was also intended to launch landers that would carry astronauts to the lunar surface for Artemis IV, currently scheduled for 2028.

Shockwaves from the blast rippled across Florida's Atlantic seaboard, with residents reporting that homes shook in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach. The orange sky was visible in Fort Pierce, more than 115 miles away. An aerospace news organization's livestream captured the fiery plume, and residents quickly filled social media with photos and videos of the explosion. Emergency officials reported there was no threat from fumes or other hazards, though flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the explosion.

Blue Origin described the incident as an "anomaly" but has not disclosed the cause. The company's founder tweeted that all employees were accounted for, safe, and uninjured, adding that "it's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it." He said the company would "rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying."

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that a full evaluation of the program's timeline would be conducted following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he wrote, pledging to work with partners on a thorough investigation and noting that impact updates to the Artemis and moon base programs would be provided as information became available.

The explosion marks the latest setback for Blue Origin. A payload from New Glenn's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit last month, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight the previous week.

The incident intensifies Blue Origin's competition with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Both companies are building facilities at or near Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions in partnership with NASA, with Artemis III planned for 2027 to test both companies' lunar lander systems.

Vice President JD Vance said Thursday that the two sides were "very close" to finalizing an agreement, though some sticking points remained. "We are still going back and forth" on nuclear issues, Vance indicated.

The agreement would extend an already fragile ceasefire that has held for several weeks. Reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a strategically critical waterway for global oil shipments, represents a significant concession from Iran, which closed it during the conflict.

President Trump announced on Tuesday that he would extend the ceasefire unilaterally, ordering the military to maintain a blockade of Iranian ports while keeping strike capabilities ready. The extension would remain in place until Iranian leaders submitted a unified peace proposal and concluded discussions.

"Based on the fact that the Government of Iran is seriously fractured," Trump wrote on Truth Social, he asked the military to "hold our Attack on the Country of Iran until such time as their leaders and representatives can come up with a unified proposal."

Trump said the U.S. was in a strong negotiating position. The president told CNBC he did not want to extend the ceasefire, insisting the country was "going to end up with a great deal" because the U.S. maintained military superiority.

Iran's response to the ceasefire extension was dismissive. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and the Islamic regime's lead negotiator, rejected what he called Trump's attempt to turn negotiations into a "table of surrender." His adviser, Mahdi Mohammadi, characterized the extension as "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike."

The Iranian government said it would not resume negotiations while facing U.S. military threats or the naval blockade of its ports. Senior figures in the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps expressed anger at Trump's recent statements, in which he suggested the U.S. had already achieved victory on key issues including Iran's nuclear program.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who has served as principal mediator, thanked Trump and pledged to continue working toward a negotiated settlement.

A first round of talks in Islamabad ten days earlier had ended without agreement on reopening the strait. The closure has severely impacted global energy markets, cutting off roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and gas supply.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned that Iran's oil storage capacity would soon fill and its wells would shut down, suggesting continued U.S. pressure through the blockade. The administration decided to maintain the blockade to preserve American leverage in ongoing negotiations.

Emergency officials reported there was no threat from fumes or other hazards, though flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the explosion.
