The United States military struck Iranian air defense systems and a drone site over the weekend, US Central Command confirmed. The strikes came as Iran attacked a US air base and Kuwait reported missile and drone attacks on its territory. The exchange represents a new phase in military tensions between Washington and Tehran in the Persian Gulf region.

The US maintains multiple military installations in the Gulf states as part of its security arrangements with regional partners. Central Command characterized the American strikes as defensive actions aimed at disabling specific threats.

The military exchange marks an escalation in an ongoing standoff between Washington and Tehran that has included diplomatic tensions and previous military incidents in the region. Neither government has indicated plans for further military action, but the strikes represent the most direct military engagement between US and Iranian forces in recent months.

Abel has been with Berkshire for 25 years and previously oversaw the company's non-insurance operations, managing dozens of businesses across fossil energy, chemicals, real estate, and retail sectors. His appointment as CEO had been anticipated since 2001 when Buffett first designated him as the eventual successor.

The Taylor Morrison acquisition reflects Berkshire's continued investment strategy in established companies with strong market positions and predictable cash flows. The homebuilder operates across multiple states and serves key growth markets throughout the country. The purchase adds to Berkshire's existing real estate portfolio, which includes substantial investments in home furnishing retailers and building materials companies.

The housing sector presents both opportunities and challenges for major investors. The U.S. housing market faces strong buyer demand paired with limited inventory in many regions. Elevated mortgage rates create headwinds, but demographic trends including millennial household formation and ongoing migration to suburban and Sun Belt markets support long-term growth prospects. Taylor Morrison maintains significant operations in these high-growth areas.

The all-cash acquisition follows Berkshire's historical pattern of purchasing businesses that require minimal management intervention and operate independently within the conglomerate's decentralized structure. The transaction was confirmed by multiple financial news sources including Bloomberg, CNBC, and the Financial Times.

At the shareholder meeting, Buffett also addressed broader economic concerns. He warned about potential consequences of trade tariffs, stating that commerce disputes could destabilize global relationships. "There's no question that trade can be an act of war," Buffett said, expressing concern about U.S. relations with the rest of the world.

Berkshire reported record annual operating profits of $47.44 billion in 2024, up 27 percent from the previous year. The company operates 189 separate businesses across insurance, railroads, energy, and other sectors, making it a significant player across multiple industries.

Meteor Explodes Over Ohio, Creating Sonic Boom Across Multiple States

A meteor streaked across the sky over Ohio on Tuesday morning, producing a powerful sonic boom that startled residents across a wide region including Pennsylvania and New York. NASA confirmed the event, which occurred around 9am local time near the city of Medina.

The meteor entered Earth's atmosphere traveling at 45,000 miles per hour. According to NASA's meteoroid environment office, the space rock measured approximately 2 meters in diameter and weighed about 6 to 7 tons, making it a small asteroid by space standards. The object became visible above Lake Erie before fragmenting as it moved southeast across Ohio's atmosphere.

Reports of the explosion came from Cleveland and surrounding areas, with the sonic boom felt as far east as Pittsburgh and into New York state. The National Weather Service's Cleveland office confirmed through satellite data that the boom resulted from the meteor. Many Ohio residents described hearing sounds similar to fireworks that "lingered and rumbled like thunder," while others thought a tree had crashed into their roofs.

The National Weather Service in Pittsburgh released video footage showing the meteor streaking across the sky. The fragments continued moving south after the initial breakup, with NASA noting that meteorites likely fell in the vicinity of Medina County, Ohio.

As of the confirmation, the National Weather Service had received no reports of debris being found. Meteorologist Brian Mitchell from the National Weather Service told the Associated Press that while some small fragments may have survived the fall, most of the asteroid burned up during its passage through the atmosphere.

Meteors are created when meteoroids enter Earth's atmosphere at extremely high speeds and burn up, producing the visible streaks of light commonly called shooting stars. According to NASA, most space rocks smaller than a football field break apart in the atmosphere. Though rare, some can explode in the air, as occurred in this case.

NASA tracks near-Earth objects and monitors meteor events as part of its planetary defense efforts. The agency noted that approximately 17,000 meteorites fall to Earth annually. Most burn up completely during atmospheric entry, though larger objects can sometimes survive and reach the ground as meteorites.

The Tuesday morning explosion serves as a reminder of the constant stream of space debris entering Earth's atmosphere. While this particular meteor posed no danger to people on the ground, the loud booms and shaking homes across multiple states demonstrated the significant energy released when such objects enter our atmosphere at high velocity.

Yesterday, 8:31 PM ET

Jerome Powell has issued a stark warning that the Federal Reserve faces a credibility crisis in his first public comments following his departure as chair. Speaking while accepting the John F. Kennedy Profiles in Courage award on Sunday evening, Powell said the central bank is undergoing a "stress test" with its reputation on the line.

Though Powell did not explicitly name the Trump administration, his remarks clearly targeted recent efforts to challenge the Fed's independence from political pressure. These efforts include a criminal investigation into cost overruns on the Fed's building renovation project and attempts to remove Fed governor Lisa Cook from her position.

Powell framed threats to the Fed's autonomy as dangers to an institution essential to American prosperity. The Federal Reserve has maintained operational independence from the executive branch since its creation, a separation widely considered vital to effective monetary policy. When the public and financial markets lose confidence in the Fed's ability to pursue its goals without political interference, the central bank's policy tools become less effective.

Powell led the Federal Reserve through a historic period of aggressive interest rate increases designed to combat inflation following the pandemic. His tenure was marked by tensions with then-President Trump, who frequently criticized Powell's decisions. Trump has since called Powell's appointment a "really big mistake."

Powell's decision to make his first public statement at a venue focused on political courage underscores the significance he places on preserving institutional integrity. His warning reflects broader concerns among economists and former Fed officials about protecting the central bank's autonomy.

The timing is particularly relevant as Kevin Warsh was recently confirmed as Powell's successor in a 54-45 Senate vote that split largely along party lines, with the exception of Democratic Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania. This was the most divisive Fed chair confirmation in history, surpassing the 70-30 vote for Ben Bernanke in 2010.

Warsh takes over leadership on May 14, stepping into a role facing immense pressure from the Trump administration to lower interest rates. At a Senate banking committee hearing, Warsh pledged to maintain Fed independence and "take politics out of monetary policy." However, Democrats raised concerns about his alignment with Trump's policy preferences, with Senator Elizabeth Warren calling him Trump's "sock puppet."

Powell announced last month that he will remain on the Fed board as a voting governor until the White House ends its scrutiny of the renovation project. Powell characterized this scrutiny as "pretext" masking an effort to pressure the central bank into rate cuts. His decision to stay signals his commitment to defending the institution during this period of political pressure.

Meteor Explodes Over Ohio, Creating Sonic Boom Across Multiple States

A meteor streaked across the sky over Ohio on Tuesday morning, producing a powerful sonic boom that startled residents across a wide region including Pennsylvania and New York. NASA confirmed the event, which occurred around 9am local time near the city of Medina.

The meteor entered Earth's atmosphere traveling at 45,000 miles per hour. According to NASA's meteoroid environment office, the space rock measured approximately 2 meters in diameter and weighed about 6 to 7 tons, making it a small asteroid by space standards. The object became visible above Lake Erie before fragmenting as it moved southeast across Ohio's atmosphere.

Reports of the explosion came from Cleveland and surrounding areas, with the sonic boom felt as far east as Pittsburgh and into New York state. The National Weather Service's Cleveland office confirmed through satellite data that the boom resulted from the meteor. Many Ohio residents described hearing sounds similar to fireworks that "lingered and rumbled like thunder," while others thought a tree had crashed into their roofs.

The National Weather Service in Pittsburgh released video footage showing the meteor streaking across the sky. The fragments continued moving south after the initial breakup, with NASA noting that meteorites likely fell in the vicinity of Medina County, Ohio.

As of the confirmation, the National Weather Service had received no reports of debris being found. Meteorologist Brian Mitchell from the National Weather Service told the Associated Press that while some small fragments may have survived the fall, most of the asteroid burned up during its passage through the atmosphere.

Meteors are created when meteoroids enter Earth's atmosphere at extremely high speeds and burn up, producing the visible streaks of light commonly called shooting stars. According to NASA, most space rocks smaller than a football field break apart in the atmosphere. Though rare, some can explode in the air, as occurred in this case.

NASA tracks near-Earth objects and monitors meteor events as part of its planetary defense efforts. The agency noted that approximately 17,000 meteorites fall to Earth annually. Most burn up completely during atmospheric entry, though larger objects can sometimes survive and reach the ground as meteorites.

The Tuesday morning explosion serves as a reminder of the constant stream of space debris entering Earth's atmosphere. While this particular meteor posed no danger to people on the ground, the loud booms and shaking homes across multiple states demonstrated the significant energy released when such objects enter our atmosphere at high velocity.
