Israeli troops have captured Beaufort Castle, a clifftop fortress in southern Lebanon, marking the deepest military incursion into the country in more than 26 years. Defence Minister Israel Katz announced the seizure of the medieval stronghold, also known as Qalaat al-Shaqif, which Israel previously occupied as a military base from 1982 to 2000.

The capture extends Israeli control significantly beyond the Litani River, where forces had previously consolidated their position. Troops are now advancing toward the Zahrani River, approximately six miles further north. Video footage showed Israeli and Golani Brigade flags flying over the castle, which overlooks southern Lebanon and holds substantial strategic value for controlling the region.

The Israeli military said it launched the operation to dismantle Hezbollah infrastructure and expand territorial control. However, some observers question the tactical necessity of the move. Professor Yagil Levy, head of the Institute for the Study of Civil-Military Relations at the Open University of Israel, described the advance as primarily "victory of image." He noted that internal Israeli debate during the 1982 invasion questioned whether capturing Beaufort was truly necessary, and suggested the current seizure attempts to demonstrate success amid growing criticism that Israel is not achieving its objectives.

The expansion of Israeli operations threatens the stability of a US-brokered ceasefire that officially began on April 17 but has never been consistently observed. Israel and Hezbollah accuse each other daily of violations. The advance also complicates separate negotiations between the United States and Iran, as Tehran insists any agreement must include an end to fighting in Lebanon.

International response has been sharply critical. France's foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, called for an emergency UN Security Council meeting to address what he described as unacceptable Israeli military operations. "Nothing can justify the prolongation of Israeli military operations in Lebanon and its increasingly deep occupation of Lebanese territory," he said.

Israeli forces appear to be positioning for a potential encirclement of Nabatieh, a city that serves as both an economic center and cultural heartland for southern Lebanon. Local residents in surrounding towns have been ordered to evacuate as operations continue.

Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam accused Israel of pursuing a "scorched-earth policy and collective punishment" through destruction of towns and forced displacement of inhabitants. He characterized the escalation as dangerous and called for a swift ceasefire, though he defended ongoing security negotiations with Israel as "the least costly path" for Lebanon.

The conflict has already displaced more than 1.2 million people since March following Israeli strikes and evacuation orders. The health ministry reported eight people killed in a strike on Sunday, including three women.

9:49 PM ET

A federal judge has ordered the removal of Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts in Washington, ruling that the institution cannot be renamed without an act of Congress.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper issued a 94-page opinion on Friday directing the Trump administration to remove all physical signage bearing Trump's name and eliminate any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials within 14 days. Cooper determined that the Kennedy Center's governing statute requires the venue to be named for President John F. Kennedy and that no other formal name or public memorial can be adopted without congressional approval.

"Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it," Cooper wrote. The judge concluded that the board of trustees overstepped its authority by unilaterally renaming the institution after Trump.

The ruling also temporarily blocked the Kennedy Center from closing this summer for proposed renovations. Trump had announced a two-year closure for the renovation project in April. Cooper criticized the board's decision-making process, saying board members were "derelict in discharging" their responsibilities by basing their decision on insufficient information and failing to consider the potential harm to programming and the center's memorial functions.

Trump responded to the decision through his Truth Social platform, stating he had instructed the Department of Commerce to arrange a full transfer of the Kennedy Center to Congress, giving lawmakers responsibility for its operation, maintenance, and management. He expressed his vision for the proposed renovations while asserting he had no interest in continuing without complete creative control.

The lawsuit was brought by Representative Joyce Beatty, an Ohio Democrat who serves on the Kennedy Center's board through her congressional position. Beatty welcomed the ruling, stating it "rightly affirms that this administration's efforts to rename and close the Center have no basis in law" and emphasized that the institution belongs to the American people rather than to Trump.

Trump took control of the Kennedy Center's board in February by appointing himself chair and replacing existing members with his appointees. The reconstituted board voted in December to add Trump's name to the institution. The renaming effort sparked significant backlash, with prominent artists and performing groups canceling bookings in protest. The musical Hamilton withdrew a scheduled run, and the National Symphony Orchestra's executive director left her position over the controversy.

The Kennedy Center, which receives federal funding, has been named for President Kennedy since Congress designated it as a national memorial in 1964. The institution hosts theater, opera, ballet, and orchestral performances along with educational programs. The legal dispute raises broader questions about presidential authority over federally funded cultural institutions and could establish precedents for future cases involving similar institutions.

Buffett, who is 94 years old, has led Berkshire Hathaway since 1965 and transformed it from a struggling textile manufacturer into a diversified conglomerate with dozens of operating businesses. Despite his advanced age, Buffett previously indicated he had no plans to retire, making the announcement a surprise to many observers. The shareholder meeting gave Buffett a lengthy standing ovation recognizing his 60 years leading the company.

The Taylor Morrison acquisition represents a significant expansion of Berkshire's housing sector presence. The conglomerate already owns Clayton Homes, which manufactures modular and manufactured homes, along with various building product companies. The purchase of Taylor Morrison, which builds homes across multiple markets nationwide, adds substantial exposure to traditional site-built homes, a segment where Berkshire has had limited direct operations.

This deal signals confidence in the residential construction market despite current economic challenges. The homebuilding industry continues navigating fluctuating mortgage rates and shifting housing demand patterns. By acquiring an established national builder with multiple market operations, Berkshire gains immediate scale in residential construction. The acquisition ranks among the largest housing deals by a major corporation in recent years.

Buffett emphasized his continued belief in Berkshire's future under Abel's management. "The decision to keep every share is an economic decision because I think the prospects of Berkshire will be better under Greg's management than mine," Buffett stated at the shareholder meeting. The move marks Dell's return to the XPS 13 product line as an affordable option in the laptop market. The company is betting that price-conscious consumers will choose a Windows machine over Apple's entry-level MacBook offering.

Apple's MacBook Neo launched at £599 in the UK (€699/$599 in other regions), making it the company's most affordable laptop. The MacBook Neo runs Apple's A18 Pro chip, the same processor found in the iPhone 16 Pro. It features a 13-inch LCD screen with 2408x1506 resolution, 8GB of RAM, and either 256GB or 512GB of storage. The device delivers around 13 hours of battery life for typical work tasks including browsing, note-taking, and email.

The MacBook Neo includes a mechanical trackpad and mechanical keyboard comparable to Apple's pricier models, though it lacks some premium features. The base model omits a Touch ID fingerprint scanner, available only on the more expensive 512GB version. The laptop also has a 1080p webcam, headphone jack, and two USB-C ports, though only one supports USB3 speeds.

Dell's XPS 13 pricing strategy represents a significant shift for the brand, which traditionally targets higher price points. By undercutting the MacBook Neo's standard price, Dell is attempting to capture buyers looking for Windows alternatives in the budget segment.

The student discount bringing the entry price to $599 mirrors Dell's previous tactics for building market share among younger users. This approach targets the same educational buyers that Apple has also pursued with its entry-level MacBook.

The timing of Dell's launch comes as laptop makers increasingly compete across both premium and budget segments. Apple's MacBook Neo demonstrated market appetite for quality entry-level laptops, prompting competitors like Dell to respond with similar offerings at comparable prices.

Dell's commitment to bringing the XPS 13 brand to a lower price point while maintaining its reputation for build quality suggests the company believes there is significant opportunity in the budget laptop market. The aggressive student pricing indicates Dell wants to make an immediate impact against Apple's new offering while the MacBook Neo is still relatively new to the market.

Both companies are now directly competing for the same segment of price-conscious consumers who want quality hardware without premium pricing.
