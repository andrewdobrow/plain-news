An outbreak of Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo has spread faster than any previous outbreak on record, with health officials confirming 65 deaths and 246 suspected cases reported so far in Ituri province.

Médecins Sans Frontières has warned that no previous Ebola outbreak has recorded so many cases so soon after its declaration. The statement underscores the severity of the current situation in this central African nation, where the virus has already crossed borders into Uganda.

Uganda's health ministry confirmed an outbreak in the country after a 59-year-old man died in a Kampala hospital following travel from the Democratic Republic of Congo. Officials at the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention expressed concern about the risk of further regional spread.

The World Health Organization estimated the death rate between 30 and 50 percent based on confirmed cases. Anaïs Legand from the WHO's high threat pathogens team said this figure means that up to five out of 10 people are likely to die from the infection.

The outbreak is particularly challenging because the affected Ituri province is a mining hub where thousands of people work in close proximity daily. The constant movement of workers through mining towns makes infectious disease control extremely difficult. Additionally, the region experiences ongoing armed conflict, which has displaced populations into overcrowded camps, further hindering transmission control efforts.

Health authorities identified the virus as the Bundibugyo strain, based on samples detected by the DRC's national research laboratory. This strain is particularly concerning because no licensed vaccine currently exists to prevent it. The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced 16 Ebola outbreaks since the virus was identified there in 1976, but previous outbreaks typically involved the Zaire strain, for which vaccines are available. Only two previous outbreaks of Bundibugyo virus have been recorded, occurring in 2007 and 2012.

Cases have been reported mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones, with suspected cases also appearing in the neighbouring city of Bunia. The Africa CDC convened an urgent meeting with authorities from the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies to coordinate response efforts.

Ebola is transmitted through direct contact with body fluids such as blood or vomit from infected people, or from dead bodies during funeral preparations. The virus causes severe hemorrhagic fever and carries a high fatality rate.

The rapid transmission has strained local health infrastructure, which lacks adequate resources to manage the crisis. Aid organizations are working urgently to provide support to medical teams struggling with limited equipment and personnel as the outbreak continues to spread across the region.

A New Glenn rocket operated by Blue Origin exploded during a test at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night, threatening NASA's ambitious timeline to build a lunar base and return astronauts to the moon. The explosion occurred during a "hotfire" test at 9 p.m. ET, destroying the launchpad in a massive fireball visible from over 100 miles away.

The incident has serious implications for NASA's Artemis program. Blue Origin had just won a contract on Tuesday to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of the space agency's $20 billion moon base. The company also competes with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission scheduled for 2028, which would return astronauts to the lunar surface for the first time since 1972.

Jeff Bezos, Blue Origin's founder, said on social media that all employees were safe and accounted for. He acknowledged the setback while expressing determination to move forward. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it," Bezos stated. "Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying."

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the space agency would conduct a full evaluation of its timeline following the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he posted. "We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

The shockwaves from the explosion were felt across Florida's Atlantic coast, with residents in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach reporting that their homes shook. An orange glow was visible as far south as Fort Pierce, 115 miles away, and residents in South Carolina hundreds of miles to the north reported seeing the illuminated sky. Emergency officials confirmed there was no threat from fumes or other hazards, though flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the explosion.

The accident marks the latest difficulty for Blue Origin as it competes for government contracts. Last month, a payload from the company's third New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the company to return to flight the previous week.

Elon Musk, CEO of rival SpaceX, commented on the explosion, saying "Most unfortunate" and noting that "Rockets are hard." Both Blue Origin and SpaceX have constructed large new facilities near Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions with NASA.

Microsoft has threatened legal action against a security researcher over the public disclosure of software exploits. The researcher, who operates under the pseudonym Nightmare-Eclipse, publicly released proof-of-concept code for six zero-day vulnerabilities without providing Microsoft advance notice.

Zero-day vulnerabilities are security flaws unknown to software makers with no available patch. Public disclosure of such vulnerabilities before fixes are developed leaves systems vulnerable to attack.

Microsoft characterized the disclosures as uncoordinated, meaning they lacked the advance notice and development time the company typically expects. The approach has drawn criticism from the security community, with observers calling it heavy-handed. The researcher has stated feeling humiliated by Microsoft's response and pledged what was described as a significant retaliation.

Microsoft involved law enforcement in the matter, with some reports suggesting the company has threatened criminal investigation. This escalation has drawn particular scrutiny, especially since Microsoft has not publicly detailed what legal violations it believes occurred.

The incident has reignited debate within the cybersecurity community about proper vulnerability disclosure practices. Security researchers who discover flaws in widely used software face a choice: report them privately to the company, disclose them publicly, or sell the information on the dark market.

Coordinated disclosure has become the industry standard, where researchers give companies time to develop and release patches before public disclosure. This approach aims to balance protecting users with allowing companies adequate time to respond. However, some researchers argue that companies sometimes move too slowly on privately reported vulnerabilities or release inadequate fixes.

The tension between large technology companies and independent security researchers reflects deeper challenges in the cybersecurity ecosystem. While companies understandably want time to address vulnerabilities before they become public knowledge, researchers sometimes grow frustrated with slow response times or feel their concerns are not taken seriously.

The researcher's use of a pseudonym complicates any potential legal action Microsoft might pursue. Some sources suggest the researcher may be a disgruntled former employee, though this remains unconfirmed.

Technology news outlets have highlighted the conflict between Microsoft and the researcher, framing it as emblematic of ongoing disputes in the industry. The situation underscores the delicate relationship between major software vendors and the independent security researchers who help identify and expose flaws in their products.

The outcome of this case could influence how both companies and researchers approach vulnerability disclosure in the future, potentially affecting the speed at which critical security flaws are addressed and made public.

His shot found the top corner after he reacted quicker than PSG's defenders to a charging run from Leandro Trossard. The goal appeared to put Arsenal on course for their first Champions League title, which would have completed a historic season following their Premier League triumph.

PSG responded with characteristic patience, eventually finding their breakthrough when Ousmane Dembele equalized from the penalty spot in the 65th minute after Khvicha Kvaratskhelia was fouled by Cristhian Mosquera. Neither side could find a winner in regular time or extra time, setting up a dramatic penalty shootout.

The shootout proved decisive when Arsenal's Eberechi Eze missed in the second round after a stuttering run, while Gabriel Magalhaes faced the deciding kick in the fifth round with everything on the line. Gabriel appeared to rush his approach, and referee Daniel Siebert delayed him slightly, adding to the pressure. The defender went for power but his shot sailed over the crossbar, handing PSG victory.

Arsenal's defensive organization had been exemplary throughout the match under Mikel Arteta's tactical plan. The London club prioritized solidity and compression of space, with standout performances from Gabriel, who made numerous tackles, and midfielder Myles Lewis-Skelly. Arsenal's transitions caused PSG problems, though the French side dominated possession without creating clear-cut opportunities in the first half.

PSG's patience eventually paid off when they worked their patterns to create the penalty that led to their equalizer. Luis Enrique's team showed resilience after falling behind, maintaining their attacking threat while Arsenal made bold substitutions including bringing on Viktor Gyokeres for Martin Odegaard.

The defeat represents heartbreak for Arsenal after they came so close to achieving an unprecedented domestic and European double. Their Premier League success earlier in the month had been their first league title in 22 years, and a Champions League triumph would have cemented one of the greatest seasons in the club's history.

PSG's back-to-back titles establish them among European football's most successful dynasties. Her editing work on the original trilogy proved instrumental in crafting the pacing and structure that made the films cultural landmarks. She also edited other major films of the era, including American Graffiti and Taxi Driver.

Lucasfilm released a tribute following news of her death. Lucas was married to Star Wars creator George Lucas from 1969 to 1983, a period that encompassed the creation and release of the original Star Wars trilogy.

Her editing contributions extended beyond the technical aspects of filmmaking. Industry professionals have long credited her with shaping key narrative decisions in the original Star Wars, helping transform raw footage into a cohesive story that resonated with audiences worldwide.

Lucas worked during an era when film editing was undergoing significant technological changes. Her career spanned the transition from traditional cutting techniques to more modern approaches, and her work on Star Wars demonstrated how editing could elevate science fiction filmmaking.

After her divorce from George Lucas, she stepped away from the film industry. Despite her reduced public profile in later decades, her influence on cinema remained substantial. Film scholars and editors continue to study her work on the original Star Wars trilogy as an example of how editing shapes storytelling.

The original Star Wars film, released in 1977, became a cultural phenomenon that spawned a multi-billion dollar franchise spanning films, television series, books, and merchandise. The editing work that helped make that first film successful earned recognition from the Academy and from peers in the film industry.

Lucas leaves behind a legacy as one of the most important editors in American cinema history. Her work demonstrated how editing serves as a fundamental storytelling tool, particularly in the science fiction and adventure genres that dominated Hollywood in the late 1970s and early 1980s.

5:00 PM ET

Microsoft has threatened legal action against a security researcher over the public disclosure of software exploits. The researcher, who operates under the pseudonym Nightmare-Eclipse, publicly released proof-of-concept code for six zero-day vulnerabilities without providing Microsoft advance notice.

Zero-day vulnerabilities are security flaws unknown to software makers with no available patch. Public disclosure of such vulnerabilities before fixes are developed leaves systems vulnerable to attack.

Microsoft characterized the disclosures as uncoordinated, meaning they lacked the advance notice and development time the company typically expects. The approach has drawn criticism from the security community, with observers calling it heavy-handed. The researcher has stated feeling humiliated by Microsoft's response and pledged what was described as a significant retaliation.

Microsoft involved law enforcement in the matter, with some reports suggesting the company has threatened criminal investigation. This escalation has drawn particular scrutiny, especially since Microsoft has not publicly detailed what legal violations it believes occurred.

The incident has reignited debate within the cybersecurity community about proper vulnerability disclosure practices. Security researchers who discover flaws in widely used software face a choice: report them privately to the company, disclose them publicly, or sell the information on the dark market.

Coordinated disclosure has become the industry standard, where researchers give companies time to develop and release patches before public disclosure. This approach aims to balance protecting users with allowing companies adequate time to respond. However, some researchers argue that companies sometimes move too slowly on privately reported vulnerabilities or release inadequate fixes.

The tension between large technology companies and independent security researchers reflects deeper challenges in the cybersecurity ecosystem. While companies understandably want time to address vulnerabilities before they become public knowledge, researchers sometimes grow frustrated with slow response times or feel their concerns are not taken seriously.

The researcher's use of a pseudonym complicates any potential legal action Microsoft might pursue. Some sources suggest the researcher may be a disgruntled former employee, though this remains unconfirmed.

Technology news outlets have highlighted the conflict between Microsoft and the researcher, framing it as emblematic of ongoing disputes in the industry. The situation underscores the delicate relationship between major software vendors and the independent security researchers who help identify and expose flaws in their products.

The outcome of this case could influence how both companies and researchers approach vulnerability disclosure in the future, potentially affecting the speed at which critical security flaws are addressed and made public.
