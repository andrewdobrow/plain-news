Yesterday, 6:47 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on its launch pad at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night during a scheduled "hotfire" test, destroying the launchpad and throwing NASA's ambitious lunar plans into uncertainty.

The massive fireball engulfed Launch Complex 36 at Kennedy Space Center at 9pm ET, creating an orange sky visible from Fort Pierce, 185 kilometers to the south. The explosion's shockwaves were felt across Florida's space coast, with residents in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach reporting shaking homes. People in South Carolina, hundreds of miles north, reported seeing a glow in the sky. Flames continued burning at the launchpad for more than two hours after the blast.

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed that all company employees were safe and uninjured. He described it as "a very rough day" on social media and acknowledged the difficulty of developing heavy-lift launch capability. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it," Bezos said. "We'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The company called the explosion an "anomaly" but has not yet identified its cause. This static fire test was the first since the Federal Aviation Administration cleared the rocket to return to flight following a previous incident where a payload ended up in the wrong orbit during a recent flight.

The explosion has serious implications for NASA's Artemis program. Blue Origin was recently awarded a contract to launch the first of three planned missions this year to begin construction of NASA's $20 billion lunar base. The company is also competing with SpaceX to provide a lunar lander for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which will mark humanity's first return to the moon since 1972.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the timeline after the explosion. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," he posted. NASA said it would work with Blue Origin to support an investigation and provide updates on any impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs.

The New Glenn rocket is the vehicle Blue Origin intends to use to launch landers to the moon for NASA, including those that will carry astronauts to the lunar surface. The explosion represents a significant setback in the company's efforts to compete with SpaceX in the commercial space industry and maintain its critical role in NASA's lunar exploration goals.

The incident comes as NASA works toward its goal of returning humans to the moon within the next two years and eventually establishing a sustained lunar presence.

Nvidia to debut first Windows PCs powered by its chips

The new computers will be powered by what Nvidia and its partners are calling the N1X l...

8:17 AM ET

Nvidia will introduce the first Windows computers running on its own processors next week, according to a report from Axios. The devices will mark the chip maker's entry into the Windows PC market, where it has traditionally supplied graphics processors rather than central processing units.

The new computers will be powered by what Nvidia and its partners are calling the N1X laptop processors. Both Microsoft and Arm have posted cryptic messages on social media ahead of the announcement, which is expected to take place at Nvidia's GTC event.

The move represents a significant shift for Nvidia, which has built its business primarily on graphics cards and, more recently, chips for artificial intelligence applications. The company now appears ready to compete directly in the market for PC processors, a space currently dominated by Intel and AMD, with recent competition from Qualcomm's Arm-based chips for Windows.

Microsoft has been working to expand Windows compatibility beyond traditional x86 processors from Intel and AMD. The company has previously partnered with Qualcomm to create Windows devices using Arm-based chips, but faced challenges with software compatibility and performance.

Nvidia's entry into this market brings a major new player with deep expertise in high-performance computing. The company has seen explosive growth in recent years thanks to demand for its AI chips, which are used to train and run large language models and other machine learning applications.

The partnership between Nvidia, Microsoft, and Arm suggests the new processors will use Arm architecture rather than the x86 design used by Intel and AMD. Arm-based chips have gained popularity in mobile devices and, following Apple's successful transition to its own Arm-based processors, are seen as increasingly viable for laptop and desktop computers.

The announcement comes at a time of significant change in the PC industry, with manufacturers exploring alternatives to traditional chip designs in pursuit of better battery life, improved performance, and integration with AI features. How well Nvidia's processors perform and whether they can overcome the software compatibility challenges that have hindered previous Arm-based Windows devices remains to be seen.
