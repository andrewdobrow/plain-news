Romanian authorities said the drone was likely intercepted by air defenses over Ukraine before altering course and entering Romanian airspace.

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte responded quickly to the incident, stating that the alliance is "ready to defend every inch" of its territory. "Russia's reckless behaviour is a danger to us all," Rutte wrote on social media following a call with Romanian President Nicuşor Dan.

The drone crashed into the roof of a residential building in Galați, a city on the Danube River west of Romania's borders with Ukraine and Moldova. The impact sparked a fire and forced the evacuation of several residents. Emergency services responded to the scene, and Romanian military forces scrambled two F-16 fighter jets and a helicopter authorized to engage targets.

Romanian Foreign Minister Oana Țoiu stated that the country would formally communicate consequences for Russia's actions and pursue next steps at the European level regarding sanctions. The foreign ministry described the incident as a "grave and irresponsible escalation" and a serious breach of international law. It also announced plans to request that NATO accelerate the transfer of anti-drone capabilities to Romania.

Romanian officials indicated they would invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty, which allows member nations to request consultations when their security is threatened. This mechanism differs from Article 5, which addresses collective defense against armed attack.

President Dan convened an emergency meeting of Romania's supreme council of national defense and instructed the foreign ministry to prepare a package of measures regarding Romania's relationship with Moscow. He emphasized that Romania would not accept the war being transferred to its citizens.

Russian President Vladimir Putin denied involvement, suggesting without evidence that the drone might have been a Ukrainian weapon. He requested that drone wreckage be handed over to Russia for independent investigation.

The incident drew condemnation from European leaders. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Russia's "war of aggression has crossed yet another line" and pledged full solidarity with Romania. France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot also denounced what he called an "irresponsible act by Russia."

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the incident demonstrated that Russian aggression poses a threat to all of Europe and the Black Sea region.

This strike represents another example of the war in Ukraine spilling across borders, adding to growing concerns about Russia's wider campaign of hybrid warfare across Europe.

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded on a launch pad at Cape Canaveral in Florida on Thursday night during a scheduled test, dealing a significant blow to the company's space ambitions and potentially disrupting NASA's lunar timeline.

The explosion occurred at 9pm ET during a "hotfire" test at Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 36. A massive fireball engulfed and destroyed the launchpad, with the blast visible from extraordinary distances. An orange sky was reported in Fort Pierce, roughly 115 miles to the south, while residents in South Carolina hundreds of miles away reported seeing a glow in the sky. The shockwaves were felt across Florida's Atlantic coast, causing homes to shake in nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach.

Blue Origin confirmed that all employees were safe and uninjured. Jeff Bezos, the company's founder, described it as a "very rough day" on social media, stating the company was already investigating the cause. "It's too early to know the root cause but we're already working to find it," Bezos wrote. "We'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying. It's worth it."

The explosion creates serious complications for NASA's ambitious plans. Just two days earlier, NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned uncrewed lunar missions this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA administrator Jared Isaacman said the agency would conduct a full evaluation of that timeline following the incident, which Blue Origin described as "an anomaly."

"Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," Isaacman wrote. "We will work with our partners to support a thorough investigation of this anomaly, assess near-term mission impacts, and get back to launching rockets."

The New Glenn rocket is critical to NASA's Artemis program. It will be used to launch landers to the moon, including the landers that will carry astronauts to the lunar surface. Blue Origin is also competing with SpaceX for contracts to provide lunar landers for the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028, which would mark the first crewed lunar landing since 1972.

This explosion represents the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin. Last month, a payload from the third flight of New Glenn ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Aviation Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared it to return to flight the previous week.

Flames were still burning at the launchpad more than two hours after the explosion. Emergency officials said there was no threat from fumes or other potential hazards.

Productivity software company ClickUp eliminated 22 percent of its workforce to replace positions with AI agents, according to Box founder Aaron Levie. The cuts add to a wave of technology sector job losses that have brought 2026 layoffs nearly level with the entire previous year despite only five months passing.

Levie described the decision as an example of what he termed AI psychosis. He argued that executives authorizing AI replacements for human workers often have limited understanding of what those jobs actually require. The comment highlights growing tension over how companies evaluate which roles artificial intelligence systems can perform.

Tech industry layoffs have accelerated sharply in 2026. The pace of job cuts through late May rivals the total number recorded across all of 2025, suggesting companies are moving quickly to restructure operations around AI capabilities.

ClickUp makes project management and collaboration software used by businesses to coordinate work. The company had not previously announced major workforce reductions. Representatives did not provide details on which departments or roles were eliminated or how the AI agents would handle the work previously done by human employees.

The software industry has seen multiple rounds of layoffs since 2023 as companies adjusted to slower growth and higher interest rates. The current wave differs in that many companies explicitly cite AI adoption as a reason for reducing headcount rather than general economic conditions.

Levie founded Box, a cloud storage and collaboration company, in 2005. He has become an active voice in debates over AI implementation in business settings. His criticism focused on a disconnect between executive decision-making and operational reality, suggesting leaders may overestimate AI capabilities while underestimating the complexity of human work.

Other technology companies have announced plans to integrate AI agents into operations but most have not specified job cuts as a direct result. ClickUp's approach represents one of the most explicit substitutions of AI for human workers publicly disclosed so far.

The broader pattern reflects how companies across industries are responding to artificial intelligence development. While some organizations have announced workforce reductions citing other factors, the technology sector has been at the forefront of companies directly linking headcount cuts to AI implementation.

Industry observers note that the speed and scale of these changes raise questions about whether companies are adequately preparing for the transition. The debate between executives who see AI as a clear path to efficiency gains and critics like Levie who question whether leadership fully understands the implications continues to intensify as more companies follow ClickUp's lead.

2:00 PM ET

A Russian drone struck a residential apartment building in the Romanian city of Galați during an overnight attack on Ukraine, wounding two people and prompting swift international condemnation. Romanian authorities said the drone was likely intercepted by air defenses over Ukraine before altering course and entering Romanian airspace.

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte responded quickly to the incident, stating that the alliance is "ready to defend every inch" of its territory. "Russia's reckless behaviour is a danger to us all," Rutte wrote on social media following a call with Romanian President Nicuşor Dan.

The drone crashed into the roof of a residential building in Galați, a city on the Danube River west of Romania's borders with Ukraine and Moldova. The impact sparked a fire and forced the evacuation of several residents. Emergency services responded to the scene, and Romanian military forces scrambled two F-16 fighter jets and a helicopter authorized to engage targets.

Romanian Foreign Minister Oana Țoiu stated that the country would formally communicate consequences for Russia's actions and pursue next steps at the European level regarding sanctions. The foreign ministry described the incident as a "grave and irresponsible escalation" and a serious breach of international law. It also announced plans to request that NATO accelerate the transfer of anti-drone capabilities to Romania.

Romanian officials indicated they would invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty, which allows member nations to request consultations when their security is threatened. This mechanism differs from Article 5, which addresses collective defense against armed attack.

President Dan convened an emergency meeting of Romania's supreme council of national defense and instructed the foreign ministry to prepare a package of measures regarding Romania's relationship with Moscow. He emphasized that Romania would not accept the war being transferred to its citizens.

Russian President Vladimir Putin denied involvement, suggesting without evidence that the drone might have been a Ukrainian weapon. He requested that drone wreckage be handed over to Russia for independent investigation.

The incident drew condemnation from European leaders. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Russia's "war of aggression has crossed yet another line" and pledged full solidarity with Romania. France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot also denounced what he called an "irresponsible act by Russia."

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the incident demonstrated that Russian aggression poses a threat to all of Europe and the Black Sea region.

This strike represents another example of the war in Ukraine spilling across borders, adding to growing concerns about Russia's wider campaign of hybrid warfare across Europe.

Lemieux came from a hockey family and passed his passion for the sport to his son Brendan, who also played in the NHL. His influence extended beyond his own achievements, as he helped foster the next generation of hockey talent.

The four Stanley Cup championships he won placed Lemieux among a select group of players who achieved sustained success across multiple teams and extended periods. His career spanned an era when the NHL expanded and grew significantly in popularity throughout North America.

Lemieux's playing style and competitive intensity made him a memorable figure in professional hockey. His success in playoff situations demonstrated his ability to perform when the stakes were highest, earning respect from teammates, opponents, and fans alike.

The hockey community is mourning the loss of a championship-caliber player whose impact on the sport extended well beyond his statistics. His contributions to multiple Stanley Cup-winning teams left a lasting legacy in NHL history.
