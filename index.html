The United States has conducted a second round of military strikes against Iranian targets within three days, according to officials. The attacks come as diplomatic negotiations between Washington and Tehran continue amid an ongoing conflict.

President Trump indicated that talks with Iran have been productive, announcing he would pause U.S. strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure for five days pending the success of ongoing discussions. Despite this announcement, Israeli forces launched a separate wave of strikes on Iranian targets in Tehran shortly afterward, according to statements from the Israeli Defense Forces.

The Israeli military said it struck military bases, weapons sites, and facilities operated by the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps. The IDF stated it would continue operations according to directives from Israel's political leadership. An Israeli Defense Ministry official told reporters that energy infrastructure would be spared from targeting, aligning with the U.S. decision to hold back strikes on power plants and related sites.

Reports indicate the scale of explosions in Tehran was substantial. An Al Jazeera correspondent described the volume of blasts as "unprecedented."

The human toll from the broader conflict continues to mount. The U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency recorded at least 206 attacks across 15 Iranian provinces in a 24-hour period, resulting in at least four casualties. According to various estimates, total fatalities from the conflict have surpassed 1,500, with some rights groups reporting figures as high as 3,230. At least 15 percent of total casualties have been children.

The Israeli military reported destroying or rendering inoperable approximately 330 of Iran's estimated 470 ballistic missile launchers since the conflict began. More than half were hit in airstrikes, while others were disabled through attacks on storage site entrances. Missile fire on Israel has decreased to approximately 10 attacks daily, down from roughly 90 at the conflict's start.

Beyond Iran, hostilities continue in other locations. Israeli forces conducted strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs, the first attacks on the Hezbollah stronghold in several days. Lebanon's health ministry reported one death from the strikes. Israel also announced it had captured two members of the Iranian-backed group in southern Lebanon.

Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for the annexation of areas in southern Lebanon, stating that Israel should "apply sovereignty" over controlled territory. He indicated the military campaign should end with what he described as "a different reality entirely," including shifting Israel's borders to the Litani River.

The escalation of military activity occurs despite ongoing ceasefire frameworks and negotiations aimed at reaching a permanent resolution to the three-month conflict between the United States and Iran.

A Google engineer faces federal insider trading charges after prosecutors allege he used confidential company information to make $1.2 million betting on Polymarket.

The longtime Google employee was charged in New York for allegedly violating insider trading laws. According to prosecutors, the engineer worked in information security at Google and used inside information to place bets on the prediction market platform.

The case centers on allegations that the engineer exploited access to internal Google data to gain an unfair advantage when placing bets. Prosecutors claim he wagered on search terms using confidential information obtained through his position at the company.

Polymarket is a cryptocurrency-based prediction market where users bet on the outcomes of future events. The platform has grown in popularity for betting on everything from political elections to sports outcomes and market trends.

The engineer's role in information security would have given him access to sensitive internal data at Google. Federal prosecutors allege he used this privileged access to identify betting opportunities on Polymarket that other users could not see.

Insider trading laws traditionally apply to stock markets, where trading on material nonpublic information is illegal. This case represents an application of those laws to the emerging world of prediction markets, where real money changes hands based on the outcomes of future events.

The $1.2 million in alleged profits came from bets the engineer placed using the confidential Google information, according to the charges filed in New York. The specific search terms or data he allegedly used were not detailed in the available information about the case.

The charges highlight the legal risks facing employees at technology companies who have access to valuable internal data. As prediction markets grow in size and sophistication, prosecutors appear willing to pursue cases involving the misuse of corporate information on these platforms.

Google has not commented on the charges against its employee. The case is being prosecuted in New York, where federal authorities have aggressively pursued insider trading cases across various markets.

A Google engineer faces insider trading charges after making $1.2 million through bets on Polymarket, according to a criminal complaint filed Thursday. The engineer wagered over $2.7 million on predictions related to Google's 2025 Year in Search campaign.

Polymarket is a prediction market platform where users bet on real-world outcomes. The platform gained prominence during recent election cycles but also hosts markets for corporate announcements and cultural events.

The charges center on whether the engineer used nonpublic information from his employment at Google to place informed bets about the company's annual Year in Search results. Google releases this campaign each December, highlighting the year's most popular search queries across various categories.

According to the complaint, the engineer risked more than $2.7 million across multiple wagers, ultimately profiting $1.2 million from the trades. Federal prosecutors allege the engineer had advance knowledge of the campaign's content through his position at the company.

Insider trading laws prohibit trading securities or other financial instruments based on material nonpublic information. While prediction markets occupy a newer regulatory space than traditional stock markets, federal authorities have increasingly scrutinized their use.

The case represents one of the first major insider trading prosecutions tied to prediction market platforms. Legal experts have debated whether existing securities laws fully cover these markets, which allow users to bet on outcomes ranging from election results to corporate decisions.

Google has not commented on the charges or the engineer's employment status. The company maintains strict policies about employees trading on confidential information, whether in traditional markets or newer platforms.

The engineer's name was included in the complaint filed in federal court. If convicted, insider trading charges can carry penalties including prison time and forfeiture of illegal profits. The case will test how courts apply decades-old insider trading statutes to emerging financial technology platforms.
