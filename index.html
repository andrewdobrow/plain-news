A few minutes ago

A federal judge has ordered the Trump administration to remove the president's name from the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts in Washington, D.C., ruling that only Congress has the authority to change the institution's official name.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper issued a 94-page opinion directing the Trump administration to take down all physical signage bearing Trump's name and eliminate any references to a "Trump Kennedy Center" from official materials within 14 days. "The Kennedy Center's organic statute makes crystal clear that the Center is to be named for President Kennedy, and it cannot bear any other formal name or public memorial based on the Board's unilateral say-so," Cooper wrote.

The controversy began when Trump appointed himself chair of the Kennedy Center's board of trustees in February, calling it a "take-over." He replaced board members with his own appointees. In December, the reconstituted board voted to add Trump's name to the institution, capping an aggressive effort to reshape Washington's arts and cultural institutions.

Cooper ruled that "the Board overstepped its statutory bounds by unilaterally renaming the Kennedy Center after President Trump." He emphasized that "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it."

The lawsuit was brought by Joyce Beatty, an Ohio Democratic representative and board member. "The Kennedy Center is an institution that belongs to the American people, not to Donald Trump," Beatty said in response to the ruling.

The decision also temporarily blocked the Kennedy Center from closing this summer for proposed renovations, which Trump had announced in a two-year closure plan. Cooper found the board's decision to halt operations was "ill-informed and seemingly preordained," noting the board relied on insufficient information and failed to consider its full statutory obligations.

Trump responded through his Truth Social platform, stating he would work with Congress on transferring ownership of the Kennedy Center to the federal government. "Unless I am free to do what I do better than anyone else, bring this Institution back, physically, financially, and artistically, I have no interest in continuing what could only be a hopeless journey," Trump wrote.

The naming dispute prompted significant backlash within the arts community. Several performing groups canceled bookings in protest, including the Tony award-winning musical Hamilton. The National Symphony Orchestra's executive director left her position, and Richard Grenell, a Trump appointee who spearheaded efforts to overhaul the institution, stepped down as president.

The Kennedy Center, which receives federal funding, has long enjoyed bipartisan support as one of the nation's leading arts venues. The court's decision may set precedents for how future administrations can alter federally connected cultural institutions and national memorials.

Israeli forces have captured Beaufort Castle, a clifftop fortress overlooking southern Lebanon, in what marks the deepest military incursion into Lebanese territory in more than 26 years. Defense Minister Israel Katz confirmed the capture of the 12th-century stronghold, which Israel previously used as a military base during its occupation of southern Lebanon from 1982 to 2000.

The offensive represents a significant escalation in the conflict with Hezbollah. Israeli forces have already established control up to the Litani River and are now pushing toward the Zahrani River, approximately six miles further north. The Israel Defense Forces stated they launched the operation to dismantle Hezbollah infrastructure and expand territorial control in the region.

Video footage showed Israeli and Golani Brigade flags flying over Beaufort Castle as shelling continued in surrounding areas. The strategic location gives Israel command of much of southern Lebanon's landscape and appears designed to support a potential encirclement of Nabatieh, a major economic and cultural center in the south.

The military advance has drawn sharp international criticism. France's foreign minister Jean-Noël Barrot requested an emergency UN Security Council meeting to address what he called unacceptable Israeli military operations. He stated that "nothing can justify the prolongation of Israeli military operations in Lebanon and its increasingly deep occupation of Lebanese territory."

Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam accused Israel on Saturday of pursuing a "scorched-earth policy and collective punishment," destroying towns and villages while forcing residents into exile. He called for an immediate ceasefire and warned that Israeli actions would not bring security or stability.

The escalation has complicated international negotiations. The advance threatens stalled talks between the United States and Iran, as Tehran has demanded that any agreement include an end to fighting in Lebanon. Regional observers suggest Israeli officials are attempting to inflict maximum damage on Hezbollah before potential diplomatic agreements impose new restrictions.

The conflict has already displaced more than 1.2 million people since March through Israeli strikes and evacuation orders. A ceasefire nominally began on April 17 but has not been observed, with both sides accusing each other of daily violations.

Some Israeli analysts question the military value of the castle seizure. Yagil Levy, head of the Institute for the Study of Civil-Military Relations at Open University of Israel, characterized the advance as primarily a "victory of image" rather than a meaningful strategic shift. He noted that criticism is emerging from within Israel's military about soldier vulnerability to drone attacks and that "Hezbollah remains intact" without a realistic disarmament plan.

Beaufort Castle holds deeper significance for Lebanon beyond its military position. Historically regarded as a symbol of resistance, it occupies an important place in southern Lebanon's political memory and cultural identity, having featured prominently in previous Israeli military campaigns.

Dell has announced a new XPS 13 laptop designed to challenge Apple's recently launched MacBook Neo. The device will be available at a promotional price of $599 for students during its July launch, with the offer running through September for back-to-school shoppers. After the promotional period ends, the laptop will cost $699 for general consumers.

The timing of Dell's announcement follows the MacBook Neo's introduction, Apple's first entry-level MacBook priced at $599. The MacBook Neo runs on an A18 Pro chip, the same processor found in iPhone 16 Pro models, and includes 8GB of RAM with either 256GB or 512GB storage options. Apple positioned the device as an accessible entry point to its MacBook lineup, priced $500 below the MacBook Air.

Dell's XPS 13 strategy appears focused on offering competitive specifications at similar price points. The company is leveraging its XPS line's reputation as a premium consumer laptop series to attract buyers who might consider Apple's offering. By introducing a temporary discount, Dell aims to build momentum during the critical back-to-school season when students make purchasing decisions.

The MacBook Neo features a 13-inch LCD screen with 2408x1506 resolution and 500-nit brightness, delivering crisp visuals comparable to Apple's more expensive models. The device maintains the company's design language with an aluminum body, rounded corners, and quality construction throughout. Despite its lower price, the Neo includes a mechanical trackpad that reviewers noted performs well, plus a headphone jack that remains uncommon on modern laptops.

Performance testing shows the Neo handles everyday tasks effectively, including complex photo editing in professional applications and light video editing. Battery life reaches approximately 13 hours during typical work activities like browsing, note-taking, and email. The machine runs macOS 26 Tahoe and comes with Apple's bundled software suite including Pages, Numbers, Keynote, Garage Band, and iMovie.

The MacBook Neo does include some trade-offs for its price. It lacks a Touch ID fingerprint scanner on the base model, a feature only available on the higher-priced 512GB variant. The screen is smaller at 13 inches compared to the 13.6-inch Air display, and lacks True Tone technology. The device includes two USB-C ports but only one supports USB3, limiting connectivity options. It cannot drive high refresh rate displays and won't work with certain docks requiring Thunderbolt support.

Dell's response indicates the premium ultraportable laptop market remains competitive as manufacturers vie for consumer attention. The XPS 13's positioning at the same price point as Apple's entry-level MacBook suggests Dell recognizes the significance of Apple's push into budget-conscious segments. The temporary discount strategy represents an aggressive approach to establishing market presence in a segment where Apple has gained considerable ground in recent years.

Though Powell did not explicitly name the Trump administration...

8:31 PM ET

Jerome Powell has issued a sharp warning about the Federal Reserve's credibility in his first public comments since stepping down as chair. Speaking while accepting the John F. Kennedy Profiles in Courage award on Sunday evening, Powell said the central bank faces a "stress test" and its credibility is on the line.

Though Powell did not explicitly name the Trump administration in his remarks, his message was unmistakable. He was responding to administration actions widely seen as attempts to undermine the Fed's independence, including a criminal investigation into the central bank's over-budget building renovation and efforts to remove governor Lisa Cook.

Powell framed threats to the Fed's independence as endangering an institution essential to American economic strength. His decision to use a high-profile award acceptance to issue this warning underscores how seriously he views the current situation.

The Federal Reserve has traditionally operated independently from political interference, a principle widely considered necessary for sound monetary policy. The criminal investigation into the building renovation marks an unusual escalation, as federal agencies typically handle budget overruns through administrative procedures rather than criminal probes.

Powell's term as Federal Reserve chair ended on May 15. However, he announced plans to remain on the Fed's board as a voting governor until the administration ends its scrutiny of the building renovation. Powell called this scrutiny "pretext" designed to pressure the central bank into lowering interest rates.

His departure comes as the Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh as the new Federal Reserve chair on Wednesday in a 54-45 vote split mostly along party lines. The confirmation was the most divisive for the position in history, with only Democratic senator John Fetterman from Pennsylvania joining Republicans in supporting Warsh's nomination.

Warsh, an Ivy League economist and former Wall Street banker, previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. During his first stint on the board, he was known as an "inflation hawk" who advocated for higher interest rates to combat inflation. He departed the Fed board in 2011 partly due to disagreements over post-financial crisis stimulus measures.

Warsh will take office on May 14, taking over leadership at a time when inflation has climbed to 3.8% and the central bank faces intense pressure from the Trump administration to lower rates. A White House spokesperson called Warsh's confirmation "a welcome step towards finally restoring accountability, competence and confidence in Fed decision-making."

At his Senate confirmation hearing, Warsh pledged to maintain Fed independence and "take politics out of monetary policy." Yet his stated willingness to lower rates despite rising inflation, combined with his refusal to confirm that Trump lost the 2020 election, prompted Democrats to express concerns about his independence.
