Yesterday, 10:03 PM ET

A chemical tank rupture at a Nippon Dynawave Packaging facility in Longview, Washington killed 11 workers in an incident that shocked the local community. The implosion occurred early Tuesday morning at the mill, which sits along the Columbia River in the southwestern Washington city.

The tank contained white liquor, an alkaline chemical solution used in paper manufacturing. The tank was approximately 60 percent full at the time it ruptured, according to fire officials. White liquor is made up mostly of sodium sulfide, sodium hydroxide and disodium carbonate and is used to break down wood chips into pulp during the paper-making process.

The initial emergency response confirmed one fatality and multiple injuries, including chemical burns. Eight employees and one firefighter sustained injuries described as critical or life-threatening. Nine plant employees were unaccounted for following the initial reports. All missing workers were later recovered, bringing the total death toll to 11.

The Nippon Dynawave facility operates as both a pulp and paper mill and liquid packaging plant. The plant employs approximately 1,000 workers and manufactures materials for tissues, printing paper, cups, plates, cartons and other products. The facility has been operating since 1953 and serves as a major employer in Longview, a city of about 38,000 people with deep historical ties to the timber and paper industries.

Following the tank rupture, white liquor spilled into a drainage ditch. State ecology department officials evaluated the environmental impact of the spill. However, authorities said the incident posed no direct threat to the surrounding community, and there was no immediate public danger from the chemical release.

The recovery operation involved multiple agencies and emergency responders working to locate and identify the missing workers. Fire Chief Scott Goldstein noted the personal impact on responders. "The people who are responders here have friends and relatives that work on site," he said. "It is something that is impactful, and we have support networks to support the workers as well as the emergency responders."

U.S. Senator Patty Murray of Washington responded to the disaster in a written statement. "My heart is with everyone who lost a loved one today, as well as the injured workers," she said, calling the implosion an "absolute tragedy."

The investigation into the cause of the tank rupture remained ongoing as authorities worked to determine what led to the failure and what safety measures were in place at the time. The Nippon Dynawave facility did not immediately respond to inquiries about the incident. As recovery efforts continued, the scene remained closed to the public while crews worked to mitigate environmental concerns related to the spilled chemical.

Yesterday, 5:46 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed at least 65 people confirmed by health officials, with 246 suspected cases reported in Ituri province. The situation has prompted urgent international concern as the virus threatens to spread beyond the currently affected region.

The outbreak has centered on mining towns in Ituri province, an area that borders Uganda and South Sudan. The constant movement of people through these mining communities presents a significant challenge to containing the disease. Uganda has already confirmed cases, including a 59-year-old man who died in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention expressed alarm about the potential for further spread. The organization's director general, Dr Jean Kaseya, stated: "Given the high population movement between affected areas and neighbouring countries, rapid regional coordination is essential."

A major concern for health officials is the strain of Ebola involved in this outbreak. Testing by the DRC's national research laboratory detected the Bundibugyo strain in 13 of 20 samples examined. Unlike the more common Zaire strain found in previous DRC outbreaks, there is no licensed vaccine available for Bundibugyo. This strain has only caused two previous documented outbreaks, in 2007 and 2012.

The World Health Organization estimates the death rate of this outbreak at between 30 and 50 percent among confirmed cases. Anaïs Legand from the WHO's high threat pathogens team noted: "It's huge. It means that up to five out of 10 people are likely to die."

Ituri province presents particularly difficult conditions for disease control. The region is a conflict zone experiencing ongoing fighting between armed groups, and medical facilities remain modest. Displaced people fleeing the violence are being forced into overcrowded camps, further complicating transmission prevention efforts.

Health workers lack adequate equipment to properly isolate patients and trace contacts. Aid agencies including Doctors Without Borders are mobilizing resources to support front-line health workers treating Ebola patients and investigating suspected cases. The organization described the virus's spread as deeply alarming.

The World Health Organization's director general visited the affected region to assess the situation and support response efforts. Officials convened an urgent meeting involving authorities from the DRC, Uganda, South Sudan, the WHO, and pharmaceutical companies to coordinate a regional response.

The DRC has experienced 16 documented Ebola outbreaks since the virus was identified there in 1976. Previous experience has shown that successful containment requires rapid case identification, patient isolation, and prevention of transmission to healthcare workers and family members. The international health community recognizes that coordinated responses involving multiple countries and organizations are essential to bringing this outbreak under control.

Yesterday, 5:06 PM ET

A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station's Launch Complex 36 in Florida on Thursday evening. The explosion occurred at 9 p.m. ET and created a massive fireball that destroyed the launchpad. The blast was visible from over 100 miles away, with an orange sky reported in Fort Pierce and a glow seen as far north as South Carolina.

The timing proved particularly significant. Just hours before the explosion, Blue Origin had received a national security launch task order from NASA. The company had been positioned as a major competitor to SpaceX for government space contracts.

Jeff Bezos, Blue Origin's founder, confirmed on social media that all employees were safe and accounted for. "Very rough day, but we'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying," Bezos said. "It's worth it." The company described the incident as an "anomaly" but has not yet disclosed the root cause.

The explosion has direct consequences for NASA's lunar ambitions. The New Glenn rocket is the vehicle Blue Origin plans to use to launch lunar landers for NASA's Artemis program, including the landers that will carry astronauts to the moon's surface. NASA announced on Tuesday that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three planned missions this year to help construct NASA's 20 billion dollar moon base.

Jared Isaacman, NASA's administrator, stated that the agency would conduct a full evaluation of the program timeline following the incident. "Spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult," Isaacman wrote. He committed to working with partners to support an investigation and assess impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs.

The explosion marks the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin. Last month, a payload from the third New Glenn flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading to a temporary FAA grounding. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared the rocket to return to flight.

Shockwaves from the explosion were felt across Florida's space coast, with residents reporting shaking homes and taking to social media for information. Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards. Flames continued burning at the launchpad more than two hours after the explosion.

The incident occurred as Blue Origin and SpaceX compete for NASA contracts under the Artemis program. Both companies have built facilities near Cape Canaveral to support crewed and cargo missions. Artemis III, scheduled for 2027, will test Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander against SpaceX's Starship system to determine which will transport astronauts to the lunar surface during the Artemis IV mission planned for 2028.

Yesterday, 11:23 PM ET

A meteor exploded over northeastern Massachusetts and southeastern New Hampshire on Saturday afternoon, creating a powerful blast that startled residents across New England. NASA confirmed the incident, which generated a sonic boom equivalent to 300 tons of TNT.

The fireball broke apart just after 2pm local time at an altitude of 40 miles, according to NASA's deputy news chief Jennifer Dooren. The meteor was traveling at approximately 75,000 miles per hour when it fragmented in the atmosphere.

The explosion produced loud booms that echoed across a wide region, with reports coming from multiple states and even extending into Canada. Hundreds of residents heard and felt the sonic boom, which prompted widespread confusion before authorities identified the source as a meteor explosion.

Sonic booms from meteors occur when space rocks travel through the atmosphere faster than the speed of sound. The sudden compression of air creates the characteristic explosive sound that can travel for considerable distances.

No damage or injuries have been reported from the event. Because the meteor broke apart at high altitude, any debris that reached the ground did so in unpopulated areas or was too small to cause harm.

The incident reflects how regularly small space rocks enter Earth's atmosphere. According to NASA, most space rocks smaller than a football field break apart during atmospheric entry and burn up completely. Explosions from meteor fragments are rare occurrences, though meteorite falls happen frequently around the world.

"There could be some small fragments, but a lot of it would have burned up in the atmosphere," explained Brian Mitchell, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service, to the Associated Press.

Residents who witnessed the event took to social media to share their experiences and seek explanations for the startling sounds and bright light. Many described the sudden noise as alarming, comparing it to various familiar sounds as they tried to understand what had happened.

The meteor explosion added to weekend celestial activity, with additional astronomical events visible in the night sky. The incident served as a reminder of Earth's exposure to space debris and the regular but largely unnoticed encounters with meteoroids entering the planet's atmosphere.

NASA's analysis of satellite data confirmed the event's origin and characteristics, providing residents with scientific explanation for the startling experience that had puzzled many across the New England region.

Victor Wembanyama led the Spurs' charge against the defending champions. The 7-foot-4 Frenchman finished with 22 points and seven rebounds, earning him the Western Conference Finals MVP award. He became emotional after the final buzzer, reflecting on the significance of reaching the Finals. "Realizing that some part of a childhood dream is going to come true," he said when asked about his tears. "We're still hungry, we want more. This feeling is so powerful, I can't explain it."

However, Wembanyama's teammates played crucial roles in the victory. Julian Champagnie scored 20 points and connected on six three-pointers. Dylan Harper made an impact off the bench, while Luke Kornet made a crucial defensive block in the fourth quarter as Oklahoma City mounted a comeback attempt. After the game, Wembanyama praised his supporting cast, saying "They don't know how much I love them, and everyone stepped up tonight."

The Thunder fought hard despite missing Jalen Williams due to a hamstring injury. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, the reigning NBA MVP, was brilliant for Oklahoma City with 35 points and nine assists. Still, no other Thunder player scored more than 20 points as the defending champions saw their title hopes end.

Thunder coach Mark Daigneault acknowledged the loss while maintaining confidence in his team. "You have to grow from every experience, including the tough ones," he said. "And it's the NBA, there are tough ones. We can also be really disappointed. There's nobody that we don't think we can beat, respectfully."

For the Spurs, the Finals matchup with New York carries historical significance. The teams last met in the 1999 NBA Finals, when San Antonio won the series 4-1. They also faced each other in the NBA Cup final earlier this season.

The Spurs secured the victory by controlling the final moments of the game. As the clock wound down and the Thunder were forced to launch desperate three-pointers from well beyond the arc, San Antonio sealed the win with a breakaway dunk.

Julian Champagnie captured the magnitude of the achievement after the final buzzer. "We never knew if we were going to get this far but when you've got the greatest player in the world things happen," he said.

San Antonio's last NBA championship came in 2014, while the Knicks have not won a title since 1973.

Trump wants to finalize the deal soon but is seeking to strengthen several provisions he considers important, particularly regarding...

Yesterday, 9:04 PM ET

President Trump has asked for several amendments to the nuclear agreement his envoys reached with Iranian counterparts, according to administration officials briefed on the matter. The request came during a Situation Room meeting held on Friday.

Trump wants to finalize the deal soon but is seeking to strengthen several provisions he considers important, particularly regarding Iran's nuclear material, according to two U.S. officials. His request has prompted another round of negotiations between the parties that could extend several more days.

The tentative agreement reached this week would extend the current ceasefire and reopen the Strait of Hormuz if approved. Under the framework being discussed, the strait would reopen with no tolls during a 60-day ceasefire extension. Iran would be permitted to freely sell oil during this period, with negotiations on its nuclear program to follow within 30 to 60 days.

In exchange, the United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports. The agreement would also include an official declaration ending the war and could release approximately 25 billion dollars in frozen Iranian assets.

However, significant disagreements remain. Iran's Fars news agency, which is close to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, reported that the Strait of Hormuz would remain under Iranian control. The agency stated that "the management of the Strait, determining the route, time, method of passage, and issuing permits will continue to be the monopoly and discretion of the Islamic Republic of Iran." It characterized Trump's assertions about the deal being nearly final as "inconsistent with reality."

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif congratulated Trump on his peace efforts and said Pakistan hoped to host another round of talks between the United States and Iran soon. Pakistan's army chief has been a key figure in the negotiations and recently held meetings with Iranian officials.

The negotiations represent a major foreign policy initiative for the Trump administration. The Strait of Hormuz is a critical waterway for global oil shipments, and its closure has had significant economic implications.

Trump announced that he had conducted phone discussions with leaders of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Bahrain, and Pakistan, as well as Turkey's president. He also spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Trump said he met with American negotiators including special envoy Steve Witkoff and adviser Jared Kushner to discuss the latest proposals. He stated he would only sign a deal "where we get everything we want," warning that if an agreement was not reached, the United States would resume strikes against Iran.

The timeline for completing the amendments and reaching a final agreement remains uncertain as both sides continue their discussions.
