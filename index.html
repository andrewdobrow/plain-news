The United States launched new military strikes against Iranian targets for the second time in three days, escalating tensions as diplomatic efforts to end the three-month conflict continue. The attacks targeted attack drones and a drone ground-control station in southern Iran, according to US officials who characterized the operations as self-defense measures.

The strikes represent a significant challenge to the ceasefire agreement reached on April 7 between Washington and Tehran. Despite ongoing peace negotiations, both countries have maintained active military operations, raising questions about the viability of diplomatic solutions.

The timing of the attacks is particularly significant given recent diplomatic momentum. Just days earlier, Trump administration officials had paused Project Freedom, an initiative launched to help guide commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz, to allow space for peace negotiations. Trump declared that "great progress has been made toward a complete and final agreement," suggesting the two sides were moving closer to a deal.

However, the renewed military action signals deteriorating conditions on the ground. The strikes have triggered immediate economic consequences, with oil prices climbing following news of the attacks. Brent crude jumped in response, reflecting trader concerns about supply disruptions through this critical waterway. Energy markets have remained volatile throughout the conflict, with fuel prices across the United States reaching four-year highs.

The strikes also had broader regional implications. Kuwait reported facing a missile and drone attack, though the source remained unclear. These developments underscore how the conflict continues to affect the wider Middle East despite ceasefire agreements.

The Strait of Hormuz has been a focal point throughout the three-month war. Iran had effectively choked off the waterway while the US Navy imposed a blockade on Iranian ports. Project Freedom was designed to coordinate the passage of stranded commercial vessels through these contested waters, though it was temporarily suspended to facilitate negotiations.

Iranian officials have warned against further US military intervention in the region. Ebrahim Azizi, head of the national security commission of the Iranian parliament, previously stated that American involvement in strait operations would be considered a violation of the ceasefire.

The negotiations remain described as fragile, with both nations maintaining military readiness while pursuing diplomatic channels. US officials have not provided detailed damage assessments from the latest strikes or indicated whether additional military action is planned. Iranian authorities have not yet issued formal responses to the attacks.

The conflict's economic impact continues to ripple globally. Energy bills are expected to rise for millions of consumers as the fallout from the Iran war reaches household budgets. The situation demonstrates how military escalations during ceasefire periods threaten to unravel diplomatic progress and destabilize international energy markets.

Yesterday, 10:47 PM ET

The Australian government filed a lawsuit against American manufacturing company 3M seeking A$2 billion in damages over contamination linked to firefighting foam.

The case centers on contamination at defense sites from chemicals known as PFAS, or per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances. These chemicals are often called forever chemicals because they persist in the environment and do not break down naturally.

The contamination is linked to firefighting foam that contained PFAS. These foams were used at military installations and defense facilities across Australia.

The A$2 billion lawsuit represents the largest case ever brought by the Australian government. The scale of the legal action reflects the extent of contamination at multiple defense sites.

PFAS chemicals have raised health and environmental concerns globally. They accumulate in soil, water, and living organisms over time. The substances have been found in groundwater near military bases where firefighting foams were used during training exercises and emergency responses.

3M manufactured firefighting foam products containing PFAS that were widely used by military and civilian firefighting operations. The company has faced similar litigation in other countries over PFAS contamination.

The Australian government's decision to pursue legal action follows years of testing and monitoring at affected defense sites. Environmental assessments documented the presence and spread of PFAS chemicals in soil and water systems surrounding military facilities.

The lawsuit seeks compensation for cleanup costs and damages related to the contamination. Remediation of PFAS contamination is expensive and technically challenging because the chemicals resist conventional treatment methods.

Australia has been investigating PFAS contamination at defense sites for several years. The government has conducted health studies and environmental monitoring in communities near affected military bases.

The case adds to growing global scrutiny of PFAS manufacturers. Regulators in multiple countries have moved to restrict or ban PFAS in firefighting foam and other products due to environmental and health concerns.

Manager Oliver Glasner departed the club on the highest note possible, winning the trophy in what was his final match in charge. The Austrian manager performed a full-length slide on the pitch in celebration and has now won three trophies in 12 months after last year's FA Cup and Community Shield victories with Palace.

The victory grants Palace automatic qualification to next season's Europa League, a competition one level above the Conference League in UEFA's hierarchy. This represents significant European football for a team that finished 15th in the Premier League this season. Glasner suggested after the final that the club now belongs in the Europa League and should be competing at that level going forward.

The Conference League triumph comes after Palace were denied a place in the Europa League despite winning the FA Cup last year. UEFA had ruled that the club breached regulations concerning multi-club ownership, a decision that prompted this unexpected path to European football through the third-tier competition.

Mateta's goal proved particularly poignant given his recent history. The striker's move to Milan in January fell through after he failed a medical, keeping him at Palace for their European campaign. His decisive finish in the final provided redemption for those circumstances.

The achievement represents a remarkable turnaround for Palace under Glasner's leadership. The club secured their place in the top flight for a 14th consecutive season despite a demanding 12-month period that included their European campaign alongside domestic duties.

Speaking after the final, Glasner paid tribute to the players and supporters, praising their fantastic spirit throughout the campaign. He insisted he harbors no regrets about his decision to depart the club despite making history in south London.

Palace chairman Steve Parish witnessed the victory from the stands next to UEFA president Aleksander Ceferin, marking a significant moment in the club's history. Thousands of supporters who gathered at Selhurst Park to watch the final on a big screen celebrated by invading the pitch as the final whistle sounded.

The Conference League represented only the second European competition appearance for either finalist in recent memory. Rayo Vallecano, who finished eighth in Spain's top division, had previously competed in the 2000-01 UEFA Cup after qualifying through their fair play record.

With Glasner departing, Palace must now find a new manager to lead the club into next season's Europa League campaign, adding complexity to their planning after the triumph in Germany.

Yesterday, 10:32 PM ET

The Department of Justice has opened a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll, who accused President Trump of sexual assault, according to multiple reports.

CNN first reported the development, stating that the probe focuses on whether Carroll committed perjury during a deposition related to her two civil lawsuits against Trump. The investigation marks a significant turn in the legal proceedings between Carroll and the president.

Carroll previously won two civil cases against Trump. In the first case, a jury found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation, awarding Carroll $5 million. In a second trial, a jury awarded her $83.3 million in damages for defamation after Trump repeatedly denied her allegations and attacked her credibility while he was president and after.

The allegations at the center of the cases date back to the mid-1990s, when Carroll says Trump assaulted her in a department store dressing room. Trump has denied the allegations and maintained that he never met Carroll.

The Justice Department investigation appears to center on statements Carroll made under oath during depositions in the civil proceedings. Perjury occurs when someone knowingly makes false statements while under oath in a legal proceeding. Federal perjury charges carry potential prison sentences of up to five years.

The timing of the investigation has drawn attention, coming after Trump returned to the White House and appointed new leadership at the Justice Department. Critics have raised questions about whether the investigation represents appropriate use of prosecutorial resources or potential retaliation against a Trump accuser.

The Justice Department has not publicly commented on the investigation. Representatives for Carroll also have not issued public statements in response to the reports.

The investigation adds another legal dimension to the long-running dispute between Trump and Carroll, which has already resulted in significant jury verdicts against the president in civil court.
