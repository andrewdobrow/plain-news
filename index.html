SEC Proposes Eliminating Climate Disclosure Rule for Public Companies

The regulation represented a comprehensive approach to climate-related financial disclosures. Under the proposed rule, all publicly traded companies would have been oblig...

12:10 PM ET

The Securities and Exchange Commission has proposed ending a regulation that would have required publicly traded companies to disclose whether they faced significant risks from climate change and its effects.

The regulation represented a comprehensive approach to climate-related financial disclosures. Under the proposed rule, all publicly traded companies would have been obligated to report on their exposure to climate-related risks and how those risks might affect their business operations and financial performance.

The SEC's move to eliminate the disclosure requirement marks a reversal in regulatory policy regarding corporate climate transparency. The commission's proposal would remove the obligation for companies to publicly report on their climate vulnerabilities.

Climate disclosure rules have been a topic of debate in corporate governance and environmental policy circles. Proponents of such regulations argue that investors need standardized information about climate risks to make informed decisions about their investments. Critics have raised concerns about the costs of compliance and the difficulty of quantifying climate-related risks.

The proposed elimination of the rule would affect thousands of publicly traded companies across all sectors of the economy. Companies in energy, manufacturing, agriculture, real estate, and financial services had been preparing to comply with the disclosure requirements.

The SEC's proposal follows broader discussions about the role of regulatory agencies in addressing climate change through corporate disclosure requirements. Financial regulators worldwide have taken varying approaches to climate risk disclosure, with some jurisdictions implementing mandatory reporting standards while others have adopted voluntary frameworks.

The commission will likely accept public comments on the proposal before making a final decision. Industry groups, environmental organizations, and investor coalitions are expected to submit feedback on the proposed rule change.

The fate of the climate disclosure requirement will affect how investors assess companies' exposure to physical risks from extreme weather events, transition risks from policy changes, and liability risks from climate-related litigation.
