The temporary halt to fighting between Israel and Hezbollah, assuming both sides maintain it, represents a pause rather than a permanent resolution. Oil and gas prices surged sharply following an escalation of attacks between Israel and Iran on energy infrastructure in the Middle East. Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, initially climbed 10 percent to $119 a barrel before settling at $110, representing a 3.3 percent gain. Crude prices have soared 60 percent since the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran began on February 28.

The price spike reflects damage to critical energy facilities across the region. QatarEnergy reported that Iranian attacks damaged facilities producing 17 percent of its liquefied natural gas export capacity, with repairs expected to take three to five years. The strikes on Ras Laffan, the world's largest LNG facility, caused what QatarEnergy described as "extensive damage." Shell also reported damage to its Pearl gas-to-liquids facility at the same location, though the fire was quickly contained with no reported injuries. Additionally, authorities in Abu Dhabi shut down operations at its Habshan gas facility and Bab oilfield following Iranian attacks.

The escalation prompted sharp reactions across energy markets. European gas prices jumped 24 percent to 68 euros per megawatt hour, the highest since late December 2022. UK gas prices more than doubled since late February, with wholesale prices rising 23 percent to 172 pence per therm on Thursday, the highest since August 2022. These increases are expected to drive up household energy bills.

The damage to Qatar's LNG hub has fundamentally altered global gas market expectations. Energy consultancy Wood Mackenzie warned that initial expectations of a two-month disruption are now likely to be exceeded, with each additional month of disruption removing about 1.5 percent from annual global LNG availability.

The conflict's impact extended to financial markets, triggering sharp sell-offs across stock exchanges. Japan's Nikkei fell 3.4 percent, South Korea's Kospi dropped 2.7 percent, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng declined 2 percent. European markets followed, with the UK's FTSE 100 closing down 2.35 percent and Germany's Dax and France's CAC each falling around 2.3 percent.

Industry observers warned of broader economic consequences. Major European airlines including Lufthansa announced that fares would rise if fuel prices remained elevated for months. Thomas Pugh, chief economist at consulting firm RSM UK, cautioned that sustained high energy prices could trigger second-round inflationary effects, potentially pushing inflation toward 5 percent and making interest rate hikes from the Bank of England more likely.

Analysts warned that oil could reach $150 a barrel if the conflict continues to disrupt energy infrastructure. Richard Meade, editor-in-chief of Lloyd's List Intelligence, noted that the strikes marked a significant escalation that expanded the risk profile for the entire Middle East Gulf energy and logistics system.

The Cleveland Browns have traded star edge rusher Myles Garrett to the Los Angeles Rams in exchange for linebacker Jared Verse and three draft picks. The transaction represents one of the biggest defensive trades in NFL history.

Garrett, 30, is a two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year and seven-time Pro Bowl selection. The Browns drafted him first overall in 2017, and he has since accumulated 125.5 sacks, 413 pressures, and 149 tackles for loss since entering the league. In 2025, he set a single-season sacks record with 23, earning All-Pro recognition even as the Browns finished 5-12 and missed the playoffs for the second consecutive year.

The trade comes after Garrett requested to leave Cleveland in 2024 following the team's 3-14 season. He expressed a desire to play for a contender with a realistic championship opportunity. Despite this request, he signed a four-year, $160 million extension with the Browns that made him the highest-paid non-quarterback in league history at the time, with an average annual salary of $40 million.

The situation remained complicated through the 2025 season. New head coach Todd Monken, hired in January to replace Kevin Stefanski, acknowledged he had not met face-to-face with Garrett since his hiring. General manager Andrew Berry ultimately negotiated a contract modification with Garrett that deferred option payments over the 2026-28 seasons, enabling the trade to happen.

"As discussions intensified we were stuck at a legitimate crossroads: do we hold on to a truly generational player who has become the identity of our team, or do we make the difficult decision that we think is best for the organization over the long run?" Berry said after announcing the trade.

The Browns will receive a 2027 first-round selection, a 2028 second-round pick, and a 2029 third-round selection along with Verse. The 25-year-old edge rusher was drafted by the Rams in the first round of 2024 and earned Defensive Rookie of the Year honors. He was also selected to the Pro Bowl this past season and has recorded 12 career sacks with 22 tackles for loss.

The Rams' acquisition marks a significant move as they pursue another championship. Los Angeles reached the NFC Championship Game last season before losing to the eventual champion Seattle Seahawks. The team won Super Bowl LVI at their home stadium SoFi in Inglewood, California, and will host Super Bowl LXI there next February.

For the Rams, Garrett's arrival gives them a formidable defensive force alongside quarterback Matthew Stafford, the reigning AP NFL MVP. This pairing will mark the first time the reigning AP MVP and Defensive Player of the Year are teammates.

The Lost Boys, a stage adaptation of Joel Schumacher's 1987 teen-vampire film, has earned 12 Tony Award nominations. Tony-winning director Michael Arden helms the production, which brings the cult classic to Broadway audiences with elaborate staging and theatrical spectacle.

Arden previously won a Tony Award for directing a musical about two robots that took home the prize for best musical. The Lost Boys nominations include two for Arden himself, recognizing his work on the production.

The stage version maintains the core story of the original film while making some notable changes. The Emerson family—older brother Michael, younger Sam, and their mother Lucy—moves to Santa Carla, California, where they discover the seaside town harbors dangerous vampire activity. Michael falls under the spell of Star, not realizing she's been tasked with turning him by the vampire leader David. Sam instead allies with a pair of would-be vampire hunters known as the Frog brothers.

Arden's direction emphasizes the film's Southern California gothic atmosphere through visual design. The production uses neon-sign lighting, pyrotechnics, and mid-air stunts to create an immersive experience. Notable sequences from the film, such as Michael's vampire initiation ritual involving a fall from a railway bridge, translate effectively to the stage with real performers executing the physical stunts.

The stage adaptation makes some textual updates while maintaining the 1987 setting. Sam is strongly implied to be gay, and one of the Frog brothers is played by a woman who insists on being referred to as male. These changes reflect contemporary expressions of gender and sexuality while the characters remain sincere in their performances.

The musical score comes from rock act the Rescues. However, critics have noted that the songs don't particularly capture the film's west-coast punk aesthetic or evoke the period convincingly. The music leans toward emo power ballads and modern Broadway styles rather than goth-rock elements that might better suit the vampire story.

The production makes significant design choices regarding scope and focus. A large Arizona postcard serves as a backdrop for a brief driving scene, exemplifying the show's elaborate production values. The streamlining of the original story, including omitting one character entirely, helps concentrate the narrative, though this also shifts focus toward the mother character's emotional journey at the expense of some of the material's youth-culture elements.

Arden has also announced plans to direct a Broadway musical adaptation of Happy Feet, expanding his portfolio of adaptations that bring unexpected source material to the stage. His approach to unusual properties continues shaping contemporary Broadway offerings.

The 12 nominations position The Lost Boys among the most recognized productions of the theater season. The Tony Awards will determine whether this vampire musical achieves recognition comparable to Arden's previous success.

The stage version maintains the core story of the original film while making some notable changes. The Emerson family—older brother Michael, younger Sam, and their mother Lucy—moves to Santa Carla, California, where they discover the seaside town harbors dangerous vampire activity. Michael falls under the spell of Star, not realizing she's been tasked with turning him by the vampire leader David. Sam instead allies with a pair of would-be vampire hunters known as the Frog brothers.

Arden's direction emphasizes the film's Southern California gothic atmosphere through visual design. The production uses neon-sign lighting, pyrotechnics, and mid-air stunts to create an immersive experience. Notable sequences from the film, such as Michael's vampire initiation ritual involving a fall from a railway bridge, translate effectively to the stage with real performers executing the physical stunts.

The stage adaptation makes some textual updates while maintaining the 1987 setting. Sam is strongly implied to be gay, and one of the Frog brothers is played by a woman who insists on being referred to as male. These changes reflect contemporary expressions of gender and sexuality while the characters remain sincere in their performances.

The musical score comes from rock act the Rescues. However, critics have noted that the songs don't particularly capture the film's west-coast punk aesthetic or evoke the period convincingly. The music leans toward emo power ballads and modern Broadway styles rather than goth-rock elements that might better suit the vampire story.

The production makes significant design choices regarding scope and focus. A large Arizona postcard serves as a backdrop for a brief driving scene, exemplifying the show's elaborate production values. The streamlining of the original story, including omitting one character entirely, helps concentrate the narrative, though this also shifts focus toward the mother character's emotional journey at the expense of some of the material's youth-culture elements.

Arden has also announced plans to direct a Broadway musical adaptation of Happy Feet, expanding his portfolio of adaptations that bring unexpected source material to the stage. His approach to unusual properties continues shaping contemporary Broadway offerings.

The 12 nominations position The Lost Boys among the most recognized productions of the theater season. The Tony Awards will determine whether this vampire musical achieves recognition comparable to Arden's previous success.
