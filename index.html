10:00 AM ET

Kane Parsons has made history as the youngest filmmaker to open a movie at number one at the North American box office. The 20-year-old YouTube creator's directorial debut, Backrooms, earned $81 million domestically and $118 million worldwide in its opening weekend, shattering industry expectations and setting a new studio record for A24.

Parsons surpassed the previous record holder Josh Trank, who was 27 when his 2011 debut Chronicle opened with $22 million. The scale of Backrooms' performance demonstrates a dramatic generational shift in how quickly young creators can transition from digital platforms to major studio releases.

The film adapts Parsons' web series concept for theatrical audiences. A24 tapped screenwriter Will Soodik to develop the feature, bringing Parsons' online horror concept to mainstream cinemas. The production design combines physical construction with digital effects to create vast, unsettling spaces. Cinematographer Jeremy Cox shot the film using a distinctive yellowish light that evokes the fluorescent glow of shopping malls and office buildings.

The cast includes Chiwetel Ejiofor as Clark, a failed architect and struggling manager of a discount furniture store, and Renate Reinsve as Mary, his therapist. The story centers on Clark's discovery of a supernatural section of wall in his store's basement that leads to an infinite network of mysterious rooms. As both characters venture deeper into this strange space, they struggle to find their way back.

The film's success exceeded what observers anticipated for a debut feature from an internet creator. A24 has built its reputation releasing art house and horror films, but had never achieved an opening weekend at this scale. The previous studio record was set by Alex Garland's 2024 film, which Backrooms more than tripled.

A24's decision to release Backrooms in wide theatrical distribution rather than limiting it to specialty venues proved strategically sound. Strong ticket sales came from audiences across the country and internationally, suggesting that Parsons' appeal extends beyond his core YouTube fanbase of English-speaking viewers.

The opening weekend performance suggests studios may pursue other internet creators with significant digital followings as potential theatrical filmmakers. Backrooms demonstrates genuine audience appetite for horror content and the commercial viability of creators who build substantial fan communities online before transitioning to traditional filmmaking.

Parsons' achievement underscores how digital platforms have created alternative pathways to studio filmmaking. Where previous generations of directors needed to work through film school or traditional industry channels, creators like Parsons can now build large enough followings to attract major studio attention directly. The box office numbers suggest audiences are willing to support films from creators they already follow online when given the theatrical opportunity.
