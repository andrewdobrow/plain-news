A Blue Origin New Glenn rocket exploded during a static fire test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida on Thursday evening, creating a significant setback for the company and NASA's lunar exploration plans. The massive fireball destroyed the launchpad at Kennedy Space Center seconds after the test began at 9pm ET, with the explosion's glow visible over 115 miles away in Fort Pierce and shockwaves felt across Florida's space coast.

The incident occurred just days after NASA announced that Blue Origin had won a contract to launch the first of three uncrewed lunar missions planned for this year to begin construction of a $20 billion moon base. NASA's Artemis program aims to return astronauts to the Moon for the first time since 1972, with missions scheduled for 2027 and 2028. Blue Origin is developing the Blue Moon lander that will transport astronauts from the Orion capsule to the lunar surface, making this explosion a direct threat to the agency's timeline.

Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos confirmed in a statement that all employees were safe and uninjured. He described it as "a very rough day" and said the company was working to determine the root cause. "It's too early to know but we're already working to find it. We'll rebuild whatever needs rebuilding and get back to flying," Bezos wrote.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman announced that a full evaluation of mission timelines would be conducted following the explosion. He acknowledged that "spaceflight is unforgiving, and developing new heavy-lift launch capability is extraordinarily difficult." The agency committed to working with Blue Origin to support a thorough investigation and assess impacts to the Artemis and Moon Base programs.

This explosion represents the latest in a series of setbacks for Blue Origin as it competes with SpaceX for NASA contracts. Last month, a payload from New Glenn's third flight ended up in the wrong orbit, leading the Federal Administration to temporarily ground the rocket. Thursday's test was the first static fire test since the FAA cleared it to return to flight.

The launchpad remained engulfed in flames for more than two hours after the explosion. Emergency officials reported no threat from fumes or other hazards. The incident triggered widespread attention on social media, with residents from nearby Cape Canaveral and Cocoa Beach reporting shaking homes and posting photos of the orange fireball visible from the beach.

NASA's plans to establish a sustained human presence on the Moon now depend on a swift investigation and resolution of whatever technical issues caused the explosion. The space industry has faced multiple setbacks in recent years as companies develop new launch systems, with comprehensive reviews typically requiring months or sometimes years before operations resume.

By October 2025, nine months into his second term, his rating had already fallen to 42 percent, matching the level Biden reached more than three years into his presidency, according to researchers at the University of Massachusetts Lowell.

Several factors appear to be contributing to the falling support. Voters have expressed significant disapproval of Trump's economic policies. An estimated 64 percent of voters disapprove of his handling of the economy, traditionally considered one of Trump's strongest issues. A majority of voters have also criticized his management of the cost of living, immigration, and the Israel-Palestine conflict.

Economic hardship has become a central concern for Americans. The nationwide average fuel price now stands at almost 4.52 dollars per gallon, up from 3.18 dollars a year ago. Among independent voters, 47 percent say his policies have personally hurt them, compared with 41 percent in the previous year.

The administration's approach to deportations has also affected his support. According to the researchers, confrontations during deportation operations, in which two U.S. citizens were killed by federal agents, undermined his backing. The fallout from the decision to join Israel in striking Iran, along with the resulting oil crisis, appears to have accelerated the decline.

Recent polling shows that most American voters believe the decision to go to war with Iran was wrong. Nearly two-thirds of voters said entering the conflict had been the wrong choice, and fewer than one in four Americans said the war had been worth the costs.

When asked about the economic hardship inflicted on Americans, Trump said: "I don't think about Americans' financial situation."
