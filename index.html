12:59 AM ET

The United States launched a second round of military strikes against Iranian targets within three days, escalating hostilities even as a fragile ceasefire technically remains in place between the two nations. The attacks have triggered severe disruptions to global oil markets and critical Middle Eastern infrastructure.

Oil prices surged dramatically following the strikes. Brent crude, the international benchmark, rallied 9 percent to reach $100.29 per barrel on Thursday before settling back to around $98, still up approximately 6 percent. West Texas Intermediate US crude climbed 8.6 percent to $94.75 a barrel. The price of oil had stood at just $60 a barrel at the start of the year and broke the $100 mark for the first time in four years on Monday, when it spiked as high as $119.

Iran responded with retaliatory strikes targeting economic and energy infrastructure across the region. Several merchant ships were struck in and around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments. Three crew members aboard the Thai-registered vessel Mayuree Naree were reported trapped following an attack. Iraq halted all operations at its oil ports after attacks on nearby tankers, while Bahrain advised residents to stay indoors following an Iranian strike on fuel tanks. Oman shifted all vessels from its main oil export terminal at Mina Al Fahal after drone strikes at another port.

The escalating conflict has damaged key oil and gas infrastructure across the Middle East. The International Energy Agency warned that the war is expected to cut the region's oil and gas production by at least 10 million barrels per day. The agency ordered the largest release of government reserves in its history on Wednesday, with its 32 member nations unanimously agreeing to release 400 million barrels of emergency crude.

Despite the massive reserve release, the conflict continues to disrupt global energy supplies. The IEA forecasts a global oil output decline of 8 million barrels per day this year, even accounting for increased production from countries including Russia. The agency cut 1 million barrels per day from its global oil demand forecasts due to lower refining and air travel in the Middle East.

Iran's military command escalated rhetoric, warning that oil prices could reach $200 a barrel. A military spokesperson stated: "Get ready for oil to be $200 a barrel, because the oil price depends on regional security, which you have destabilised."

Goldman Sachs raised its forecast for Brent crude in the fourth quarter of 2026 to $71 per barrel, up from $66. The soaring energy costs are expected to weigh on global economic growth, potentially reducing demand further. Asian stock markets came under pressure, with Japan's Nikkei 225 dropping 1.6 percent and South Korea's Kospi index falling 1.2 percent. European natural gas prices rose 7.7 percent for a second consecutive day.

Yesterday, 10:47 PM ET

The Australian government has filed a lawsuit against 3M seeking A$2 billion in damages over contamination from firefighting foam at defense sites. The case represents the largest ever legal action brought by the Australian government.

The lawsuit centers on contamination from chemicals known as PFAS, often called forever chemicals because they persist in the environment and human body. These substances were used in firefighting foams at military installations across Australia.

3M, a US manufacturing giant, produced the foam products that contained the chemicals. PFAS compounds have been linked to various health concerns and can contaminate soil and water supplies at sites where the foams were used or stored.

The Australian government is seeking compensation for the cleanup costs and environmental damage at multiple defense sites where the contamination occurred. The chemicals can migrate through soil and groundwater, affecting areas beyond the original application sites.

The scale of the legal action reflects both the extent of the contamination and the significant costs associated with remediation efforts. Defense installations typically used these foams extensively for training exercises and emergency response preparations over many years.

The case joins a growing number of legal actions worldwide against manufacturers of PFAS-containing products. Multiple countries and jurisdictions have moved to restrict or ban the use of these chemicals in firefighting foams due to environmental and health concerns.

3M has faced similar litigation in other countries, including the United States, where the company has settled cases related to PFAS contamination. The company previously announced plans to exit PFAS manufacturing by the end of 2025.

The Australian lawsuit seeks to recover funds the government has spent or will spend addressing the contamination at affected sites. Cleanup operations at PFAS-contaminated sites can take years and require specialized techniques to remove or contain the chemicals.

Rayo's manager Iñigo Pérez sent substitutes onto the pitch to pressure Palace's defense, but the English club held firm. The Spanish side reached their first final in 102 years of existence and created several chances, particularly in the first half when they dominated possession. Palace, by contrast, were largely unimpressive before the interval. However, they transformed their performance after halftime, with Mateta hitting the woodwork on another occasion and the Rayo goalkeeper making important saves.

The final was attended by around 15,000 Palace supporters who traveled to Germany. Before the match, both sets of fans unveiled large tifos, with Palace's banner referencing their rescue from financial ruin and their recent FA Cup victory.

Glasner paid tribute to the team's spirit following the match, describing the achievement as making history in south London. He insisted he had no regrets about his decision to depart the club despite winning three trophies in 12 months. The manager's tactical adjustments during the second half proved decisive, as Palace shifted from their first-half struggles to control the match and secure the trophy.

The Conference League title marks a significant turning point for a club that had finished 15th in the Premier League. Their path through European competition to becoming continental champions represents an extraordinary transformation under Glasner's leadership.

The Department of Justice has opened a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll, who accused President Trump of sexual assault, according to multiple reports.

CNN first reported the investigation, which focuses on whether Carroll committed perjury during a deposition she gave as part of her two civil lawsuits against Trump. The probe marks a significant shift in the government's relationship with Carroll, who won substantial judgments against Trump in civil court.

Carroll filed two defamation lawsuits against Trump after he denied her allegations that he sexually assaulted her in a department store dressing room in the 1990s. Trump called her accusations false and suggested she fabricated the story to sell books. Juries in federal court found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation, awarding Carroll millions of dollars in damages.

The Justice Department has not publicly commented on the reported investigation. Federal perjury charges require prosecutors to prove that a witness knowingly made false statements under oath during testimony that was material to the proceeding.

The opening of a criminal investigation into a plaintiff who successfully sued the president raises questions about potential conflicts of interest within the Justice Department. The department has traditionally maintained independence from White House influence in criminal matters, though that wall has been tested throughout various administrations.

Carroll's legal team has not issued a public statement about the reported investigation. The defamation verdicts against Trump came after years of legal battles, during which Carroll testified extensively about her allegations and the impact of Trump's denials on her reputation and career.

The investigation comes at a time when Trump has faced multiple legal challenges, though many have been resolved or dismissed. The former president has consistently denied all of Carroll's allegations and has criticized the civil verdicts as politically motivated.

Prosecutors in the Southern District of New York are reportedly handling the investigation, according to the reports. That office has jurisdiction over federal crimes committed in Manhattan, where Carroll's depositions took place.
