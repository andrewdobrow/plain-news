A few minutes ago

A divided panel of appeals court judges has ruled that a Trump administration policy illegally banned transgender troops from military service. The decision from the US Court of Appeals for Washington DC represents a significant legal setback for the Pentagon's implementation of the restrictions.

The three-judge panel determined that the ban on transgender military service was "arbitrary, and based on animus," according to the majority opinion. However, the split nature of the decision indicates disagreement among the judges about the full scope of the ruling's implications.

The court's decision allows transgender service members currently in the military to remain in their positions. However, the armed services can continue to block transgender individuals from enlisting in the future, creating a complicated legal landscape for prospective service members.

The Trump administration is expected to challenge the ruling, and legal experts anticipate the case will eventually reach the US Supreme Court. The decision adds to ongoing legal battles over military service eligibility and anti-discrimination protections.

The legal challenge was brought on behalf of transgender service members who argued that the policy discriminated against them and prevented qualified individuals from serving their country. Before the ban was implemented, the Pentagon had developed policies to integrate transgender service members, including protocols for medical care and transition-related procedures.

The case has been working its way through the federal court system, with various rulings at different levels addressing the legality of the restrictions. Advocates for transgender rights have argued that qualified individuals should not be excluded from military service based on gender identity.

Supporters of the restrictions have cited concerns about military readiness and unit cohesion, though legal challenges have questioned whether these rationales provide sufficient constitutional justification for the policy.

The Pentagon will need to determine its next steps following the ruling. Options available to the administration include appealing to a higher court or revising its current policies regarding transgender service members.

The split decision from the appeals court suggests that legal and political disagreements over transgender military service remain significant. Different judges have reached different conclusions about whether the policy can withstand constitutional scrutiny, foreshadowing potentially contentious arguments if the case reaches the Supreme Court.

The ruling affects current and prospective military personnel who identify as transgender. As the case moves through the legal system, the final resolution will likely have substantial implications for military personnel policies and broader questions about anti-discrimination protections in federal employment and service.

Russia launched a massive overnight assault on multiple Ukrainian cities using hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles, killing at least 16 people and injuring many others. Among the confirmed dead was an eight-year-old boy, whose body was recovered from the ruins of an apartment building.

According to Ukraine's air force, Russia deployed 73 missiles and 656 drones in the coordinated attack, including eight hypersonic Tsirkon missiles. The strikes targeted five major cities: Kyiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Poltava, and Kharkiv. The bombardment lasted through the night, with fires breaking out across affected areas and multiple residential blocks sustaining significant damage.

In Dnipro, five people were killed and numerous others injured. Rescue workers discovered four bodies in the ruins of a destroyed house and continued searching for additional victims the following morning. Oleksandr Hanzha, the regional head of Dnipropletrovsk, reported that the strikes damaged apartment blocks, businesses, and private residences. Another person was killed in a separate strike in the same region on Saturday afternoon, bringing the local death toll to at least six.

Ukrainian authorities reported successfully intercepting most of the incoming drones, though many still reached their targets. Odesa and Kharkiv also came under attack, with at least three people wounded in those cities.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the pattern of Russian tactics on social media, stating that "strike drones, cruise missiles and a significant amount of ballistics" targeted civilian infrastructure. He noted that residential buildings, energy facilities, and enterprises sustained damage across multiple locations. Following the attacks, Zelenskyy appealed to Donald Trump to send Patriot missiles to Ukraine to strengthen its air defense capabilities.

The overnight assault came after Russia had issued repeated warnings of a major strike over the preceding week. This delay between threats and execution appeared designed to inflict psychological pressure on Ukrainian civilians. The strategy represented a tactical shift for Moscow, weaponizing the threat itself alongside the eventual bombardment.

The strikes also followed a prisoner exchange on Friday in which Russia and Ukraine exchanged 193 service members as part of US-brokered negotiations. Those talks have produced no progress on key issues that might end Russia's invasion.

Ukrainian forces responded with their own long-range drone operations deep inside Russian territory. A woman was killed and a man seriously injured when Ukrainian drones struck the Russian border region of Belgorod.

The latest assault underscores the continuing toll of Russia's campaign against Ukrainian population centers, combining conventional military strikes with psychological warfare tactics against civilian populations.

3:54 AM ET

The Trump administration proposed a 25 percent tariff on Brazilian imports, citing what it described as unfair trade practices. The announcement represents the latest expansion of the administration's tariff agenda through Section 301 investigations, a mechanism that allows the government to impose trade penalties on countries it determines engage in practices harmful to American commerce.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 permits the United States Trade Representative to investigate and respond to foreign trade practices considered unjustified, unreasonable, or discriminatory. The administration has increasingly used this tool to rebuild its tariff framework following earlier trade policy initiatives. Brazil joins a growing list of countries facing heightened trade barriers as part of this strategy.

The proposed tariff applies broadly to Brazilian goods entering the United States. Brazil exports a range of products to the US market, including agricultural commodities, steel, and manufactured goods. The 25 percent rate would significantly increase costs for importers and could affect supply chains dependent on Brazilian inputs. American companies that rely on Brazilian materials or finished products may face higher expenses, which could be passed along to consumers.

Brazil has not yet formally responded to the tariff proposal. The South American nation is a major trading partner of the United States, and the tariff could prompt retaliatory measures or diplomatic efforts to resolve the dispute. Previous tariff actions under Section 301 have led to prolonged trade tensions and negotiations, with varying outcomes depending on the countries involved.

The administration's move signals continued willingness to use tariffs as a tool for addressing perceived trade imbalances. The proposal will likely undergo a review period during which affected parties can submit comments before the tariff takes effect. The timing and implementation details will determine the full economic impact on both countries and on businesses operating across their shared trade relationship.

Rick Adelman, a Hall of Fame basketball coach who compiled 1,042 regular season wins during his 23-year NBA career, has died at 79. Multiple NBA organizations and media outlets confirmed his passing.

Adelman coached five NBA teams: the Portland Trail Blazers, Golden State Warriors, Sacramento Kings, Houston Rockets, and Minnesota Timberwolves. He became particularly well-known for his time leading the Trail Blazers in the 1990s and the Kings in the early 2000s, where his teams gained recognition for their strong offensive play and emphasis on team-based basketball.

With 1,042 regular season wins, Adelman ranks among the winningest coaches in NBA history. His teams made the playoffs 16 times throughout his career, and he guided the Trail Blazers to the NBA Finals on two occasions. The Basketball Hall of Fame recognized his contributions to professional basketball with his induction.

Throughout his coaching tenure, Adelman worked with numerous All-Star players and earned respect across the league for his offensive systems and talent management. His coaching philosophy centered on ball movement and developing players, principles that defined his approach with each franchise he led.

The Sacramento Kings released a statement acknowledging his death, joining other organizations in mourning his passing. The Trail Blazers, Kings, and numerous other NBA teams recognized the significant impact Adelman had on professional basketball.

Major sports media outlets including ESPN, Yahoo Sports, and The New York Times reported on his death, reflecting his widespread influence on the sport. After retiring from his coaching position, Adelman remained connected to the basketball community and maintained relationships within professional basketball circles.

Adelman's career success and longevity in the NBA made him one of the most respected figures in basketball coaching history. His emphasis on team play and offensive strategy influenced how basketball was played during his era, and his legacy extends beyond his win-loss record to include the many players he developed and the coaching principles he established. The basketball world mourned the loss of one of its most accomplished and respected coaches.

Owens said CBS News and 60 Minutes are institutions that should not employ partisans and ideologues. His remarks came as the show operates under new leadership following significant staff departures.

The criticism reflects escalating tensions at the program. Veteran correspondent Sharyn Alfonsi, who spent nearly two decades at CBS News and more than a decade at 60 Minutes, saw her contract expire without renewal. She accused the network of penalizing her for refusing to compromise factually accurate reporting after an editorial dispute over a segment about a prison in El Salvador.

Alfonsi's contract lapse came after CBS News editor-in-chief Bari Weiss shelved the prison story. In a statement, Alfonsi warned that the network was abandoning its commitment to fearless, independent reporting. She argued that CBS management was choosing access journalism over accountability and protecting power rather than scrutinizing it.

"This was not a routine corporate transition," Alfonsi wrote. "It was a deliberate choice to penalize a journalist for refusing to sanitize factually accurate reporting, and it sends a chilling message to the entire newsroom."

Fellow correspondent Scott Pelley expressed similar concerns during a heated staff meeting. According to reporting, Pelley directly confronted the show's newly appointed executive producer Nick Bilton and CBS News managing editor Charles Forelle, taking aim at Weiss over the personnel changes and editorial decisions affecting the program.

The departures extend beyond Alfonsi. Correspondent Cecilia Vega also left as part of a broader overhaul. Additionally, Anderson Cooper opted to depart the show after the shake-up. In comments tied to his exit, Cooper emphasized that "the independence of 60 Minutes has been critical" and expressed hope that the program would maintain its core identity.

The turmoil represents a significant challenge for a program that has been considered one of broadcast journalism's most respected institutions since its debut in 1968. The show built its reputation on investigative reporting and in-depth interviews, earning numerous awards and maintaining strong viewership for decades.

The public nature of the criticism from multiple former and current staffers underscores the depth of concern about the program's editorial direction. Multiple journalists have characterized recent changes as representing corporate interference in editorial decisions and a departure from the show's traditional mission of holding power accountable.

The 59th season will proceed without several prominent correspondents, marking a significant transition for the newsmagazine as it navigates questions about its future editorial approach and institutional values.
