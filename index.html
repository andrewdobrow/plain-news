2:34 PM ET

President Trump has appointed Bill Pulte, head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, as acting director of national intelligence, days after Tulsi Gabbard's departure from the role. Pulte, an heir to the PulteGroup home construction fortune, has no known background in national security or intelligence work.

Trump announced the decision on his Truth Social platform, saying "William has deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America." Pulte will retain his position leading the FHFA, which oversees federal housing lenders Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

The appointment has generated significant concern among Republicans and observers. Senate Majority Leader John Thune told reporters that the intelligence community needs "professionals" and cautioned that Pulte would face "a lengthy road ahead" if Trump nominated him for permanent confirmation. "We don't need a weaponized" national intelligence director, Thune said, noting that any permanent appointment would require Senate approval and extensive hearings.

Pulte's tenure at the FHFA has drawn scrutiny. Since taking the role, he has used the agency to level allegations against Trump's political opponents, including accusing Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook of mortgage fraud. Cook has denied wrongdoing. A former high-ranking official described his handling of the Cook case as "very bizarre."

Critics have highlighted Pulte's lack of government experience before joining the Trump administration. Upon arriving at the FHFA, he fired members of the boards of both Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and appointed himself as chair. The New York Times reported that Pulte no longer holds any official connection to PulteGroup, having been removed from the company's board in 2020.

His family has also distanced itself from him. The Pulte Family Charitable Foundation issued a statement in 2023 saying that Pulte "is not a representative of, nor has he served in any capacity" for the organization, and that some of his public communications "may have created confusion" with the family's charitable endeavors.

Gabbard's exit from the intelligence director role came after a turbulent tenure marked by being largely sidelined during Trump's military operations and foreign policy decisions. Trump had been asking cabinet members as early as April about replacing her, according to people briefed on internal discussions.

The position of director of national intelligence coordinates intelligence gathering across federal agencies including the CIA and NSA. The role was created after the September 11 attacks to improve communication between intelligence agencies. The acting designation allows Pulte to serve without Senate confirmation for a limited period, though any permanent appointment would require approval from the Senate intelligence committee and full chamber confirmation.

1:48 PM ET

Russia launched a massive overnight assault using hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles against multiple Ukrainian cities, killing at least 22 people and trapping others beneath collapsed buildings. The coordinated attack targeted Kyiv and other major population centers across the country.

According to Ukraine's air force, Russia fired 73 missiles and 656 drones in the overnight strikes. The weapons included eight hypersonic Tsirkon missiles. The main targets were Kyiv, the central cities of Dnipro and Zaporizhzhia, and the eastern cities of Poltava and Kharkiv.

In Dnipro, the strikes proved particularly devastating, with at least five people killed in that city alone. Local authorities reported that four bodies were found in the ruins of a destroyed house, and workers continued searching for survivors on Saturday morning. The attacks caused fires to break out across Dnipro and partly destroyed several apartment blocks, businesses, and a private house. Another person was killed in a separate strike in the region on Saturday afternoon. Overall, at least 34 people were injured across the strikes.

Odesa and Kharkiv were also targeted by drones, with at least three people injured in those cities. Ukrainian air defense officials said they repelled most of the more than 600 Russian drones launched during the assault, which appears to have been the largest attack in several days.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the Russian tactics on social media, noting that the strikes focused on civilian infrastructure. "Most of the targets are ordinary infrastructure in cities," Zelenskyy wrote. "Residential buildings, energy, and enterprises have been damaged."

The overnight assault followed a prisoner exchange on Friday, in which Russia and Ukraine exchanged 193 service members. That exchange was one outcome of US-brokered negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv, though the talks have made no progress on key issues that would end Russia's invasion.

In response to the strikes, Zelenskyy asked US President Donald Trump to send Patriot missiles to Ukraine to help defend against future attacks.

Ukraine also carried out some of its longest-distance drone strikes deep inside Russian territory during the same period. In Russia's border region of Belgorod, a woman was killed and a man seriously injured by a Ukrainian drone strike.

Ukrainian authorities continued to assess the full extent of the damage and casualties from the overnight attack. Emergency services worked to clear debris and search for survivors in affected areas across multiple cities.

SpaceX has acquired Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company xAI in a transformative merger that could reshape the aerospace and AI sectors. The deal, valued at $1.25 trillion combined, consolidates major parts of Musk's business empire ahead of a planned public offering expected in early summer.

The merger brings together SpaceX's rocket launch operations and satellite internet service Starlink with xAI's artificial intelligence technology, including the Grok chatbot and the social media platform X. In a joint statement, the companies described the combined entity as "the most ambitious, vertically-integrated innovation engine on (and off) Earth, with AI, rockets, space-based internet, direct-to-mobile device communications and the world's foremost real-time information and free speech platform."

The deal reportedly values SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion. The combined business is on track for a stock market listing that would exceed $1 trillion in valuation, positioning it as one of the largest IPOs ever.

The merger announcement cited space-based artificial intelligence infrastructure as a key strategic rationale. The companies stated that current AI advancement depends on massive terrestrial datacenters requiring enormous amounts of power and cooling. They argued that global electricity demand for AI cannot be met through ground-based solutions without causing hardship to communities and the environment. "In the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale," the announcement said.

Musk has pursued plans to deploy datacenters and solar-powered satellites in space to power artificial intelligence systems, an undertaking of significant technical and financial complexity.

SpaceX has experienced dramatic valuation growth in recent years. The company sent a letter to investors in December revealing an expected valuation of $800 billion, based on its dominance in satellite launches and extensive contracts with the U.S. federal government.

xAI has similarly expanded its valuation amid the artificial intelligence investment boom. The company announced a $20 billion Series E funding round last month from major investors, valuing it at $230 billion.

The IPO is expected to occur in early summer, with speculation that the timing could coincide with a planetary alignment and Musk's birthday in late June.

This consolidation represents Musk's broader strategy of integrating his various business ventures. Earlier in 2025, xAI acquired the platform X in an all-stock transaction. Tesla also revealed plans to invest $2 billion in xAI last month.

The news provides a positive development for Musk following recent challenges. Tesla released an earnings report showing declining revenues and weakness in its automobile business, and Musk has faced scrutiny following the Department of Justice's release of documents related to Jeffrey Epstein.

Dalglish revealed the news himself, saying: "As my inadvertent social media post has indicated, I am currently undergoing treatment for cancer. Unlike my mobile phone use, the treatment is going well." He expressed regret that the matter had become public, stating: "Ideally, this would have remained private because that's the way it should be, but my useless technology skills have forced my hand."

The former player and manager requested privacy for himself and his family as he continues his treatment. Dalglish enjoyed a legendary playing career at multiple clubs, beginning with Celtic before his famous move to Liverpool, where he became a central figure during the club's dominant era. He won numerous league titles and European trophies as a player and later managed Liverpool through significant periods, including the aftermath of the Hillsborough disaster in 1989, when he provided crucial support to affected families.

Beyond his time at Liverpool, Dalglish managed Blackburn Rovers, leading them to the Premier League title in 1995, and also managed Newcastle United. His contributions to football earned him a knighthood, recognizing both his sporting achievements and his broader impact on the game.

The announcement came on the same day that another Liverpool legend, John Barnes, revealed he had undergone prostate removal following a cancer diagnosis. Barnes said he is now "fine" and encouraged men to undergo testing and speak openly about such health issues, describing it as a taboo subject that needs greater awareness.

Dalglish remains widely considered Liverpool's greatest player and most revered figure at the club, respected not only for his outstanding talent as both player and manager but also for his character and leadership during the club's most challenging moments.

The turmoil at the Kennedy Center has triggered widespread backlash from the arts community. Renowned composer Philip Glass, an 88-year-old Kennedy Center Honors recipient, withdrew the world premiere of his Symphony No. 15 "Lincoln" scheduled for June 12 and 13 in protest. Glass stated that the values of the Kennedy Center under its current leadership directly conflict with the message of his symphony, which portrays Abraham Lincoln. He felt obligated to withdraw the premiere.

Glass's decision reflects broader opposition from musical artists who oppose the administration's policies. The Kennedy Center has experienced a series of cancellations from performers voicing their concerns. In December, the Trump administration changed the venue's name to the "Trump-Kennedy Center," a move that intensified the backlash.

The administration has pursued what it describes as controversial changes to the institution's name and mission. Critics argue that the Trump administration's focus on what it considers "woke" or overly leftist culture politicizes the arts and alienates performers. Rhode Island senator Sheldon Whitehouse, an ex officio member of the Kennedy Center governing board, compared the situation to a takeover. "When the brigands took the ship, their first instinct was to loot it for their own benefit and hire their friends," he told the Guardian.

The National Symphony Orchestra has served as the Kennedy Center's resident orchestra since the venue opened in 1971. The ensemble performs classical concerts and provides the pit orchestra for performances at the venue. As a public-private partnership, the Kennedy Center relies on federal funding and private donations.

According to Folds, the workers at the Kennedy Center are the most harmed by the current situation, particularly the musicians of the National Symphony Orchestra. While he did not specify the exact operational or financial challenges the orchestra faces, he indicated that the ensemble's existence is now imperiled.

The crisis at the Kennedy Center occurs at a time when many performing arts institutions continue to struggle with financial pressures following pandemic-related disruptions. The National Symphony Orchestra, one of the nation's major orchestras, now faces an uncertain future under the new leadership at what has long been the nation's premier performing arts venue.
