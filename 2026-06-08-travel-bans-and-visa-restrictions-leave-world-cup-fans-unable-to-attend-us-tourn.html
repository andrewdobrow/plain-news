U.S. 2026-06-08

Travel bans and visa restrictions leave World Cup fans unable to attend U.S. tournament

Fans from around the world are expressing frustration that U.S. travel restrictions and visa requirements are preventing them from attending the 2026 World Cup, with many feeling excluded from a tournament designed to celebrate global sport.

The restrictions affect potential attendees from multiple countries who face either outright travel bans or challenging visa application processes. The complaints arrive as the United States prepares to co-host the tournament with Canada and Mexico, marking the first World Cup held on American soil since 1994.

The tension between U.S. immigration policy and the demands of hosting a global sporting event has become increasingly apparent. World Cup tournaments typically attract hundreds of thousands of international visitors over several weeks, requiring host nations to accommodate massive numbers of short-term travelers from all countries. The current U.S. travel framework was not originally designed to manage such a large influx.

FIFA, soccer's international governing body, has not publicly stated whether it is working with U.S. authorities to establish temporary visa pathways or exemptions for ticketed fans. Other World Cup host nations have previously streamlined their processes by implementing temporary visa waivers or simplified application procedures to ensure fans could attend matches. It remains uncertain what measures the U.S. will implement.

The access challenges complicate an already complex logistical undertaking. The 2026 World Cup will be the first to feature 48 teams, an expansion from the traditional 32-team format, and matches will be distributed across cities in three countries across multiple time zones. The tournament is projected to generate billions in economic activity, but achieving those figures depends significantly on international visitors being able to enter the country.

Emma Hayes, writing about the tournament's scope, notes the geographical challenges involved. She describes how vast North America is, emphasizing that the land mass of England could fit comfortably into Georgia alone. With 104 matches scheduled across 16 venues spanning three time zones, traveling between games will be taxing for fans already facing high ticket prices.

The scale of the tournament presents unprecedented challenges. Teams will need to adjust to extensive travel between matches played thousands of miles apart in different countries. Fans planning to follow their national teams will encounter similar complications, potentially discouraging attendance.

The combination of travel restrictions, visa difficulties, and the tournament's logistical demands has created barriers that may limit the international attendance the event typically generates. This raises concerns about whether the World Cup, promoted as a celebration of global sport, will be equally accessible to fans worldwide or whether immigration policies and geography will determine who can participate in this historic tournament.
