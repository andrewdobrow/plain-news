Congress delays government funding and Iran war money until after midterms

Republican lawmakers have voted to postpone major legislative decisions until after the November midterm elections, setting up a crowded agenda for the final weeks of the year. The House overwhelmingly passed a bill to temporarily fund the government, pushing the deadline past Election Day and into the lame-duck session.

The postponed items include government funding, additional money for the ongoing conflict with Iran, and the extension of expanded surveillance authorities. GOP leaders chose to avoid these potentially divisive votes in the weeks leading up to the election, when control of the House hangs in the balance.

The decision creates a compressed timeline between the election and the Christmas recess, when lawmakers will need to address multiple high-stakes issues simultaneously. The lame-duck period, when some members will have already lost their seats or be preparing to leave office, typically makes legislative negotiations more challenging.

The strategy reflects the political sensitivity of these issues in an election year. Government funding debates often lead to shutdown threats, while votes on war spending and surveillance powers can divide both parties. By postponing these votes, Republican leadership aims to avoid giving Democratic challengers ammunition in competitive races.

The Iran war funding has become increasingly urgent as the conflict escalates. The U.S. has launched new airstrikes on Iranian targets, prompting counter strikes by Tehran targeting American interests in multiple locations in the Middle East, including intense ballistic missile attacks on U.S. bases. President Trump warned after Tuesday's U.S. strikes that any Iranian retaliation would be met with responses at "a much harder and higher level."

The escalating exchange of attacks has intensified concerns about a wider renewal of hostilities in the region. Russia's Vladimir Putin has signaled unwavering support for Iran, with Tehran thanking Moscow for backing amid the escalating Middle East war.

The surveillance authorities in question are set to expire, and their extension has drawn opposition from privacy advocates within both parties. Congressional leaders will need to navigate these competing pressures in a shortened timeframe after voters have already rendered their verdict on the current Congress.

The bloated lame-duck agenda will be difficult to complete by Christmas, according to congressional observers. The decision to delay these major decisions reflects the political calculations of Republican leadership heading into the midterm elections, but creates significant challenges for legislative action once the election results are determined.
