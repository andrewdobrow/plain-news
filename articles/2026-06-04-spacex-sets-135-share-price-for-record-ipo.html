SpaceX targets largest stock market debut in history with $135 per share IPO

SpaceX announced it will sell 555.6 million shares at $135 each in what would become the largest initial public offering in history, seeking to raise approximately $75 billion. The offering is scheduled to commence next week following a roadshow that began this week, in which investment banks pitch the company to potential investors.

At this valuation, SpaceX would achieve a market value of $1.77 trillion, making it worth more than all but five companies in the S&P 500 index. Nvidia, the semiconductor leader, remains the most valuable company at $5.2 trillion.

The IPO could substantially increase founder Elon Musk's wealth. According to Forbes, Musk's current net worth stands at $825 billion, with his SpaceX stake valued at $542 billion. If the offering proceeds as planned, he could become the world's first trillionaire, though he is not selling any personal shares in this offering. Musk will retain 82.4 percent voting power in the company following the IPO.

The space exploration company, formally known as Space Exploration Technologies Corp, was founded in 2002 and has become central to Musk's vision of establishing a self-sufficient city on Mars. SpaceX dominates commercial space transportation and provides crucial services to NASA for most of its rocket launches. The company also operates a satellite internet system and has entered the artificial intelligence sector.

SpaceX acquired Musk's xAI company to develop solar-powered infrastructure supporting AI technology demands. This move aligns with broader industry trends, as competitors including Anthropic and OpenAI are also pursuing public offerings or major fundraising rounds. Alphabet announced this week plans to raise $80 billion in equity specifically to fund artificial intelligence infrastructure investments.

The wave of substantial capital raises across the tech sector reflects efforts to finance the massive datacentres required for AI operations. These fundraising activities underscore how central infrastructure investment has become to competition in advanced technology.

The IPO represents a significant opportunity for the banking sector. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, with JP Morgan, Bank of America, Citigroup and 19 other banks participating. Jamie Dimon, CEO of JP Morgan Chase, is hosting a discussion with high-net-worth clients this week to discuss the SpaceX opportunity, according to reports. The discussion will include SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell and chief financial officer Bret Johnsen.

Market observers expect SpaceX to carry substantial weight in the S&P 500 given its size, meaning most investors with retirement accounts or pension plans will gain exposure to the company's stock performance. The listing comes as markets show mixed signals for technology stocks, though investor enthusiasm for space industry companies appears robust.
