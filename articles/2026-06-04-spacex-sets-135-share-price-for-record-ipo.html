SpaceX Prepares for Stock Market Debut as IPO Details Emerge

SpaceX is preparing to go public through an initial public offering priced at $135 per share, with the company planning to raise $75 billion from what would be the largest IPO in history. The stock market launch is scheduled for June 12, marking a transformative moment for the space industry and a potentially historic milestone for founder Elon Musk.

The company plans to sell 555.6 million shares, which would give SpaceX a market value of $1.77 trillion. Only six companies in the S&P 500 index are currently valued higher, with Nvidia leading at $5.2 trillion. The previous IPO record holder is Saudi Aramco, which floated in 2019 at a valuation of $1.7 trillion and raised $25.6 billion.

Musk is not selling any shares in the offering and will retain 82.4 percent of voting power in the company. According to Forbes, Musk's stake in SpaceX is valued at approximately $542 billion. If the IPO proceeds as planned, Musk's total net worth could potentially exceed $1 trillion, making him the world's first trillionaire.

SpaceX's sheer size means it will have substantial weighting in the S&P 500, exposing most people with investment accounts or pension funds to the company's stock performance. Up to a quarter of shares may be reserved for individual investors rather than institutional funds and banks, a larger allocation than is typical for mega-IPOs.

The sprawling SpaceX business encompasses three main divisions: the rocket-launching unit, the Starlink satellite broadband service, and the AI company that includes X, the social media platform formerly known as Twitter. While the overall business is loss-making, Starlink operates profitably. SpaceX lost $4.9 billion in 2025 on revenues of $18.7 billion, though revenue grew by one-third compared to the previous year.

The offering arrives as SpaceX continues developing its Starship vehicle, central to the company's Mars ambitions. The company has established itself as a leader in reusable rocket technology and operates critical contracts with NASA, which depends on SpaceX rockets for most of its launches. SpaceX has also acquired Musk's xAI company to develop solar-powered infrastructure supporting AI operations.

The IPO represents a significant opportunity for Wall Street banks competing for roles in the deal. Goldman Sachs leads the underwriting alongside Morgan Stanley, with JP Morgan, Bank of America, Citigroup, and 18 other banks participating. JP Morgan Chase boss Jamie Dimon is reportedly hosting discussions about the IPO with the bank's high net worth clients this week.

Some market observers view the offering as an opportunity to invest in Musk's entrepreneurial ventures and gain exposure to the AI boom. Longer-term speculation suggests a potential merger between SpaceX and Tesla could eventually consolidate Musk's various technology interests into a single investment vehicle.
