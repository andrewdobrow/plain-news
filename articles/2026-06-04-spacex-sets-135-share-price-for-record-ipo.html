SpaceX Sets $135 Share Price for Record IPO

SpaceX set a target price of $135 per share for its initial public offering, valuing Elon Musk's space exploration company at $1.77 trillion. The offering would mark the largest stock market debut in history, with the company planning to raise $75 billion when shares begin trading on Nasdaq next week.

The IPO could make Musk the world's first trillionaire, adding to his existing wealth from Tesla and other ventures. SpaceX announced the pricing target earlier than market analysts expected, signaling strong confidence in investor demand for the offering.

SpaceX has grown into the dominant player in commercial spaceflight over the past decade, operating the Starlink satellite internet service and providing launch services for NASA, the Pentagon, and commercial customers. The company holds contracts worth billions of dollars with the US government and has become the primary provider of crew and cargo transport to the International Space Station.

The company's valuation dwarfs previous IPO records and reflects investor appetite for space technology and satellite communications businesses. SpaceX's Starlink network now serves millions of subscribers globally, providing a recurring revenue stream alongside its launch business.

The offering comes as technology stocks have shown volatility, with chip makers facing investor concerns about growth forecasts. SpaceX's decision to move forward with the IPO despite market turbulence suggests the company and its underwriters believe demand will remain strong. Trading is scheduled to begin next week under a ticker symbol that has not yet been announced.
