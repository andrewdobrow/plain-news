Golden Tempo wins Belmont Stakes after Kentucky Derby victory

Golden Tempo won the 158th running of the Belmont Stakes, extending an earlier victory at the Kentucky Derby. The horse crossed the finish line at Saratoga Race Course in a nine-horse field that competed under rainy conditions.

The Belmont Stakes is one of thoroughbred racing's three Triple Crown events. Golden Tempo's success at both the Kentucky Derby and Belmont Stakes represents a significant achievement in horse racing. However, the horse did not compete in the Preakness Stakes, the middle leg of the Triple Crown series.

Weather conditions affected the race at Saratoga Race Course, where rain created slower track conditions throughout the competition. These circumstances influenced the pace and performance of all entries in the field. The win adds to Golden Tempo's strong showing in major stakes races this season.

The 158th edition of the Belmont Stakes featured multiple Grade 1 stakes races throughout the day at the upstate New York track. The Belmont Stakes has been held at different venues throughout its history. This year's running took place at Saratoga rather than at its traditional Belmont Park location.

Golden Tempo's victories in two of racing's most prestigious events represent a major accomplishment for the horse's ownership and training team. The Kentucky Derby and Belmont Stakes wins establish the horse among the year's top thoroughbred racing competitors, despite not attempting a full Triple Crown sweep.

The Triple Crown has been called the most difficult feat in American sports, requiring a horse to win three prestigious races held at different distances in different states over a five-week span. Only 13 horses have swept all three events since Sir Barton accomplished the feat in 1919. The most recent Triple Crown winner was Justify in 2018.

An increasing number of Derby winners have recently skipped the Preakness because of the short turnaround between the two races. Industry discussions have explored moving the Preakness to three weeks after the Derby rather than its current two-week timing, a change aimed at encouraging top horses to compete in all three Triple Crown races.

The racing landscape continues to shift as Churchill Downs Inc recently acquired intellectual property rights to the Preakness Stakes and the Black-Eyed Susan Stakes from 1/ST Maryland LLC. The acquisition brings the Preakness under the same corporate ownership as the Kentucky Derby. Churchill Downs said the transaction is expected to close after the 2026 running of the Preakness.

Meanwhile, media rights for the Preakness are in flux. NBC's broadcasting contract expires after this year's running. Multiple bidders including NBC, Fox, Amazon and Netflix have expressed interest in acquiring the next rights deal, underscoring the event's continued value in the sports landscape.
