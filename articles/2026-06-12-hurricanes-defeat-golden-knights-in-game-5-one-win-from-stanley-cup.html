Hurricanes defeat Golden Knights in Game 5, one win from Stanley Cup

The Carolina Hurricanes moved within one victory of their first Stanley Cup championship after defeating the Vegas Golden Knights 4-2 in Game 5 on Thursday in Raleigh, North Carolina.

Jordan Staal scored his sixth goal of the Stanley Cup final as Carolina secured the win to take a 3-2 series lead. The Hurricanes' top line delivered the offensive firepower needed to control the game and push Vegas to the brink of elimination.

Andrei Svechnikov also scored twice in the victory, highlighting Carolina's improved scoring production after several competitive back-and-forth contests earlier in the series. The performance from the team's top forwards proved decisive in a high-stakes playoff environment where consistency has been difficult to maintain.

The Hurricanes will attempt to clinch the second Stanley Cup title in franchise history when the series shifts to Vegas for Game 6 on Sunday night. A victory in Las Vegas would end Carolina's long championship quest, a scenario that represents a historic moment for the organization and its supporters.

The Golden Knights face elimination and must win on the road to force a decisive Game 7 and keep their championship hopes alive. Vegas had controlled portions of the series but struggled to find answers for Carolina's offense in Game 5. The Hurricanes capitalized on their scoring opportunities and maintained their composure throughout the contest.

Carolina's defensive structure and goaltending have been consistent throughout the postseason, providing a solid foundation for their run to the Stanley Cup final. These elements have allowed the team to withstand pressure and remain in control through the series despite some earlier inconsistency on the offensive end.

The Hurricanes' path to this position has been remarkable given how distant a championship seemed when the regular season began. Through their playoff performance, the team has worked its way to the brink of winning it all, earning a chance to potentially clinch at home in the next matchup.

For Carolina's fan base, this represents an unprecedented opportunity. The franchise has never won a Stanley Cup title, making this series run a watershed moment for the organization. The team now sits just one victory away from ending that historic drought.

Vegas will need to regroup and find a way to slow Carolina's offensive attack when the series resumes in Las Vegas. The Golden Knights showed resilience throughout the playoffs to reach the final, but the Hurricanes' current momentum and position in the series presents a formidable challenge for the defending champions.

Game 6 will determine whether Carolina can complete their championship quest or if Vegas can force a Game 7 back in Raleigh.
