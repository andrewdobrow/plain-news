Meta begins dismantling $2 billion Manus acquisition after Beijing demands reversal

Meta has started unwinding its $2 billion acquisition of Manus following a directive from Beijing ordering the deal reversed. The social media company is now working to dismantle the transaction, which had been one of its largest recent acquisitions.

The reversal comes after Chinese authorities intervened to block the deal. Manus, the company at the center of the acquisition, had been purchased by Meta as part of its expansion strategy. Beijing's demand for the deal's reversal represents a direct intervention in a major American tech transaction.

The move highlights ongoing tensions between Chinese regulatory authorities and American technology companies operating in or seeking to expand into Chinese markets. For Meta, which has faced regulatory scrutiny in multiple jurisdictions, the forced unwinding represents a significant setback to its strategic plans. The $2 billion price tag made the Manus acquisition one of the company's more substantial recent investments.

The process of dismantling an acquisition of this scale involves complex legal and financial steps. Meta will need to reverse ownership structures, potentially unwind integrated operations, and address contractual obligations created during the merger process. The timeline for completing the unwinding has not been disclosed.

This development adds to a growing list of instances where Chinese regulatory intervention has affected American tech companies' business plans. The forced reversal of a completed $2 billion acquisition demonstrates Beijing's willingness to exercise authority over deals involving companies that operate within or have ties to Chinese markets, even when those deals involve major American corporations.
