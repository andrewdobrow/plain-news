Russian Stadium Says It Will Not Host Planned Kanye West Concerts

A Russian stadium has announced it will not host two scheduled Kanye West concerts, creating significant uncertainty around the performances. The venue's decision directly contradicts claims made by Russian promoters organizing the shows, who maintain the concerts will proceed as planned.

The announcement leaves the status of West's Russian tour in limbo. While promoters have insisted the performances will go forward, they have not disclosed an alternative venue or explained how they intend to move forward without the originally scheduled location.

West's troubles securing performance spaces reflect broader challenges he has faced in recent years. The artist, who legally changed his name to Ye in 2021, has experienced multiple cancellations and lost business opportunities following various public statements and actions that generated widespread criticism.

Recent legal setbacks have added to these difficulties. Earlier this year, Ye lost a copyright lawsuit brought by four musicians who composed an instrumental sample used during a 2021 listening event in Atlanta. The four men, Khalil Abdul-Rahman, Sam Barsh, Josh Mease and Dan Seeff, created the instrumental in 2018. When Ye performed his album Donda at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in July 2021, an early version of the song Hurricane featured their uncleared sample. Though Ye later removed the sample from the publicly released version and added the four men to the songwriting credits, they argued they deserved compensation for the sample's use at the paid listening event.

"There was no deal, no agreement, no licence, and no clearance," the plaintiffs' lawyer argued in Los Angeles court. A jury sided with them, awarding a six-figure sum. During the hearing, Ye told the court: "I pride myself on giving people what they deserve," though he also claimed "a lot of people try to take advantage of me." His legal team characterized the verdict as a "failed shakedown."

This copyright loss represents the second lawsuit Ye lost this year. He was previously ordered to pay 140,000 dollars to a handyman who claimed he was not paid for renovation work at a Malibu property owned by Ye.

Beyond legal disputes, antisemitic remarks and imagery associated with Ye have caused significant controversy. Despite this, he was booked for a three-night performance run at London's Wireless Festival scheduled for July. However, following opposition from Jewish groups and politicians including UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer and London Mayor Sadiq Khan, corporate sponsors withdrew from the festival. The UK Home Office subsequently banned Ye from entering the country, leading to the festival's cancellation.

The Russian stadium's refusal to host his concerts appears consistent with the broader pattern of venues and promoters distancing themselves from Ye.
