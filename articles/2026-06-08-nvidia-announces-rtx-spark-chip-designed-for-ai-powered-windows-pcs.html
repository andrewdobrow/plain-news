Nvidia and Microsoft unveil new PC chips designed for AI workloads

Nvidia announced RTX Spark, which the company describes as its most efficient PC chip to date, alongside Microsoft's Surface Laptop Ultra at the Computex technology trade show. The announcements represent a joint effort by the two companies to redesign Windows PCs around artificial intelligence capabilities.

The RTX Spark chip features modified Cortex-X925 cores, according to die analysis. Researchers identified attributes borrowed from MediaTek's Dimensity 9400 and Dimensity 9500 processors, specifically adapted to handle PC workloads. Nvidia positioned the chip as purpose-built for personal AI applications running on consumer hardware.

Microsoft showcased its Surface Laptop Ultra at the same event, integrating the new chip architecture into its laptop line. The device represents Microsoft's hardware response to growing demand for AI-capable personal computers. Nvidia and Microsoft framed the collaboration as part of a broader initiative to bring AI processing capabilities directly to consumer PCs rather than relying solely on cloud-based services.

The announcement reflects Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's strategy to compete across multiple layers of the AI technology stack. By entering the PC chip market with a focus on efficiency and AI-specific processing, Nvidia expands beyond its established position in data center graphics processors. The company now competes directly in the consumer PC market, where Intel and AMD have historically dominated.

The timing of the announcements at Computex, a major industry trade show for computer hardware, signals both companies' intent to establish early leadership in the emerging market for AI-enabled personal computing devices. The RTX Spark chip and Surface Laptop Ultra are expected to ship later this year, though specific release dates were not provided at the event.
