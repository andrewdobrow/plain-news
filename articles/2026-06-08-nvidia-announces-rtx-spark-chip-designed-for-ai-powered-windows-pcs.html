Nvidia announces RTX Spark chip designed for AI-powered Windows PCs

Nvidia has launched the RTX Spark, a new processor combining microprocessor and graphics chip capabilities for Windows laptops and desktop computers. The chip, developed in collaboration with Microsoft and Taiwan-based MediaTek, is designed to run artificial intelligence agents locally on personal computers rather than relying on cloud computing.

The RTX Spark will reach consumers this year through major computer manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP. According to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, speaking at the Computex conference in Taiwan, the chip represents a fundamental reimagining of personal computers "for the first time in 40 years." The company spent three years collaborating with Microsoft on the project.

The chip's most significant feature is its ability to enable AI agents to navigate computers autonomously, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interactions. Despite its power for running complex AI workloads, the chip maintains a design that allows computers to remain thin and light. Die analysis reveals the processor uses modified Cortex-X925 cores, with design elements adapted from MediaTek's Dimensity 9400 and Dimensity 9500 chips, modified specifically to handle typical PC workloads.

Microsoft demonstrated the technology through the Surface Laptop Ultra at Computex, showcasing how integrated AI processing can become fundamental to computer architecture rather than an optional feature. This collaboration signals a strategic shift in how PC manufacturers approach hardware design and product development.

Nvidia's move into consumer PC chips represents a significant expansion beyond its dominant position in data center graphics cards and AI infrastructure. By focusing on efficient local AI processing, Nvidia directly competes with established PC processor makers including Intel, Apple, Qualcomm and AMD. The company is simultaneously developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents, with early adoption from companies including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Industry analysts have compared the significance of this moment to major technology shifts. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, stated the RTX Spark "looks to transform the traditional app-centric PC to a real useful agentic AI personal computer which will eventually be in every home in coming years as private edge AI agents become pivotal."

Jensen Huang addressed concerns about AI's impact on employment, stating that suggestions AI would reduce demand for software engineers represented "complete nonsense." He argued the technology would increase hiring by making workers more productive, claiming that software engineer hiring numbers were actually increasing rather than declining.

The move opens a new business line for Nvidia, though analysts note this will develop over time. The chip wars intensified as Intel announced plans to ship an AI-focused graphics processor later this year, featuring cheaper memory and cooling technology than competitors. Meanwhile, Arm CEO Rene Haas stands to receive a pay package worth more than one billion dollars if the company achieves exceptional growth targets by 2031.
