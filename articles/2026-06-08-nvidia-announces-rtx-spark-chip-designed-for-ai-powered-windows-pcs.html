Nvidia announces RTX Spark chip for Windows PCs

Nvidia has launched the RTX Spark, a new processor designed to bring artificial intelligence capabilities directly to laptops and desktop computers. The move marks the company's entry into the consumer PC chip market, putting it in direct competition with Intel, Qualcomm, AMD and Apple.

The RTX Spark combines microprocessor and graphics processing capabilities and was developed through a three-year collaboration between Nvidia and Microsoft. The chip is designed to run AI agents locally on devices rather than relying on cloud computing. According to Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, this technology will allow AI agents to navigate PCs autonomously, potentially replacing traditional mouse and keyboard interactions.

The chip incorporates design elements from Taiwan's MediaTek, with die analysis showing it features modified Cortex-X925 cores and attributes taken from MediaTek's Dimensity 9400 and Dimensity 9500 processors. These mobile-based designs have been adapted specifically for PC workloads while maintaining power efficiency. Nvidia has described the RTX Spark as "the most efficient PC chip ever built."

The RTX Spark will launch this year and will be used by major computer manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP, paired with Microsoft's Windows software. Microsoft has already showcased the Surface Laptop Ultra featuring the new processor. Despite the chip's power, Nvidia says computers using it will remain thin and light.

Huang described this development as reinventing the PC "for the first time in 40 years," positioning it as essential for the AI era. The company is also developing the Vera CPU, designed for AI agents and being adopted by early customers including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX.

Analysts view the RTX Spark as potentially transformative. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, compared the moment to the introduction of the iPhone, ChatGPT and DeepSeek, suggesting the chip could transform traditional app-focused computers into "agentic AI personal computers" that will become commonplace in homes.

The move represents Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in datacentre and graphics processing into consumer computing. While strategically significant, some analysts note this venture may take time to generate substantial earnings, as Nvidia's current financial performance remains heavily dependent on AI infrastructure and datacentre demand.

Nvidia's entry into the PC market comes as competitors intensify their AI chip efforts. Intel plans to ship its own AI chip later this year, featuring cheaper memory and cooling technology. The company has also announced the Xe3P graphics processing unit, designed for the emerging generation of AI agents.

When asked about concerns that AI will eliminate jobs, Huang dismissed the notion as "complete nonsense," arguing instead that the technology will increase hiring of software engineers by making workers more productive.
