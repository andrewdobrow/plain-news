Nvidia unveils RTX Spark chip for Windows PCs powered by personal AI

Nvidia has launched RTX Spark, a new chip designed to bring artificial intelligence capabilities directly to laptops and desktop computers. The announcement at Computex in Taiwan signals the company's expansion beyond data centers into consumer computing, positioning it against competitors including Intel, Apple, Qualcomm and AMD.

The RTX Spark combines a microprocessor with graphics processing capabilities and was developed in collaboration with Microsoft and Taiwan's MediaTek. According to Jensen Huang, Nvidia's chief executive, the chip represents the first major reimagining of the PC in 40 years. Rather than relying on cloud-based AI processing, the RTX Spark is designed to run AI agents locally on computers, potentially allowing these agents to navigate PCs autonomously and replace traditional mouse and keyboard interactions.

Huang emphasized that despite its power, computers equipped with the chip will remain thin and light. The RTX Spark is scheduled for launch this year and will be integrated into devices from major manufacturers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP, all running Microsoft's Windows software.

This move follows three years of collaboration between Nvidia and Microsoft to establish what both companies describe as a reinvention of personal computing for an AI-driven era. The development reflects growing demand for devices capable of processing AI models locally rather than depending solely on cloud infrastructure. Local processing offers potential benefits including improved privacy, reduced latency and offline functionality.

The chip's arrival opens a new business line for Nvidia, though industry analysts suggest significant growth in this segment will take time to materialize. Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, described the RTX Spark as a transformative moment comparable to the iPhone, ChatGPT and DeepSeek, suggesting it could convert traditional app-focused PCs into useful AI agents that eventually reach most homes.

Nvidia's broader strategy extends beyond graphics processing. The company recently introduced the Vera CPU, designed specifically for AI agents and already adopted by early users including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX. These developments demonstrate Nvidia's push to establish presence across multiple layers of the AI technology stack, from cloud infrastructure to personal computing devices.

The competition is intensifying. Intel announced plans to ship its own AI chip later this year, featuring cheaper memory and cooling technology. The company unveiled a new graphics processing unit called Xe3P, codenamed Crescent Island, which Intel's vice-president of AI products described as purpose-built for the upcoming generation of AI agents.

Huang dismissed concerns about AI eliminating jobs, calling such fears "complete nonsense" and arguing that the technology would instead increase demand for software engineers by making workers more productive.
