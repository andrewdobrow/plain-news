Stock Markets Fall Sharply as Strong Jobs Report Dims Rate Cut Hopes

U.S. stock markets experienced a significant selloff on Friday, with major indices closing sharply lower after a strong employment report sparked concerns about interest rate policy. The S&P 500 fell 2.6 percent in its worst single-day decline of the year, while the Nasdaq dropped 4 percent in its steepest fall since April 2025. The Dow declined 695 points. The losses ended nine consecutive weeks of market gains.

The market downturn was driven by disappointing expectations about Federal Reserve rate cuts. Employers added 172,000 jobs in May while the unemployment rate remained at 4.3 percent, demonstrating continued resilience in the labor market despite economic headwinds. This robust hiring data convinced investors that the Fed would maintain elevated interest rates for longer than previously anticipated, triggering a sharp rise in Treasury yields with the 10-year yield climbing above 4.53 percent.

Higher interest rates for an extended period could slow economic growth and reduce corporate profitability, especially for growth-focused companies that depend on lower rates to justify their valuations. Technology stocks bore the heaviest losses, with semiconductor companies hit particularly hard. Chipmakers and other AI-related stocks tumbled across the board, with shares in companies like Nvidia and other data analytics firms experiencing significant declines despite some reporting improved revenue outlasts.

The market weakness extended beyond the United States. Asian markets followed with their sharpest slides in seven months, as indices in Japan and South Korea dropped more than 5 percent from recent record highs. European markets in the UK, France and Germany also declined slightly.

Several major bank executives warned of potential market corrections ahead. The chief executives of Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs joined JPMorgan Chase's Jamie Dimon in cautioning about market vulnerability. Analysts noted growing concerns about technology company valuations, particularly in the artificial intelligence sector. Many investors have highlighted that the vast majority of AI investment has been concentrated in a small group of companies, yet meaningful returns on these investments remain elusive.

The strong jobs report revealed underlying economic tensions. While headline employment figures appeared robust, wage growth has not kept pace with inflation, and consumer sentiment remains pessimistic. Young workers entering the job market face particular challenges starting their careers, a situation researchers indicate can have lasting effects on lifetime earnings and career advancement.

The market volatility also extended to riskier assets. Bitcoin briefly dipped below $100,000 for the first time since June as investors withdrew money from speculative investments amid broader economic concerns. The cryptocurrency had reached record highs above $126,000 in early October but has since faced significant downward pressure.
