Central Florida faces flooding threat through Friday as heavy rain continues across multiple counties

Central Florida is under a flood watch through Friday as several more days of heavy rain threaten multiple counties already saturated by recent storms. The watch covers Volusia, Seminole, Orange, Osceola, Flagler, Okeechobee, Brevard, Indian River, St. Lucie, and Martin counties, with the heaviest additional rainfall forecast along coastal areas.

Over the past 24 hours, several communities recorded significant rainfall totals. Port Orange measured 3.32 inches, while Daytona Beach received 2.21 inches and Ponce Inlet recorded 2.18 inches. Cocoa and Viera saw 1.99 inches and 1.95 inches respectively. Wednesday's forecast called for periods of showers and thunderstorms, with heavier rain possible across portions of Volusia, Seminole, Orange, Osceola, and Brevard counties. Repeated rounds of rain increase the risk of localized flooding, particularly in areas that have already received substantial precipitation.

The weather system is expected to bring sustained winds between 15 and 25 miles per hour, with gusts reaching 30 miles per hour. Wednesday through Friday are being designated as severe weather warning days for coastal communities. Drier conditions are forecast to return Saturday, with rain chances dropping to 30 percent, followed by mostly sunny weather Sunday.

The flooding threat comes two weeks after Brevard County experienced more than 20 inches of rain in some areas. Palm Bay is still dealing with cleanup from that earlier storm system.
