U.S. and Iran approach deadline for peace and nuclear negotiations

The United States and Iran are moving past a deadline they set to reach an agreement on peace and Iran's nuclear program, with negotiations showing mixed signals about progress.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told the Senate Foreign Relations Committee that Iran has agreed to discuss aspects of its nuclear program it had previously refused to negotiate. "There is the prospect before us, which could happen today, it could happen tomorrow, it could happen next week – that for the first time, certainly in my memory, they have agreed to negotiate aspects of their nuclear program that just a month ago" they would not, Rubio said.

However, Tehran announced it was halting peace talks and moving to fully close the Strait of Hormuz, citing Israeli military operations in Lebanon as a ceasefire violation. Iran's state-affiliated Tasnim News Agency reported that Tehran would stop exchanging messages with Washington through intermediaries. President Trump later disputed this, saying conversations "have been going on continuously."

Rubio outlined a two-phase framework for negotiations. Reopening the Strait of Hormuz is a precondition for talks, not a bargaining chip. This would require Iran to announce clearly it is not charging tolls, help remove mines it placed there, and commit not to fire on ships. Rubio rejected offering sanctions relief simply to reopen the vital waterway. "Any sanctions relief is condition-based," he said, and is tied to Iran's nuclear program.

Phase two would require Iran to commit to disposing of its stockpile of highly enriched uranium and to negotiate "severe and long-term limitations, and or cancellation of enrichment activity."

Regarding Iran's military capabilities, Rubio claimed the country's missile program had been "substantially degraded" and its drone-building capability "eroded." However, The New York Times reported in May that Iran had retained roughly 70 percent of its prewar missile stockpile. Analysts noted that over 85 percent of Iran's ballistic missile, drone and naval defense industrial base has been damaged or destroyed, though this may limit Iran's ability to replace weapons rather than indicating immediate military collapse.

The blockade the U.S. launched in April is costing Tehran "hundreds of millions of dollars a day" in lost oil revenue, according to Rubio. The ceasefire agreed in April had included an Iranian commitment to reopen the strait, but Trump later said Iran had "knowingly failed" to honor that pledge.

Rubio also claimed that Mojtaba Khamenei, who succeeded his father Ali Khamenei after the supreme leader was killed in February's opening strikes, was alive and increasingly engaged in the regime's efforts, though Khamenei has not been seen in public since assuming office.
