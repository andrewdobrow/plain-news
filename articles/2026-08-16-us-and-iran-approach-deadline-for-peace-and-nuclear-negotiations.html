US and Iran miss deadline for nuclear and peace agreement

The United States and Iran failed to reach a comprehensive agreement by the end of a 60-day negotiation period intended to produce limits on Tehran's nuclear program and resolve their ongoing conflict. President Trump had said in June that a cease-fire would lead to broader deals on nuclear restrictions and ending the war, but the two countries remain far apart on key issues.

The breakdown comes amid escalating military tensions. In recent days, Iran launched drone and missile attacks against Bahrain and Kuwait after new US strikes on sites in southern Iran. Trump responded with threats, posting on social media that if talks fail, "the Islamic Republic of Iran will no longer exist." However, a US official reported late Sunday that both sides had agreed to halt recent hostilities and renew talks, with technical discussions set to continue on all areas of their memorandum of understanding.

The primary dispute centers on control of the Strait of Hormuz, a strategically critical waterway that carried a fifth of the world's oil and liquid gas supplies before the war. The US has promoted a southern lane along the Omani coast, while Tehran wants ships to use a northern route through its waters under Iranian control. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, stated in Baghdad that "any attempt to establish new or separate arrangements from those currently being carried out by the Islamic Republic of Iran, will only lead to further complications" and increase tension.

The US military accused Iran of violating the cease-fire by attacking commercial shipping attempting to use the southern corridor. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed responsibility for the attacks, saying that "violating the ceasefire will lead to a complete halt of ongoing processes."

Beyond the strait dispute, mediators from Qatar and Pakistan have been unable to bridge gaps on other contentious issues, including sanctions relief for Tehran and the future of Iran's nuclear program. Observers note that Iran is using its ability to threaten shipping as leverage not just in negotiations with Washington, but to intimidate neighboring countries and establish greater regional dominance.

The expiration of the negotiation deadline leaves the diplomatic framework uncertain. Both governments must now decide whether to extend talks, modify their terms, or abandon the negotiating framework entirely. Leaders in Tehran and Washington face domestic political pressures to avoid a return to full conflict and appear committed to the cease-fire for now, despite frequent threats from both sides.

The failed deadline represents a setback for Trump administration efforts to reshape US relations with Iran through a combination of military pressure and diplomatic engagement. The June cease-fire had been presented as the foundation for a larger set of agreements addressing nuclear development, regional conflicts, and the broader security relationship between the two countries.
