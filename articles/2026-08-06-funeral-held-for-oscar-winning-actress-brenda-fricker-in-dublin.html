Funeral held for Oscar-winning actress Brenda Fricker in Dublin

Brenda Fricker, who became the first female Irish actor to win an Oscar, has died aged 81. The Dublin-born actress gained international recognition for her role in My Left Foot, winning best supporting actress at the 1990 Academy Awards ceremony.

In the 1989 film, directed by Jim Sheridan, Fricker played the mother of Christy Brown, a man with cerebral palsy who had muscular control over only one of his feet. The film achieved significant critical acclaim, with Daniel Day-Lewis winning best actor for his portrayal of Brown. Fricker's performance was praised for its authenticity and emotional depth, showcasing her ability to convey both warmth and resilience.

Phil Belfield, her agent, released a statement to the BBC saying: "We will never see her like again and the world is lesser for the lack of her. I was honoured to know, love and work with her and she will always have a place in my heart and in the heart of so many film and TV fans the world over."

Born in Dublin in 1945, Fricker initially worked as an art editor at the Irish Times before pursuing acting. She secured small roles in television productions including Of Human Bondage and Upstairs, Downstairs. In 1977, she was cast as a nurse in the long-running British soap opera Coronation Street, followed by a regular role in the hospital drama Casualty in 1986.

The success of My Left Foot transformed her career, establishing her as a versatile character actor in demand for roles across Hollywood, British, and Irish productions. She appeared opposite Richard Harris in The Field and Albert Finney in A Man of No Importance. Her American film credits included Angels in the Outfield, A Time to Kill, and Veronica Guerin. She became widely recognized for playing the "pigeon lady" in Home Alone 2: Lost in New York in 1992.

Throughout her later career, Fricker continued working in film and television, with her final credit being The Swallow, directed by Tadhg O'Sullivan. Beyond her professional achievements, Fricker published a memoir in 2025 in which she revealed details of past sexual abuse and rape. Speaking to the Guardian about her experiences, she reflected on the psychological impact, saying: "You think it's your fault. You really do."

Fricker was married to Barry Davis between 1979 and 1988. Her contributions to film and television, particularly her groundbreaking achievement as the first female Irish Oscar winner for acting, leave a significant legacy in both Irish and international cinema. Her work continues to be recognized as an example of exceptional supporting performance that enriched the films she appeared in.
