Gang attack in Haiti leaves 47 dead and more than 50 kidnapped

A gang raid near the Haitian capital has killed at least 47 people and resulted in the kidnapping of more than 50 others, according to United Nations officials. The attack heightens fears about the ongoing violence plaguing the country as armed groups continue to operate with relative impunity despite international efforts to restore security.

The raid took place in an area close to Port-au-Prince, where gangs have expanded their territorial control in recent years. Residents in affected communities have expressed growing desperation over the lack of protection from authorities, demanding stronger security measures to counter the escalating violence. The Haitian government has struggled to contain gang activity, which has displaced thousands of families and disrupted daily life across large portions of the country.

Gang violence in Haiti has surged following the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse, creating a power vacuum that armed groups have exploited. Criminal organizations now control significant portions of Port-au-Prince and surrounding areas, engaging in kidnapping for ransom, extortion, and territorial battles. The violence has contributed to a humanitarian crisis, with limited access to food, healthcare, and basic services in gang-controlled neighborhoods.

International efforts to stabilize Haiti have faced significant challenges. A multinational security force, led by Kenya, deployed to Haiti earlier this year to support the national police, but gang violence has persisted. The kidnapping economy remains lucrative for criminal groups, with families often forced to pay substantial ransoms for the release of loved ones. Human rights organizations have documented widespread abuses, including sexual violence and forced recruitment of children by gangs.

The latest massacre underscores the fragility of security in Haiti and the urgent need for a coordinated response. Local officials and community leaders have called for increased international support and resources to strengthen law enforcement capabilities. The United Nations continues to monitor the situation, but the path to restoring order remains uncertain as gangs maintain their grip on large swaths of the country.
