One dead, nine wounded in Midland, Texas mass shooting; suspect killed

A shooting in Midland, Texas on Friday left one person dead and nine others injured, according to city authorities. The suspected shooter engaged in a standoff with police for approximately two hours before being killed later that afternoon.

Law enforcement responded to reports of gunfire in an industrial area of the city shortly after 8 a.m. local time. Midland Mayor Lori Blong confirmed the incident on Friday morning. The shooting eventually ended near a veterinary hospital in the area.

Witnesses reported hearing what sounded like at least 40 shots fired during the incident. Andrea Mendias, who works at an auto body shop near the veterinary hospital, provided video footage showing officers emerging from an armored police vehicle and law enforcement deploying robots in the surrounding area.

All nine injured victims were transported to Midland Memorial Hospital for treatment. Hospital officials reported that four patients were in operating rooms receiving emergency care, while five others were in stable condition. The hospital's emergency department was placed on lockdown as a security precaution.

Midland is located approximately 300 miles west of Fort Worth and Dallas in the heart of Texas's oil region. The city has a population of about 140,000 residents.

The shooting marks another violent incident in a community with recent experience of mass casualty events. Six years ago, a gunman who had been fired from an oil services job carried out a shooting rampage across the Odessa and Midland areas, killing seven people and wounding two dozen others as he fired at random while driving through the region.

Authorities continued investigating the circumstances of Friday's shooting, working to establish a timeline of events and determine the suspect's motive. No information was released regarding the identities of the victims or the shooter at the time of reporting.

The incident adds to a troubling pattern of mass shootings across Texas in recent years, reigniting national conversations about gun violence and public safety. The state has experienced multiple high-profile mass casualty events, including shootings in El Paso, Uvalde, and Sutherland Springs.

Law enforcement agencies worked to piece together details about how the shooting unfolded and what circumstances led to the violence. The standoff between the suspect and police lasted roughly two hours before ending with the suspect's death, effectively concluding the active threat to the community.
