Georgia attorney general urges SEC action against LSU over disputed player eligibility

Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr has asked the Southeastern Conference to take action against Louisiana State University over the school's decision to roster basketball player RJ Luis Jr., escalating a dispute over the former St. John's guard who signed contracts with NBA teams. Carr's letter accuses LSU of showing contempt for conference rules.

Luis, an All-American transfer, is at the center of a legal and administrative fight over his NCAA eligibility after signing professional contracts. LSU added Luis to its basketball roster despite the SEC's objections and an ongoing court battle over whether his prior NBA agreements disqualify him from collegiate competition. The conference has expressed frustration with LSU's actions.

The dispute has reached a point where some SEC members are considering boycotting games against LSU if head coach Will Wade plays Luis on the court, according to reports. The potential boycotts would represent an extraordinary escalation in the standoff between the conference and one of its member institutions.

The case tests NCAA rules governing player compensation and professional contracts at a time when college athletics is navigating rapidly changing policies around athlete earnings. LSU maintains it will keep Luis on its roster, while the SEC and state officials from other member states argue the university is violating eligibility standards that apply across the conference.
