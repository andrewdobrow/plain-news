Google increases stock sale to $85 billion to fund AI expansion

Alphabet, Google's parent company, has increased its planned equity raise to $85 billion, making it the largest stock offering in corporate history. The company is pursuing this massive capital raise to finance its artificial intelligence infrastructure investments and address surging demand for its AI services.

The original plan called for an $80 billion raise, which already dwarfed the world's three largest initial public offerings combined. Saudi Aramco raised $25.6 billion in 2019, Alibaba brought in $21.8 billion in 2014, and SoftBank collected $21.3 billion in 2018. The increase to $85 billion underscores Alphabet's commitment to scaling its AI operations as competitors race to develop advanced technology.

The fundraising includes a $10 billion investment from Berkshire Hathaway, the investment group led until recently by Warren Buffett. The deal also consists of a $30 billion initial raise and a $40 billion flexible mechanism that can be deployed gradually over time. Half of the $80 billion is designated for scaling AI infrastructure and global computing capacity, while $40 billion will cover administrative changes related to employee equity award tax obligations.

Alphabet stated that it is experiencing unprecedented demand for its AI solutions and services from both enterprises and consumers, with customer demand exceeding available supply. The company plans to expand its foundational infrastructure to support the significant growth opportunity ahead.

The massive fundraising reflects the capital-intensive nature of the modern AI sector. Alphabet previously indicated that capital expenditure would reach between $180 billion and $190 billion this year, with another significant increase expected in 2027. According to Matt Britzman, a senior equity analyst at Hargreaves Lansdown, the fundraising represents "a clear sign that the AI arms race is moving into a more capital-hungry phase." He added that "long gone are the days when the tech giants were capital-light free cashflow machines."

The stock sale also raises questions about the economics of the AI boom. Despite massive investment in AI infrastructure across the technology industry, meaningful returns to investors have so far been limited. Jim Reid, a market strategist at Deutsche Bank, noted that Alphabet's fundraising reminds investors of the "unprecedented scale of the AI spending boom."

Alphabet shares fell as much as 4.4% following the announcement, and the broader Nasdaq index declined nearly 0.5%. The company is tapping investors for capital before some of its major AI rivals pursue public listings. Anthropic, which developed the Claude chatbot, has filed confidentially for an IPO and is now valued at $965 billion. OpenAI and Elon Musk's xAI are also scheduled to go public this year.
