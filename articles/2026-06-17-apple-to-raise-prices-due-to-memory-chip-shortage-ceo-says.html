Apple to raise iPhone prices due to memory chip shortage

Apple plans to increase iPhone prices in response to a global shortage of memory chips, CEO Tim Cook said in a recent interview. Cook described the current supply situation as unsustainable, indicating the company can no longer absorb the rising costs of components needed for its devices.

The shortage stems from increased demand for high-capacity memory chips driven by artificial intelligence features. Apple's push to integrate advanced AI capabilities into its Siri assistant requires 12 gigabytes of DRAM in newer iPhone models, double the memory used in previous generations. This surge in demand has strained suppliers Samsung and SK Hynix, creating upward pressure on component pricing across the industry.

In a recent statement, Cook said the company is "doing our best to mitigate the huge increases that are being passed to us" but emphasized that "price increases are unavoidable." The company has been attempting to shield customers from these rising costs, but the situation has become untenable.

The base model iPhone 17, currently in development, will not support all of the new AI features planned for iOS 27 due to insufficient memory capacity. However, the iPhone 18, expected in a later release cycle, is confirmed to include 12 gigabytes of RAM in its base model, enabling full AI functionality across all variants of that generation.

Industry observers note that the memory chip crunch affects Apple's operations in multiple ways beyond just procurement costs. The company must balance the technical requirements of its AI ambitions against manufacturing constraints and consumer price sensitivity. The shortage has forced difficult decisions about which features to prioritize and which device tiers receive upgraded specifications.

Apple has not specified the size of the planned price increases or which iPhone models will be affected first. The company typically adjusts pricing based on regional markets and currency fluctuations, but a component-driven price increase would represent a shift from recent pricing strategies that held costs steady despite inflation in other areas.

The timing of these price increases remains uncertain as Apple continues negotiating with memory chip suppliers. The company's ability to manage costs while delivering the AI features consumers expect will be crucial during the coming product cycles. How aggressively Apple implements price increases and which markets see the largest hikes could significantly impact consumer demand and the company's competitive position in the smartphone market.
