Chris Sale throws complete-game shutout as Braves sweep Dodgers

Atlanta Braves pitcher Chris Sale threw a complete-game shutout and struck out 11 batters as the Braves completed a three-game sweep of the Los Angeles Dodgers. Sale's dominant performance marked his first shutout since 2019 and included pitches topping 100 mph, showcasing a level of velocity rarely seen from the veteran left-hander this late in his career.

The shutout capped a sweep that saw the Braves take all three games from the Dodgers in Atlanta. Sale pitched all nine innings without allowing a run, blanking one of baseball's highest-scoring offenses. His 11 strikeouts and continued ability to reach triple digits on the radar gun highlighted a resurgent season for the 37-year-old pitcher, who has battled injuries in recent years.

The Dodgers left Atlanta without a win in the series, continuing what has been an inconsistent stretch for the team. Los Angeles has been tinkering with its rotation in recent weeks, searching for stability as the season progresses. The sweep represents a significant setback for a team with postseason aspirations, particularly given the lopsided nature of the series finale.

Sale's complete game was a rarity in modern baseball, where starting pitchers are typically pulled after five or six innings to preserve their arms. His ability to go the distance while maintaining velocity into the late innings demonstrated both his stamina and command. The performance evoked memories of Sale's prime years with the Chicago White Sox and Boston Red Sox, when he was widely considered one of baseball's elite starting pitchers.

The Braves' sweep strengthens their position in the National League playoff race while dealing a blow to the Dodgers' momentum. For Sale, the outing represents a career milestone and a validation of his continued effectiveness despite advancing age and past injury concerns. The complete-game shutout was his first in seven years, a span that included multiple seasons lost to arm injuries and Tommy John surgery recovery.
