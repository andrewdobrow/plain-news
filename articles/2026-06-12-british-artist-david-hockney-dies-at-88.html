British Artist David Hockney Dies at 88

David Hockney, the legendary British painter whose vibrant works helped define contemporary art across seven decades, has died at 88. He passed away peacefully at home on June 11, 2026, just one month before his 89th birthday.

Hockney rose to prominence during the 1960s as a pop artist and became internationally recognized for his swimming pool paintings that captured the essence of Los Angeles life. Works such as "A Bigger Splash" and "Portrait of an Artist (Pool With Two Figures)" depicted scenes of desire and loss beneath California's bright skies. The latter painting became one of the most expensive works by a living artist when it sold at auction in 2018.

Born in Bradford, West Yorkshire, in 1937, Hockney came from what he described as a "radical working-class family." He studied at Bradford College and London's Royal College of Art, where he developed a reputation as a talented but rebellious artist. He famously submitted a work called "Life Drawing for a Diploma" instead of painting a required female model, depicting a male figure from an American physique magazine instead. The college bent its rules to award him his diploma.

Throughout his career, Hockney refused to accept artistic boundaries. His 1961 painting "We Two Boys Together Clinging," named after a Walt Whitman poem, depicted gay relationships with unprecedented honesty at a time when homosexuality remained illegal in Britain. Later works continued exploring themes of queer life with openness and wit.

Beyond swimming pools, Hockney's work encompassed perspective-shifting photo-collage portraits, abstract landscape paintings, and experiments with emerging digital technologies including iPad and 3D art creation. He remained constantly inventive, producing new work throughout his eighth decade.

King Charles paid tribute to Hockney as "a giant of the world of art and painting, a Yorkshireman through and through." The king noted Hockney's "irrepressible charm, talent and constant innovation," recalling how the artist wore his signature yellow Crocs to a Palace occasion.

Tracey Emin described Hockney as "a great artist and a wonderful man, who with the power of art changed the perception of Britishness." Prime Minister Keir Starmer said his "vivid, instantly recognisable work influenced generations of artists."

The Tate Britain director Alex Farquharson called Hockney "an endlessly inventive artist" who "taught us about the joy of looking, seeing things the rest of us failed to notice." The Tate plans to stage a major exhibition of his work next year, along with a multimedia installation at Tate Modern.

Hockney is survived by his long-time partner Jean-Pierre Gonçalves de Lima, his brothers Philip and John, and numerous relatives. His publicist said his enduring legacy reflects "his underlying enthusiasm for life" and his signature phrase: "Love Life."
