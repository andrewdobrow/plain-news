British artist David Hockney dies at 88

David Hockney, one of the most important figures in contemporary art across the 20th and 21st centuries, has died at age 88. His representatives confirmed he passed away peacefully at home on June 11, 2026, just one month before his 89th birthday.

Hockney became famous during the 1960s as a pop artist and gained particular recognition for his paintings of swimming pools that became synonymous with Los Angeles culture. His most celebrated works, including A Bigger Splash and Portrait of an Artist (Pool With Two Figures), captured scenes of leisure and emotion beneath California's bright skies. However, his six-decade career extended far beyond these iconic pool paintings. He explored perspective-shifting portrait work using photo-collage techniques, experimented with abstract landscape painting, and later investigated 3D technology as a medium for artistic creation.

Born in Bradford, West Yorkshire in 1937, Hockney grew up in what he described as a radical working-class family that nurtured his artistic talents. He studied at Bradford College and sold his first painting, a portrait of his father, for 10 pounds at the Yorkshire Artists Exhibition in 1957. After completing national service as a hospital orderly, he enrolled at London's Royal College of Art in 1959, where he quickly established himself as a rebellious talent willing to challenge conventions.

During the 1960s, Hockney became a recognizable figure in London and American social circles, known for his bleached blonde hair, thick-rimmed glasses, and bohemian lifestyle. Yet beneath this playboy reputation lay an artist driven by strong work ethic and artistic vision. His work from this period, such as We Two Boys Together Clinging and Cleaning Teeth, Early Evening, depicted gay life with unusual honesty for an era when homosexuality remained illegal in Britain.

After relocating to Los Angeles in the mid-1960s, Hockney's style matured into more restrained, emotionally complex work. Paintings like Man in Shower in Beverly Hills demonstrated his developing realist approach. His 1972 masterpiece, Portrait of an Artist (Pool With Two Figures), sold for $90.3 million in 2018, setting a world record for a living artist at that time.

Even after suffering a stroke in 2012 that temporarily affected his speech, Hockney continued creating art with characteristic determination.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer called Hockney "one of Britain's most celebrated artists" whose "vivid, instantly recognisable work influenced generations of artists." Alex Farquharson, director of London's Tate Britain, described Hockney as "an endlessly inventive artist, with a unique vision of the world" and noted that his passing "brings to a close an extraordinary body of work characterised by reinvention."

The Tate plans major exhibitions of his work next year, including a multimedia installation in the Turbine Hall at Tate Modern.
