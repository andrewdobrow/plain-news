Microsoft AI chief says company freed from OpenAI to pursue superintelligence

Microsoft's artificial intelligence leadership announced the company is pursuing an independent path in AI development, signaling a major shift away from its previous reliance on OpenAI. The statement came during Microsoft's Build 2026 developer conference, where the company also revealed new AI models aimed at reducing dependence on OpenAI and cutting costs for developers.

This marks a significant turning point in the partnership between the two companies. Microsoft had previously invested billions into OpenAI and woven its technology throughout products including Bing and Office applications. The pivot indicates Microsoft is now building proprietary AI systems rather than primarily relying on OpenAI's offerings.

At Build 2026, Microsoft showcased new AI capabilities and developer tools for Windows applications. The company emphasized its vision for Windows as a dependable platform for development work, while also announcing improvements to workplace technology and productivity features.

The shift reflects Microsoft's broader strategy to gain greater control over its AI infrastructure and decrease reliance on external technology partners. By developing its own models, Microsoft expects to reduce operational expenses while gaining more flexibility in how it deploys artificial intelligence across its product lineup. The approach also gives the company direct control over its AI research and development direction.

The changing relationship between Microsoft and OpenAI comes against the backdrop of significant legal developments. A jury recently ruled in favor of OpenAI's leadership in a high-profile lawsuit brought by Elon Musk. The jury found Sam Altman, OpenAI, its president Greg Brockman, and Microsoft not liable for Musk's claims of unjust enrichment and breach of contract. The case centered on OpenAI's transition from a nonprofit to a for-profit structure. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed with the jury's decision and dismissed Musk's claims, noting substantial evidence supported their findings. The verdict was delivered after less than two hours of deliberation.

The jury determined that Musk's 2024 lawsuit fell outside the statute of limitations, as OpenAI argued that Musk was aware of plans for a for-profit structure as early as 2017. William Savitt, OpenAI's lead attorney, characterized the case as a "hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor." Musk stated he would appeal the verdict.

The evolution between Microsoft and OpenAI reflects a broader industry trend where major technology companies increasingly view AI capabilities as fundamental strategic assets. Rather than outsourcing these critical functions, firms are building internal expertise and infrastructure. For Microsoft, this represents a calculated move to establish independence in what many consider the defining technology of the coming decade. The company's shift toward proprietary development provides greater autonomy over its AI future while reducing its vulnerability to external partners.
