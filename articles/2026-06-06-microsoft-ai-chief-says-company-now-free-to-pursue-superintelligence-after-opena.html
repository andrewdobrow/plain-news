Microsoft AI chief says company now free to pursue superintelligence after OpenAI split

Microsoft's artificial intelligence chief said the company has been freed from its relationship with OpenAI to pursue superintelligence development independently. The statement came during remarks at the company's Build 2026 conference, marking a public acknowledgment of the deteriorating partnership between the two organizations.

The comments highlight a significant shift in one of the technology industry's most prominent collaborations. Microsoft invested billions of dollars in OpenAI and integrated its technology across Windows and other products. The relationship helped propel both companies to the forefront of the artificial intelligence race that intensified following the release of ChatGPT.

Microsoft has been testing a wearable AI gadget aimed at office workers, according to separate reports from the conference. The company also announced updates to Windows intended to make it a more trusted platform for software development. The announcements came as Microsoft emphasized workplace technology and artificial intelligence integration.

Reid Hoffman, a Microsoft board member since 2017, announced his departure from the board to focus on his artificial intelligence drug discovery startup Manus. Hoffman's exit ends a profitable decade on Microsoft's board during a period of significant growth for the company. His departure was characterized as a shift to founder mode with his own venture.

The fraying relationship between Microsoft and OpenAI represents a major development in the artificial intelligence sector. Both companies had positioned their partnership as central to their AI strategies. Microsoft's pivot toward independent superintelligence development suggests the company believes it can advance its AI capabilities without relying on OpenAI's technology. The move could reshape competitive dynamics in an industry where partnerships and collaborations have played a central role in rapid technological advancement.
