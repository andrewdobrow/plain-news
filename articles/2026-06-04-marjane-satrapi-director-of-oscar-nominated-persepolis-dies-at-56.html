Marjane Satrapi, Oscar-Nominated Persepolis Creator, Dies at 56

Marjane Satrapi, the French-Iranian artist and graphic novelist best known for her groundbreaking memoir Persepolis, has died at age 56. Her family stated that she "died of sadness" following the death of her husband, Swedish producer Mattias Ripa, who passed away on April 8 of the previous year.

Born in 1969 in Rasht, Iran, Satrapi was raised in Tehran by her father, an engineer, and her mother, a dress designer. As a teenager, her parents sent her to Europe to continue her education and escape the restrictions of Iran's Islamic Republic. She eventually settled in France in 1994 and became a French citizen in 2006.

Satrapi gained international acclaim with Persepolis, a graphic novel memoir published in 2000. The work tells the story of a rebellious young girl navigating Iran's upheaval following the 1979 revolution and the establishment of the Islamic Republic, before being sent alone to Europe at age 14. The memoir became an international publishing phenomenon, selling millions of copies and establishing Satrapi as one of the most widely read Iranian authors globally.

In a 2020 interview, Satrapi reflected on her unlikely success, saying she initially had little expectation the work would reach publication. "With Persepolis, I didn't even think I'd find a publisher. I thought I'd make 50 photocopies for my friends to read," she told El País.

The 2007 animated film adaptation, which Satrapi co-directed with Vincent Paronnaud, became an international success. It earned Satrapi a place in Oscar history as the first woman nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Satrapi later directed five feature films, including the 2019 biopic Radioactive, starring Rosamund Pike as Marie Curie. In recent years, she returned to comics, coordinating Woman, Life, Freedom in 2024, a collaborative graphic work bringing together 17 Iranian and international artists to examine the protest movement following the death of Mahsa Amini in 2022.

Discussing her choice of medium, Satrapi once explained that drawing represented humanity's first language, preceding writing and speech. Throughout her career, she emphasized that her work aimed to reassure young Iranians they were heard and supported by the outside world.

President Emmanuel Macron paid tribute to Satrapi, describing her as "a great artist who turned her Iranian childhood into a universal tale." Her work challenged Western assumptions about Iranian society and culture, earning widespread recognition for its ability to convey complex historical and personal experiences through illustration and narrative.
