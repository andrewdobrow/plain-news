Senate passes $70 billion immigration enforcement bill

The Senate passed legislation early Friday to provide $69.5 billion in funding for immigration enforcement operations through 2029. The vote was 52 to 47, ending weeks of delays caused by disputes over provisions unrelated to border security.

The bill funds President Donald Trump's immigration enforcement agencies, including the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives and other federal operations. The legislation faced fierce opposition from several Republicans upset that it did not bar the Trump administration from creating an anti-weaponization settlement fund worth $1.776 billion intended to compensate political allies.

The settlement fund became a flashpoint during negotiations, with critics arguing it should not be included in border security legislation. The final version passed without restrictions on the fund, allowing the administration to proceed with payments to individuals it claims were targeted by previous government actions.

The budget reconciliation package now moves to the House for final passage before reaching President Trump's desk for signature. The legislation represents one of the largest single investments in immigration enforcement in recent years, providing funding certainty for border operations through the remainder of Trump's term and beyond.

The passage followed a late-night session marked by procedural battles and amendments. Supporters argued the funding was essential to carry out the administration's border security priorities, while opponents raised concerns about both the cost and the inclusion of the settlement provision.
