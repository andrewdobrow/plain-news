Senate passes immigration enforcement funding bill

Senate Democrats have blocked a funding package for the Department of Homeland Security, pushing the agency toward a partial shutdown at midnight Friday. The vote fell short of the 60 votes needed to overcome a filibuster, with senators splitting largely along party lines at 52-47 in favor of continuing the department's funding.

The blockade reflects Democratic anger over the Trump administration's immigration enforcement operations and recent deaths in Minneapolis. Last month, federal agents shot and killed two US citizens, Renee Good and Alex Pretti, in the city. The controversy intensified when Tom Homan, the Trump administration's border czar, announced that ICE was largely withdrawing its operations from Minneapolis.

Democrats have demanded significant reforms to how ICE operates before agreeing to fund the agency. Their proposed changes include requiring agents to wear identification and body cameras, and prohibiting enforcement activities near schools, medical facilities, churches, polling stations, childcare facilities and courts. Democrats also want local and state law enforcement to have guaranteed authority to investigate potential crimes by federal agents, including excessive use of force.

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer said the party was seeking common-sense changes. "From day one, I've said unless there were really strong, meaningful reforms to rein in ICE and stop the violence, there will not be Democratic votes to fund ICE," he said. "We've been pushing commonsense reforms, the very types of things that local police and sheriffs throughout America routinely follow. Today's strong vote was a shot across the bow to Republicans. Democrats will not support a blank check for chaos."

Republicans support body cameras but have resisted other proposals, arguing they would restrict agents' operations. Republican Senator James Lankford of Oklahoma criticized Democrats' approach, saying their reaction to the Minneapolis incident would harm broader homeland security efforts including the Coast Guard, Secret Service, and FEMA.

The shutdown's impact may be limited. ICE and Customs and Border Protection received 75 billion dollars in funding last year through what the Trump administration called a flagship spending bill. This means immigration enforcement agencies will continue operating even if the general DHS funding lapses. Instead, the shutdown would primarily affect agencies like the Transportation Safety Administration, FEMA, and the US Coast Guard.

Democratic Senator Maggie Hassan of New Hampshire noted that the department has substantial funding available regardless. "Even if the current funding bill stalls or is not passed that would fund the Department of Homeland Security, the department has an extraordinary amount of money at its disposal right now because it was funded during the big tax bill that my Republican colleagues passed last summer," she said.

The House will now decide whether to take further action on the legislation.
