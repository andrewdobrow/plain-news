House passes $70 billion immigration enforcement bill funding ICE and Border Patrol through 2029

The House of Representatives approved a $70 billion funding measure on Tuesday that will support immigration enforcement agencies through the remainder of President Trump's term. The Secure America Act passed 214-212, largely along party lines, and now awaits the president's signature.

The bill allocates $38 billion to Immigration and Customs Enforcement, $26 billion to Customs and Border Protection, and $5 billion to the Department of Homeland Security through September 2029. The Senate had already approved the legislation last week.

The vote represents a significant political victory for House Republicans, who have maintained a historically narrow majority. Kevin Kiley, an independent aligned with Republicans, joined all Democrats in voting against the measure.

"With today's vote, House and Senate Republicans have officially ended the third Democrat government shutdown of this Congress," House Speaker Mike Johnson said after the passage. Johnson credited Republicans with preventing future shutdowns by securing long-term funding for these agencies.

The bill ends a months-long standoff between the two parties. Democrats had blocked funding for ICE and CBP beginning in January following federal agents' killing of two U.S. citizens in Minneapolis during immigration enforcement operations. That standoff triggered a 75-day Department of Homeland Security shutdown beginning in mid-February. The department reopened in late April after Democrats agreed to fund all DHS operations except ICE and CBP, while Republicans pursued separate, extended funding for those agencies.

Democrats unanimously opposed the final bill, arguing it provides insufficient oversight. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said the legislation would "waste $70 billion in taxpayer money to give a blank check to ICE without any guardrails, any oversight, any accountability."

Republicans countered that the measure represents essential law enforcement funding. House Majority Leader Steve Scalise told supporters they were "voting to fund law enforcement" and "voting to secure America's borders." He framed opposition as an effort to "defund the police" and allow undocumented immigrants to enter the country.

The bill's passage resolved several controversies that had complicated its progress. Senate Republicans removed a proposed $1 billion allocation for security improvements at a Trump White House ballroom after the chamber's parliamentarian ruled it could not be included under budget reconciliation procedures.

The measure also became entangled with a separate Republican proposal for an approximately $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund. While some GOP lawmakers sought to bar this fund through amendment language, those efforts failed. The acting attorney general has stated the proposal is dead, though Trump has refused to rule out its creation in recent interviews.

The legislation ensures that immigration enforcement agencies will have stable funding through the 2028 presidential election without requiring annual congressional appropriations battles.
