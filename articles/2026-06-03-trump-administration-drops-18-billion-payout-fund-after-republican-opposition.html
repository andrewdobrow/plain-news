Federal judge warns Justice Department against reviving $1.8 billion payout fund

A federal judge has reopened Donald Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service, ruling that the settlement lacks sufficient detail and may represent "collusion" and "fraud on the court."

Miami Judge Kathleen Williams made the decision after receiving a motion from a bipartisan group of 35 former federal judges who questioned whether the settlement was legitimate. Trump had dropped the lawsuit last week, and acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the government was "forever barred" from auditing Trump family tax returns in exchange.

The case centers on a leak of Trump and his sons' personal and business tax returns by a former contractor. The settlement also created a controversial $1.8 billion fund to compensate people claiming they were victims of federal government targeting, commonly referred to as the "anti-weaponization" fund.

The former judges argued that Trump had used the IRS lawsuit to obtain "unlawful private benefits" for himself and his family while creating a fund that would distribute taxpayer money "without constitutional or congressional authority." They contended Trump had rushed the settlement to shield the deal from judicial oversight and "short-circuit" the court's ability to review its terms.

In her order, Williams said she wanted to investigate the circumstances of the settlement and determine who would benefit from it. She noted that she is "empowered to investigate serious misconduct" and ordered Trump's attorneys to respond by June 12 about whether the parties truly opposed each other in court and whether the case should be reopened because the court was "the victim of a fraud."

Williams also questioned the "forever" provision shielding the Trumps from IRS scrutiny, noting that only Blanche signed it.

The settlement has drawn widespread criticism. Many fear the anti-weaponization fund will primarily benefit Trump supporters convicted of crimes related to the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot, though the fund is not explicitly limited by political affiliation. Supporters and critics alike, including former Trump attorney Michael Cohen, have indicated they intend to apply for compensation.

Charles Littlejohn, an IRS consultant employee who leaked the Trump tax information, was sentenced to five years in prison in 2024. The leaked data showed Trump paid little or no income tax in several years after earning millions from his television show The Apprentice.

The anti-weaponization fund is facing multiple legal challenges. A federal judge in Virginia's eastern district has already issued a temporary block on its establishment and any disbursements. The controversy continues as courts examine whether the settlement represents legitimate judicial process or an improper use of executive power.
