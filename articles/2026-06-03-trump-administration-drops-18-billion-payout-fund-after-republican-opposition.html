Federal judge declines to immediately block Trump administration's $1.8 billion fund

A federal judge rejected an emergency request to block the Trump administration's $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund, though the Justice Department has stated the program is no longer active.

The Justice Department created the fund to compensate individuals it says were wrongly targeted by federal agencies under previous administrations. A government watchdog group filed an urgent motion seeking a court order to prevent any payments from the fund. While the judge declined to grant immediate relief, the court warned the Justice Department against reviving the payout program.

The $1.8 billion initiative represents one of the largest discretionary spending projects undertaken by the Trump administration. Critics have questioned both the legal authority for such payments and the standards used to determine who qualifies for compensation.

The watchdog group expressed doubt about assurances that the fund has been suspended. Their lawsuit challenges the constitutional basis for the program, arguing that Congress did not authorize such payments and that the administration lacks authority to distribute taxpayer funds in this manner. The group contends that any revival would violate constitutional limits on executive spending power.

The case continues in federal court, with the judge signaling concern about the program while stopping short of issuing an immediate injunction. The Trump administration has not indicated whether it intends to restart payments or permanently abandon the fund.

More recently, Todd Blanche, the acting US attorney general, told Congress that the administration was abandoning plans to establish the fund entirely. The decision came after Senate Republicans expressed opposition to using taxpayer money to pay people who claimed unfair prosecution, potentially including those convicted during the January 6 Capitol riot.

Blanche, who previously served as Trump's personal lawyer, made a separate announcement with less public attention: the Internal Revenue Service will be barred from continuing audits of the president, his family, and their "affiliates." This arrangement grants Trump immunity from IRS audits of his past tax returns, an exemption most Americans cannot obtain.

Legal experts say disputes over the weaponization fund raise significant questions about executive branch spending authority and congressional appropriations power that could take months to resolve through the courts. The fund had generated substantial controversy over its lack of transparency regarding administration procedures and evaluation standards for determining compensation eligibility.

The combination of these developments reveals the administration's broader approach to protecting Trump and his political allies from government scrutiny, both through direct compensation programs and through blocking ongoing tax investigations.
