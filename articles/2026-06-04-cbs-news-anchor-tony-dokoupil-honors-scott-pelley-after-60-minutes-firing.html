Scott Pelley Speaks Out After 60 Minutes Firing, Calls for Removal of Bari Weiss

Scott Pelley, a veteran CBS News journalist who spent decades at 60 Minutes, was fired by the network after publicly criticizing editor-in-chief Bari Weiss during a heated staff meeting. Pelley has since accused the network's new leadership of interfering with reporting and pushing him to compromise journalistic integrity.

The conflict erupted following a wave of layoffs at 60 Minutes that removed the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents including Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi. During a Monday morning meeting with newly appointed executive editor Nick Bilton and other CBS News executives, Pelley directly attacked Weiss's leadership. "She's murdering 60 Minutes," Pelley said. "She does not love this place. She was brought in to kill it and is doing exactly that."

After the meeting, Weiss informed Pelley that his behavior was inappropriate. He was terminated the following day, with Bilton citing Pelley's "incivility and contempt" in the dismissal message. Bilton wrote that Pelley had "hijacked" the staff meeting to disparage him and his qualifications.

Pelley has not remained silent since his termination. He issued a public statement accusing CBS News executives of silencing employees and instructing him "to inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. He emphasized that 60 Minutes has maintained its position as America's top-rated program because audiences trust its integrity and quality.

Pelley has also claimed that management interfered with a segment examining an immigration officer's killing of Minneapolis protester Renee Good. According to Pelley, Weiss requested changes to the broadcast, allegedly because the reporting echoed statements made by President Trump.

Pelley's firing marks a significant moment at the storied news program, which is facing major changes. Anderson Cooper announced his departure earlier this year, leaving 60 Minutes with only three full-time correspondents as it enters its 59th season: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Norah O'Donnell also contributes to the program.

The situation has drawn commentary from prominent figures connected to 60 Minutes' history. Sarah Safer, daughter of legendary 60 Minutes correspondent Morley Safer, expressed outrage at the current direction of the program. Rome Hartman, a longtime 60 Minutes producer, called Pelley "among the all-time greats" and noted the irony that Weiss's organization, the Free Press, positions itself against cancel culture while terminating someone for pushing back during a staff meeting.

Pelley joined CBS News in 1989 and served as chief White House correspondent and anchor of the CBS Evening News before becoming a 60 Minutes correspondent. His departure represents a major loss for the program as it navigates leadership changes under new parent company ownership.
