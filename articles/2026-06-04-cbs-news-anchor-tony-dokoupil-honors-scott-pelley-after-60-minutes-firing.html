Scott Pelley Thanks Supporters After 60 Minutes Firing

Scott Pelley posted a message on Instagram thanking supporters after his firing from 60 Minutes. The veteran correspondent wrote that fans have been kind and described them as the wind in his sails.

Pelley's termination came after a heated confrontation with the show's newly appointed executive editor Nick Bilton. The firing was part of a broader shake-up at the long-running CBS news magazine that saw multiple staff members let go in recent weeks.

According to reports, Pelley clashed with network brass over changes at 60 Minutes, where he had worked since 2004. During a Monday morning meeting with Bilton and other CBS News executives, Pelley criticized Bari Weiss, the network's editor-in-chief who joined CBS in October. Pelley said Weiss was "murdering 60 Minutes" and accused her of being brought in to damage the program.

When Pelley met with Weiss and executives the following day, he was told his behavior had been inappropriate. He subsequently informed staff members that he expected to be terminated. That evening, Bilton sent Pelley a message stating he had been "terminated for cause effective immediately." In the message, Bilton specifically addressed Pelley's conduct during their meeting, saying Pelley had "hijacked" the gathering to disparage Bilton's qualifications and intentions.

Bilton also notified 60 Minutes staff of the decision, acknowledging that Pelley meant a great deal to many of them. He stated he had made repeated attempts to have direct conversations with Pelley over the weekend and tried to find common ground during their Tuesday afternoon meeting.

Pelley's departure represents a significant loss for the program. With journalist Anderson Cooper announcing earlier this year that he would be leaving 60 Minutes, the show now has only three full-time correspondents remaining: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Norah O'Donnell contributes to the show as well.

The recent firings have raised concerns among CBS News insiders about the show's future. Besides Pelley, correspondents Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi were also terminated in the recent round of cuts. The departures represent a dramatic transformation for a program that has been the most-watched show in news for decades.

Pelley began his career at CBS News in 1989 and had served as chief White House correspondent and anchor of the CBS Evening News from 2011 to 2017. He originally joined the 60 Minutes franchise in 1999 as a correspondent for the spin-off show 60 Minutes II before moving to the main program.
