Serena and Venus Williams receive Wimbledon doubles wildcard

Serena Williams and Venus Williams have been granted a wildcard to compete together in the women's doubles at Wimbledon this month. The All England Club announced the decision on Tuesday morning in what organizers described as one of the most highly anticipated wildcard announcements in recent memory.

The announcement marks a significant moment for Serena, who is returning to professional tennis after four years away from competition. The 44-year-old did not request a singles wildcard and has remained noncommittal about whether she will compete in singles play at the tournament. Venus, who is 46, has also not received a singles wildcard. She has continued to play sporadically on the professional tour since her debut in 1994, taking time away only due to health-related issues.

The Williams sisters are among the most successful doubles partnerships in tennis history. Together, they have won 14 Grand Slam doubles titles, the second-highest total in the Open Era. They have claimed six women's doubles titles at Wimbledon specifically, the highest total in the Open Era at that venue. Their partnership has also yielded three Olympic gold medals in women's doubles, and they reached the number one ranking in WTA doubles.

The wildcard entry allows the sisters to bypass the standard qualification process and compete directly in the main draw. Wimbledon organizers typically reserve wildcard spots for players who may not meet current ranking requirements but have exceptional circumstances or significant historical connections to the tournament. The Williams sisters clearly fit this category given their storied record at the All England Club.

Serena recently returned to competition at Queen's Club, where she won her first match alongside Victoria Mboko against Erin Routliffe and Nichole Melichar Martinez. However, Mboko subsequently withdrew from their next match and the remainder of the grass court season after suffering a torn medial collateral ligament. Serena is continuing her comeback preparations by competing at the Berlin WTA tournament this week alongside world number 10 singles player Karolina Muchova.

The wildcard announcement also included other notable decisions. Maja Chwalinska, who reached the French Open women's singles final as a qualifier, received a wildcard despite not meeting the rankings cutoff. Her sharp recent rise to number 21 in the world rankings will make her an unusual combination of both wildcard and seeded player at the Grand Slam tournament. Additionally, retiring players Stan Wawrinka and Grigor Dimitrov received singles wildcards, while British veteran Dan Evans received a doubles wildcard with compatriot Henry Searle.

The appearance of the Williams sisters will generate significant attention for the tournament and their presence on court together has historically drawn large crowds and television audiences.
