US and Iran exchange strikes for second consecutive day

The United States and Iran continued their direct military confrontation for a second day, launching fresh strikes across the Middle East and raising concerns about the collapse of a fragile ceasefire. The escalating exchanges came as negotiations to end the conflict stalled, with both nations showing no signs of backing down.

The new round of American airstrikes targeted Iranian cities including Tehran, Bandar Abbas, and areas along the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday morning. The assault appeared more intense and geographically wider than the previous day's strikes, though Iran has not disclosed details about damage sustained. Explosions were reported across multiple Iranian locations as the US military described its attacks as a "proportional response" to Iran's downing of an American Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz on Monday.

Iran responded by targeting American assets in neighboring countries. Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy's Fifth Fleet headquarters, sounded its missile alert sirens following the Iranian strikes. Kuwait closed its airspace due to the Thursday morning attack. Jordan's US embassy warned of potential Iranian strikes, though the country did not officially acknowledge any damage.

The strikes represent the third round of military exchanges this week. The first occurred between Iran and Israel over the weekend, followed by two separate US-Iran confrontations.

Trump administration officials framed the attacks as necessary responses to Iranian aggression. "The strikes are in response to Iran's unwarricted and continued aggression," US Central Command stated. However, Trump also threatened further military action, suggesting potential strikes against Iranian power plants and bridges if negotiations did not progress.

The escalating violence threatens a two-month-old ceasefire that has been deteriorating for weeks. International mediators, including a delegation from Qatar, have attempted to negotiate a permanent peace agreement, but significant obstacles remain. Iran has demanded the lifting of international sanctions, the unfreezing of billions of dollars in frozen assets, and control over the Strait of Hormuz. The United States has insisted that any deal must prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons.

Trump has expressed frustration with the negotiation process, accusing Iranian officials of "playing us for suckers." He claimed the negotiations were close to completion before Iran stalled discussions.

The situation was further complicated by the ongoing conflict between Hezbollah and Israel in Lebanon. Iran has insisted that any ceasefire must include the Lebanese front, while Israel and the United States have sought to keep the two conflicts separate.

Iranian state media reported that previous US strikes damaged two water reservoirs in southern Iran, leaving approximately 20,000 residents without access to water. A tanker crew member was also reported missing following a suspected US military strike on Iranian shipping routes.

As midterm elections approach, Trump has shown interest in securing a peace deal, though significant gaps between the two nations' positions remain unresolved.
