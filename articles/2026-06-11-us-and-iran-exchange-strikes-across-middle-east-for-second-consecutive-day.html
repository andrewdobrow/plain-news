US and Iran Exchange Strikes Across Middle East for Second Consecutive Day

The United States and Iran engaged in direct military exchanges for a second day, with the conflict expanding to involve Israel and raising serious concerns about broader regional warfare in the Middle East.

Israel launched airstrikes on Iran following Iranian missile attacks on northern Israel, marking the first direct exchange between the two countries since a ceasefire agreement on April 8. The Israeli military struck Iranian military installations, including a petrochemical complex, and said it dismantled Iranian defense systems across multiple locations. Iranian state television reported explosions in Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz, and Tehran. The Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps responded by targeting two military bases in Israel as part of Operation Victory, or "Nasr."

The strikes intensified an already volatile situation. Yemen's Houthi rebels, aligned with Iran, fired missiles at Israel and announced a complete ban on Israeli shipping in the Red Sea, threatening to disrupt a crucial global trade route. Missile fragments from Iranian attacks caused damage to homes in a West Bank settlement, though no injuries were reported.

The conflict has created significant diplomatic complications. According to a senior US official, President Trump called Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu before the Iranian attack to urge restraint and delay any retaliation. However, Israel proceeded with strikes anyway. Trump publicly called for both countries to stop their attacks, writing on his Truth Social platform: "Israel and Iran must immediately stop 'shooting'."

Iranian officials rejected suggestions that Netanyahu acted independently from the United States. A foreign ministry spokesperson stated that "no one believes that the Zionist regime would carry out any action without prior coordination and cooperation with the United States." Some Iranian analysts pointed to what they described as video evidence of American involvement, noting that Israel lacks certain long-range cruise missile capabilities attributed to US naval assets in the eastern Mediterranean.

The White House did not respond to inquiries about whether it coordinated with Israel or approved the strikes. This ambiguity has complicated ongoing peace negotiations between the US and Iran, which had been stalled. Iranian hardliners in parliament had already pressured negotiators to abandon talks, and suspicions about American involvement could further derail diplomatic efforts.

The escalating military exchanges rattled global markets. Brent crude oil jumped 3.50 dollars to 96.59 dollars per barrel, while Asian stock markets fell sharply in early trading as investors reacted to potential disruptions in Middle Eastern oil supplies and regional stability.

The geographic spread of the military strikes, spanning from Iran to Israel to the Persian Gulf, underscores how quickly the conflict has expanded beyond bilateral tensions. Military observers warned that the situation bears similarities to previous periods of acute US-Iran hostility, though the current direct military exchanges between multiple nations represent a potentially more dangerous phase with significant consequences for regional stability.
