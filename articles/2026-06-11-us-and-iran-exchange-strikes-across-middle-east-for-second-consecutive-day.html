US and Iran exchange strikes for fifth consecutive day

The United States and Iran conducted sustained military strikes against each other for the fifth straight day, with explosions reported across Iran overnight following attacks from neighboring Gulf states in the region.

The US military completed an additional round of strikes on July 14 at 10pm Eastern Time, with fighter aircraft, drones, and naval vessels launching precision munitions during a seven-hour operation. US Central Command said the strikes targeted dozens of military positions near the strait of Hormuz and Iranian coastal areas, including missile and drone sites, naval capabilities, and coastal defense systems. The military said these attacks were designed to further degrade Iran's ability to threaten commercial shipping.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps responded by targeting what it described as command-and-control, logistics, fuel and military equipment facilities belonging to the US Fifth Fleet in Bahrain. The Guards said this was in response to US actions in the Indian Ocean and efforts to control the strait of Hormuz and restrict shipping routes. They warned that if Washington sought to block the region's oil and gas exports by controlling maritime routes, other export routes serving US and allied interests could also be closed, stating regional energy exports would be "for everyone or for no one."

Iran's revolutionary guards also claimed they attacked Kuwait's Mina Abdullah facility and destroyed a US military logistics center. The Iranian army said it targeted US military facilities at Azraq base in Jordan for a second time. Kuwait reported that its air defenses were intercepting Iranian attack drones, and sirens sounded in Bahrain following the attacks.

President Trump threatened to escalate the conflict further, telling Fox News: "Next week comes the power plants, next week comes the bridges, unless they get to the table and negotiate." However, Trump backtracked on an earlier threat to heavily tax ships passing through the strait of Hormuz.

Despite the military escalation, a top Iranian official indicated some openness to dialogue. Iran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the US decision to renew a naval blockade "has, in a way, dismantled the Islamabad memorandum," referring to an interim deal signed in mid-June that included a 60-day timeline to negotiate an agreement on Iran's nuclear program. However, talks have stalled as fighting over the strait intensified.

US Central Command commander Admiral Brad Cooper said Iran had intentionally targeted civilians by attacking seven commercial ships in the past week, resulting in nearly a dozen civilian crew members killed, missing, or injured.

The US Treasury also imposed new sanctions targeting Mohammad Hossein Shamkhani's network, which the department said is a major force behind Iran's oil exports and has expanded into global containerized shipping and commodities trading.

This represents the longest sustained direct military confrontation between the two countries in recent memory, raising international concerns about wider regional conflict and disruption to critical shipping lanes and global energy markets.
