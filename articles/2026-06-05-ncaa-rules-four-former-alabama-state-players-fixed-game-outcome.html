NCAA rules four former Alabama State players fixed game outcome

The NCAA has announced that four former Alabama State basketball players from the 2024-25 team have been ruled permanently ineligible after accepting payment from gamblers to fix the outcome of a game during that season.

The ruling affects the player who scored the winning basket in Alabama State's first NCAA tournament victory, along with three of his teammates. All four players are now barred from any future NCAA competition.

The decision represents one of the most significant game-fixing cases in recent college basketball history. The NCAA's announcement did not specify which game was targeted or provide details about the amounts of payment involved in the scheme.

Game-fixing scandals have periodically emerged in college basketball over the years, but cases involving multiple players from the same team remain relatively uncommon. The involvement of the player who delivered the decisive moment in Alabama State's tournament breakthrough adds another layer to the case, as it raises questions about the integrity of a historic moment for the program.

The NCAA's investigation uncovered evidence that the players accepted money from individuals with gambling interests to manipulate the outcome of at least one game during the 2024-25 season. The organization's enforcement process ultimately determined that the players violated NCAA rules regarding amateurism and ethical conduct.

Permanent ineligibility represents the NCAA's most severe penalty for student athletes. It prevents players from competing in any NCAA-sanctioned sport for the remainder of their collegiate careers and eliminates their ability to transfer to other NCAA institutions while maintaining eligibility.

The ruling does not appear to have resulted in additional penalties against Alabama State University itself at this time, though the NCAA sometimes takes additional action against institutions in game-fixing cases depending on whether they determine that the school failed in its compliance responsibilities.

The case underscores ongoing concerns about the intersection of gambling and college athletics. As sports betting has expanded across the United States in recent years, colleges and the NCAA have heightened efforts to educate athletes about the dangers of associating with gamblers and the serious consequences of attempting to fix games.

The NCAA has strict rules prohibiting athletes from engaging with gambling operations in any capacity that could compromise the integrity of competition. Even accepting payment to lose intentionally or manipulate game outcomes by specific margins violates these standards and triggers permanent ineligibility.

The announcement serves as a reminder of the NCAA's commitment to maintaining competitive integrity, though it also highlights challenges the organization faces in policing athlete conduct off the court. The case involves players who sought financial benefit through illegal gambling schemes rather than through authorized compensation channels available to athletes.
