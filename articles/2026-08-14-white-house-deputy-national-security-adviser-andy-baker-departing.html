White House deputy national security adviser Andy Baker departing

White House deputy national security adviser Andy Baker is leaving the administration in the coming weeks, according to reporting from Axios. The departure marks a significant shift in the White House's national security leadership structure.

Baker has been a central figure in the administration's foreign policy and national security operations over the past 18 months. Before taking on his current role, he served as national security adviser to Vice President Vance earlier in the administration. He was succeeded in that position by Cliff Sims during the summer.

During his tenure, Baker was directly involved in some of the administration's most sensitive diplomatic initiatives. His work included participation in negotiations with Iran this year, placing him at the center of one of the White House's most consequential foreign policy efforts.

In a statement to Axios, Baker reflected on his time in government: "This has been an amazing journey. I'm proud of everything we've achieved and, of course, am incredibly grateful to the President, Vice President, and Secretary Rubio."

The transition will see some continuity in national security operations. Cliff Sims will remain in his role as national security adviser to the Vice President. Additionally, Mike Needham will continue serving as White House deputy national security adviser, maintaining some stability within the team that manages the administration's international affairs.

Baker's exit comes as the administration continues to navigate complex Middle East diplomacy and other significant foreign policy challenges. His involvement in Iran negotiations had made him one of the most visible figures in shaping the administration's approach to some of its most challenging international relationships.

The White House has not announced a replacement for Baker's position as deputy national security adviser. Beyond confirming that Baker will depart in the coming weeks, the administration has provided limited detail about the timing or circumstances of his departure.

The change represents an evolution in the personnel structure that has guided White House decision-making on international affairs since the administration took office. Despite Baker's departure, the administration appears focused on maintaining continuity through retaining other key figures in the national security apparatus.
