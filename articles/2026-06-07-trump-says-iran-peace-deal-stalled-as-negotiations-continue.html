Trump says Iran peace deal stalled as negotiations continue

President Trump said Iran has not agreed to a deal to end the ongoing conflict because the country is strong and proud, but indicated Tehran will ultimately have to reach an agreement. "They're strong, they're proud, there are things they never thought they'd be doing that they're going to have to do," Trump said, suggesting Iran has no choice but to accept terms in negotiations aimed at ending hostilities between the United States and Iran.

The president acknowledged the difficulty of the diplomatic process without specifying what leverage or circumstances would force Tehran to the negotiating table. Trump stated the deal would either be "great and meaningful, or there will be no deal at all."

Iran's negotiating team has taken a more cautious stance about the talks. Esmail Baghaei, the spokesperson for Iran's negotiating team, said that while negotiators have reached conclusions on many issues, an agreement is not imminent. "It is correct to say that we have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion. But to say that this means the signing of an agreement is imminent – no one can make such a claim," Baghaei said at a foreign ministry press briefing.

Baghaei also pointed to confusion in American positions and Israeli interference as obstacles to reaching a deal. He insisted that a ceasefire in Lebanon must be included in any memorandum of understanding that would lead to Iran allowing commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and the United States lifting its blockade of Iranian ports. Regarding the strait, Baghaei clarified that discussions centered on "fees for navigational services" rather than tolls.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio expressed more optimism about the negotiations. "There were some talks going on in Qatar today, so we'll see if we can make progress. I think it's a lot of talking back and forth going on about specific language in the initial document," Rubio told reporters in Jaipur during an official visit to India. He added that the Strait of Hormuz would open "one way or another."

The conflict between the United States and Iran has created significant diplomatic and logistical challenges extending beyond the negotiating table. These complications have affected international events, including the World Cup, which begins Thursday. FIFA declined Iran's request to relocate its group stage matches from U.S. stadiums to venues in Canada or Mexico.

Iranians are largely banned from traveling to the United States under current restrictions. The Iranian soccer team will have to fly in and out of the country for each match without overnight stays, creating an exhausting schedule for the three group stage games on U.S. soil.

The negotiations represent a test of whether diplomatic channels can resolve the conflict between Washington and Tehran while addressing the humanitarian and logistical consequences of the ongoing tension.
