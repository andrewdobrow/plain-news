Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America customers report service outages

Thousands of customers at major U.S. banks reported trouble accessing services Monday afternoon, with Chase Bank citing a software bug in its hardware systems as a likely cause. Chase, the largest banking institution in the country, said the issue was temporary and not systemic or cyber-related. The outages also affected Wells Fargo and Bank of America, though Chase said the bug in its systems may have impacted other banks as well.

Nearly 5,000 reports of Chase Bank service disruptions appeared on the Downdetector website. Chase told customers that some transactions were taking longer than usual to complete but that they still had access to their accounts and funds. The bank, a subsidiary of JP Morgan Chase, serves more than 86 million customers at 5,000 branches across the United States and 7.4 million small businesses. It is also the top credit card issuer in the country.

US Bank said some of its systems were not directly affected, but clients using Zelle, a digital payment platform, received error messages saying their payments could not be sent. Zelle said its service was operating normally and that issues at other banks could be affecting payments. Customers also reported problems with Truist, PNC and Capital One, though those companies said they had not been impacted.

By early Monday evening, problems appeared to be easing for many customers. As of 6 p.m. Eastern Time, Chase, Bank of America and US Bank had seen a decline in the number of reported outages. The exact nature of the software bug remained unclear.
