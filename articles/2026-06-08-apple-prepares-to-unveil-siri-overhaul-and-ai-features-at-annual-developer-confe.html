Apple unveils AI-powered Siri upgrade at developer conference

Apple announced a major overhaul of its Siri digital assistant at its Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday, integrating the voice tool with the company's new artificial intelligence platform called Apple Intelligence. The upgraded Siri, set to launch in the fall, will function more like advanced AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than a traditional search-based question-and-answer tool.

The announcement represents a significant shift in Apple's approach to artificial intelligence after years of delays and consumer frustration. The company has faced criticism for lagging behind competitors like Google in deploying AI features to its devices.

Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained that the company rebuilt Siri from the ground up with generative AI at its core. The redesigned assistant will have its own dedicated app and offer more conversational interactions. Powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a partnership between the two companies, the new Siri can access Apple's native app ecosystem on phones and computers to assist with planning, information retrieval and directions.

During a product demonstration, Rockwell showed how Siri identified a beach location from a photograph, then retrieved a friend's address and provided directions to the beach in Santa Cruz, California with a stop at the friend's house, all within the Siri interface. The tool will also handle proofreading, shopping, calendar management and camera functions like extracting nutrition information from food images. Initially, the service will only be available in English.

Alongside the Siri upgrade, Apple announced new child safety features designed to give parents greater control over their children's device use. The company acknowledged both the benefits of children having devices and the associated risks, particularly for children under 13. Parents will use an "easy-to-use setup assistant" to customize content their children can access and gradually expand permissions as they age.

New safeguards include requiring children to request permission before browsing unfamiliar websites in Safari and automatically blurring messages containing gore or violence. The screen time interface has been simplified to show parents which apps their children use and for how long. Parents can set daily time allowances either manually or following recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics, which Apple partnered with on these features.

The conference also featured updates to iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, along with new Apple Intelligence features integrated into Safari, messaging and the Home app. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, addressed the company's previous lag in AI development, stating that some competitors appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve."

The timing of these announcements comes after Apple agreed to settle a class-action lawsuit in May for $250 million, with some consumers receiving up to $95 in payouts over allegations that the company falsely advertised Siri's capabilities.
