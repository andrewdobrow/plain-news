Apple prepares to unveil Siri overhaul and AI features at annual developer conference

Apple is moving forward with a significant upgrade to Siri and its broader artificial intelligence strategy, with leadership changes underway at the company. The shift comes as Apple looks to close the gap with competitors in generative AI capabilities.

John Giannandrea, Apple's head of artificial intelligence, is stepping down after seven years with the company. He will be replaced by Amar Subramanya, a veteran AI researcher who previously served as corporate vice-president of AI at Microsoft and spent 16 years at Google, where he led engineering for the Gemini AI assistant.

The leadership transition signals Apple's intention to intensify its focus on AI development. Subramanya will report to Craig Federighi, Apple's head of engineering, who has taken on an expanded role overseeing AI efforts across the company. Tim Cook, Apple's CEO, emphasized that Federighi "has been instrumental in driving our AI efforts, including overseeing our work to bring a more personalized Siri to users next year."

Apple unveiled its Apple Intelligence product suite in June 2024 but has been slower than competitors like Google in integrating generative AI features across its products. The company has introduced some incremental updates, including real-time language translation in new AirPod earphones and an AI-generated voice for fitness app workouts, but more substantial changes remain pending.

The promised Siri overhaul has faced repeated delays. Craig Federighi addressed this during the company's developer conference in June, explaining that "this work needed more time to reach our high-quality bar." Cook later confirmed that Apple was "making good progress on a more personalized Siri" and committed to releasing it in the coming year.

Subramanya brings significant experience to the role. His work on Gemini at Google positioned him at a leading AI company, while his time at Microsoft exposed him to enterprise AI applications. The appointment reflects Apple's determination to strengthen its competitive position in a rapidly evolving landscape where voice assistants and AI capabilities have become central to consumer expectations.

Apple's historical approach has emphasized on-device processing and privacy protections, which could differentiate its AI strategy from cloud-dependent competitors. With billions of users across iPhones, iPads, Macs, and wearables, the company has substantial reach for deploying new AI capabilities.

The company announced that this represents "a new chapter" as it "strengthens its commitment" to artificial intelligence. The leadership change, combined with upcoming software updates and a focus on Siri improvements, suggests Apple is positioning itself to demonstrate meaningful progress in AI development during a period when consumer expectations and competitive pressure continue to accelerate.
