NASA orders astronauts to shelter in spacecraft during ISS air leak repair

NASA directed five astronauts aboard the International Space Station to shelter in their docked Dragon spacecraft and prepare for a possible evacuation while Russian space officials attempted to repair air leaks in the station's Russian segment.

The four crew members of NASA's SpaceX Crew-12 mission and a fifth American astronaut, Chris Williams, who had been aboard the station for 190 days, received the shelter-in-place order. The astronauts included American astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, French astronaut Sophie Adenot of the European Space Agency, and Russian cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev. Two other Russian cosmonauts, Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergey Mikayev, remained aboard the station.

The air leaks were discovered in the transfer tunnel of the Zvezda Service Module during pressurization of the module's transfer chamber. Roscosmos specialists identified two leak sites. They quickly sealed one leak using a two-component sealant called Germetal-1. The second potential leak was located on the conical portion of the transfer chamber. Russian officials stated there was no immediate threat to crew safety or station systems.

After approximately two hours of sheltering, NASA instructed the five astronauts to end safe-haven procedures and return to normal operations aboard the station. The decision came as Roscosmos paused structural repair efforts to assess additional measurements and data from the leak sites.

"Given this development, NASA has instructed the crew members inside the Dragon spacecraft to end the safe haven procedures and return to planned operations aboard the International Space Station. We look forward to working with Roscosmos on a collaborative approach to address the leaks," said Bethany Stevens, a NASA spokesperson.

The Zvezda Service Module, which measures approximately 43 feet long, serves as a critical component of the station. It contains living quarters, life support systems, communications equipment, electrical power distribution, data processing systems, flight control systems, and propulsion systems. The module was the first fully Russian contribution to the ISS and includes docking ports for Russian Soyuz and Progress spacecraft.

Air leaks have presented a persistent challenge for the Russian segment of the space station. Roscosmos has dealt with cracks and leaks in its station compartments for more than five years, with NASA's Office of Inspector General identifying them as a top safety risk. In June, a chartered spaceflight carrying astronauts from India, Poland, and Hungary was postponed due to similar leak concerns.

The International Space Station operates as a collaborative effort between NASA, the Canadian Space Agency, the European Space Agency, Japan's space agency, and Roscosmos. The station has maintained continuous human occupation for 25 years, orbiting approximately 250 miles above Earth.
