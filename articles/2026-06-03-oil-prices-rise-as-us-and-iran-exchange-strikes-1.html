Oil prices rise as US and Iran exchange strikes

Oil prices increased following Iranian drone attacks on Kuwait's main international airport and a broader exchange of military strikes between Iranian and American forces in the Persian Gulf region.

Iranian drones heavily damaged Kuwait's airport, killing at least one person and injuring dozens. The attack came in response to earlier American military actions, including a U.S. strike on an Iranian oil tanker attempting to break through an American blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on Iran's Qeshm Island. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps claimed it attacked the U.S. Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain with missiles and drones, though the U.S. military's Central Command denied the attack was successful.

Kuwait briefly shut down its main airport following the Iranian strikes. The incident marks the latest escalation in a series of military exchanges that have tested a fragile ceasefire agreement between the two countries.

The timing of these strikes comes as efforts to secure a lasting ceasefire appear stalled despite optimistic public statements from American officials. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told the Senate Foreign Relations Committee that a deal with Tehran was within reach and claimed Iran had agreed to negotiate aspects of its nuclear program previously off the table.

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi responded by emphasizing the ceasefire's scope. "The ceasefire between Iran and the US is unequivocally a ceasefire on all fronts, including in Lebanon. Its violation on one front is a violation of the ceasefire on all fronts. The US and Israel are responsible for the consequences of any violation," Araghchi said.

The strikes on Kuwait's airport raised immediate concerns about potential disruptions to energy supplies throughout the Middle East. Kuwait is a major oil producer and OPEC member, and the region as a whole accounts for a substantial portion of global oil output. Any military escalation in the area creates uncertainty for energy markets worldwide.

The incident also highlighted risks to civilian infrastructure and international travel in the region. Kuwait has historically balanced relationships with both Western nations and regional neighbors, placing it in a precarious position during broader conflicts between Iran and the United States.

Financial markets responded to the heightened geopolitical tensions, with stock indexes declining as investors assessed the potential for further escalation. Energy sector stocks and oil futures experienced increased trading activity as market participants repositioned holdings in response to the military developments and the ongoing uncertainty surrounding peace negotiations.

The exchange of strikes underscores the fragility of current diplomatic efforts and the continued volatility in one of the world's most strategically important regions for global energy security.
