U.S. and Saudi Arabia Launch Joint Airstrikes on Iranian Proxies in Iraq

The United States and Saudi Arabia conducted joint military strikes on Iranian-backed proxy groups in Iraq on Wednesday, according to U.S. Central Command. The operation marks a significant expansion of the conflict, drawing Saudi Arabia directly into military action alongside American forces. Global oil prices surged following the announcement as markets reacted to the escalating tensions in the Middle East.

U.S. Central Command said the strikes targeted proxy groups that had launched attacks against U.S. military bases in the region and Saudi energy infrastructure. The coordinated military action represents the first known joint strike operation between Washington and Riyadh against Iranian-aligned forces in Iraq. The attacks come amid broader hostilities involving Iran and its regional proxies across multiple countries.

The involvement of Saudi Arabia signals a widening of the conflict beyond direct U.S.-Iran confrontation. Saudi energy facilities have been targeted by Iranian proxies in recent attacks, threatening global oil supplies and contributing to market volatility. The kingdom's participation in the strikes demonstrates a willingness to take direct military action to protect its critical infrastructure.

Oil markets responded immediately to news of the joint operation, with prices jumping as traders assessed the potential for further disruption to Middle Eastern energy production and export routes. Saudi Arabia remains one of the world's largest oil producers, and any threat to its facilities carries significant implications for global energy supplies. The price spike reflects concerns that the conflict could expand further or lead to sustained attacks on energy infrastructure across the Gulf region.

The strikes add another dimension to an already complex regional crisis, with multiple countries now engaged in military operations related to Iranian proxy activities. U.S. forces have been stationed at bases throughout Iraq and the broader Middle East, making them targets for militia groups aligned with Tehran. The decision by Saudi Arabia to join the U.S. in offensive operations represents a notable shift in the kingdom's approach to countering Iranian influence in the region.
