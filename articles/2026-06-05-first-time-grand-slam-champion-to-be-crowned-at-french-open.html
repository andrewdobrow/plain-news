Alexander Zverev wins French Open for first Grand Slam title

Alexander Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in a tense five-set final. The second-seeded German player finally secured the major championship that had eluded him throughout his career, despite previous success at every other level of professional tennis.

Zverev had previously won Masters 1000 events, claimed the ATP Finals twice, and earned an Olympic gold medal in Tokyo 2020, but he had lost in all three of his previous Grand Slam finals. The French Open final represented his fourth major final appearance and his first victory at this stage.

The match was marked by considerable tension and nerves on both sides of the net. Zverev dominated the opening set 6-1, but Cobolli fought back to win the second set 6-4 as the German's familiar problems with his forehand and second serve resurfaced. Zverev regained control in the third set with a 6-4 victory, completely outserving his opponent throughout the set. Cobolli crumbled under pressure in the set's final moments, spraying three wild unforced errors.

However, Zverev's nerves intensified as the finish line approached. In the fourth set, he appeared overwhelmed by the moment, though Cobolli also struggled. The set went to a tiebreaker where Cobolli, serving at 5-4 with just 39 percent first-serve success, mounted a brilliant comeback. The Italian saved the championship with a searing running forehand winner to force a fifth set.

In the decisive final set, Cobolli's energy reserves were exhausted. He immediately surrendered his opening service game and never recovered, allowing Zverev to pull away convincingly with a 6-1 victory.

After Cobolli missed an overhead on the second championship point, Zverev dropped to his back and began sobbing. He later addressed his team, saying: "We've been through injuries. We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're grand slam champions now and that's what counts."

Zverev's path to the final was significantly aided by the absence of top competitors. Top seed Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were eliminated in consecutive days, while Carlos Alcaraz was absent due to injury. Cobolli, the world number 14, reached his first Grand Slam final without playing in the semifinal after his Italian compatriot Matteo Arnaldi withdrew due to illness.

Speaking after his victory, Zverev reflected on his complex relationship with Roland Garros. "This court is so special to me in so many ways," he said. "I've had the best moments of my life on this court, I had the worst moment of my life on this court. I was laying in that corner over there with seven broken ligaments and two fractured bones. I lost a grand slam final here two years ago. But now, finally, it's a happy end."
