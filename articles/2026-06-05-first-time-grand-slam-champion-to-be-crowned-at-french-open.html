Zverev wins French Open for first Grand Slam title

Alexander Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in a tense five-set final. The second-seeded German player overcame both his opponent and his own mental struggles to end years of frustration at the sport's biggest tournaments.

Zverev had previously won at every other level of professional tennis. He claimed multiple Masters 1000 titles, won the ATP Finals twice, and earned Olympic gold at Tokyo 2020. However, he had lost in all three of his previous Grand Slam finals, making this victory at Roland Garros a crucial breakthrough moment.

The tournament presented Zverev with an unusual opportunity. Top players Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were eliminated in consecutive days, while Carlos Alcaraz was absent due to injury. This left Zverev as the strongest remaining competitor in the draw. Cobolli, ranked world number 14, proved to be the only top-25 player in his path to the final.

The final itself was a nerve-wracking affair. Both players struggled with tension and nerves throughout the match, producing uneven tennis at times. Zverev appeared particularly uncomfortable during stretches, especially in the second half. His forehand and second serve, recurring problem areas in his career, resurfaced under pressure.

Cobolli, competing in his first Grand Slam final, felt the moment intensely. He began the match poorly, unable to compete in the opening set. He found his footing in the second set, pressuring Zverev's serve and striking his forehand with increasing confidence to level the match.

Zverev reasserted control in the third set, reestablishing his service rhythm while Cobolli struggled under pressure. The momentum appeared to be shifting decisively toward the German, but the approaching finish line intensified his nerves. During the fourth set, Zverev looked overwhelmed by tension and appeared to be in the early stages of cramps. Yet Cobolli, feeling the moment even more acutely, was unable to capitalize.

The fourth set went to a tiebreak that Cobolli nearly won. He shanked a straightforward smash on his first set point but responded with a running forehand winner to force a fifth set.

That escape proved to be the turning point. Cobolli completely lost momentum at the start of the final set, giving away his opening service game cheaply and never recovering. Zverev dominated the fifth set to secure the title.

The victory marks a significant milestone for Zverev, who joins the ranks of active Grand Slam champions after multiple near misses. The win came in his fourth Grand Slam final appearance and validates his status among tennis's elite players.
