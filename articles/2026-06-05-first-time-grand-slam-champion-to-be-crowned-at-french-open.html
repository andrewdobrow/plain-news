Zverev wins French Open for first Grand Slam title

Alexander Zverev claimed his first Grand Slam championship at the French Open, defeating Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in a tense five-set final. The second seed's victory came after three previous Grand Slam final losses, finally delivering the major title that had eluded him throughout his career.

Zverev dominated the opening set, but Cobolli mounted a comeback in the second as the German's familiar weaknesses emerged. Zverev's forehand troubles and second serve issues resurfaced, allowing the Italian to level the match. The German regained control in the third set, completely outserving his opponent. However, nerves overwhelmed Zverev in the fourth set as he approached the finish line. Cobolli forced a tiebreaker and won it with a running forehand winner to force a deciding set. In the fifth, Cobolli's energy completely depleted, and Zverev pulled away to secure the championship.

Throughout his career, Zverev had won at nearly every other level of professional tennis. He captured Masters 1000 events, triumphed twice at the ATP Finals, and earned Olympic gold in Tokyo in 2020. Yet the Grand Slam title remained elusive until now. This edition of the French Open was unusually chaotic, with top players eliminated early. Top seed Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were bounced from the tournament in consecutive days, while Carlos Alcaraz was absent due to injury. With the strongest competitors gone, Zverev was clearly the most dominant player remaining in the draw.

Cobolli, ranked world number 14, reached his first Grand Slam final in unexpected fashion. He didn't need to strike a ball in the semifinal after his Italian compatriot Matteo Arnaldi withdrew due to illness. The underdog felt the moment intensely from the start, unable to compete in the opening set. He showed more composure in subsequent sets, using his drop shot effectively and approaching the net courageously, but ultimately couldn't maintain the intensity required to overcome Zverev.

After victory, Zverev addressed his team, saying: "We've been through injuries. We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're grand slam champions now and that's what counts."

Zverev also reflected on his history at Roland Garros, speaking of the trauma of a serious injury sustained there. "This court is so special to me in so many ways," he said. "I had the worst moment of my life on this court. I was laying in that corner over there with seven broken ligaments and two fractured bones. I lost a grand slam final here two years ago. But now, finally, it's a happy end."

The victory marks a breakthrough moment for the German player and answers longstanding questions about his ability to perform when it matters most on tennis's biggest stages.
