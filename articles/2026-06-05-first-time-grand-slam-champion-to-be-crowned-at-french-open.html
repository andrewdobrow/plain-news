Andreeva wins French Open at 17 for first Grand Slam title

Russian teenager Mirra Andreeva won the French Open women's singles title, defeating qualifier Maja Chwalinska in the final at Roland Garros. At 17 years old, Andreeva claimed her first Grand Slam trophy in what she described as a battle against internal challenges as much as her opponent.

Andreeva topped Chwalinska to secure the championship, capping a tournament where both players exceeded expectations. The Russian teen's victory marks a breakthrough moment in her young career, adding a major title to her developing resume on the professional tour.

After the match, Andreeva thanked her coach, her team, and notably herself for the achievement. She said she had to overcome many demons inside to win the championship, acknowledging the mental aspect of competing at the highest level of tennis. The teen's self-awareness and candor about the psychological challenges drew attention from tennis observers and former champions.

Chwalinska, a qualifier who reached the final without hotel accommodations or major sponsorship support, reflected on what she called unforgettable three weeks at Roland Garros. The Polish player's unexpected run to the championship match represented one of the tournament's most surprising storylines, as she defeated higher-ranked opponents including Diana Shnaider in the semifinals.

The final brought together two young players who had navigated different paths to the championship stage. Andreeva entered as the more established name with higher rankings and previous tour success, while Chwalinska fought through qualifying rounds and the main draw as an underdog. The tournament showcased emerging talent in women's tennis, with both finalists under intense scrutiny from tennis analysts and former champions like Chris Evert, who praised aspects of Andreeva's game and competitive approach.
