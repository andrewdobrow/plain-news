Alexander Zverev Wins French Open Four Years After Wheelchair Exit

Alexander Zverev captured his first Grand Slam title at the French Open, defeating Italy's Flavio Cobolli 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 in a tense five-set final. The victory marks a remarkable turnaround for the German player, who four years earlier was carried from the same court in a wheelchair after suffering severe injuries.

The second-seeded Zverev had previously reached three Grand Slam finals without winning, despite achieving success at every other level of professional tennis. He had won Masters 1000 events, claimed the ATP Finals twice, and earned an Olympic gold medal in Tokyo in 2020. The French Open title had eluded him until now.

Zverev's path to the title became clearer when top seed Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic were eliminated from the tournament in consecutive days, while Carlos Alcaraz withdrew due to injury. Cobolli, ranked 14th in the world, emerged as the only top-25 player Zverev faced on his route to the final.

The final itself proved emotionally draining for both competitors. Cobolli, reaching his first Grand Slam final without playing a semifinal match after his Italian compatriot Matteo Arnaldi withdrew with a virus, felt the pressure intensely from the start. He was overwhelmed in the opening set but regrouped to take the second.

Zverev showed his dominance in the third set by completely outserving Cobolli, but nerves crept in as the finish line approached. He struggled with his forehand and second serve in the fourth set, forcing a tiebreak that Cobolli won with a running forehand winner to level the match. However, the Italian's energy was depleted, and he lost his opening service game in the final set without ever recovering.

When Cobolli missed an overhead on the second championship point, Zverev dropped to his back and began sobbing with emotion.

In his victory speech, Zverev addressed his team: "We've been through injuries. We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're grand slam champions now and that's what counts."

He reflected on the court's significance in his life, saying it had given him both the best and worst moments. He referenced the corner where he lay with seven broken ligaments and two fractured bones during his 2022 exit, and his loss in a Grand Slam final two years prior. "But now, finally, it's a happy end," Zverev said.

The Philippe-Chatrier crowd enthusiastically received his victory, marking the culmination of his long journey back from injury to claiming tennis's biggest prize.
