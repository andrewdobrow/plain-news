Mexico opens 2026 World Cup with match against South Africa

The 2026 FIFA World Cup began Thursday in Mexico City as the host nation took on South Africa in the tournament's opening fixture. The match marked the start of a World Cup jointly hosted by the United States, Mexico, and Canada, making it the first time three countries have shared hosting responsibilities.

The opening ceremony featured international performers including Shakira, who contributed to the tournament's official soundtrack. Mexican singer Alejandro Fernández and other artists participated in pre-match festivities at the stadium. This ceremony was the first of three planned opening events, with additional ceremonies scheduled at venues in the United States and Canada.

Mexico scored the tournament's first goal against South Africa during the opening match. The atmosphere in Mexico City reflected both celebration and tension, as fans gathered to mark the historic occasion while demonstrations took place outside the venue. The capital city was selected to host the opening fixture as Mexico pursues advancement from the group stage on home soil.

The 2026 tournament format distributes matches across North American stadiums in all three host countries over the coming weeks. This marks Mexico's third time hosting the World Cup, following tournaments in 1970 and 1986. The expanded format spreads games across a broader geographic area than any previous World Cup.

On the pitch, Mexico controlled much of the opening match. Player Gutierrez emerged as one of the standout performers during the first half, creating several chances from inside-left. He nearly added a second goal when he attempted a curling effort toward the far corner but skewed the shot wide. Mexico's Jimenez also had opportunities to extend the lead, though one chance went just over the bar.

South Africa struggled to mount an offensive threat throughout the early stages. Their defense was tested repeatedly as Mexico's attacking players pressed forward consistently. South Africa's Mbokazi had one of their few chances when he headed a cross wide of the goal. The team appeared disorganized in their approach during the opening period.

Mexico's midfield controlled possession through much of the match. Players like Montes, Quinones, and Gutierrez combined well in building attacking moves. Alvarado provided crosses from the flank while the team maintained pressure on South Africa's defense.

The opening match set the tone for the expanded 48-team tournament. Mexico will look to leverage their home advantage as they advance through the group stage and pursue a deep run in the knockout rounds. South Africa faced the challenge of recovering from a difficult opening performance as the tournament progresses.
