Mexico defeats South Africa 2-0 in opening match of 2026 World Cup

Mexico kicked off the 2026 FIFA World Cup with a 2-0 victory over South Africa at Azteca Stadium in Mexico City. The match, which marked the start of the tournament, drew a capacity crowd and saw three players sent off during a heated contest between the co-host nation and the African side.

Julian Quinones opened the scoring for Mexico, netting the first goal of the 2026 World Cup. The forward's clinical finish gave the home team an early advantage in front of the raucous crowd at Mexico City Stadium. Raul Jimenez later sealed the victory with a second goal for El Tri.

The match became increasingly intense as three red cards were issued during the contest, reducing South Africa to nine men by the final whistle. The disciplinary issues overshadowed what was otherwise a celebratory occasion for Mexico and the tournament. Despite the numerical disadvantage, South Africa failed to find the back of the net against the host nation.

The 2026 World Cup marks the first time the tournament has been co-hosted by three nations: Mexico, the United States, and Canada. The opening ceremony featured performances by international star Shakira and other artists from the tournament's official soundtrack, setting a festive tone before kickoff. The event showcased Mexican culture and hospitality as the nation welcomed the global football community.

Mexico's opening win puts them in a strong position as they pursue advancement from the group stage. The three points give El Tri early momentum in their campaign, though the disciplinary issues that marred the match may raise concerns for tournament organizers about maintaining control in future games. South Africa will need to regroup quickly and address their discipline problems as they look to recover from the opening defeat.
