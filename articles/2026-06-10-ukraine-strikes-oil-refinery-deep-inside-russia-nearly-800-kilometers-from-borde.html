Russia Launches Deadly Strikes on Kyiv, Killing at Least 21

Russia deployed nearly 500 drones and more than 70 missiles in an hours-long assault on Kyiv and other parts of Ukraine in the early hours of Thursday, killing at least 21 people and injuring dozens more. City Mayor Vitali Klitschko described it as the worst Russian attack on the capital during more than four years of sustained air strikes against Ukraine.

Explosions rocked the city for several hours as waves of drones, cruise missiles, and ballistic missiles descended on multiple locations. Ukrainian air defense systems worked to intercept incoming weapons, but the sheer volume of the attack overwhelmed defenses across numerous areas. As dawn broke, fires burned at strike sites throughout Kyiv, with debris hitting residential buildings in several districts and a hotel on one of the central boulevards.

One of the most devastating impacts occurred on the left bank of the Dnipro River, where a large section of a nine-story apartment block was destroyed, reduced to rubble. Rescue teams spent the day searching through the remains hoping to find survivors. The death toll rose throughout Thursday and officials warned it could climb higher, with about 70 people hospitalized and rescue operations continuing.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had warned Ukrainians on Wednesday that a massive attack was likely. Speaking during a visit to Dublin, he urged people to use shelters and take precautions. In response, more than 50,000 residents took refuge in metro stations overnight, with some bringing mattresses and tents to sleep underground as the platforms became crowded.

Damage was recorded across 30 locations in the city, with most strikes hitting ordinary residential buildings. The Russian defense ministry claimed the attack targeted military facilities and energy infrastructure in retaliation for recent Ukrainian strikes, though it also hit regions outside the capital.

Upon returning to Kyiv, Zelenskyy visited one attack site and criticized international allies, saying that if they had provided air defense missiles as agreed, the damage would have been significantly less. "We are fighting alone. The victims are only Ukrainians," he stated.

The assault came as Ukraine has intensified long-range drone strikes against Russian oil refineries in recent weeks, causing fuel shortages across multiple Russian regions and prompting some areas to introduce petrol rationing. Ukrainian officials said they intended to maintain this pressure on Russian infrastructure.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, visiting Japan, rejected claims that the strikes constituted legitimate retaliation. He stated that in the conflict between an aggressor and a defending nation, Ukraine has every right to strike military targets in Russia while Russia has no right to attack Ukrainian territory.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer called the attacks "a stark reminder that while Ukraine continues to pursue peace, Putin continues to inflict suffering and violence on the Ukrainian people."
