Russia launches deadliest strike on Kyiv this year, killing at least 30

Russia carried out what city officials are calling the most massive attack on Kyiv in over four years of full-scale war, deploying nearly 500 drones and more than 70 missiles against the Ukrainian capital and other regions in the early hours of Thursday. The assault killed at least 27 people, with the death toll expected to rise as rescuers continue searching through rubble.

The hours-long barrage struck locations across a wide geographic area of the city, with at least 30 separate damage sites recorded. The strikes hit residential buildings in several districts, a hotel on a central boulevard, and damaged critical infrastructure. In one location on the left bank of the Dnipro River, a nine-story apartment block was partially destroyed, reduced to rubble as rescuers worked through the day searching for survivors. About 70 people were admitted to hospital, and dozens more were injured.

Mayor Vitali Klitschko described it as the worst attack on the capital during the entire war. The Russian Defence Ministry said the strikes targeted military facilities and energy infrastructure, claiming the assault was retaliation for Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian facilities. However, Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha rejected this characterization, stating it was "immoral" to equate attacks by an aggressor nation with those of a country defending itself.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had warned Ukrainians on Wednesday that such an attack might occur, urging people to take shelter. In response, more than 50,000 people took refuge in metro stations across the capital overnight, with some bringing mattresses and tents to sleep underground. When dawn broke, fires were burning at multiple sites across the city as waves of missiles and drones had repeatedly targeted the area.

Zelenskyy returned early from a trip to Ireland to visit attack sites on Thursday afternoon. He criticized Ukraine's allies for not fully following through on agreements to provide air defense missiles, saying the damage would have been less severe with adequate defensive systems. "We are fighting alone," he said. "The victims are only Ukrainians."

The timing of the assault comes as Ukraine has intensified long-range drone strikes against Russian oil refineries, causing fuel shortages across multiple Russian regions and forcing some areas to introduce petrol rationing. Ukrainian officials have indicated they plan to maintain pressure on Russian military and infrastructure targets.

The UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer called the attacks "a stark reminder that while Ukraine continues to pursue peace, Putin continues to inflict suffering and violence on the Ukrainian people." The assault underscores the continued vulnerability of Ukrainian cities despite international military support and ongoing efforts to strengthen air defenses against increasingly large-scale Russian bombardments.
