Kennedy Center removes Trump name from facade following court rulings

Workers removed Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center's facade in Washington, D.C., after the performing arts venue missed a federal judge's deadline to complete the task. The removal began in the early hours of Saturday, following a judge's rejection of an emergency appeal to block the work.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper had ordered the Kennedy Center to remove all references to "Trump Kennedy Center" by Friday at 11:59 p.m. local time. Crews had erected scaffolding on Friday as onlookers gathered into the evening, though storms delayed the removal work until the following morning. The actual removal then proceeded behind a screen.

The building's exterior had displayed "The Donald J Trump and The John F Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts" as of Monday afternoon, before the work began. Following the removal, the facility will be known as "The John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts" or simply the "Kennedy Center."

In his 94-page ruling, Judge Cooper determined that the Kennedy Center's organic statute makes clear the institution is named for President Kennedy and cannot bear any other formal name or public memorial without congressional action. "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it," Cooper wrote.

The judge's order also addressed a broader dispute over the venue's governance. Trump's handpicked board had previously approved a $257 million "revitalization project" that would have closed the center for two years. Cooper temporarily blocked this renovation plan as part of his decision.

The Kennedy Center's general counsel issued a memo directing employees to immediately change email signatures, letterheads, and other documents to remove Trump's name. The deadline for completing changes to templates, forms, signage, brochures, and website pages was set for June 12, 2026.

The removal of Trump's name represents a significant change to the visual identity of one of Washington's most recognizable landmarks along the city's monumental core. The Kennedy Center operates as a federally chartered organization that also receives private donations and functions as the nation's cultural center and memorial to President John F. Kennedy.

Following the judge's decision, Trump responded with a lengthy statement on social media, expressing his position on the matter and the legal proceedings. The dispute centered on questions about the institution's authority to modify naming agreements and building signage based on its governing structure and governance guidelines.
