Houthi rebels seize control of key Red Sea strait as Yemeni army withdraws to defend Aden

Iran-backed Houthi rebels have taken full control of the Bab al-Mandab Strait and nearby offshore islands after forcing the army of Yemen's internationally recognized government to withdraw from the Red Sea coast to defend the interim capital, Aden. The strait, which accounts for around 12 percent of global trade with 70 to 150 ships passing through daily, connects the Red Sea to the Indian Ocean.

The Houthi advance follows the breakdown of a truce with Saudi Arabia in July and a failed government counter-offensive. The rebels seized the initiative with a rapid push down the Red Sea coast, prompting the Yemeni army to retreat. The advance complicates Saudi Arabia's ability to export oil, particularly after a U.S.-Israel war with Iran in February led Tehran to restrict oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. Saudi Arabia had shifted to pumping oil overland through its East-West Pipeline to a Red Sea terminal at Yanbu, but that pipeline was hit by Houthi drones in July.

Nicholas Hopton, a former British ambassador to Yemen and Iran, said the decision to prioritize Aden's defense over other areas suggests the war is not going well for the Saudi-backed government. He warned that if Aden, a city of roughly 1.25 million, were to fall to the Houthis, it would precipitate further escalation in the regional crisis and additional fragility in the global economy.

Mohammed al-Basha, founder of the U.S.-based Basha Report, said the Houthis would face significant challenges capturing the heavily fortified city and that any attempt would come at a high human cost. He said the rebels appear to be seeking a negotiated surrender by exploiting divisions among southern factions. Former British defense attaché to Yemen Iain Smailes warned the advance could trigger oil price spikes and enable increased weapons smuggling from Iran and the Horn of Africa, extending Iranian and Houthi influence in East Africa.
