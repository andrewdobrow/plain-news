Russian Attack Sets Fire to Historic Orthodox Cathedral in Kyiv

Russia launched a massive missile and drone attack on Kyiv that damaged the Dormition Cathedral, part of the Pechersk Lavra monastery complex and a UNESCO world heritage site recognized as one of Ukraine's most significant religious and cultural landmarks.

The overnight assault involved 70 missiles and 611 drones, according to Ukraine's military. Air defense systems intercepted 50 missiles and 582 drones. The attack killed five people in Kyiv and drove residents to underground shelters as heavy explosions shook the capital throughout the night. Six people were reported injured.

The cathedral fire represented a strike on a location of profound importance to Ukrainian Christians and the nation's cultural heritage. Metropolitan Epiphanius, head of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, described it as an attack on "one of the holiest places in the Christian world." Footage showed flames reaching toward the cathedral's domes as the monastery burned.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the cathedral attack "one of Russia's most serious crimes against Christian culture to date." He urged G7 leaders meeting in France to increase pressure on Moscow and provide more support for Ukraine's air defense capabilities. Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko posted images of the burning monastery and wrote that the attack demonstrated "the true face of Russia's Orthodox values."

Beyond the cathedral, Russia struck multiple locations across Kyiv. The Oleksandr Dovzhenko national film studio, which houses Ukraine's largest and oldest costume collection, was also hit. Damage was reported at 16 locations across the capital. France's foreign ministry compared the cathedral attack to a strike on Notre Dame, with its foreign minister describing Russia's actions as demonstrating "cruelty."

Russia denied targeting the cathedral, claiming it had been hit by a U.S.-made Patriot air-defense missile. However, Ukrainian security officers photographed remains of two Shahed drones at the monastery site, contradicting Russia's account. A young priest volunteering as an army chaplain said the explosion at 4:55 a.m. was "massive" and shook the building. He noted that for Russians, "there is no sanctuary" and accused them of having "no values."

Ukraine announced it would pursue procedures within UNESCO and other international mechanisms to respond to what it called "state barbarism." As dawn broke and the monastery continued burning, staff rang bells in defiance.

Outside Kyiv, at least five people were killed in the city of Kharkiv in what appeared to be a double-tap strike targeting emergency responders. The attack followed warnings from Vladimir Putin that Moscow would conduct "systemic" strikes on Ukraine amid reports of mounting Russian battlefield setbacks.
