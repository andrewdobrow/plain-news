Gene Shalit, Today Show Film Critic for 40 Years, Dies at 100

Gene Shalit, the television personality known for his distinctive mustache and bow ties, died Friday at age 100. His family confirmed to NBC News that he "passed away peacefully today after 100 years of an amazing life."

Shalit became a recognizable fixture on NBC's Today Show, where he spent four decades reviewing films and books for morning television audiences. He appeared on the program from 1970 until his retirement in 2010, making him one of the longest-serving contributors in the show's history.

His signature appearance and playful approach to criticism helped bring film and book reviews to a mass audience at a time when such coverage was primarily found in newspapers and specialty publications. Shalit's accessible style and use of wordplay distinguished him from more serious critics of his era, and his reviews became known for their lighthearted tone and clever language.

During his 40-year tenure on Today, Shalit reviewed thousands of films and books, offering his perspectives on the latest cultural releases to millions of viewers each morning. His presence on the program made him one of the most recognizable television personalities of his generation. The combination of his distinctive look and his entertaining reviewing style earned him a dedicated following among the show's audience.

When Shalit retired in 2010 at age 84, his departure marked the end of a significant era in television criticism. For decades, his voice had been one of the most influential in shaping how American audiences thought about movies and literature. His ability to reach such a wide audience with his cultural commentary reflected a time when morning television could be a primary source of entertainment criticism for many viewers.

Shalit's career in broadcasting began in the early 1970s when he joined Today as a book and film reviewer. He gradually became a permanent fixture on the program, appearing regularly to share his thoughts on new releases. His approach to criticism emphasized accessibility and humor rather than academic analysis, making his reviews appealing to the general public.

His death marks the end of an important chapter in American television history. Shalit represented a generation of television personalities who could command large audiences and influence cultural conversations through their presence on morning news programs. His distinctive style and long-running role on Today helped establish the format for entertainment criticism on television, setting a template that would influence how movies and books were discussed on the medium for years to come.

NBC News confirmed his passing, with tributes from across the television industry noting his remarkable career and the lasting impact of his unique presence on American television.
