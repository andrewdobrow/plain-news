NASA names four astronauts for Artemis III lunar mission training

NASA has announced the crew for its Artemis III mission, revealing an all-male team of four astronauts selected to advance the agency's plan to return humans to the Moon. The announcement was made at NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston on Tuesday.

Commander Randy Bresnik will lead the crew, joined by European Space Agency pilot Luca Parmitano and NASA mission specialists Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas. The team is scheduled to launch in 2027 for a two-week mission in low Earth orbit.

Unlike previous Artemis missions, the Artemis III crew will not travel to the Moon or approach it closely. Instead, they will remain near Earth to test docking procedures and life support systems for two competing lunar landers: Blue Origin's Blue Moon and SpaceX's Human Landing System. These tests will prepare systems for Artemis IV, the planned first crewed lunar landing since 1972, currently targeted for 2028.

Bresnik brings extensive spaceflight experience to the mission. The Marine colonel previously commanded the International Space Station and flew on Space Shuttle missions. He holds the distinction of being one of only two NASA astronauts to have a child born while in space.

Parmitano, an Italian Air Force fighter pilot, survived a harrowing incident during a 2013 spacewalk when his helmet filled with liquid. He has also earned recognition as the first disc jockey to broadcast from space, and an asteroid bears his name.

Rubio, an Army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the NASA record for the longest single spaceflight duration at 371 days. He is based in Miami, Florida.

Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer, comes from Miami as well. He has not previously flown to space but trained as part of the backup crew alongside Artemis II astronauts during their preparation.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman praised the crew and the broader space program, describing it as "Earth's first starfleet." He highlighted the multiple spacecraft that will operate simultaneously during these missions, including vehicles from the United States, Russia, and China, calling it "the very beginning of Earth's first Starfleet."

The Artemis III mission depends on successful completion of ongoing development work by private contractors. Blue Origin recently experienced a setback when its New Glenn rocket was destroyed in a launchpad explosion on May 28 in Florida. Company officials have expressed confidence that both the rocket and launchpad will be ready for the 2027 flight, with NASA pledging active support to resolve technical challenges.

The mission represents a significant step forward in NASA's long-term strategy to establish sustained lunar exploration and eventually support missions to Mars.
