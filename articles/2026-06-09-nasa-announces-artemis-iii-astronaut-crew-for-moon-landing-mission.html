NASA names four astronauts for Artemis III moon landing mission in 2027

NASA has announced the crew of four astronauts selected for the Artemis III mission, marking a major milestone in the agency's plan to return humans to the moon. The crew will launch in 2027 for a two-week mission to low Earth orbit that will test critical systems before a planned lunar landing on Artemis IV in 2028.

The Artemis III crew consists of NASA astronaut and commander Randy Bresnik, European Space Agency pilot Luca Parmitano from Italy, and NASA mission specialists Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas. The all-male crew will practice docking their Orion capsule with lunar landers developed by private companies SpaceX and Blue Origin during their orbital mission.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman described the group as part of "Earth's first starfleet" as he unveiled the crew and detailed the next stages of the agency's moon program. The Artemis III mission represents a significant step forward in testing the systems and procedures needed for eventual human landings on the lunar surface.

The Artemis program has already achieved historic milestones. The recent Artemis II mission, which launched earlier this year, completed a record-breaking journey around the moon. That crewed flight surpassed the maximum distance achieved by Apollo 13, traveling more than 250,000 miles from Earth. The four astronauts on Artemis II were the first lunar travelers since Apollo 17 in 1972, and the mission included historic achievements including sending the first person of color, the first woman, and the first non-American on a lunar mission.

During their five-day journey around the moon, the Artemis II crew experienced remarkable moments. Commander Reid Wiseman and his crewmates captured stunning images of Earth and witnessed the lunar far side from closer than any humans since the Apollo program. Despite the spectacular views, the mission also dealt with practical challenges, including a malfunctioning toilet that caused problems throughout the flight.

The Artemis program uses NASA's Space Launch System rocket and Orion spacecraft, representing years of development following significant delays and cost overruns. The Space Launch System finally achieved its inaugural crewed flight with Artemis II after being rolled back to its hangar for repairs multiple times before launch.

Future Artemis missions will focus on the lunar south pole region, an area of scientific interest that differs from the Apollo landing sites near the moon's equator. The south pole contains permanently shadowed craters that may hold water ice, a resource that could support future exploration and establish a permanent lunar base.

The progression of Artemis missions demonstrates NASA's commitment to a multi-decade exploration program involving international partners and commercial companies. These missions will test capabilities needed for longer-duration stays on the moon and eventual missions beyond Earth orbit, laying the groundwork for human exploration of Mars.
