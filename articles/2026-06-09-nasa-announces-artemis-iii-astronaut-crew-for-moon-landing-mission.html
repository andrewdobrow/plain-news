NASA names crew for Artemis III moon landing mission

NASA announced the four astronauts selected for the Artemis III mission on Tuesday, marking a significant step toward returning humans to the lunar surface. The crew will conduct a two-week orbital test flight in 2027 before the planned first crewed lunar landing on Artemis IV in 2028.

The crew consists of three American astronauts and one European. Randy Bresnik, a Marine colonel and former International Space Station commander, will serve as mission commander. Frank Rubio, an Army Black Hawk helicopter pilot and flight surgeon, holds the record for the longest single spaceflight by a NASA astronaut at 371 days. Andre Douglas, a systems engineer and Coast Guard reserve officer, will round out the NASA contingent. Luca Parmitano, an Italian astronaut with the European Space Agency, will pilot the mission.

Unlike the Artemis II mission that circled the moon in April, Artemis III will remain in lower Earth orbit. The crew will test docking procedures and life support systems with lunar landers developed by Blue Origin and SpaceX. These tests will determine which lander system performs best ahead of the actual moon landing on Artemis IV.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman described the effort as "Earth's first starfleet" during the announcement in Houston. He emphasized the scale of the undertaking, noting the numerous spacecraft that will operate simultaneously in orbit, including vessels from multiple nations and companies.

The Artemis program represents an international collaboration. Parmitano's selection reflects the partnership between NASA and the European Space Agency, which provides service modules for the Orion spacecraft. The mission involves contributions from multiple countries participating in lunar exploration efforts.

The Artemis IV landing in 2028 will mark the first crewed lunar touchdown since Apollo 17 in 1972. NASA plans to land astronauts near the moon's south pole, a region containing water ice in permanently shadowed craters that differs from the equatorial sites visited during the Apollo program.

Blue Origin's lunar lander program faced a setback on May 28 when its New Glenn rocket exploded during a launch pad test in Florida. John Couluris, Blue Origin's senior vice-president of lunar permanence, acknowledged the "significant anomaly" but expressed confidence that the company and launch pad would be ready for next year's flight. Jeremy Parsons, NASA's acting deputy associate administrator for the moon to Mars program, stated that NASA is actively working with Blue Origin to address the incident and ensure mission success.

The Artemis III crew will undergo intensive training for the complex mission. Bresnik brings extensive spaceflight experience from previous shuttle missions and ISS command. Rubio's record spaceflight duration demonstrates his capability for extended missions. Parmitano, a former fighter pilot, survived a dangerous spacesuit malfunction during a 2013 spacewalk from the ISS. Douglas, though new to spaceflight, trained as part of the Artemis II backup crew.
