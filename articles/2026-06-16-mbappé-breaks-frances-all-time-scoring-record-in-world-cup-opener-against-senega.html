Messi scores hat trick to tie World Cup all-time scoring record

Lionel Messi scored three goals in Argentina's 3-1 victory over Algeria in their opening match of the 2026 World Cup, bringing his career World Cup total to 16 goals and tying Miroslav Klose's all-time record. The reigning champions began their title defense with Messi leading the attack in what multiple observers described as a masterful performance.

Messi's hat trick came on a day packed with star performances at the tournament. The 39-year-old forward demonstrated his continued dominance on the sport's biggest stage, adding to his legacy in what could be his final World Cup appearance. His three goals propelled Argentina to victory in their Group stage opener.

The achievement places Messi alongside Germany's Miroslav Klose, who set the previous record across four World Cup tournaments between 2002 and 2014. Messi has now competed in six World Cups, having made his tournament debut in 2006. He finally captured the trophy that had eluded him throughout his career when Argentina won the 2022 World Cup in Qatar.

Argentina entered the tournament as defending champions after their triumph four years ago. The victory over Algeria gives them three points in their opening Group match as they seek to become the first team to win consecutive World Cups since Brazil accomplished the feat in 1958 and 1962.

Messi now has the opportunity to break Klose's record outright as Argentina continues through the tournament. The forward's performance silenced any questions about whether he could maintain his elite level at age 39, delivering what observers called one of the finest individual displays of the tournament's opening matches.
