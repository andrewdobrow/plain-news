Messi equals World Cup scoring record with hat trick in Argentina win

Lionel Messi scored three goals to equal the all-time World Cup scoring record as defending champions Argentina defeated Algeria 3-0 in their opening match of the 2026 tournament. The hat trick brought Messi's World Cup career total to 16 goals, matching the record set by Germany's Miroslav Klose.

The performance marked another historic milestone for the 38-year-old forward, who is appearing in his sixth World Cup tournament. Messi's three goals powered Argentina to a comfortable victory as they began their title defense. The win gave the reigning champions three points in their opening group stage match.

Messi shared top billing on a day featuring strong performances from other global stars. Kylian Mbappe scored twice for France in their win over Senegal, breaking France's national team goal record in the process. Erling Haaland also scored in his World Cup debut, continuing his pattern of finding the net in his first appearances in major competitions.

The Argentine captain has now scored World Cup goals across six different tournaments, a span covering nearly two decades of international competition. His previous World Cup appearances date back to 2006, and he finally captured the trophy in 2022 when Argentina defeated France in the final. The 2026 tournament is expected to be his final World Cup.

Argentina's opening win sets them on course in group play as they seek to become the first team to successfully defend the World Cup title since Brazil in 1962. Messi now needs just one more goal to claim the outright World Cup scoring record for himself, surpassing Klose's mark that has stood since the 2014 tournament.
