Trump proposes government ownership stake in AI companies

President Trump surprised technology executives by proposing that the United States government take a small ownership stake in major artificial intelligence companies. The president raised the idea during remarks to reporters aboard Air Force One, suggesting the American public should benefit financially from companies he said would become worth trillions of dollars.

"There's something very interesting about it, where it almost becomes a partnership with the American public," Trump told reporters. "It's like you make them partners in this revolution. It would be a beautiful thing. It would make them rich." The proposal represents a significant potential shift in the relationship between the federal government and private technology companies.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has previously advocated for a similar concept. The idea would create a direct financial connection between taxpayers and the artificial intelligence industry, which has received significant attention from policymakers concerned about both economic competitiveness and national security implications.

The proposal comes as the administration has taken other steps to influence the technology sector. No formal legislation or executive action has been announced to implement the ownership stake idea. The reaction from other technology executives and congressional leaders has not been reported.

The concept raises questions about how such ownership would work in practice, including how stakes would be determined, whether participation would be mandatory for companies, and how returns would be distributed to the public. Trump framed the idea as giving Americans a share in what he described as a technological revolution that will generate enormous wealth.
