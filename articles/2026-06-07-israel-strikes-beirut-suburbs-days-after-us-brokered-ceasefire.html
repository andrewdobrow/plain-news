Israel strikes Beirut suburbs days after US-brokered ceasefire

Israel launched airstrikes on Beirut's southern suburbs on Sunday, escalating tensions just days after a ceasefire proposal was announced. The Israeli military said the strikes targeted "terrorist headquarters" in response to Hezbollah firing at Israeli territory. The attacks killed two people and wounded 11, according to Lebanon's state news agency.

The strikes hit two apartments in separate buildings, covering streets with rubble and triggering evacuations from the southern suburbs. Israeli media reported that the US had been informed before the operation, though Washington had previously asked Israel not to strike Beirut itself.

The renewed violence marks a significant breakdown of diplomatic efforts. Lebanon's government and Israel had agreed to a ceasefire proposal that was being negotiated in Washington, but Hezbollah rejected the terms. The militant group has stated it will not accept any ceasefire that does not include Israeli troop withdrawal from Lebanon and an end to Israeli strikes across the country.

Israeli officials said they had intercepted Hezbollah rocket fire on Sunday morning, though Hezbollah did not claim responsibility for those attacks. The strikes represent the most serious escalation since a ceasefire was established in mid-April.

The escalation has prompted a strong response from Iran. Ebrahim Rezaei, spokesperson for parliament's foreign policy committee, said on social media that Iran would deliver "a decisive and painful response" to the strikes. He wrote: "We will give a decisive and painful response to the Zionist regime's attack on the suburbs. Watch the sky of the occupied territories tonight." Iran had previously warned that any Israeli attack on Beirut would be considered a violation of a US-Iran ceasefire and would trigger Iranian retaliation.

Israeli media reported expectations of a limited attack from Iran and Hezbollah in response to Sunday's strike. The situation has revived concerns about broader regional conflict after tensions had appeared to ease following the April ceasefire.

The fighting in Lebanon began on March 2 when Hezbollah launched rockets at Israel following the killing of Iran's supreme leader. Israeli strikes have killed more than 3,613 people in Lebanon, while Hezbollah has killed at least 30 Israeli soldiers and 3 Israeli civilians.

The conflict has complicated broader Middle East peace negotiations. Iran has insisted that Lebanon be included in any comprehensive ceasefire agreement with the US, while US officials have indicated they are not making Lebanon a requirement for an Iran deal.

The uncertainty around negotiations in Washington and the renewed strikes raise questions about the viability of further diplomatic breakthroughs. Hezbollah's rejection of the ceasefire proposal and Israel's decision to strike Beirut suggest that the parties remain far apart on fundamental terms for ending the conflict.
