Israel strikes Beirut days after US-brokered ceasefire proposal

Israeli warplanes carried out dozens of strikes across southern Lebanon on Tuesday, undermining a ceasefire agreement that US President Donald Trump claimed to have brokered just days earlier. Trump said on Monday that he had convinced Israel's Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and representatives of Hezbollah to halt all fighting, but the intensive bombing campaign that followed challenged that assertion.

The Israeli military justified the strikes by accusing Hezbollah of violating the ceasefire that was officially established on April 17 but had never been fully respected by either side. Lebanon's state news agency reported 30 Israeli strikes in the south on Tuesday alone. Near the city of Sidon, rescue workers recovered six bodies from one family, including two children and a woman, following an Israeli strike. The military also issued evacuation warnings for the southern city of Nabatiyeh ahead of additional bombing.

The timing of the escalation highlighted tensions between Trump's diplomatic efforts and military realities on the ground. According to Axios, Trump called Netanyahu "fucking crazy" for ordering the Beirut strikes and accused him of jeopardizing broader peace negotiations with Iran. However, Channel 12, an independent Israeli news network, reported that Trump and Netanyahu had actually agreed Netanyahu would refrain from attacking Beirut's southern suburbs if Hezbollah stopped firing at Israel.

Defense Minister Israel Katz stated that Washington had endorsed Israel's principle of striking Beirut's Shia neighborhood, known as Dahiyeh, if Hezbollah continued attacks. "If Israeli towns continue to be attacked, we will evacuate and strike the Shia Dahiyeh quarter in Beirut, Hezbollah's stronghold," Katz said.

Hezbollah's political leadership rejected any partial ceasefire arrangement. Senior official Mahmoud Qmati told AFP that the group would not accept limited peace measures and warned that "any aggression against the suburbs of Beirut could lead to a deeper and stronger response." Hezbollah has not claimed responsibility for recent strikes in Israel, instead saying it attacked Israeli troops who have advanced into southern Lebanon to establish a security buffer zone.

The renewed fighting has complicated Trump's broader Middle East strategy. A ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah would support Washington's efforts to negotiate with Iran, but the escalating violence in Lebanon prompted Iran's political leadership to suspend further negotiations, insisting that a Lebanese ceasefire was a precondition for any broader truce with the United States.

Since mid-March, Trump has repeatedly claimed he is close to reaching a deal with Tehran. A key priority is reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping route whose potential closure threatens global oil markets and regional economies.
