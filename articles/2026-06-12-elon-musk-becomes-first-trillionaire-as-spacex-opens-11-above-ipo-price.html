Elon Musk Becomes First Trillionaire as SpaceX Opens 11% Above IPO Price

Elon Musk has officially become the world's first trillionaire following SpaceX's record-breaking initial public offering on Friday. The company's shares opened at $150, representing an 11% gain over the $135 IPO price and marking the beginning of trading for the largest IPO in history.

The IPO raised $75 billion and initially valued SpaceX at $1.77 trillion. However, as shares continued trading throughout the day, the company's valuation climbed higher. At one point during the opening auction process, shares were indicated to trade at $174, which would have pushed SpaceX's total valuation to approximately $2.28 trillion.

Musk's wealth crossed the trillion-dollar threshold based on his approximate 38% ownership stake in SpaceX, combined with his Tesla holdings worth around $280 billion. Forbes had estimated his net worth at $982.6 billion based on the IPO price alone, making the stock's opening pop sufficient to push him past the historic milestone.

SpaceX operates across multiple business sectors, functioning as a rocket manufacturer, satellite internet service provider, and artificial intelligence company. The firm also owns X, the platform formerly known as Twitter. This diversified portfolio has attracted significant investor demand, with the company's shares climbing even higher as trading continued. Reports indicated that shares eventually reached $162.58, representing a 20% jump from the IPO price.

The strong market debut reflects broader investor confidence in SpaceX's growth prospects. Tracker funds and other index-based investors provided additional buying pressure after SpaceX gained fast-track approval to join the Nasdaq index, according to market analysts.

The timing of SpaceX's public debut drew particular attention from wealth inequality advocates. The anti-poverty charity Oxfam released polling showing that 60% of British respondents believe it is unacceptable for any individual to hold more than one trillion pounds in personal wealth. The organization pointed out that Musk's trillionaire status means he holds more wealth than the poorest 46% of the global population, or approximately 3.8 billion people, combined.

SpaceX demonstrated its operational capabilities even as it transitioned to public company status. On the same day as the IPO, the company launched a Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station to deploy 29 Starlink satellites into low Earth orbit. SpaceX Chief Operating Officer Gwynne Shotwell highlighted this achievement during the Nasdaq opening bell ceremony, noting that few companies would conduct a major rocket launch on their first day of public trading.

The historic IPO marks a significant milestone for SpaceX, transforming it from a privately held aerospace company into a publicly traded corporation with direct investor participation.
