Gunman kills six in attack on Krakow's main square, three more bodies found at his home

At least six people were killed and several others injured when a gunman opened fire in central Krakow on Saturday afternoon, and police later found three bodies at his home. The attacker, identified as a 25-year-old Polish man, drove into Krakow's Main Market Square shortly before 15:00 local time and began firing at random people from his car. Armed officers shot and arrested him within three minutes, preventing further casualties. A minister said early investigations indicate he was inspired by far-right ideologies and described him as an ultra-nationalist.

The six confirmed dead include four women and two men, among them a British national. Three victims are Polish and two have not been identified. Sixteen people were taken to hospital, including five foreigners and the attacker. Two police officers were shot while detaining the gunman, one described as being in very critical condition after being shot three times. When authorities searched the suspect's home, they found three bodies, two women and one man.

The attacker was wearing military-style attire and had a military-grade weapon, though authorities confirmed he was not a soldier. Police found multiple pieces of weapons and ammunition in his vehicle. Polish media reported the suspect had a firearms permit and may have obtained the weapons from his family home. Counter-terrorism police are supporting the search of his residence.

The shooting occurred in one of the largest medieval squares in Europe, a popular tourist destination. Witnesses described hearing more than 20 shots from what sounded like multiple different firearms. A passing tram was shot at during the attack, shattering windows, though no passengers were injured. Prime Minister Donald Tusk traveled to Krakow and said Poland had not previously experienced such drastic cases of terrorism.
