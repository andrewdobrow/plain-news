Zverev reaches French Open final after defeating Mensik in straight sets

Alexander Zverev advanced to the French Open final by defeating Czech player Jakub Mensik with a scoreline of 7-5, 6-2, 3-6, 6-3. The second-seeded German, ranked world number three, will contest his fourth Grand Slam final on Sunday.

Zverev has experienced heartbreak at the sport's biggest events multiple times. He lost his first Grand Slam final at the 2020 US Open to Dominic Thiem in a fifth-set tiebreak after holding a two-set lead and serving for the match in the final set. He was subsequently defeated by Carlos Alcaraz at the 2024 French Open and by Jannik Sinner in the 2025 Australian Open final.

His opponent in the final will be tenth-seeded Italian player Flavio Cobolli in what will be Cobolli's first Grand Slam championship match. Cobolli advanced to the final after his semifinal opponent Matteo Arnaldi withdrew due to a virus, with the announcement coming just 25 minutes before their scheduled match was due to begin.

Arnaldi's withdrawal proved particularly cruel given the difficulties he has faced this year. The Italian player suffered from a chronic foot injury that limited his ability to train and compete regularly, resulting in four first-round exits before he turned his season around on clay courts. He won a Challenger event in Italy and squeezed into the French Open main draw as world number 104. By reaching the semifinals, he had spent 19 hours and 42 minutes on court across the tournament.

Following the announcement of Arnaldi's withdrawal, the two Davis Cup teammates held an unusual press conference on the interview podium, sitting far apart from one another. Arnaldi explained the severity of his condition, stating that he had vomited multiple times on Thursday night and experienced dizziness. "Every time I get up I feel dizzy and I don't feel like the best," he said.

Cobolli expressed sympathy for his friend while also acknowledging his own achievement. "When he came to me I almost cried," Cobolli said. "I was completely sad for him but at the same time I'm really happy for my results this week."

The 24-year-old Cobolli is guaranteed to break into the world's top 10 following the tournament, regardless of the final's outcome. He and Zverev have met four times previously, with Zverev leading their head-to-head record 3-1, though Cobolli won their most recent clay court encounter in Munich in April.

For Zverev, this represents another significant opportunity to claim the Grand Slam title that has eluded him despite his success at every other level of professional tennis, including Masters 1000 titles, the ATP Finals, and Olympic gold.
