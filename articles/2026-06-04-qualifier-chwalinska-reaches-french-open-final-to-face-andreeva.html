Zverev and Cobolli compete in French Open final

Alexander Zverev and Flavio Cobolli faced off in the French Open men's singles final at Roland-Garros, with both players seeking their first Grand Slam title. The match represented a significant moment for each competitor on one of tennis's biggest stages.

Zverev, a veteran on the professional tour, arrived at the final after multiple previous attempts at major championships. He lost the 2020 US Open final to Dominic Thiem from two sets up and fell to Carlos Alcaraz in the 2024 French Open final. More recently, he lost to Jannik Sinner in the 2025 Australian Open final. This fortnight presented what observers described as the opportunity of a lifetime, with both Alcaraz sidelined by injury and Sinner eliminated by illness.

The German player's path to the Roland-Garros final carried particular significance: he became the first player since Mats Wilander in 1988 whose opponents in the round of 16, quarterfinals, semifinals and final had never reached those stages before. Despite not playing at his absolute best, Zverev proved sufficiently strong for every opponent he faced.

Cobolli, the Italian competitor, arrived at his first Grand Slam final in what represented a breakthrough moment in his professional career. The 26-year-old had developed into a top-10 talent, displaying a tremendous forehand, excellent returning ability and exceptional movement around the court. Observers noted he was perhaps the quickest player on tour and competed with intensity characteristic of elite competitors.

According to Coach Calv Betton, a respected analyst, "Cobolli is a quality player with no stand out weaknesses. He can live with Zverev off the ground and even beat him." However, Betton also noted that modern men's tennis operates under constraints: "The men's game now they all play the same. There isn't much tactically. They're all baseline ball strikers and it's just whoever hits it better."

Tactically, some analysts suggested Cobolli's best approach involved keeping Zverev moving rather than simply hitting winners. This strategy could include drop-shots to exploit Cobolli's net skills, angled balls breaking the sidelines and deep balls toward corners. Such tactics aimed to prevent Zverev from planting his feet and settling into extended baseline exchanges.

While many observers considered the final a potential mismatch favoring Zverev, the reality proved more nuanced. Zverev possessed weaknesses in his forehand and volleying that Cobolli could potentially exploit. The question remained whether the pressure of finally reaching a Grand Slam final would inspire Zverev or paralyze his performance when he needed it most.

For both players, this match offered the opportunity to secure a career-defining victory and join the ranks of Grand Slam champions, though the paths they traveled to reach this stage differed considerably in experience and circumstances.
