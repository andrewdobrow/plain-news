Global stocks fall as South Korean market plunges and Middle East tensions drive oil prices higher

Stock markets around the world fell on Monday as a technology sector selloff deepened and renewed hostilities between Iran and Israel sent oil prices climbing. The declines were particularly sharp in Asia, where South Korea's Kospi index dropped nearly 9 percent at one point, forcing trading to be briefly suspended.

The sharp decline in South Korean equities reflected a broader retreat from technology stocks. Foreign investors have sold billions of dollars worth of Korean stocks this year, with the latest selloff hitting major chip makers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix hard. Their share prices fell 9 percent and 6 percent respectively. The technology sector decline rippled across global markets, as investors reassessed valuations in artificial intelligence companies that had driven gains in recent months.

Markets in other Asian regions also registered significant losses. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 3 percent, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.5 percent. European markets similarly declined, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 index falling nearly 0.9 percent. Chip manufacturers and technology firms were among the biggest losers, with companies like ASML falling 3.2 percent and Aixtron dropping almost 6 percent. Finland's Nokia fell 5 percent.

Investors have grown more nervous about the valuation of artificial intelligence stocks, particularly as interest rate concerns mount. Tech companies at the forefront of the AI boom are seeking substantial funding to finance massive capital expenditure plans. Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, said "markets are now pricing in a greater likelihood of an interest rate rise from the Federal Reserve this year."

"Fears of higher interest rates come just as tech giants are seeking fresh funding to help finance eye-watering capital expenditure plans," she said. "There is concern that assets being invested in today could become obsolete further down the road."

Oil prices jumped nearly 5 percent after Iran and Israel exchanged direct strikes over the weekend. Brent crude rose to $97.60 a barrel, up from $93 a barrel the previous week. The escalation in hostilities has heightened concerns about potential disruptions to oil supplies from the region, particularly raising fears that the conflict could prevent the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel through which approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and gas supply normally flows.

The combination of the technology sector retreat and Middle East tensions created a risk-off environment across global financial markets. U.S. stock futures pointed to a lower opening on Wall Street, continuing downward momentum from Asian and European trading sessions. The declines followed a steep selloff in tech stocks the previous week, when the tech-heavy Nasdaq index lost nearly 5 percent of its value and the S&P 500 dropped 2 percent on a weekly basis, ending nine weeks of gains.
