Global stock markets fall as tech shares slide and Middle East conflict escalates

Stock markets across Asia and Europe declined sharply on Monday following a steep sell-off in US technology stocks and rising concerns about valuations in the artificial intelligence sector. The sell-off was compounded by renewed military conflict between Iran and Israel, which sent oil prices surging.

In Asia, losses were particularly severe. South Korea's Kospi index slumped nearly 9% at one point, forcing trading to be briefly suspended. Chip manufacturers led the decline, with Samsung Electronics dropping 9% and SK Hynix falling 6%. Japan's Nikkei 225 index fell 3%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.5%.

European markets also opened lower. London's FTSE 100 fell 0.4%, with travel and aerospace companies including British Airways parent IAG and jet engine maker Rolls-Royce among the biggest losers. Oil companies BP and Shell bucked the trend with gains. Stock markets in Germany, France and Spain also declined. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index fell nearly 0.9%, with semiconductor firms and technology companies suffering sharp losses. Chip equipment maker ASML fell 3.2%, while German tech firm Aixtron dropped nearly 6% and Finland's Nokia fell 5%.

The technology sector retreat followed a prolonged rally in artificial intelligence stocks that had driven valuations to elevated levels. Investors are increasingly concerned about how major technology companies will fund their substantial capital expenditure plans, particularly if interest rates rise. Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, noted that markets are now pricing in a greater likelihood of a Federal Reserve rate increase this year. She stated: "The demand is voracious right now, but there is concern that assets being invested in today, at a time when the technology is so expensive, could become obsolete further down the road."

Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo, said the market was becoming more selective about AI investments. "Investors now want clearer proof of earnings delivery, monetisation, capex discipline and funding returns," Chanana said. "This looks more like a positioning reset than a regime break. The AI story is not over, but easy AI enthusiasm may be."

Oil prices jumped nearly 5% to $97.60 per barrel after Iran and Israel exchanged direct strikes. The conflict has heightened concerns about potential disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel through which about a fifth of the world's oil and gas normally flows. Analysts warned that escalation could prevent the waterway's reopening and prolong inflationary pressures on the global economy.

The combination of falling technology valuations and rising oil costs created significant turbulence across global financial markets. US markets were expected to open lower on Monday, continuing the decline that ended nine weeks of consecutive gains for the broader S&P 500, which dropped 2% on a weekly basis.
