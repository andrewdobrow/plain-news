U.S. stock futures fall as Iran strikes Israel, South Korean stocks plunge 8%

U.S. stock futures declined and oil prices jumped after Iran launched attacks on Israel, breaking a ceasefire that had held since April. South Korea's Kospi index plunged 8% in early trading as global markets reacted to renewed tensions in the Middle East.

Iran announced the strikes represent the start of what it described as a full week of attacks on Israel. The offensive marks the first Iranian military action against Israel since a ceasefire took effect in April. Oil prices rose more than $2 per barrel in response to the escalation, with traders assessing potential disruptions to energy supplies from the region.

The market turmoil extended a retreat in global stocks already underway due to concerns about potential interest rate increases. Citibank maintained its bullish outlook on oil prices, citing the lack of progress in ongoing U.S.-Iran diplomatic talks. The combination of geopolitical uncertainty and monetary policy fears sent investors away from riskier assets.

U.S. stock futures showed losses across major indexes as trading opened for the week. The selloff followed a technology sector decline that had already pressured markets. Oil analysts noted that the Middle East tensions could support higher energy prices in coming sessions if the conflict continues to escalate.

The renewed hostilities raised questions about the stability of the April ceasefire and the potential for broader regional conflict. Markets remained focused on both the immediate security situation and the longer-term implications for global energy supplies and economic growth.
