Global stocks fall as tech sector sells off and Middle East tensions escalate

Stock markets across Europe and Asia declined sharply on Monday as technology shares experienced a significant downturn and renewed military conflict between Iran and Israel unsettled investors. The twin pressures of sector-specific weakness and geopolitical risk created turbulent trading conditions worldwide.

The technology sell-off reflected growing investor concerns about valuations in the artificial intelligence sector. Major chip manufacturers saw substantial losses, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dropping 9% and 6% respectively in South Korea. The South Korean Kospi index fell nearly 9% at one point, forcing a brief trading suspension. In Japan, the Nikkei 225 declined 3%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.5%. European tech companies also suffered steep losses early in the day, with semiconductor firms like ASML down 3.2% and BESI down 4.5%, while German chip equipment maker Aixtron dropped nearly 6%.

The market weakness came after a sharp sell-off in US technology stocks the previous Friday, when the Nasdaq index lost nearly 5% and the S&P 500 fell 2%, ending nine weeks of consecutive gains. Investors expressed growing nervousness about the spending plans of major technology companies pursuing artificial intelligence development. According to Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, concerns had intensified around higher inflation and potential interest rate rises. "Tech giants with deep cash pockets are seeking fresh funding to finance eye-watering capital expenditure plans," she noted, adding that worries exist about whether assets invested today at current high prices could become obsolete.

Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo investment bank, suggested the correction reflected a shift in investor sentiment. "The market is becoming more selective on AI," Chanana said. "Investors now want clearer proof of earnings delivery, monetisation, capex discipline and funding returns."

The escalation of Middle East hostilities added a second layer of concern to markets. Iran and Israel exchanged direct military strikes, raising fears about the stability of a fragile ceasefire arrangement. Oil prices initially surged nearly 5% to $97.60 per barrel as traders assessed potential disruptions to energy supplies. Brent crude later retreated to $94.60 per barrel after Iran announced it was halting military operations against Israel, following statements from Donald Trump calling for both sides to cease military action immediately.

In London, the FTSE 100 opened down 0.4%, with airlines and aerospace companies among the biggest fallers. However, oil firms BP and Shell gained on expectations of higher energy prices. German, French and Spanish markets also declined before recovering some losses as the Middle East situation stabilized. US markets were expected to open higher on Monday as investor sentiment improved following de-escalation signals from both Iran and Israel.
