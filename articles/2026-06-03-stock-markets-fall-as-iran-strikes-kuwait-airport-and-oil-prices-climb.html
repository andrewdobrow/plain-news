Markets Fall as Investors Await Inflation Data and Monitor Middle East Tensions

Stock markets declined globally as investors braced for inflation data while monitoring escalating tensions in the Middle East. The Strait of Hormuz remained closed following a weekend of military exchanges between Iran and the United States, disrupting oil markets and rattling investor confidence.

Britain's FTSE 100 fell 58.5 points, or 0.55%, to close at 10,609 points, retreating from a six-week high reached Friday. European markets also retreated, with the Stoxx 600 dropping around 0.9% in late trading. Germany's DAX and France's CAC 40 each lost approximately 1%, while Italy's FTSE MIB fell 1.2%.

The oil market showed volatility, with prices rising 5.5% to reach $95.20 a barrel after reversing half of Friday's losses. However, the Strait of Hormuz closure kept energy markets on edge. UK month-ahead gas prices climbed 3% to around 100 pence per therm.

In the United States, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both fell, with the Nasdaq dropping 0.55% to 24,334 points. The decline came after the Nasdaq had posted 13 consecutive daily gains on Friday, the longest winning streak since early 1992. Travel stocks experienced particular weakness, with Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings falling 4%.

The Middle East situation created fresh uncertainty after a chaotic weekend. Though Iran and the United States had signaled Friday that the strait had reopened, it remained closed Monday. Ships attempting to navigate the waterway either turned back after coming under fire or tested channels without passing through completely. The U.S. also seized an Iranian vessel in the Gulf of Oman.

The ceasefire between the two nations is set to expire mid-week, with expectations for peace talks still uncertain. President Trump said extending a two-week ceasefire was "highly unlikely" unless a deal materialized, stating he would not be rushed into an agreement.

Anthony Willis, senior economist at Columbia Threadneedle Investments, noted that developments over the weekend had muddied the situation. He pointed to the limited ship movement through the strait and escalatory rhetoric from both sides, including U.S. threats against Iranian infrastructure and Iranian pushback on nuclear concessions.

Joe Mazzola, head trading and derivatives strategist at Charles Schwab, described the shift from Friday's optimism as "the Monday blues after Middle East anxiety re-ignited over the weekend."

The consumer price index report looms as investors assess whether the data will clarify the Federal Reserve's interest rate path or add to market uncertainty. The combination of inflation concerns, geopolitical instability, and ongoing technology stock pressures has left global markets sensitive to developments on multiple fronts. A lasting resolution to Middle East tensions and clarity on inflation remain critical factors for market direction in the coming weeks.
