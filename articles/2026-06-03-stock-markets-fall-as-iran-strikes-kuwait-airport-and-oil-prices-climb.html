Asian markets tumble as tech stocks sell off and oil prices surge

Asian stock markets fell sharply as technology stocks reversed recent gains and oil prices jumped following renewed military strikes in the Middle East. South Korea's Kospi index dropped 7%, while Japanese markets also declined after a weeks-long rally in tech shares. The sell-off in Asian technology stocks followed similar declines in U.S. Big Tech equities, marking the worst day for Wall Street in months.

Oil prices climbed more than $3 per barrel after Iran fired missiles at Israel, raising concerns about Middle East supply disruptions and casting doubt on the fragile ceasefire in the region. The price surge came despite an effective shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz, which has already stranded vast amounts of the world's oil. U.S. stock futures showed mixed results as investors weighed the impact of escalating regional tensions.

The technology sector faced particular pressure as AI-related trades unwound, threatening a sustained bull run that had driven Korean and other Asian tech stocks to record levels in recent weeks. The broader market decline reflected growing uncertainty about the stability of both technology valuations and Middle East energy supplies.

OPEC Plus announced plans to increase oil production by 188,000 barrels per day, though analysts described the move as largely symbolic given the ongoing disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. The combination of supply constraints and renewed conflict in the region has created significant volatility in energy markets.

The dual pressures of falling tech stocks and rising oil prices present challenges for global economic stability. Higher energy costs threaten to increase inflation pressures, while the retreat in technology shares raises questions about the sustainability of recent market gains driven by optimism about artificial intelligence and related technologies.
