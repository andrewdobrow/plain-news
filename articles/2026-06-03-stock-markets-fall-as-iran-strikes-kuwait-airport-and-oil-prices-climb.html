Asian stocks plunge as tech selloff intensifies and Middle East tensions spike oil prices

Asian stock markets dropped sharply after technology stocks suffered their worst selloff in months, driven by a combination of profit-taking following weeks of gains and renewed military strikes between Iran and Israel that sent oil prices surging.

South Korea's Kospi index plunged 7 percent, leading regional losses as investors unwound positions in technology stocks that had rallied to record highs in recent weeks. Japan's Nikkei 225 gained more than 5 percent, Australia's S&P/ASX 200 jumped 2.55 percent and Hong Kong's Hang Seng was up 3.1 percent, while China's CSI 300 index gained 3.2 percent. The selloff followed a sharp decline on Wall Street, where Big Tech stocks led broader market losses in their worst single-day performance in months.

The downturn marked a sudden reversal for tech stocks that had been buoying markets with a prolonged rally driven by artificial intelligence investments. The retreat highlighted concerns about the sustainability of recent gains built on expectations for AI-related earnings growth.

Oil prices jumped more than 3 percent after Iran and Israel exchanged military strikes, raising concerns about the stability of Middle East relations and the potential impact on global energy supplies. Crude prices climbed more than $3 per barrel as traders assessed the potential for further conflict and disruptions to shipping through critical waterways.

The dual pressures of the technology sector retreat and geopolitical tensions created widespread uncertainty across Asian trading floors. Investors shifted away from riskier assets as concerns mounted about both the sustainability of the artificial intelligence boom that had driven recent gains and the economic impact of potential energy disruptions.

Wall Street rallied strongly following the market turmoil, with the S&P 500 advancing 2.5 percent and the Nasdaq Composite climbing 2.8 percent. The Dow Jones industrial average gained more than 1,300 points, or 2.9 percent. Travel and leisure stocks surged across global markets on the ceasefire developments, while oil company shares tumbled as crude prices fell from earlier highs.

European stock markets also rallied strongly, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 index gaining 3.7 percent, its biggest one-day rise in a year. The FTSE 100 in London closed up 2.5 percent at its highest end-of-day level since the start of the conflict.

Analysts noted that markets would continue monitoring the fragile truce between the parties. According to Kathleen Brooks, a research director at trading platform XTB, a complete breakdown of the ceasefire could send oil prices surging back above $110 per barrel for Brent crude, potentially causing stocks and bonds to slump.

The market turbulence highlighted the fragility of recent gains in technology stocks and the ongoing vulnerability of global markets to Middle East instability, with trading activity suggesting investors were reassessing positions while preparing for potential volatility from both sectors.
