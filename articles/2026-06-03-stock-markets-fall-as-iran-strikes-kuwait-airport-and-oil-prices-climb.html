Asian markets plunge as tech stocks sell off and oil prices surge on Middle East strikes

Asian stock markets fell sharply as technology stocks continued a global selloff and oil prices jumped following Iranian missile strikes on Israel. South Korea's Kospi index plunged more than 5%, while markets in Japan also declined after weeks of gains in tech-related shares. U.S. stock futures traded mixed as investors assessed the escalating tensions in the Middle East.

The technology sector bore the brunt of selling pressure across the region. South Korean tech heavyweights followed a significant decline in Wall Street's artificial intelligence-linked stocks. On Thursday, the U.S. benchmark S&P 500 and Dow Jones industrial average each closed down 1.7%, while the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.3%. Major tech companies like Nvidia, valued at $4.5 trillion, led stocks lower, declining 3.6% as it continues to face fallout from Japanese investor SoftBank's disclosure that it had sold its entire stake in the firm for $5.8 billion.

The selloff reflects broader concerns that technology firms may now be overvalued after an extraordinary rally driven by artificial intelligence optimism that had propelled global stock markets to record highs. Analysts described the current situation as a correction in the artificial intelligence sector combined with market rotation. "There's a lot of uncertainties about the state of the economy," said Peter Cardillo, chief market economist at Spartan Capital Securities. "What we're going through is a little bit of a correction in the market in the AI sector and we're seeing market rotation."

Oil prices climbed more than three dollars per barrel after Iran fired missiles at Israel, raising doubts about the fragile ceasefire in the region. The attacks put renewed pressure on energy markets already strained by the effective shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz, which has stranded vast amounts of the world's oil supply. The price surge came even as OPEC Plus announced plans to increase output by 188,000 barrels per day, a move analysts described as largely symbolic given current supply constraints.

The dual pressures of geopolitical risk and sector-specific weakness created difficult conditions across Asia-Pacific markets. Investors shifted focus from growth stocks to assessing the potential economic impact of prolonged Middle East conflict. The combination of rising energy costs and falling equity values raised concerns about broader market stability.

The developments marked a sharp reversal from recent optimism. Market confidence has also been tested by remarks from senior Federal Reserve officials on interest rate policy, as well as uncertainty surrounding the U.S. economy following a lengthy government shutdown. Analysts pointed to how quickly market sentiment can shift when geopolitical events intersect with sector-specific weakness, underscoring the interconnection between energy security and technology sector performance.
