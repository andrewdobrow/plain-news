Global stock markets slide as tech shares tumble and oil prices surge

Stock markets fell across Europe and Asia on Monday following a sharp sell-off in US technology stocks and renewed military conflict in the Middle East. Oil prices surged nearly 5 percent to $97.60 a barrel after Iran and Israel exchanged strikes, raising concerns about potential disruptions to global energy supplies.

Asian markets experienced the sharpest declines. South Korea's Kospi index fell nearly 9 percent at one point, forcing trading to be briefly suspended. The sell-off was led by major chip makers, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dropping 9 percent and 6 percent respectively. Japan's Nikkei 225 index fell 3 percent, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.5 percent.

In Europe, the pan-European Stoxx 600 index fell almost 0.9 percent. Chip firms including BE Semiconductor Industries and ASML fell 4.5 percent and 3.2 percent respectively. Germany's Aixtron dropped nearly 6 percent, and Finland's Nokia fell 5 percent. London's FTSE 100 opened down 0.4 percent, with airline and jet engine stocks among the biggest fallers. Oil company shares including BP and Shell rose on the day.

The technology sell-off followed steep declines late last week in the United States. The tech-heavy Nasdaq index lost nearly 5 percent of its value, while the S&P 500 dropped 2 percent on a weekly basis, ending nine weeks of gains.

Investors are reassessing valuations in artificial intelligence stocks amid concerns about how technology giants will fund their expansion plans. According to Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, "Fears of higher interest rates come just as tech giants are seeking fresh funding to help finance eye-watering capital expenditure plans."

Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo investment bank, said the market was becoming more selective. "Investors now want clearer proof of earnings delivery, monetisation, capex discipline and funding returns. This looks more like a positioning reset than a regime break. The AI story is not over, but easy AI enthusiasm may be."

The rise in oil prices followed the first direct exchange of strikes between Iran and Israel since a ceasefire paused fighting in April. Traders worry that continued clashes could prevent reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel through which roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and gas supply normally passes.

The combination of geopolitical tensions and technology sector weakness created significant headwinds for global investors. US stock futures were expected to open lower as the sell-off extended from Asian and European markets into American trading, with investors weighing economic growth prospects against rising inflation concerns.
