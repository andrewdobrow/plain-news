Trump Says US Will Strike Iran Again After Day of Conflicting Signals

President Trump said the United States will strike Iran "hard" again, reversing course just one day after discussing a potential peace agreement with Tehran. The president warned Iran "will have to pay the price" for taking too long to finalize a deal, signaling a return to military threats after weeks of alternating between war rhetoric and peace negotiations.

Trump accused Iran of "playing us for suckers" and said the country faces consequences for the delay. The conflicting messages have created confusion about the administration's ultimate goals in the region and left unclear when or how the conflict might end.

The shifting stance came after Trump spent more than two hours in the White House Situation Room with senior advisors but did not immediately announce a final decision on negotiations. According to the New York Times, Trump had not made a determination at that time.

Earlier, Trump outlined terms he said Iran must accept in a peace deal, including permanently renouncing nuclear weapons, opening the Strait of Hormuz to all traffic without tolls, eliminating mines in the waterway, and allowing the US to remove highly enriched uranium from an Iranian nuclear site. He also said he would lift the US naval blockade against Iran, though it remained unclear if this would be contingent on an agreement.

Iranian officials signaled defiance in response. The semi-official Tasnim news agency reported that no final agreement had been reached and described Trump's statement as "in line with his usual pattern of making unilateral and egotistical statements." Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei told state media that exchanges of messages were continuing but "no final agreement has been reached yet."

Iran's top negotiator, Mohammad-Bagher Ghalibaf, wrote that Iran had "no trust in guarantees or words - only actions are the measure." He added that Iran would seize concessions through military strength rather than dialogue if talks failed.

The military tensions in the region remained high. The US military said it fired on an oil tanker attempting to transport Iranian oil in violation of the American blockade on Iranian ports. The strike on the Settebello occurred in the Gulf of Oman. India's government reported that three Indian sailors were missing and 21 crew members had been rescued after the incident.

Tehran has vowed retaliation for any further attacks. The conflict has already disrupted shipping in the Gulf of Oman, a critical waterway for global oil transport, and drawn regional powers into the standoff.

Vice President JD Vance hinted that an agreement was close, though observers noted that many difficult issues could be deferred to subsequent negotiations. The uncertainty has left US allies and adversaries confused about the direction of American policy.
