Software bug disrupts Chase banking nationwide, slowing Zelle and transactions at other banks

A software bug in JPMorgan Chase's on-premise hardware systems disrupted banking services nationwide on Monday, affecting both online access and in-branch transactions and causing delays at other financial institutions. The bank said the issue was not cyber-related and that all customers retained access to their accounts and funds, though servicing some customers was taking longer than usual. Chase said it was working to resolve the problem by the end of the day.

The outage also prevented Chase customers from transferring money via Zelle, the peer-to-peer payment network. A Zelle representative said the technical issues on its platform originated with Chase and that Zelle itself was operating normally. U.S. Bank confirmed its Zelle customers were experiencing issues, though the bank's own systems were not directly impacted. Customers at Wells Fargo also experienced delays, while Capital One said it had not been affected.

Chase said other banks may have been impacted by transaction slowness only to the extent they were party to a transaction with the company, and the issue was not systemically affecting the entire banking system. Regulators were in touch with the affected banks immediately and quickly determined the problem was contained and service restoration was underway, according to a person familiar with the situation. Chase serves approximately 94 million customer relationships in the United States through 5,000 physical branches and 15,000 ATMs.

The Chase outage was the latest in a series of nationwide technology failures over the past week, though there is no evidence the incidents are connected. Microsoft Outlook went offline for approximately 48 hours last week due to what it called a capacity upgrade, and the Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop Friday for several hours over what officials said was a botched facilities upgrade. The pattern has raised questions about the resilience of critical systems used by tens of millions of Americans daily.
