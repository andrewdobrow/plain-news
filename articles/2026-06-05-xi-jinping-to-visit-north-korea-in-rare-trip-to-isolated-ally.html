Xi Jinping to visit North Korea next week for first time since 2019

Chinese President Xi Jinping will travel to North Korea on June 8-9 to meet with leader Kim Jong Un, marking his first visit to the country in five years. The trip occurs just weeks after Xi held separate meetings with the leaders of the United States and Russia, both of whom significantly influence North Korea's foreign policy direction.

The announcement of Xi's journey has drawn considerable attention among foreign policy experts. Satellite imagery had sparked early speculation about the visit before official confirmation came through. The timing suggests China wants to reassert its position in Pyongyang during a period when North Korea has grown closer to Russia in recent years.

Xi will encounter a more independent Kim Jong Un than in previous encounters. North Korea's strengthening alliance with Russia has diminished Pyongyang's traditional reliance on China for economic support and diplomatic backing. This shift has given Kim greater negotiating power with Beijing, which could complicate Xi's efforts to influence North Korean decisions on nuclear weapons and broader regional security matters.

The visit represents an uncommon face-to-face meeting between the two leaders. China has historically been North Korea's main economic supporter and has defended the country at the United Nations, but the relationship has experienced tension as Kim has pursued stronger connections with Moscow. The Russian-North Korean partnership has involved military cooperation and economic dealings that work outside traditional Chinese networks.

Xi's decision to travel to Pyongyang shows China recognizes the need to actively engage with Kim to preserve influence over the Korean Peninsula. The meetings will probably discuss regional security issues, economic partnership opportunities, and the shifting power dynamics in Northeast Asia as the United States, China, and Russia manage an increasingly strained geopolitical situation.

North Korea's emboldened position stems partly from its reduced dependence on Chinese support. As Russia has become a more significant ally, Kim has gained leverage in his dealings with Beijing. This dynamic makes Xi's visit particularly significant, as he attempts to maintain China's role as North Korea's primary international backer during a time of geopolitical realignment.

The visit underscores how the broader competition between major powers continues to reshape relationships across East Asia. China's need to court Kim demonstrates the stakes involved in maintaining regional influence. As these three powers navigate their competing interests, North Korea's position as a strategic actor has become more valuable and more contested than it has been in previous years.
