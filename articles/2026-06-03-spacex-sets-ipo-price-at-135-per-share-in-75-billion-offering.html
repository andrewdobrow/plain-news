SpaceX sets share price at $135 ahead of largest initial public offering

Elon Musk's SpaceX has announced a share price of $135 for its initial public offering, positioning the rocket manufacturer to become the most valuable company ever to go public. The pricing values SpaceX at $1.75 trillion, surpassing Saudi Aramco's 2019 IPO in both total valuation and capital raised.

The company announced the share price earlier than market observers had anticipated. This timing suggests SpaceX is moving quickly to capitalize on current market conditions as it transitions from private to public ownership.

The path to this announcement included multiple revisions to SpaceX's target valuation in recent weeks. Financial analysts tracking the offering noted that SpaceX lowered its initial targets at least twice before settling on the current $135 per share figure. These downward adjustments may reflect broader concerns about stock market conditions and investor appetite for large public offerings.

SpaceX has not disclosed how many shares it plans to sell or what percentage of the company will be offered to public investors. The company is working to attract both institutional and retail investors willing to purchase shares at the announced price.

The decision to access public capital markets comes as SpaceX's operational demands have grown significantly. The company's activities span satellite launches, government contracts, and development of next-generation spacecraft. These expanding operations appear to require more capital than private investment rounds alone can provide.

The IPO represents a major milestone for SpaceX, which has remained privately held since its founding. Going public will mark a significant shift in how the company operates and is governed. The offering is expected to close in the coming weeks, pending market conditions and regulatory approval.

Saudi Aramco's 2019 public debut had previously set the record for largest IPO. That offering raised substantial capital and valued the Saudi oil company at approximately $1.7 trillion. SpaceX's planned IPO would exceed that historic benchmark in both metrics, reflecting the growing value placed on space industry companies and Musk's ventures.

The timing of SpaceX's IPO coincides with increased investor interest in space technology and commercial spaceflight. The company has established itself as a major player in launching satellites, resupplying the International Space Station, and developing reusable rocket technology. Its government contracts provide a stable revenue stream alongside its commercial operations.

The public offering will give SpaceX direct access to capital markets, allowing it to fund future growth and expansion plans. For public investors, the IPO creates an opportunity to own shares in one of the world's most ambitious space exploration companies. The outcome will likely influence how other aerospace and technology companies approach their own public market debuts.
