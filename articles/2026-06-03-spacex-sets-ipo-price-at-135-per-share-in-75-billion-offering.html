SpaceX valued at $1.77 trillion ahead of public share sale

SpaceX has confidentially filed for an initial public offering on the US stock market, according to Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal. The filing marks a major milestone for Elon Musk's rocket company, which could be valued at approximately $1.75 trillion when shares begin trading.

The confidential filing process gives regulators time to review the company's financial disclosures before the offering becomes public. Bloomberg reported the IPO could take place as early as June, positioning it as one of the most high-profile public offerings in market history. The listing is expected to be massive, with significant investor demand anticipated for the space transportation and satellite communications company.

SpaceX has grown into a dominant force in the American space industry over the past decade. The company holds extensive, lucrative contracts with the US government for defense and space travel. NASA relies heavily on SpaceX rockets for the majority of its launches, making the company central to the nation's space program. Beyond rockets, SpaceX operates Starlink, a satellite internet subsidiary that has become one of the largest satellite operators globally. Starlink is estimated to generate over half of SpaceX's revenue and has gained geopolitical significance, with entities including the Ukrainian military depending on it for communications and operations.

The company recently expanded its ambitions by acquiring Musk's artificial intelligence firm xAI in February. The acquisition reflected SpaceX's plans to develop solar-powered datacenters in space to support the growing computational demands of AI development. Despite trailing competitors like OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude in AI capabilities, xAI was valued at $250 billion in the deal.

In recent months, SpaceX has shifted its strategic focus toward orbital datacenters and assisting NASA with upcoming moon landing missions. The company is working with prominent international banks, including Barclays in the UK, to coordinate the IPO and manage share orders.

The public offering represents a dramatic shift for SpaceX, which has operated as a private company since its founding in 2002. The move to public markets will provide access to capital for future expansion while allowing early investors to realize returns on their holdings.

Some analysts have questioned whether the company can justify its valuation given its substantial ongoing investments and current financial position. SpaceX is investing heavily in its Starship rocket program and expanding Starlink's satellite network, both requiring significant capital expenditure.

The IPO is expected to substantially increase Musk's personal wealth. Already the world's richest person, a successful public offering could position Musk to become the world's first trillionaire, given his substantial ownership stake in SpaceX. The listing comes alongside other major tech IPOs expected this year, including potential offerings from OpenAI and other artificial intelligence firms.
