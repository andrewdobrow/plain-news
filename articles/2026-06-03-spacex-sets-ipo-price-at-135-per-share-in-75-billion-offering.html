SpaceX sets $135 share price for largest IPO in history

SpaceX has announced a share price of $135 for its initial public offering, valuing Elon Musk's rocket company at $1.77 trillion. The offering is positioned to surpass Saudi Aramco's 2019 IPO as the largest ever in both total valuation and capital raised.

At the announced price, the IPO would bring in approximately $75 billion in new funding for SpaceX. The company plans to use the proceeds to expand its operations in artificial intelligence and continue its launch services business.

The public offering reflects investor confidence in SpaceX's dominant position across several high-growth sectors. The company operates the Falcon and Starship rocket programs and serves as a primary launch provider for NASA and commercial satellite operators. SpaceX is also building out its Starlink satellite internet network, which has become an increasingly important revenue stream.

The IPO valuation could have significant implications for Musk personally. His substantial ownership stake in SpaceX means the company's public market debut could push his personal wealth toward the trillionaire mark, making him potentially the world's first person to reach that level of wealth.

This milestone marks a major transition for SpaceX, which has operated privately since its 2002 founding. The timing of the public offering aligns with the company's expansion into new business areas. SpaceX recently secured tax exemptions in Texas for a $55 billion artificial intelligence chip manufacturing facility known as the TerraFab project. Texas officials approved the tax breaks despite some opposition from local residents in Grimes County.

The chip manufacturing plant represents SpaceX's latest diversification effort beyond its core space launch business. The facility is intended to support the company's growing artificial intelligence infrastructure ambitions, which executives have identified as a critical area for future growth.

SpaceX's move into semiconductors follows the company's pattern of vertical integration, where it develops multiple components of its business in-house rather than relying on outside suppliers. This approach has allowed SpaceX to reduce costs and maintain tight control over its technology development.

The record-breaking IPO comes at a time when SpaceX faces increasing competition in the commercial space industry, though the company maintains its leadership position. The Starlink network continues to expand globally, and the company has secured major government contracts beyond NASA work.

With the IPO now formally priced, SpaceX is prepared to begin trading on public markets. The offering represents a watershed moment for the commercial space industry and demonstrates strong investor appetite for companies operating at the intersection of space exploration, satellite communications, and artificial intelligence development.
