SpaceX Sets $135 Share Price for Record-Breaking IPO

SpaceX announced a share price of $135 for its initial public offering, valuing Elon Musk's rocket maker at $1.75 trillion. The company plans to offer 555.6 million shares, positioning the IPO to raise at least $75 billion and surpass the 2019 Saudi Aramco offering as the largest in history. The announcement came earlier than market observers had anticipated.

The valuation makes SpaceX one of the most valuable private companies to go public and could push Musk's personal net worth past $1 trillion. JPMorgan Chase is leading the offering, with CEO Jamie Dimon personally pitching the opportunity to the bank's wealthiest clients through special presentations. The bank is using simulcasts to reach its ultra-rich customer base for the high-profile stock sale.

The IPO comes as SpaceX expands beyond its core rocket business into other technology sectors. The company recently secured tax breaks from Grimes County, Texas, for a $55 billion artificial intelligence chip manufacturing plant, despite local opposition to the incentives. County commissioners approved the tax exemption for the facility, which SpaceX calls the Terafab project.

SpaceX has grown from a startup focused on reusable rockets into a company with multiple revenue streams, including its Starlink satellite internet service and contracts with NASA and the Department of Defense. The company has conducted numerous launches and developed the Falcon and Starship rocket systems. The public offering will allow SpaceX to raise capital for continued expansion while giving early investors and employees an opportunity to sell shares.

The timing of the IPO reflects confidence in the company's valuation despite broader market volatility. Stock markets experienced significant declines this week, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling more than 600 points amid tensions in the Middle East. The SpaceX offering will test investor appetite for high-growth technology companies in an uncertain economic environment.
