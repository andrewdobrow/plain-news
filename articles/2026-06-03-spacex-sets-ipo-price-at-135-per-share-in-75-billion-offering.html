SpaceX Sets $135 Share Price for Record-Breaking IPO

SpaceX has set its initial public offering share price at $135, valuing Elon Musk's rocket maker at $1.77 trillion across 555.6 million shares. The offering is poised to exceed the 2019 initial public offering of Saudi Aramco in both valuation and total money raised, making it the largest IPO in history. The company moved earlier than expected to set a target price for buyers as it prepares for its stock market debut.

The $1.77 trillion valuation represents a significant milestone for the space exploration company, though analysis from Morningstar suggests the company is worth less than half of its IPO target. The discrepancy between the company's self-valuation and independent assessments highlights the challenge investors face in pricing a company operating in the emerging commercial space industry. The $75 billion in capital the IPO is expected to raise would set a new record for funds gathered in a single public offering.

The IPO timing comes as SpaceX secures tax breaks for major ground-based operations. Grimes County in Texas approved a tax exemption for SpaceX's $55 billion AI chip manufacturing plant, despite local opposition to the deal. The facility represents part of SpaceX's broader Terafab project, valued at $119 billion according to some estimates. The tax breaks were approved ahead of the company's public offering.

The successful IPO could push Elon Musk's personal wealth to unprecedented levels. Financial analysts have noted that the offering could make Musk a trillionaire, given his substantial ownership stake in SpaceX. The valuation places SpaceX among the most valuable companies ever to go public.

SpaceX's move to the public markets marks a shift for a company that has operated as a private entity while building a dominant position in commercial space launches and satellite internet services through its Starlink network. The company has not disclosed specific plans for how it will use the IPO proceeds, though the capital infusion would provide resources for its ambitious Mars exploration goals and continued expansion of its satellite constellation.
