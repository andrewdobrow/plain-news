Congress demands answers on Nvidia CEO's reported ties to alleged Chinese spy

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is facing congressional scrutiny after reports surfaced that he had a relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence operative. Sen. Chris Coons pledged an investigation, while a group of House Republican committee chairs requested a briefing from Huang by November 11 to determine whether U.S. national security has been compromised.

Multiple news outlets reported Friday that Alice Huang, a tech executive with whom Jensen Huang had contact, was recruited by Chinese intelligence officials to influence the Nvidia CEO's position on U.S. chip export policy toward China. The New York Times and Financial Times reported she was specifically tasked with convincing him to lobby U.S. officials to allow exports of advanced chips to China. The Financial Times also reported that the two had a romantic relationship.

Nvidia has denied that Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang was anything beyond a typical business contact and said no sensitive information was shared. The Chinese government has also denied involvement. The company did not respond to a request for comment Saturday.

The House Republican chairs who requested the briefing asked Huang to explain his relationship with Alice Huang and detail Nvidia's controls over sensitive information. Nvidia produces chips central to advanced artificial intelligence development, a technology priority for the Trump administration as it seeks to maintain a competitive edge over China. Reports indicate Nvidia chips have reached Chinese companies despite export restrictions.
