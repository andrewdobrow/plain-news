Inflation Accelerates to Fastest Pace in Three Years as Energy Costs Climb

Consumer prices jumped at their fastest rate in three years during April, with the personal consumption expenditures price index rising 3.8% over the 12-month period. The surge reflects elevated energy prices following the escalation of military conflict between the United States and Iran, which has disrupted shipping routes and global supply chains.

The inflation acceleration presents serious challenges for American households. Real disposable income, adjusted for inflation, fell for a third consecutive month in April. Gasoline prices shot up 12.3% during the month alone, and have increased more than 50% since the war began at the end of February. Beyond fuel, consumers face higher costs for food, housing, utilities, and transportation services.

The economic strain is taking a political toll. A Reuters/Ipsos survey showed President Trump's approval rating falling to near its lowest level since returning to the White House, with declining support even among Republicans. Trump had won the 2024 election largely on promises to lower inflation, which stood at 2.4% before the Iran conflict began.

Wage growth has not kept pace with rising prices, compressing purchasing power for workers. Consumers are managing the squeeze through temporary measures. Tax refunds provided cushion in April, and households have drawn down savings, with the savings rate dropping to 2.6%, the lowest level since June 2022. When adjusted for inflation, real consumer spending edged up just 0.1% in April.

The broader economic picture has darkened. The government revised down first-quarter consumer spending growth to 1.4% from the previously reported 1.6%, and slashed overall GDP growth to 1.6% from 2.0%. Financial markets now expect the Federal Reserve to maintain interest rates in the 3.50% to 3.75% range well into 2027.

Inflation was already elevated before the war began, partly due to Trump's sweeping import duties. Core PCE inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy components, rose 3.3% year-over-year in April. According to Olu Sonola, head of US economics at Fitch Ratings, "The inflation picture is becoming increasingly uncomfortable for the Fed. Price pressures are likely to persist over the next few months."

The combination of geopolitical uncertainty and domestic price pressures creates a difficult position for policymakers. The Federal Reserve must weigh the risks of persistent inflation feeding into underlying prices against concerns about slowing economic growth. The White House has pressured the Fed to lower rates, though newly sworn Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has one vote among 12 members who set policy. As consumers pull back spending and rebuild savings, economic growth faces headwinds that may prove difficult to overcome.
