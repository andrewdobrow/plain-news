Trump Administration Announces Deal for US Control of Venezuelan Oil Assets

President Donald Trump announced Friday that his administration has reached an agreement with Venezuela's interim president Delcy Rodríguez that would give the United States majority control over 65 billion barrels of proven oil reserves in the South American country.

Trump stated the deal on his social media platform, noting that it was brokered through a partnership with private business interests. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth led the negotiations.

Rodríguez, Venezuela's interim president, characterized the arrangement as a measure intended to help revive her country's struggling economy. The Trump administration described the agreement as historic, representing a significant shift in US-Venezuelan energy relations.

The specific terms and mechanisms of the deal remain unclear. Trump's announcement did not provide details about how oil extraction, distribution, and revenue sharing would be structured, or the timeline for implementation.

This arrangement marks a notable departure from previous US approaches to Venezuela. The agreement involves working directly with private Venezuelan business entities that maintain connections to the Venezuelan government, a strategy that represents a change in diplomatic positioning.

The deal has sparked criticism from Venezuelan opposition groups. Reports of the US attempt to secure a substantial and long-term stake in Venezuela's energy reserves have generated significant backlash, with opponents describing the move as predatory and unconstitutional. Critics have characterized the arrangement as a land grab that prioritizes American interests over Venezuelan sovereignty.

The Venezuelan opposition's concerns center on the long-term implications of ceding control over the country's most valuable natural resource to foreign interests. They argue that such an agreement undermines national autonomy and resource management.

The agreement could have substantial economic implications for both nations, depending on implementation details and whether it leads to increased oil production and exports. Access to Venezuela's vast oil reserves positions American energy interests in a significant way as global energy markets remain a priority.

The Trump administration's willingness to directly engage with Venezuelan entities connected to the government represents a notable strategic shift. Previous administrations maintained greater distance from Venezuela's government and associated businesses, particularly during periods of political tension.

The deal comes at a time when the Trump administration has been actively reshaping US energy policy. However, this Venezuelan agreement contrasts sharply with recent administration actions in other energy sectors. In March, federal officials announced they would pay nearly $1 billion in taxpayer dollars to French energy firm TotalEnergies to cancel major offshore wind projects off the coasts of New York and North Carolina. That arrangement has prompted seven states to file lawsuits challenging its legality.

The Venezuelan oil agreement now awaits implementation as both countries work to structure the practical details of how American interests will gain access to and benefit from the nation's extensive proven reserves.
