United States opens World Cup with 4-1 win over Paraguay

The United States men's national team launched their 2026 World Cup campaign with an emphatic 4-1 triumph over Paraguay in front of 70,492 fans, showcasing the attacking prowess that coach Mauricio Pochettino has been cultivating since his arrival. The performance represented a commanding statement from the co-hosts in their opening match.

Forward Folarin Balogun was the standout performer, scoring twice to lead the American offensive display. Christian Pulisic also delivered a strong showing as the team controlled the match throughout. The emphatic scoreline demonstrated the quality within a squad widely considered the most talented in the history of the US men's program.

The Americans played with the style Pochettino has aimed to instill, using a fluid formation that shifted between a 3-4-2-1 shape in attack and a 4-4-2 defensively. The team's ability to impose their game on Paraguay from the opening whistle set the tone for what became a dominant performance. This victory marked a successful beginning for the American side on home soil.

Pochettino, who arrived on a record $6 million annual contract, has faced scrutiny over his 22-month tenure, which has produced 15 wins, 10 losses, and a draw. Despite that mixed record, the coach has consistently maintained that the United States can win the World Cup. Yet observers acknowledge the team faces genuine challenges ahead, particularly in a group featuring Paraguay, Australia, and Turkey. The quality of opposition the Americans may encounter in the knockout stages remains a question mark given previous struggles against elite sides.

The tournament itself carries significant stakes beyond simple sporting success. The US performance will be viewed as a referendum on both the assembled roster and the potential of American soccer more broadly. Christian Pulisic, the team's biggest star, emphasized the opportunity before the tournament began. "We have really good players playing in top clubs in the world, we have a good team," Pulisic told reporters. "We're going to do the best we can to prove ourselves right."

What constitutes success for the Americans remains an uncomfortable question. Elite nations like Spain, France, Brazil, and Argentina expect nothing less than winning the trophy. Meanwhile, smaller nations simply aim to represent themselves well. The United States sits somewhere in between, with expectations shaped by its pool of talent and the fact that the tournament is being hosted on home soil.

A run that exceeds the previous modern benchmark of the 2002 quarter-final appearance could potentially elevate American soccer's profile domestically. However, converting on-field success into genuine public engagement and household names remains the larger challenge for the program.

In other opening day action, Canada secured their first ever World Cup point with a 1-1 draw against Bosnia and Herzegovina. Cyle Larin came off the bench to score the equalizer for the co-hosts.
