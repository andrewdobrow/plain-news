Stock Indexes Rise as Chip Rally Continues and Oil Prices Fall on Iran Deal Progress

Major US stock indexes climbed as chip stocks extended their rally and oil prices retreated following statements from President Trump about potential progress in negotiations with Iran. The market movements reflected investor optimism about both the technology sector and prospects for easing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

Stock markets across Asia and Europe rebounded after days of heavy losses driven by Middle East tensions. South Korea's KOSPI index rose by almost 10%, while Japan's Nikkei gained 1.9%. The broader MSCI Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan jumped 2.7%. This recovery followed a particularly difficult period, with South Korea's KOSPI posting its biggest ever fall of 12% earlier in the week.

Chip stocks led the gains in the technology sector, continuing a momentum that has benefited from sustained demand and positive sentiment around artificial intelligence applications. South Korean semiconductor manufacturers, which supply two-thirds of global memory chips, saw their stocks rebound along with broader market recovery. However, industry leaders have expressed concerns about the potential for prolonged conflict in Iran to disrupt supplies of important semiconductor manufacturing materials and drive up energy costs.

Oil prices pulled back from recent highs after Trump indicated the US and Iran were moving toward a potential deal, though he provided no specific details about timing or terms. The retreat in crude prices came after Israel and Iran agreed to halt strikes against each other, reducing immediate concerns about supply disruptions in the Persian Gulf region. Brent crude had previously risen to $84 a barrel following reports of attacks on US oil tankers in the region.

Energy markets had experienced significant volatility as the conflict escalated, with traders pricing in risks to oil infrastructure and shipping routes. The prospect of reduced tensions offered some relief to global markets monitoring the situation closely. Qatar, the Gulf's biggest liquefied natural gas producer, had suspended activity at its facilities and declared force majeure on gas exports, with sources indicating a return to normal production could take at least a month.

The market reaction underscored how closely investors are tracking both geopolitical developments and sector-specific trends. Stephen Innes, managing partner at SPI Asset Management, noted that intelligence from US military channels suggests Iran's conventional military capacity is deteriorating after significant naval losses and sustained airstrikes. He pointed to positive economic signals including solid US economic data and a strong jobs report as additional support for stock markets.

The gains in stock indexes were not uniform throughout trading sessions. Technology stocks experienced volatility, with some segments swinging between gains and losses. The potential for an Iran deal carries significant implications for energy prices and global supply chains, while the performance of chip stocks reflects broader confidence in technology sector fundamentals despite concerns about funding requirements for artificial intelligence infrastructure expansion.
