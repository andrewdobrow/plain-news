Trump vows US response after Iran shoots down Apache helicopter

President Trump said Tuesday that the United States must respond after Iran shot down an American Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. Trump announced the incident through a social media post, confirming that Iran had downed one of the advanced helicopters overnight. Both crew members aboard the aircraft were safely rescued and remain unharmed.

The two Army aviators were recovered through a first-of-its-kind rescue operation involving a drone boat called Corsair. According to a Central Command spokesperson, the Corsair picked up the crew members and transported them to another location on the water, where they were then transferred to a helicopter for safety. The successful operation demonstrates how military technology is increasingly combining automated systems with human personnel to respond quickly to dangerous situations.

The incident marks a significant escalation in tensions between the United States and Iran as the two nations continue diplomatic efforts to resolve their broader conflict. Trump did not specify what form the American response would take or when it might occur.

Energy Secretary Chris Wright noted Tuesday during an Atlantic Council event that oil traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is rising significantly despite the ongoing tensions. The strait serves as a critical shipping chokepoint for global oil shipments, and any disruption there can have wide-ranging effects on energy markets worldwide.

The rescue operation using the Corsair drone boat highlights how future military conflicts may be conducted. Officials have emphasized that this technology allows for rapid response in high-risk situations while reducing danger to additional personnel. The operation also offers insights into how humans and automated military machinery can work together in combat zones.

Trump has expressed frustration this week that American allies, including Japan, have not done more to help safeguard the Strait of Hormuz following his military actions against Iran. During a meeting with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi in the Oval Office on Thursday, Trump criticized allies for not being informed about the military operation in advance. When a Japanese reporter questioned why allies like Japan were not told before military action, Trump responded that surprise was essential, then asked rhetorically, "Who knows better about surprise than Japan?" before adding, "Why didn't you tell me about Pearl Harbor?"

Takaichi later explained to reporters that Japan understands the importance of keeping the strait safe, but she outlined the limitations Japan faces under its constitution regarding military operations abroad. For Japan to deploy its Self-Defense Forces internationally remains politically sensitive in the officially pacifist nation, where many voters support the post-war constitution that renounces war.

The incident underscores the continuing instability in the region, even as diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran proceed. The Strait of Hormuz has long been a flashpoint for military confrontations between American and Iranian forces.
