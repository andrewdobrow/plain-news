Israel Strikes Southern Lebanon as Hezbollah Campaign Stalls US-Iran Peace Talks

Israel carried out fresh airstrikes in southern Lebanon on Tuesday while also ordering residents of 16 villages to evacuate northward. The military operations targeted areas where Hezbollah remains active, continuing a conflict that has killed at least 2,534 people in Lebanon since fighting began on March 2.

The strikes followed Hezbollah's deployment of small, fiber-optic-guided drones against Israeli soldiers in southern Lebanon. These drones, which can travel up to 9 miles and evade Israeli air defenses due to their wired guidance systems, have been used to attack Israeli forces almost daily since a ceasefire was established on April 17. Hezbollah claimed the Tuesday attack wounded several Israeli soldiers, though the Israeli military did not confirm casualties beyond stating that interceptor missiles were fired at incoming drones.

The continued fighting undermines the nominal ceasefire agreement. A ceasefire text allows Israel to conduct strikes in self-defense, language identical to a 2024 agreement under which Israel struck Lebanon more than 15,000 times. Hezbollah has rejected the ceasefire negotiations, with group leader Naim Qassem calling direct talks between Lebanon and Israel a "grave sin" on Monday. Hezbollah stated it will not surrender its weapons, a key Israeli and Lebanese government demand.

The military operations coincide with stalled negotiations between the United States and Iran. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said any permanent truce must include a deal on Iran's nuclear program. This statement came after Tehran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil supply, in exchange for the United States lifting its blockade of the waterway.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Hezbollah still poses threats to northern Israel and promised continued strikes against what he described as Hezbollah infrastructure. Netanyahu told Trump that Israel needed to respond to Hezbollah attacks to restrain the group. The US subsequently asked Israel to ensure its response remained "calculated and limited."

The ceasefire was originally negotiated after the US requested Israel enter talks with Lebanon, apparently to prevent Israeli military operations from disrupting Iran negotiations. Since April 17, Israel and Lebanon have held two ambassador-level meetings, including one at the White House with Trump present.

Lebanon's government has limited ability to control Hezbollah's actions, a reality complicated by the country's history of inter-communal conflict. Analysts have questioned whether normalizing relations between Lebanon and Israel, which Trump seeks as a diplomatic achievement, remains realistic given widespread Lebanese opposition to Israel. A more achievable outcome may be a limited armistice agreement rather than full normalization.
