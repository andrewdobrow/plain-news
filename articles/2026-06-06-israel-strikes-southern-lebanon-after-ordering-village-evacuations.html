Trump Says Iran Shot Down U.S. Helicopter Over Strait of Hormuz

President Trump announced that Iranian forces shot down a U.S. Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz and declared that the United States "must, of necessity, respond to this attack." The aircraft went down early Tuesday morning off the coast of Oman, and two crew members were rescued by an American unmanned drone boat in approximately two hours. Both aviators are reported to be safe and in stable condition.

However, Iran's state media denied the accusation. According to Reuters, Iranian military sources stated that no air operations took place in the Strait of Hormuz over the past 24 hours. U.S. Central Command has also indicated the cause of the helicopter crash remains under investigation, stopping short of confirming Trump's claim.

Iran's foreign minister, Seyed Araghchi, warned that "foreign forces in proximity to our territory are at constant risk on account of their own human errors, plain accidents, or potentially being caught in crossfire." He added that Iran "prefer(s) the language of diplomacy but speak(s) other languages too," suggesting willingness to respond militarily if provoked.

The helicopter incident comes amid escalating regional tensions. Israeli forces continued airstrikes on southern Lebanon, targeting the Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah. An Israeli strike on the Lebanese city of Tyre killed at least eight people and wounded 32 others before the military issued evacuation warnings for the area. Israel also reported that one of its soldiers was shot by a gunman who infiltrated from Lebanon.

Trump told the BBC that he had spoken with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, emphasizing the need to "use a lot of common sense" in the conflict. Trump stated that negotiations with Iran are in their "final throes" and suggested a potential peace deal could be reached.

The dispute over the Strait of Hormuz reflects deeper disagreements about international law. Iran claims the waterway is exclusively Iranian-Omani territory and asserts the right to defend against foreign forces. However, Western diplomats argue that Iran's proposed management of the strait would violate international freedom of navigation principles by imposing tolls and restricting passage based on ship nationality. While Iran signed the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea in 1982, it never ratified the treaty, meaning Tehran believes it is bound by customary international law allowing more restrictive passage rights rather than the treaty's transit passage rules.

Britain, Canada, France, and Norway announced coordinated sanctions Tuesday against Israeli networks involved in financing and carrying out violence in the occupied West Bank. France specifically banned Israeli finance minister Bezalel Smotrich from entering the country due to settler violence against Palestinians.
