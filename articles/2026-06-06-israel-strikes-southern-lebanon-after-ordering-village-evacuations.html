Israel Strikes Southern Lebanon as Hezbollah Campaign Complicates US-Iran Peace Talks

Israel carried out fresh airstrikes on southern Lebanon on Tuesday while ordering residents of 16 villages to evacuate northward, as fighting persisted despite a ceasefire agreement reached in April. The military operations targeted areas where Hezbollah maintains a significant presence.

Hezbollah responded with drone attacks on Israeli soldiers in the region, claiming to have injured several troops. The Lebanese militia has deployed small, fiber-optic-guided drones that have proven difficult for Israeli air defenses to intercept. These wired aircraft produce limited radio signals that evade radar detection and have a range of up to 9 miles. Since the ceasefire began on April 17, Hezbollah has used the drones to attack Israeli soldiers almost daily in southern Lebanon.

The fighting has resulted in substantial casualties. Israeli airstrikes killed 18 people and wounded 88 more in Lebanon over the weekend alone. Since the conflict between Hezbollah and Israel began on March 2, at least 2,534 people have been killed and 7,863 wounded in Lebanon, according to the Lebanese health ministry. Hezbollah rocket fire has killed two Israeli civilians during the same period.

The continued military operations have become a significant complication for broader diplomatic efforts. Negotiations between the United States and Iran have stalled, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio stating that any permanent ceasefire must include a nuclear agreement with Iran. The tensions have undermined attempts to use the Lebanon ceasefire as a stepping stone to wider regional peace.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended the military campaign, saying Hezbollah's remaining weapons continue to threaten northern Israel. "They have about 10% of the missiles they had at the start of the war. But these still trouble the residents of the north," Netanyahu said. He pledged further strikes against what he characterized as Hezbollah infrastructure. Israeli media reported that Netanyahu told President Trump that Israel needed to respond to maintain restraint from the armed group. The US subsequently asked Israel to ensure any response remained "calculated and limited."

The ceasefire agreement included a clause allowing Israel to conduct strikes in self-defense, a provision Israel has used extensively. The text covers a "yellow line" security zone in southern Lebanon where Israeli troops operate across at least 55 villages. Israel has continued demolishing homes in this area.

Lebanon's government faces significant challenges controlling the situation. The Lebanese government negotiated the ceasefire with Israel in Washington but has little ability to influence Hezbollah's actions. Hezbollah has rejected the direct negotiations, with group leader Naim Qassem calling them a "grave sin" on Monday. The group has refused to disarm, a key Israeli demand, stating the negotiations do not concern them.
