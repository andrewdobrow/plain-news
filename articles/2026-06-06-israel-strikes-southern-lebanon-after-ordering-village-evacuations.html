Israel Strikes Southern Lebanon After Cease-Fire Falters

Israel launched fresh attacks across southern Lebanon on Friday, targeting what it described as Hezbollah positions, just one day after a US-brokered ceasefire proposal collapsed. The strikes came after Hezbollah rejected a deal that would have required the militant group to stop firing while allowing Israel to continue its military operations.

The Israeli military issued forced evacuation orders for nine villages in southern Lebanon before the bombardment began. Thousands of residents fled their homes, with hundreds of families leaving Anqoun, a village sheltering at least 2,500 displaced people. Roads to Sidon, the nearest large city, became congested with cars as families sought safety.

Israeli forces carried out extensive airstrikes and artillery strikes across the region, including drone strikes on vehicles in the Nabatieh area and attacks on the town of Kfar Tebnit. The strikes occurred as Israeli troops deepened their invasion further south, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordering the military to advance after capturing Beaufort Castle, a medieval crusader fortress, earlier in the week.

Hezbollah responded to the Israeli military presence near the castle with rocket barrages targeting Israeli troops. The fighting underscored the fragility of ceasefire efforts and the challenges of negotiating without the militant group's direct participation.

The proposed ceasefire, which Israel and Lebanon's government had discussed, was rejected by Hezbollah as tantamount to surrender. Hezbollah is not a party to the negotiations between Israel and Lebanon's government. Instead, the group communicates through Lebanon's parliament speaker, Nabih Berri, who had previously suggested Hezbollah would stop firing if fighting ceased entirely.

On Friday, Berri stated that Hezbollah would only withdraw from the area south of the Litani River, about 18 miles from the Lebanon-Israel border, if Israeli troops withdrew from Lebanon and a ceasefire was truly unconditional. He also criticized provisions allowing Israeli forces to maintain presence in certain "pilot zones" while Lebanese troops returned to other areas.

Israel currently occupies more than 608 square kilometers of Lebanese territory. On Thursday, Israeli forces withdrew from the town of Dibbin, marking the first pullback since fighting began on March 2. Lebanese troops and UN peacekeepers entered the town on Friday.

Netanyahu told ministers he would not seek cabinet approval for the ceasefire unless Hezbollah first agreed to its terms. Several ministers opposed the fragile arrangement, but Netanyahu refused their demands for a formal vote, saying there was no deal to approve until Hezbollah endorsed it.

The conflict has caused significant casualties. More than 3,500 people have been killed by Israeli strikes in Lebanon, while Hezbollah has killed at least 29 Israeli soldiers and three Israeli civilians in the country.

The Lebanese prime minister, Nawaf Salam, expressed frustration with the ongoing conflict, stating that Lebanon cannot continue to be a battleground for wars fought by other nations.
