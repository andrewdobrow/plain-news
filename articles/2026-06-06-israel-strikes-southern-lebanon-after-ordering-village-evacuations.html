Israel Strikes Southern Lebanon After Ordering Village Evacuations

Israel carried out military strikes across southern Lebanon following evacuation orders for nine villages in the region. The offensive has killed thousands and displaced over a million people.

The Israeli military ordered residents to leave towns including Anqoun, which was sheltering at least 2,500 displaced people before the evacuation. Families fled toward Sidon, the nearest major city, as roads became congested with vehicles. Israeli airstrikes, drone strikes, and artillery fire hit multiple areas including Anqoun, the Nabatieh region, and the town of Kfar Tebnit, which sits near Beaufort Castle, a medieval fortress Israeli troops captured this week.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered troops to advance further into southern Lebanon following the castle's seizure. Israel has now issued evacuation orders for Nabatieh, which is normally one of southern Lebanon's largest cities but is now deserted. Israeli forces currently occupy more than 608 square kilometers of Lebanese territory.

The military operations continue despite ceasefire negotiations. A US-brokered ceasefire agreement reached on Wednesday was rejected by Hezbollah on Thursday. The proposed deal would have required Hezbollah to stop firing while allowing Israel to continue airstrikes. Hezbollah characterized this arrangement as "surrender," according to statements from the militant group.

The ceasefire proposal included "pilot zones" where the Israeli military would withdraw from certain areas and Lebanese forces would return to prevent Hezbollah from entering. Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri, who represents Hezbollah's positions in negotiations, criticized this approach. He stated that Hezbollah would only withdraw from areas south of the Litani River, located 18 miles from the Lebanon-Israel border, if Israeli troops completely left Lebanon and if a ceasefire was unconditional.

Netanyahu told cabinet ministers he would not seek government approval for the ceasefire unless Hezbollah agreed first. Some ministers opposed the fragile agreement and wanted a formal cabinet vote, but Netanyahu rejected these demands, saying there was nothing to approve while Hezbollah remained opposed.

Fighting between Hezbollah and Israel began on March 2, following the militant group's rocket attacks on Israel. The conflict has killed more than 3,500 people in Lebanon from Israeli strikes, while Hezbollah operations have killed at least 29 Israeli soldiers and three Israeli civilians in Lebanon.

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said Friday that "Lebanon can no longer be a field for wars fought for others, nor can the south and its people continue to pay the price for decisions they did not make."

The ceasefire negotiations involve the US, Israel, and Lebanon, but Hezbollah is not a direct party to the talks. The discussions have wider implications, as Iran has linked success of a Lebanon ceasefire to its own negotiations with Washington.
