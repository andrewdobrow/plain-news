Israel Strikes Southern Lebanon as U.S.-Iran Peace Talks Stall

Israel launched extensive strikes in southern Lebanon this week, escalating military operations against Hezbollah even as diplomatic efforts between the United States and Iran show signs of breakdown. The timing underscores the difficulty of achieving a regional ceasefire while military campaigns continue.

The Israeli military said it had struck 550 targets since the beginning of the week, a significant increase in tempo. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said his military is "deepening its operation" in Lebanon, with troops clashing with Hezbollah fighters along a strategic river in southern Lebanon as Israeli forces pushed farther north. Hezbollah confirmed point-blank combat with Israeli forces in a strategic town just beyond an Israeli-declared "yellow line" in the country's south.

The escalation comes amid a broader deterioration in U.S.-Iran negotiations. While Iran and the United States appeared to step back from direct military confrontation earlier in the week, new strikes and inflammatory rhetoric suggest those hopes may be premature. U.S. forces shot down four Iranian attack drones near the Strait of Hormuz and struck a ground control station in Iran's Bandar Abbas. Iran responded with a retaliatory attack on an American base in Kuwait, according to Iranian state media.

President Trump said Iran wants to make a deal but that the United States is "not satisfied" with current terms. "Either that or we'll have to just finish the job," Trump said at a cabinet meeting. He also made pointed remarks about Oman, a U.S. ally, saying the strait would be "open to everybody" and "Oman will behave just like everybody else or we'll have to blow them up." The White House did not immediately clarify the statement.

A major unresolved issue in talks is whether a U.S.-Iran ceasefire will cover Israel's operations against Hezbollah. Iran has insisted that Lebanon must be included in any agreement, while Israel has shown no willingness to halt military action against what it considers an immediate security threat on its northern border.

The humanitarian toll in Lebanon continues to mount. More than 1.2 million Lebanese have been displaced by Israeli strikes and evacuation orders since March, according to Lebanon's health ministry. Israeli strikes have killed more than 3,200 people. The ceasefire announced on April 16 appears close to collapse, with the World Health Organization reporting at least 608 people killed in Lebanon since the truce took effect.

Israeli military officials said 10 of its soldiers had been killed since the ceasefire began, with six killed by Hezbollah's explosive drones. Israel issued further evacuation warnings for residents of Tyre in Lebanon's south as operations continued.
