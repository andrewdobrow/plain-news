Israel Launches New Strikes in Southern Lebanon as Tensions With Iran Persist

Israel carried out new airstrikes across southern Lebanon following the rejection of a U.S.-brokered ceasefire agreement by Hezbollah. The strikes killed six people on Friday and forced thousands of residents to flee their homes after Israeli military issued evacuation orders for nine villages in the region.

The Israeli military ordered residents to leave multiple areas, including the village of Anqoun, which was sheltering at least 2,500 displaced people. Roads to Sidon, the nearest major city, became congested with families seeking safety. The airstrikes and artillery attacks targeted towns including Kfar Tebnit and struck vehicles in the Nabatieh area with drone fire. Israeli troops have also advanced deeper into southern Lebanon following the capture of Beaufort Castle earlier in the week.

The military operations intensified after Hezbollah rejected a ceasefire proposal that would have allowed Israel to continue carrying out airstrikes while the militant group stopped firing. Hezbollah called the agreement tantamount to "surrender." The rejection came despite negotiations involving the Israeli government and Lebanon, though Hezbollah was not a direct party to the talks.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated he would not seek cabinet approval for the ceasefire proposal unless Hezbollah first agreed to its terms. "At the moment, there is no deal," he told ministers. "Hezbollah is opposed, and therefore I am not making a decision."

Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam commented on the situation, saying "Lebanon can no longer be a field for wars fought for others, nor can the south and its people continue to pay the price for decisions they did not make." Parliament speaker Nabih Berri, who has served as Hezbollah's representative in negotiations, stated that Hezbollah would only withdraw from the area south of the Litani River if Israeli troops fully withdrew from Lebanon and a ceasefire was unconditional.

Israel currently occupies more than 608 square kilometers of Lebanese territory. The strikes represent a continuation of the military campaign that began on March 2, following Hezbollah's rocket attacks on Israel.

The Israeli campaign against Hezbollah has complicated ongoing U.S.-Iran peace negotiations. The United States has been working to broker an agreement with Tehran, but Israeli military operations against Iranian-backed groups in Lebanon create friction in the diplomatic process. President Trump has reportedly grown frustrated with Netanyahu's campaign in Lebanon as it complicates talks with Iran.

Fighting between Hezbollah and Israel has resulted in more than 3,500 deaths in Lebanon from Israeli strikes, while Hezbollah has killed at least 29 Israeli soldiers in Lebanon and three Israeli civilians. The conflict highlights the delicate balance Israel faces as it pursues security objectives while the United States attempts diplomatic progress with Iran.
