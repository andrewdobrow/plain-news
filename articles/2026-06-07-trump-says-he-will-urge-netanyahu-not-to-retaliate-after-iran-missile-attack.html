Trump urges Netanyahu not to strike Iran after missile attack

President Trump told Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to hold off on retaliating against Iran's missile attack and to allow more time for diplomatic efforts, according to a senior U.S. official and an Israeli source familiar with the conversation. The call took place after Iran launched a barrage of missiles at Israel, marking the first such attack in two months.

Trump indicated before the call that he intended to urge Netanyahu against a military response. His effort to restrain Israel reflects the Trump administration's concern that escalating tensions between Israel and Iran could derail ongoing U.S. negotiations with Tehran. The missile strike from Iran came after a period of relative stability following an April ceasefire.

Despite Trump's appeal, the Israeli Air Force later conducted strikes on military targets in central and western Iran, according to a statement from the Israel Defense Forces. These strikes represented the first time Israel has targeted Iran since the April ceasefire began. A U.S. defense official said the U.S. military was not involved in the Israeli operation.

The exchange of fire carries significant consequences. Further military escalation could unravel the fragile U.S.-Iran negotiations and potentially lead to a broader regional conflict. The situation marks a critical test of Trump's ability to manage Middle East tensions while maintaining diplomatic channels with Tehran.

Trump has rejected suggestions that military action involving Iran contradicts his 2024 campaign pledge of pursuing "no new wars" during his White House run. He dismissed the idea that launching military operations against Iran represents a departure from that message.

The Treasury Department is also considering using Iranian assets to assist Gulf allies in rebuilding from damage caused by Iran during the conflict, according to reporting from CBS News citing a source with knowledge of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's thinking.

The direct intervention by Trump with Netanyahu underscores how seriously the administration views the potential consequences of military escalation. Officials worry that continued strikes could complicate broader strategic goals in the Middle East and derail efforts to reach a lasting peace agreement.

The situation demonstrates the delicate balance Trump must maintain between supporting Israel's security concerns and preventing regional conflict from spiraling beyond control. His administration faces pressure from multiple directions: from Israel seeking to respond to threats, from Iran responding to Israeli actions, and from the need to maintain diplomatic channels that could lead to a resolution.

The coming days will likely determine whether Trump's diplomatic efforts can succeed in restraining further military action or whether the cycle of retaliation will continue.
