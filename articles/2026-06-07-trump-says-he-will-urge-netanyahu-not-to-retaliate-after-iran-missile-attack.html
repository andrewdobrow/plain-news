Trump urges Netanyahu not to strike Iran as tensions escalate

President Trump appealed to Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to refrain from retaliating against Iran's missile attack and to allow more time for diplomatic efforts, according to a senior U.S. official and an Israeli source familiar with the conversation. The call came as the Israeli military confirmed it had struck targets inside Iran in response to an Iranian missile attack.

Trump told reporters before the call that he intended to ask Netanyahu not to respond to Iran's strike, which Tehran said was motivated by an earlier Israeli attack on Beirut. The exchange of strikes between the two nations risks escalating tensions across the entire Middle East region and could undermine ongoing U.S. negotiations with Tehran.

The Trump administration did not authorize the initial Israeli strike in Beirut, according to a senior U.S. official. Despite Trump's appeal for restraint, Israel proceeded with its retaliatory strikes on Iranian military targets. The sequence of events marks a significant escalation in the months-long conflict between Israel and Iran.

Trump's attempt to limit Israel's military response reflects his administration's broader strategy to prevent escalating Israel-Iran tensions from derailing diplomatic channels with Tehran. The president has focused on maintaining negotiations while managing regional conflicts and supporting American allies in the area.

When asked about whether the military actions contradicted his 2024 campaign promise of avoiding new wars, Trump dismissed the suggestion. He argued that his approach does not betray his "no new wars" message as he campaigned for the White House.

The confrontation between Israel and Iran has created significant diplomatic challenges for the administration. Saturday's emergency UN Security Council meeting grew contentious after Secretary General António Guterres condemned both the U.S.-Israeli strikes and Iran's retaliatory attacks as violations of international law.

The timing of events placed particular focus on the Trump administration's international engagement. The first lady presided over a UN Security Council meeting on Monday focused on children, technology, and education in conflict zones. Melania Trump emphasized education's role in preventing conflict and called for protecting children's access to learning. The session came just days after the escalated strikes between Israel and Iran.

Iran's UN ambassador criticized the timing as hypocritical, noting that Washington was convening a meeting on child protection while simultaneously launching airstrikes on Iranian cities. Iranian state media reported that strikes had killed at least 165 people at a girls' school in southern Iran, though the Israeli military said it was unaware of strikes in that area, and the U.S. military said it was investigating the reports.

The crisis has tested Trump's ability to balance multiple diplomatic objectives including supporting Israel, pursuing negotiations with Iran, and maintaining broader regional stability. The outcome of these efforts remains uncertain as both nations have demonstrated willingness to conduct military operations despite presidential appeals for restraint.
