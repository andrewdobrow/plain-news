Trump to press Netanyahu against retaliating for Iranian missile attack

President Trump said he will call Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to urge him not to retaliate after Iran launched ballistic missiles at northern Israel on Sunday, threatening a ceasefire the administration has worked to broker. Trump told Axios he would press Netanyahu to stand down, saying both sides have now struck each other and further escalation is unnecessary.

The Iranian attack marked the first such strike in two months and came after Israel struck Beirut earlier. The Israeli Defense Forces confirmed multiple waves of ballistic missiles were fired at northern Israel. A U.S. official told NewsNation that Trump has been briefed on the incident. The attack raised immediate concerns about the fragility of the ceasefire arrangement between the United States and Iran.

Trump framed the situation as a cycle both countries need to end. He told Axios that Israel had conducted its strike and Iran had now responded, characterizing the exchanges as both sides having their opportunity to act. The president indicated he sees no need for another round of attacks and hopes to preserve prospects for a lasting peace deal in the region.

The strikes threaten to derail ongoing efforts to negotiate a broader agreement aimed at ending the conflict. Diplomats have worked for weeks to establish terms that could hold, but the Sunday night attack underscored how quickly tensions can reignite. The timing comes as the Trump administration approaches the 18-month mark of its second term, with Middle East policy remaining a central focus.

Former national security adviser John Bolton warned separately that Trump's handling of the Iran situation could trigger nuclear proliferation in the Middle East. Bolton wrote in an opinion piece that contradictory decisions during the conflict have created conditions for a potential arms race, regardless of whatever deal the administration ultimately reaches with Tehran. The administration now faces the immediate task of preventing further escalation while trying to keep diplomatic channels open.
