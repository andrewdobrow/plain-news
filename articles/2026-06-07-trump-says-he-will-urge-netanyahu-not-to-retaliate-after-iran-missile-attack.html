Trump says he will urge Netanyahu not to retaliate after Iran missile attack

President Trump said he plans to call Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to discourage him from responding to Iran's recent missile strikes. Speaking to Axios, Trump suggested both sides have already taken military action and further retaliation is unnecessary.

"I am going to call Bibi right now and tell him not to retaliate. Each of them had their fun. Israel had its strike, and Iran had its strike. We don't need another one," Trump said.

The exchange of strikes represents a serious threat to the fragile ceasefire that has held since early April. Iran launched multiple waves of missiles at Israel on Sunday, marking the first direct Iranian missile attack since the ceasefire took effect. The assault came in response to an Israeli strike on Beirut's southern suburbs earlier that day. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps reported targeting the Ramat David Air Base, and the Israeli military confirmed it had been attacked.

The timing of the escalation creates complications for ongoing U.S.-Iran negotiations. Trump's administration has been racing to prevent the military exchange from derailing peace efforts and potentially reigniting broader conflict in the region. The success of Trump's planned intervention will depend largely on whether Netanyahu chooses to hold back Israeli forces despite the Iranian attack.

Netanyahu has previously stated that the conflict will continue as long as Iran maintains a stockpile of highly enriched uranium that could be used for nuclear weapons. In a recent interview with CBS's 60 Minutes, Netanyahu said the war is "not over" because enrichment sites must be dismantled. He suggested that removing the fissile material would require entering Iranian territory, either through military action or as part of a negotiated agreement. Netanyahu said Trump has expressed willingness to pursue this approach.

Trump's diplomatic push comes as the month-old ceasefire shows signs of strain from multiple fronts. Beyond the Israel-Iran exchange, regional tensions have been elevated by drone strikes in other areas. Meanwhile, the U.S. has presented a peace proposal to Iran consisting of a 14-point framework intended to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and establish guidelines for future nuclear negotiations. Iranian state media reported that Iran has responded to this proposal through Pakistani mediators, though details of the response have not been disclosed.

Trump's intervention represents direct presidential involvement in attempting to stabilize the region. Whether Netanyahu will accept Trump's urging to refrain from retaliation remains unclear. The coming hours will be critical in determining whether the ceasefire can survive this latest military exchange or whether a new cycle of escalation will begin.
