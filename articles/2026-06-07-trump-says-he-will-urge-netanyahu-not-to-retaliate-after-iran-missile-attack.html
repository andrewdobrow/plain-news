Trump tells Netanyahu not to retaliate after Iran fires missiles at Israel

President Trump said he plans to call Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to discourage him from retaliating for Iran's missile attack on Israel, according to statements the president made to Axios. Trump characterized the recent exchanges as both sides having taken their turn and suggested further escalation was unnecessary.

"I am going to call Bibi right now and tell him not to retaliate. Each of them had their fun. Israel had its strike, and Iran had its strike. We don't need another one," Trump told Axios. The White House confirmed to NewsNation that the president had been informed about the missile launches.

Iran fired multiple waves of ballistic missiles at northern Israel on Sunday night, according to the Israeli Defense Forces. The attack marked Iran's first strike in two months and came after Israel targeted Beirut. The incident put immediate pressure on a fragile ceasefire agreement between the United States and Iran.

The missile launches came as the Trump administration works to establish a comprehensive peace agreement to end the conflict. The ceasefire faced early testing from the overnight attack, with Trump now moving quickly to prevent further escalations that could undermine his diplomatic objectives in the Middle East.

Meanwhile, Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu flew to Washington for a hastily arranged meeting with Trump, concerned about the direction of nuclear negotiations. Netanyahu worried that Trump's advisers, including his son-in-law Jared Kushner and special envoy Steve Witkoff, might agree to a deal that only addresses Iran's nuclear program while ignoring ballistic missiles, proxy forces, and human rights issues. Netanyahu planned to present Trump with new intelligence about Iran's military capabilities and long-range missile development.

Iran expressed frustration about Netanyahu's intervention, urging the United States to act independently from Israeli pressure. Ali Larijani, head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, warned American officials not to let Netanyahu's statements shape nuclear negotiations. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei stated that America should resist destructive Israeli influences.

The talks between Washington and Tehran began in Oman on Friday, with plans for additional rounds of negotiations. Iran has indicated willingness to dilute its highly enriched uranium stockpile to 60 percent purity, though this remains far above the 3.75 percent limit established under the 2015 nuclear deal. The Iranian government insists on maintaining its right to uranium enrichment for nuclear power plants, a position that could complicate future agreements.

Trump has positioned military assets in the region, deploying the aircraft carrier Abraham Lincoln and three accompanying warships capable of striking Iranian targets. The United States has also strengthened air defenses at military bases across the Middle East in preparation for potential further escalation.
