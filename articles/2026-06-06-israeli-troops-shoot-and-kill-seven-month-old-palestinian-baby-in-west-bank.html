Israeli military fires on car in West Bank, killing 7-month-old baby

Israeli troops shot and killed a seven-month-old Palestinian baby after opening fire on a vehicle in the occupied West Bank, according to Palestinian officials. The infant's body was wrapped in a Palestinian flag and carried by his father to the graveyard during the funeral ceremony.

The Israeli military stated that a soldier opened fire after troops perceived a vehicle accelerating toward them. However, the baby's grandmother, who was in the car at the time of the shooting, disputed this account of events. The military has not provided additional details about the circumstances of the shooting or whether the troops faced any immediate threat.

The incident reflects broader patterns of violence in the West Bank. According to the UN, 230 Palestinians were killed by Israeli forces and settlers in the West Bank during 2025. Israeli military operations in the territory have intensified significantly, with troops conducting regular patrols and security operations.

The killing adds to rising tensions in the occupied territory where Israeli security forces maintain a significant presence amid ongoing conflict. The discrepancy between the military's account of the shooting and the family's version highlights conflicting narratives that often emerge from violent encounters in the West Bank.

Recent statements by senior Israeli military officials have drawn attention to how operations are conducted in the territory. Maj Gen Avi Bluth, head of the Israeli army's central command in the West Bank, stated in a closed forum that his troops were "killing like we haven't killed since 1967," according to comments reported by the Haaretz newspaper. Bluth has not denied the authenticity of the account.

Bluth acknowledged that his military approach involved what he called discrimination between how Palestinians and Jewish settlers are treated. He said Israeli soldiers had shot 42 Palestinian stone-throwers on West Bank roads in the previous year, while noting that the army did not shoot Jewish settler militants for comparable actions. "Yes, it involves discrimination," he stated, according to the reported remarks.

The general also described loosening legal constraints on soldiers, including permitting troops to shoot Palestinians attempting to cross the separation barrier into Israel at the knee or below as a deterrent. He described such individuals as "potential terrorists" and referenced what he called "limping monuments" in Palestinian villages.

Palestinian officials have condemned the shooting of the infant as an unjustified use of lethal force against civilians. The incident highlights the vulnerability of Palestinian civilians in the West Bank, where they are subject to military law while Israeli settlers are judged by civilian courts.

The Israeli military has not announced whether it will conduct a formal investigation into the soldier's decision to open fire on the vehicle.
