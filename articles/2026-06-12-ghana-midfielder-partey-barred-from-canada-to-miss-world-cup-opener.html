Ghana midfielder Partey barred from Canada, to miss World Cup opener

Ghana will face Panama in their opening match of the 2026 World Cup without midfielder Thomas Partey after Canadian immigration authorities denied his entry to the country. The former Arsenal player will miss the Group stage match scheduled for Wednesday in Toronto.

The 32-year-old Partey was charged with five counts of rape and one count of sexual assault by Metropolitan police in July 2025, charges to which he pleaded not guilty. He faced two additional rape charges in February 2026 and again pleaded not guilty to those allegations. Despite these legal proceedings, Partey was permitted to travel with Ghana's squad to the United States on June 4 and participated in a training camp in Boston ahead of the tournament.

However, Canadian immigration rejected Partey's visa application ahead of Ghana's opening fixture. FIFA confirmed the decision in a statement, clarifying that the organization plays no role in immigration processes of host nations. "As with previous FIFA events, the host government ultimately determines who receives a visa and is admitted into the country," the statement said.

Canada's immigration department declined to comment on the specific case but explained that staff apply immigration rules "consistently and without exception, regardless of nationality, profile, or role in the tournament." The department emphasized that officers are trained decision-makers who assess eligibility and admissibility in accordance with Canadian immigration laws.

Partey currently plays for Spanish club Villarreal after leaving Arsenal in June 2025. He had joined the English Premier League club from Atletico Madrid in 2020 for approximately 45 million pounds. Previously, he lived in Potters Bar in Hertfordshire while playing in England.

The midfielder's absence removes significant experience from Ghana's midfield during a crucial tournament opener. Ghana competes in a group with England and Panama. Following their match against Panama in Toronto, Ghana will face England in their second group game on June 23 in Boston.

The 2026 World Cup marks the first time the tournament has been held across three nations, with Canada, the United States, and Mexico serving as co-hosts. Ghana will need to develop alternative tactical approaches for the Panama match and potentially adapt their strategy depending on how long Partey remains unavailable. The Black Stars will look to advance from the group stage despite losing one of their most experienced international players for at least the opening match.
