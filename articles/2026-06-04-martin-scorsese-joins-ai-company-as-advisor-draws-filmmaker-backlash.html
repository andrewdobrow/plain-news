Martin Scorsese Joins AI Company as Advisor, Draws Filmmaker Backlash

Martin Scorsese announced a partnership with AI company Black Forest Labs as an advisor, sparking immediate criticism from filmmakers and artists who accused him of abandoning principles that protect creative workers. The Oscar-winning director said he would help the firm develop AI tools for storyboarding, calling the technology creatively freeing.

Boots Riley, director of Sorry to Bother You, responded publicly to the announcement, speculating that Scorsese accepted the role for financial reasons and does not care about the consequences because he believes AI will ultimately fail. Riley suggested Scorsese received substantial compensation for the partnership. Kane Parsons, the 20-year-old director behind the viral Backrooms project, told The Australian that using AI in filmmaking defeats the purpose of the work itself, saying he gets no enjoyment from using those tools.

The backlash reflects widespread concern in the entertainment industry about artificial intelligence replacing human artists and technicians. Many filmmakers view AI-generated imagery as a threat to the livelihoods of storyboard artists, concept designers, and other visual development professionals who have traditionally created pre-production artwork. Critics argue that prominent directors lending credibility to AI companies undermines efforts to protect those jobs.

Scorsese's involvement comes as AI companies race to develop tools that can generate images, videos, and other creative content from text prompts. Black Forest Labs specializes in generative AI technology that could potentially automate tasks currently performed by human artists. The Guardian reported that artists accused Scorsese of throwing workers under the bus by endorsing the technology.

The director has not publicly responded to the criticism. His decision to partner with an AI firm marks a significant shift for a filmmaker known for championing traditional filmmaking craftsmanship and working with longtime collaborators across multiple departments. The controversy highlights the growing divide in Hollywood over whether AI represents a useful tool or an existential threat to creative employment.
