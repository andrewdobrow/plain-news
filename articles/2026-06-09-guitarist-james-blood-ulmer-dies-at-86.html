Guitarist James Blood Ulmer Dies at 86

James Blood Ulmer, the US guitarist celebrated for his avant-garde fusion of jazz, blues and funk, has died at 86. He passed away on June 3, according to a statement from his family, who described him as fearless in both his music and spirit.

Born Willie James Ulmer in South Carolina in 1940, Ulmer began his music career in funk bands, moving between Pittsburgh, Columbus and Detroit while backing musicians including Jewel Bryner and Hank Marr. In the early 1970s, he relocated to New York and made a dramatic artistic shift. "I ain't never thought nobody could make no money playing free music," he later reflected. "So I always played structured blues, rhythm playing, dance music, or something like that. And I abandoned it! When I came to New York, it was like ... I just went totally another way."

In New York, Ulmer played with jazz luminaries including Art Blakey, Joe Henderson and Rashied Ali. More significantly, he was mentored by saxophonist Ornette Coleman, who introduced him to harmolodic theory, an approach that avoided traditional keys and harmonics in favor of greater freedom in sound. This philosophy shaped Ulmer's entire career, characterized by instinctive, unbounded playing even as he developed his songwriting abilities.

Coleman co-produced Ulmer's debut album, Tales of Captain Black. His next release, Are You Glad to Be in America?, came out on the UK's Rough Trade label. The title track became a signature song with its spirited social commentary, and Ulmer subsequently supported punk and rock bands including Public Image Ltd and Captain Beefheart. He demanded focus from audiences at these performances, telling them to "shut the fuck up" and give him five minutes to win them over or leave.

Collaborations with saxophonist Arthur Blythe on albums including Lenox Avenue Breakdown and Illusions led to Ulmer's own record deal with Columbia. Between 1981 and 1983, he released three progressive albums: Free Lancing, Black Rock and Odyssey. Rolling Stone hailed him after Free Lancing as "the most original electric guitarist to emerge since the late Jimi Hendrix," praising his pairing of virtuosic bluesy guitar with tight funk arrangements and soulful vocals.

Ulmer formed the Music Revelation Ensemble with saxophonist David Murray, bassist Amin Ali and drummer Ronald Shannon Jackson, debuting with No Wave in 1980 and eventually releasing seven albums. He also formed the group Phalanx and continued recording through subsequent decades, shifting focus toward blues while maintaining his distinctive approach. His 2001 album Memphis Blood: The Sun Sessions earned his only Grammy nomination for best traditional blues album.

Ulmer retired in 2024 after performing at the Detroit jazz festival, ending a career that fundamentally reshaped how guitarists approached fusion between avant-garde and popular music forms.
