Trump Defends New Iran Deal at G7 Summit, Threatens Renewed Bombing

President Trump defended his tentative agreement with Iran at the G7 summit in France, warning that the United States would resume military action if Iran fails to comply with the deal's terms. Trump acknowledged the agreement remains incomplete, saying "nobody knows what it is" because "it isn't even fully negotiated yet."

The proposed deal reportedly offers Iran sanctions relief and access to approximately $20 billion in frozen assets. In exchange, Iran would agree to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and negotiate on its nuclear program over a 60-day period beginning June 5 in Pakistan. The agreement also reportedly requires Iran and the United States, along with their respective allies, to cease fighting. Israel would end its offensive in Lebanon under the terms being negotiated.

Trump has faced significant criticism from fellow Republicans who questioned the deal's details and sought thorough briefings before finalization. In response, Trump insisted on social media that his agreement would be "THE EXACT OPPOSITE" of the 2015 deal negotiated by President Barack Obama, which Trump withdrew from in 2018. He stated that both sides must take time to get the agreement right, emphasizing that "there can be no mistakes."

At the G7 summit, Trump angrily rejected suggestions that the United States would contribute to a $300 billion rebuilding fund for Tehran. He argued his agreement was superior to Obama's deal and insisted the U.S. would not pay "even 10 cents to Iran."

Despite domestic criticism, Trump backed a joint statement from G7 leaders welcoming the proposed peace deal. The statement, however, indicates that a follow-on agreement will be necessary to address Iran's ballistic missile program, an issue not directly covered in the memorandum of understanding scheduled to be signed Friday.

A key point of ongoing dispute centers on the unfreezing of Iran's assets held in Qatar, valued at approximately $12 billion. The United States has demanded this asset release be made conditional on progress regarding Iran's enriched uranium handover.

Trump defended himself against critics, stating "I don't make bad deals" and urging people not to listen to "the losers, who are critical about something they know nothing about." He maintained that the U.S. blockade of Iran's ports would remain in effect until an agreement was reached, certified, and signed.

The summit has also addressed Ukraine's war aims, with Trump indicating he was warming to Ukraine's position. Trump is scheduled to hold a concluding news conference and will meet with French President Emmanuel Macron over dinner at Versailles palace. Observers noted Trump appeared subdued throughout the summit meetings.
