Trump Threatens to Resume Bombing Iran Hours After G7 Backs Preliminary Deal

President Trump warned that the United States would resume military strikes against Iran if he became dissatisfied with a preliminary peace agreement between the two countries. The threat came just hours after leaders from the Group of 7 nations publicly endorsed the deal as a breakthrough.

The G7 leaders, gathered for their annual summit, issued a joint statement expressing support for the framework agreement and indicating their readiness to contribute to its implementation. The statement represented unified backing from major Western democracies and Japan for the diplomatic initiative aimed at ending the conflict between Washington and Tehran.

Trump made his threat during remarks to reporters following the G7 statement. He did not specify what conditions would trigger renewed bombing campaigns or which aspects of the preliminary agreement might prove unsatisfactory. He had previously indicated that potential targets for new attacks would include power stations and other civilian infrastructure.

The president's warning introduced fresh uncertainty into the diplomatic process. While international partners publicly championed the accord as a path toward regional stability, Trump's willingness to threaten military action underscored the conditional nature of his commitment to the agreement. His remarks raised questions about how other nations might respond should the United States withdraw from or abandon the framework.

Trump's stance contrasted sharply with his earlier statements that the United States was in a strong negotiating position and would "end up with a great deal." He announced an extension of a two-week ceasefire with Iran, citing requests from the Pakistani government and saying he would hold off on attacks until Iranian leaders submitted a unified peace proposal.

The preliminary agreement faces significant domestic skepticism. Many Senate Republicans expressed concerns about the deal's terms and said they needed thorough briefings before it was finalized. Senator Lindsey Graham, a close Trump ally and longtime Iran hawk, stated that descriptions of the agreement differed between how Iran and the United States characterized it, and said lawmakers needed to examine the actual terms carefully.

Iranian officials have expressed doubt about Trump's intentions. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the speaker of Iran's parliament who emerged as the regime's lead negotiator in recent talks, dismissed the ceasefire extension through an adviser, calling it "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike."

The conflict between the United States and Iran had disrupted energy production and transportation throughout the Middle East, affecting global fuel costs. The preliminary agreement's full details have not been publicly disclosed, though CNN obtained a draft version of the memorandum of understanding. As Trump concludes his G7 summit meetings, observers expect he will face additional questions about the uncertain agreement and his broader foreign policy agenda.
