Commerce Department orders Anthropic to shut down AI models over security concerns

The Commerce Department has directed Anthropic to shut down its Fable and Mythos AI models, citing national security concerns over a potential vulnerability in the Fable 5 system. The government action targets what officials describe as a jailbreak that could pose security risks.

Anthropic has publicly disputed the government's decision to recall the models. In a blog post, the company stated it disagrees that discovering a narrow potential jailbreak justifies pulling a commercial model deployed to hundreds of millions of people. The company's response signals tension between AI safety protocols and government oversight.

The shutdown comes amid a broader conflict between Anthropic and the Trump administration. The Pentagon previously designated Anthropic as a "supply chain risk," the first time this blacklisting tool has been used against a U.S. company. In response, Anthropic filed two lawsuits against the Department of Defense, alleging that the government's decision was unlawful and violated the company's First Amendment rights.

According to Anthropic, the conflict stems from the company's attempt to implement safeguards against the military's potential use of its AI models for mass domestic surveillance or fully autonomous lethal weapons. The company accused the Trump administration and Pentagon of pursuing "punitive actions" against it for refusing to comply with ideological demands from the government. In its California lawsuit, Anthropic stated that "the constitution does not allow the government to wield its enormous power to punish a company for its protected speech."

Despite the legal dispute, Anthropic emphasized its commitment to national security. The company noted that its AI model Claude has been deeply integrated into the Department of Defense over the past year and was previously the only AI model approved for use in classified systems. Anthropic stated it had collaborated with the Pentagon to modify its systems for specific military needs and wanted to continue such negotiations.

A company spokesperson said that seeking judicial review did not change Anthropic's "longstanding commitment to harnessing AI to protect our national security," but that legal action was necessary "to protect our business, our customers and our partners." The company indicated it would pursue dialogue with the government alongside its court challenges.

In its lawsuit, Anthropic alleged that the government's actions were "harming Anthropic irreparably" by seeking to "destroy the economic value created by one of the world's fastest-growing private companies." However, Anthropic's CEO had previously told CBS News that the supply chain risk designation's impact was "fairly small" and the company would be "gonna be fine."

The shutdown represents a significant intervention in the commercial AI sector, affecting models that had reached widespread deployment. The incident raises questions about the balance between innovation and security concerns, as well as the extent of federal authority over AI development and deployment.
