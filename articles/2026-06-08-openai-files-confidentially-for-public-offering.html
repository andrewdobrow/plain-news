OpenAI files confidentially for public offering

OpenAI has filed confidentially to go public on the US stock market, marking a major milestone for the artificial intelligence company. The filing comes just over a week after its primary competitor Anthropic made a similar announcement, intensifying the competition between the two firms.

The company submitted a confidential Form S-1 to the Securities and Exchange Commission, which allows OpenAI to keep its financial details private during the initial review process. In a blog post announcing the move, OpenAI stated: "We recently submitted a confidential S-1. We expect it to leak so we're just announcing it." The company added that it has not yet decided on a timeline for going public, noting that "it may be a while because there are things we want to do that are likely easier as a private company."

OpenAI's expected valuation exceeds $850 billion, positioning it as one of the most highly valued listings in market history. The company achieved this valuation in a funding round completed in March, even while facing challenges in profitability and missing certain revenue and user growth targets.

The path to this moment reflects OpenAI's rapid transformation since its founding in 2015 as a non-profit research organization. The company gained widespread attention after releasing ChatGPT in 2022 and subsequently converted to a for-profit structure. The chatbot quickly accumulated hundreds of millions of users and fundamentally changed how people work across sectors including entertainment, healthcare, education and employment.

OpenAI's pursuit of an IPO comes as the AI sector experiences a major funding wave. Beyond Anthropic's competing filing, SpaceX, which owns Elon Musk's AI company xAI, is also preparing to go public at an expected valuation of $1.75 trillion.

The filing follows a significant legal victory for OpenAI. Musk had sued the company, alleging it illegally converted from a non-profit to for-profit status and unjustly enriched itself and its founders. He sought to force the company to revert to non-profit status, which could have complicated its IPO plans. A jury panel ruled that the case fell outside the statute of limitations and found OpenAI not liable.

Despite the positive momentum, OpenAI faces ongoing legal challenges. The company has been sued in more than a dozen cases in which individuals claim that ChatGPT has worsened mental health crises and contributed to violent acts. These lawsuits could present obstacles as the company moves forward with its Wall Street debut.

Nevertheless, OpenAI has continued to strengthen its market position through major partnerships with companies including Microsoft, Google and Nvidia, as well as agreements with the Trump administration.
