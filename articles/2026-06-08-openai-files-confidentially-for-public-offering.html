OpenAI files confidentially for public offering

OpenAI has filed confidentially to go public on the US stock market, the company announced Monday. The ChatGPT maker is expected to be valued at more than $850 billion.

In a blog post, OpenAI stated: "We recently submitted a confidential S-1. We expect it to leak so we're just announcing it. We have not decided on timing yet; it may be a while because there are things we want to do that are likely easier as a private company. But it's a complicated set of tradeoffs and this gives us the option to go public sooner if that ends up being best."

An S-1 is an investor prospectus that companies submit to the Securities and Exchange Commission ahead of an initial public offering. The confidential filing process allows firms to submit their IPO paperwork to regulators without immediately revealing financial details to the public.

The move comes just over a week after Anthropic, OpenAI's main rival and maker of the Claude chatbot, announced its own confidential IPO filing with the SEC. The near-simultaneous filings from the two leading artificial intelligence companies intensify the competitive race between them to reach the public markets.

OpenAI has raised billions in venture funding from investors including Microsoft, while Anthropic has secured substantial backing from Google and other investors. Both companies are now pursuing paths that will subject them to quarterly earnings reports and shareholder oversight once they become publicly traded entities.

The timing reflects growing investor appetite for artificial intelligence companies as the technology gains wider adoption across industries. Both firms will need to demonstrate sustainable business models and revenue growth to justify their valuations as they transition from private operations to public market scrutiny.

OpenAI's announcement that it expects its filing to become public underscores the practical reality that confidential submissions often leak before companies are ready for full disclosure. By announcing the filing themselves, the company controlled the narrative around the announcement rather than waiting for information to emerge through other channels.

The confidential filing process typically extends over several months as regulators review submissions and companies prepare for roadshows to pitch their offerings to institutional investors. The SEC's review period gives both companies time to make adjustments to their financial disclosures before they formally proceed with a public offering.

Neither company has provided a specific timeline for when their shares might begin trading. OpenAI indicated that the decision to go public depends on weighing various factors, suggesting the company wants to remain private for the near term if certain strategic objectives are easier to accomplish outside the public market structure. The competition between the two AI firms to complete their offerings could influence how the market receives both debuts.
