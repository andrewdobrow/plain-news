US launches strikes on Iran after helicopter downing

The United States military launched retaliatory strikes against Iran on Tuesday evening following Iran's downing of an American Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. US Central Command forces began the strikes at 5 p.m. Eastern Time at President Trump's direction, describing them as a proportional response.

The Apache helicopter crashed off the coast of Oman early Tuesday morning while on patrol. Both crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat called a Corsair, a 24-foot vessel. The military confirmed both aviators were safe and uninjured after spending roughly two hours in the water.

President Trump announced the incident on Truth Social, stating that Iran had shot down the helicopter. "There were two pilots involved, both are safe and uninjured. Nevertheless, the United States must, of necessity, respond to the attack," Trump wrote. The actual cause of the crash remained under investigation, though Trump attributed it to Iranian action.

The retaliatory strikes came as tensions escalated in the Middle East following recent exchanges between Iran and Israel. Iranian state television reported that Israeli attacks killed at least two members of the country's air defense units. The region has remained unstable since the US and Israel began striking Iran on February 28, which has disrupted global energy prices and raised the cost of food and other essentials worldwide.

Apache helicopters have been critical assets for American military operations in the region, particularly for enforcing a blockade on Iranian crude oil shipments designed to pressure Tehran into making a deal. The helicopters have also been used by the United Arab Emirates to shoot down Iranian drones.

Despite the military action, Trump indicated he remains optimistic about negotiations with Iran. Earlier Tuesday, before the helicopter incident, Trump expressed confidence that a deal could be reached within "two or three days," though he provided no details supporting this assessment.

Trump previously stated that while the US could conduct extensive bombing campaigns, doing so would be costly in both lives and time. "If we go and bomb, which we could do very easily if we want, and we spend another two or three weeks bombing, they'll have nothing left whatsoever. But you won't have the strait open for months," he said. "If we do the bombing, you know, a lot of people are going to be killed. Who wants to do that? I don't."

Iranian state media reported explosions in Hormozgan province, which borders the Strait of Hormuz, following the announcement of US strikes. Central Command has not disclosed the specific targets of the military action.

The situation highlights the delicate balance the Trump administration is attempting to maintain: responding forcefully enough to the loss of military assets while preserving space for continued diplomatic negotiations with Iran.
