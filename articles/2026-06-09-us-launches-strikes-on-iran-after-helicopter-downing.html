US Launches Strikes on Iran After Helicopter Downed Over Strait of Hormuz

The United States launched military strikes against Iran on Tuesday after a US Army Apache helicopter went down near the Strait of Hormuz. President Donald Trump blamed Iranian forces for downing the aircraft and said the country "must, of necessity, respond to this attack."

US Central Command confirmed that strikes began at 10pm UK time, targeting radar and air defense facilities along the Strait of Hormuz. The strikes mark a significant escalation that endangers a ceasefire agreement announced by the two countries in April.

The helicopter crashed at approximately 3:30am local time off the coast of Oman during a patrol operation. A US official told the Associated Press that the helicopter collided with an Iranian drone, though it remained unclear whether the collision was intentional. Trump initially stated the helicopter had been "shot down," though official statements said the incident was under investigation.

Both crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat. The vessel, a 24-foot craft called a Corsair, located the aviators after they spent about two hours in the water. US Central Command spokesperson Captain Tim Hawkins called it the first known drone rescue at sea by the US military.

However, Trump later downplayed the incident in an interview with the Wall Street Journal, calling it "wasn't a big deal" and noting that "the pilot is fine."

Central Command described the military response as "a proportional response to unjustified Iranian aggression," signaling that officials were not seeking a return to full-scale war. The Strait of Hormuz serves as a critical waterway for global energy supplies, with roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum passing through the narrow passage between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula.

The helicopter incident occurred as the region remained unstable following an exchange of fire between Iran and Israel on Monday, which further strained the ceasefire. Iranian state television reported that Israeli attacks killed at least two members of the country's air defense units.

The conflict that began on February 28 has shaken the global economy, raised energy prices worldwide, and made basic goods including food more expensive. Officials have struggled to convert the April ceasefire into a permanent agreement, particularly as Israel intensifies military operations in Lebanon against the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militia.

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded to the strikes on social media, saying Iran "will leave no attack or threat unanswered. Leave our region if you want to be safe." Iran's chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf stated: "We prefer the language of diplomacy, but we speak other languages far more fluently."

Hours before the strikes, Trump had expressed renewed optimism about negotiations, saying there was "a good chance" of reaching a deal within "two or three days."
