ADNOC oil tanker attacked in Strait of Hormuz, one crew member missing

An oil tanker operated by ADNOC Logistics & Services was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of November 17, leaving one crew member unaccounted for and eleven others injured, the Abu Dhabi national oil company unit confirmed Wednesday. All other crew members were safely evacuated from the vessel.

The attack marks the second on an ADNOC vessel in recent weeks. On November 10, another ADNOC tanker sustained damage following an attack in UAE territorial waters in the Arabian Gulf. ADNOC Logistics & Services operates more than 340 owned vessels and 600 chartered ships.

Maritime attacks in the region have escalated in recent weeks, with Iran reporting nearly daily incidents of tankers hitting naval mines in corridors Iran has designated as unauthorized in the Strait of Hormuz. The UK Maritime Trade Operations has also received frequent reports of attacks in the area. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy warned tanker owners that the southern passage through the strait had been mined, stating that cooperation with U.S. military officials would put vessels at risk.

The foreign ministers of Iran and Oman met earlier this week to discuss regional security and the importance of maintaining safe maritime corridors to ensure uninterrupted shipping and trade flows. ADNOC emphasized the importance of protecting seafarers and safeguarding freedom of navigation and maritime security.
