Trump pardons former GOP congressman convicted of insider trading

President Donald Trump issued a full pardon for Steve Buyer, a former Indiana Republican congressman who was convicted of insider trading in 2023. The pardon clears Buyer of criminal charges related to stock trades he made using confidential information.

Buyer, who represented Indiana in the House of Representatives, was found guilty of securities fraud after prosecutors proved he traded stocks based on inside information about corporate mergers. The conviction resulted in prison time and marked a significant fall for the longtime Republican lawmaker who had served in Congress for nearly two decades.

The pardon is the latest in a series of clemency actions by Trump targeting political allies and former Republican officials. Presidential pardons erase federal convictions and restore civil rights, though they cannot undo state-level charges or civil penalties that may have been imposed alongside criminal sentences.

Buyer's legal team had previously argued for leniency, though the specifics of their clemency petition were not made public. The pardon power, granted to presidents under the Constitution, allows the chief executive to grant clemency without approval from Congress or the courts.

The decision adds Buyer to a growing list of individuals pardoned by Trump during this term. Presidential pardons have historically generated controversy when issued to political associates or individuals with connections to the administration, raising questions about the appropriate use of executive clemency powers.
