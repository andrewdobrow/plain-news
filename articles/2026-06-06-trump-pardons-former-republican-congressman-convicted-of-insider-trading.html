Trump pardons former congressman convicted of insider trading

President Donald Trump issued a full pardon to Steve Buyer, a former Republican congressman from Indiana who was convicted of insider trading in 2023. The White House announced the pardon in a proclamation dated June 4, describing it as a full, complete, and unconditional pardon without providing an explanation for the decision.

Buyer was convicted of using nonpublic information to purchase stocks after leaving office. The former lawmaker served in the House of Representatives representing Indiana before his departure from Congress. His conviction in 2023 followed federal prosecution for securities fraud related to his stock trading activities.

The pardon eliminates the legal consequences of Buyer's conviction, including any remaining penalties or restrictions resulting from his guilty verdict. Presidential pardons are an executive power granted by the Constitution, allowing the president to forgive federal crimes and restore civil rights to those convicted.

This pardon continues a pattern of clemency decisions by Trump, who has previously granted pardons and commutations to political allies and supporters. The lack of explanation in the proclamation follows the standard format for presidential pardons, which are not required to include justification. However, pardons for individuals convicted of financial crimes have historically drawn scrutiny from government ethics observers.

The timing of the pardon, coming more than two years after Buyer's conviction, places it within Trump's current term in office. Former members of Congress who receive pardons for criminal convictions remain ineligible to hold federal office again unless additional steps are taken to restore those specific rights.
