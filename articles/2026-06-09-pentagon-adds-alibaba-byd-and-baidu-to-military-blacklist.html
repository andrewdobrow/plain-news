Pentagon adds BYD and Alibaba to list of firms linked to Chinese military

The Pentagon has added Chinese electric vehicle maker BYD and tech giant Alibaba to its list of companies it says have ties to China's military. The designation warns American firms of potential risks in doing business with the flagged companies.

The updated list, released by the Department of Defense, also includes search engine operator Baidu and biotech firm WuXi AppTec. The Pentagon compiles the list under a congressional mandate to identify companies it believes are working with or supporting the Chinese military. Being placed on the list does not trigger automatic sanctions, but it signals to US businesses and investors that engaging with these firms may carry national security implications.

BYD has grown into one of the world's largest electric vehicle manufacturers and has expanded its global reach in recent years. Alibaba operates major e-commerce platforms and cloud computing services used internationally. Both companies have operations and business relationships spanning multiple countries. The Pentagon did not detail specific activities that led to their inclusion on the military-linked firms list.

The designation comes at a time when US-China relations remain strained over trade, technology access, and security concerns. American officials have increasingly scrutinized Chinese technology companies, citing worries about data security and potential military applications of commercial technologies. Previous additions to the Pentagon list have prompted some US firms to reconsider partnerships and investment decisions.

Chinese companies placed on similar US government lists have typically denied military ties and challenged the designations. The inclusion of prominent firms like Alibaba and BYD marks a significant expansion of the Pentagon's tracking of Chinese corporate entities it views as supporting military modernization efforts. The list serves as a tool for informing US government procurement decisions and advising American companies on potential security risks in their supply chains and business partnerships.
