Pentagon adds Alibaba, BYD, and Baidu to military blacklist

The United States Department of Defense has designated several major Chinese technology companies as supporting the Chinese military, adding them to a blacklist that restricts American investment. The list includes electric vehicle maker BYD, e-commerce giant Alibaba, and search engine company Baidu, according to the Pentagon announcement.

The designation marks a significant escalation in U.S. efforts to limit Chinese military advancement through commercial technology partnerships. Companies placed on the list face restrictions on receiving American capital, as U.S. investors are prohibited from holding stakes in entities deemed to be supporting Chinese military modernization efforts. The Pentagon's classification stems from findings that these firms provide technological support or resources that enhance the capabilities of China's armed forces.

BYD, which has become one of the world's largest electric vehicle manufacturers, and Alibaba, which operates major cloud computing and e-commerce platforms, represent cornerstone companies in China's technology sector. Baidu, often called China's Google, operates artificial intelligence research divisions alongside its search engine business. The inclusion of such prominent firms signals Washington's broadening interpretation of which commercial activities constitute military support.

The blacklist had previously included these companies before being temporarily adjusted, making this move a restoration rather than an initial designation. The Pentagon maintains the list as part of regulations requiring annual updates on Chinese military companies operating in commercial markets. American institutional investors and pension funds that hold shares in these companies will need to divest their positions to comply with the restrictions.

The designation comes during a period of heightened tension between Washington and Beijing over technology access and national security concerns. U.S. officials have increasingly focused on preventing American capital and technology from contributing to Chinese military capabilities, particularly in areas like artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing, and aerospace. The Chinese government has previously criticized such measures as economic interference and protectionism disguised as security policy.
