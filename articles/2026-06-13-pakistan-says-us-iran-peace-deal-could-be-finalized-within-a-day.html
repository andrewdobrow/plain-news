Trump Announces US-Iran Deal Signing for Sunday as Tehran Questions Timeline

President Trump announced on Saturday that a peace agreement with Iran would be signed the following day, claiming the deal would prevent Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and reopen the strait of Hormuz to international shipping. However, Iranian officials quickly disputed the timeline, with Tehran's foreign ministry saying no final decision had been made on when a signing would occur.

In a post on Truth Social, Trump stated that Iran "no longer want a Nuclear Weapon, nor will they have one, either through purchase, development, or any other form of procurement." He added that the Hormuz Strait would open immediately after signing and that the US relationship with Iran under his administration would be "much different and better" than under previous presidents.

Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said on Saturday that Islamabad was preparing for an electronic signing within 24 hours, followed by technical-level talks the following week. He expressed confidence that the deal would form a foundation for lasting peace in the region.

Esmaeil Baghaei, Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson, took a more cautious approach. He said an exact signing date had not been determined and that a signing would not happen on Sunday. He suggested the possibility of a signing occurring in the coming days but made no firm commitment.

The conflicting statements reflected deeper disagreements between the sides over the deal's substance and implementation. US officials claimed the agreement would result in the destruction of Iran's enriched uranium and dismantling of its nuclear program, with no frozen assets released until Iran met certain conditions. Iran's version, according to reports from its semi-official news agency, described a different outcome, including the release of 24 billion dollars in frozen assets and only a 60-day period for nuclear negotiations.

The proposed deal also outlined different understandings on regional security. According to Iranian sources, the agreement would address conflicts across multiple fronts, including Lebanon, where Israel has conducted operations against Hezbollah. The deal would suspend sanctions on Iran's oil sales, allow Iran to charge for passage through the strait of Hormuz, and lift the US naval blockade on Iranian ports.

Trump has previously claimed numerous times that a deal was imminent, only to revert to threats of military action against Iran. Recent weeks have seen intense clashes between Iran and Israel, with the conflict disrupting critical shipping routes and threatening global energy supplies.

Both sides are attempting to frame a potential agreement as a victory to their respective domestic audiences. Iran's foreign minister stated that his country had emerged stronger from the conflict, while US officials said the deal met Trump's core objectives. The coming days will determine whether the two sides can overcome their differences and finalize an agreement.
