Trump Extends Iran Ceasefire While Threatening Economic Pressure

President Trump announced an extension of a ceasefire with Iran on Tuesday, pausing military action while demanding Iranian leaders submit a unified peace proposal. The announcement came after weeks of escalating tensions and failed diplomatic efforts between the two nations.

Trump stated on his Truth Social platform that he was extending the ceasefire "until such time as their leaders and representatives can come up with a unified proposal." He credited requests from Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and other officials in his decision to delay military strikes. However, Trump emphasized that the U.S. military would "remain ready and able" and that the blockade on Iranian ports would continue.

The decision marked a sharp reversal from Trump's earlier statements. Hours before announcing the extension, Trump said he "expected to be bombing" and that the military was "raring to go." The administration has threatened to target Iranian power stations and civilian infrastructure in any renewed attacks.

Iran's response has been dismissive. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and the regime's lead negotiator, rejected the ceasefire extension. His adviser, Mahdi Mohammadi, wrote on social media that Trump's move was "a ploy to buy time for a surprise strike" and that "the time for Iran to take the initiative has come."

The dispute centers on several key issues. Iran has insisted the U.S. must end its blockade of Iranian ports before negotiations can resume. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned that the blockade would continue, stating that within days Iranian oil storage facilities would be full and Iranian wells would shut down.

Trump has also announced plans for what he calls "economic D-Day" against Iran, declaring it "the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country." Bessent has promised to announce "the toughest sanctions in history" against Iran. The administration has threatened to target any country, business, or banking institution that supports Iran economically.

The ongoing conflict has disrupted global energy markets. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping lane through which about a fifth of the world's oil and gas passes. The International Energy Agency called the combined effects of this conflict and Russia's war with Ukraine "the biggest crisis in history" for global energy markets. Oil prices have risen above ninety dollars per barrel as a result.

Pakistan has emerged as a key mediator in the dispute. Prime Minister Sharif thanked Trump for the ceasefire extension and pledged to continue efforts toward "a negotiated settlement of the conflict."

The first round of talks in Islamabad ten days earlier ended without agreement on reopening the strait or other key issues. Iran's foreign minister has rejected U.S. threats, calling them "desperate" and insisting that Iran will not negotiate "under the shadow of threats."
