Iran vows to overcome sanctions after Trump threatens economic pressure

Iran signaled it is preparing for prolonged economic conflict with the United States after a two-month ceasefire expired without progress toward a peace deal. The country's leadership indicated it would adopt a more aggressive military stance if negotiations fail, while President Trump demanded that Tehran "put up the white flag of surrender."

The breakdown in talks has intensified tensions between the two countries. Trump threatened not only stricter economic measures against Iran but also said he would "bomb the shit out of" Oman if the country "gets in the way" of his efforts to end the conflict. This represents the second time Trump has directed such threats at the longtime US strategic partner.

Iran responded by preparing for what officials described as a "fully offensive" military posture. An Iranian military spokesperson said vessels attempting to pass through the Strait of Hormuz would "find several beautiful holes in their hulls," signaling a more confrontational approach to international shipping in the waterway.

The escalating rhetoric has already affected global energy markets. Brent crude oil rose above $90 a barrel for the first time since late July, driven by investor concerns about potential disruptions to oil flows. The strait normally handles roughly a quarter of global seaborne oil trade. Shipping through the waterway has slowed dramatically, with only six commercial vessels transiting on Monday compared to the higher volumes seen before tensions escalated.

Trump claimed during his Fox News interview that he was "not in a hurry" to reach an agreement with Iran, despite previously suggesting the conflict would end soon. He said he would not impose a fresh deadline on Tehran, adding that he has no time schedule for resolving the dispute.

Market analysts warned that the combination of Middle East tensions and Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine could create a "double shock" to energy prices. Some economists predicted that escalation could continue through November, potentially causing significant spikes in oil and gas prices if intense conflict persists in the Gulf region.

The expiration of the ceasefire without a diplomatic breakthrough suggests both sides are preparing for extended confrontation rather than compromise. Iran's emphasis on developing economic self-reliance and its military warnings indicate the country is not banking on sanctions relief through negotiation. Instead, Iranian leadership appears focused on strategies to withstand prolonged American economic pressure while maintaining military deterrence capabilities.

The situation underscores how stalled diplomatic efforts are giving way to renewed tensions, with consequences extending beyond US-Iran relations to affect global energy security and international commerce.
