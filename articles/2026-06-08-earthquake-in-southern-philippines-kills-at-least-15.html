At least 32 killed in magnitude-7.8 earthquake in southern Philippines

A powerful magnitude-7.8 earthquake struck the southern Philippines early Monday morning, collapsing buildings and killing at least 32 people while injuring more than 200 others. The quake also generated a 3-foot tsunami that reached nearby coastlines across the region.

The earthquake centered about 8 miles southwest of General Santos City on the island of Mindanao, with a depth of 6.2 miles according to initial reports from the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology. It struck at 7:37 a.m. local time. The US Geological Survey recorded the magnitude as 7.8 with a depth of 34 miles.

Video verified by news organizations showed significant structural damage in General Santos City and surrounding areas. The upper floor of a Jollibee restaurant collapsed, and outer concrete walls of a commercial complex gave way. In Davao del Sur, part of a high school building collapsed as students were gathering outside. A convenience store in General Santos City had its entrance destroyed, with broken glass and overturned benches scattered across the street.

Robert Dagon of the General Santos City police told news agencies that many buildings were affected but officials were focused on ongoing rescue operations. A spokesperson for the civil defense agency reported 19 people feared dead, 134 injured, and 12 missing in initial counts, though these figures were still being verified.

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center initially warned that waves up to 10 feet were possible on some Philippine coasts and waves up to 3 feet on some Indonesian and Malaysian coastlines. The center later said the threat had largely passed.

Power outages were reported across the affected region, and authorities urged residents to move to higher ground and avoid entering damaged buildings due to the risk of aftershocks. The civil defense office warned of potential dangers from aftershocks following the main quake.

The earthquake struck as public schools were reopening for a new academic year, with students present in several buildings during the tremor. Video from Mahayhay elementary school in Davao showed panicked pupils scrambling for safety during a morning flag ceremony. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. ordered the suspension of schools in affected areas until further notice, stating that "the safety of our children comes first." The Philippines Red Cross reported providing support to traumatized students at three high schools.

The Philippines sits along the Pacific Ring of Fire, an arc of seismic faults that encircles the Pacific Ocean. This geographic location makes the country one of the world's most disaster-prone nations, regularly experiencing earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. The archipelago also faces approximately 20 typhoons and tropical storms annually.

Government agencies continued assessing damage and clearing routes for rescue operations, with evacuation centers operational across the affected areas.
