Ben Stokes removed from England squad after nightclub incident, Joe Root named interim captain

Ben Stokes has been removed from England's squad for the second Test against New Zealand at The Oval following a nightclub incident in London. Joe Root has been named as captain for the match, stepping into a leadership role after previously captaining the team.

The England and Wales Cricket Board launched an investigation into a breach of team protocols after Stokes and Gus Atkinson were found at a nightclub in the early hours of Monday morning. The incident occurred at the Rex Rooms on Kings Road in London, hours after England's convincing victory in the first Test at Lord's. Both players have been referred to the Cricket Regulator, an independent disciplinary body that can impose suspensions and unlimited fines.

The incident took place during an end of season party involving players from Saracens rugby club. It marks another disciplinary matter for the England team, which has faced several off-field challenges in recent months. The ECB stated: "Ben Stokes and Gus Atkinson were present at a nightclub in the early hours of Monday morning when an incident took place."

The removal of both players represents a significant blow to the squad. Atkinson, 28, has been England's leading wicket-taker since his debut in July 2024, claiming 76 wickets in 17 matches. His absence alongside Stokes creates notable gaps in the team's bowling and batting capabilities as they prepare for the second Test starting on 17 June.

This incident comes amid broader concerns about discipline within the England team. Earlier this year, white-ball captain Harry Brook was involved in an altercation with a bouncer outside a nightclub in Wellington before an ODI. Brook and Jacob Bethell were subsequently reprimanded by the Cricket Regulator. Additionally, during the Ashes tour, videos of inebriated England players circulated on social media while the team was on a break in Noosa, Australia. In response, England management introduced a midnight curfew, which Stokes and Atkinson have now breached.

England's managing director of men's cricket, Rob Key, previously expressed his concerns about drinking culture within the squad. "I don't agree with a drinking culture," Key said. "If it's true that it became a stag do and people are out drinking all the time excessively, that's not acceptable."

Men's coach Brendon McCullum had emphasized the need for discipline this summer. "Playing professional sport is a great career and it's great fun, but ultimately you're representing your country and you've got some responsibilities and obligations to carry yourself in the right way," McCullum stated.

Stokes is reportedly considering his position as Test captain. Reports indicate he spent time meeting with his agent and advisers to discuss his future with the team. Root's appointment as interim captain draws on his extensive experience, having previously captained England in 64 Test matches before stepping down from the role.
