Deb Haaland wins New Mexico Democratic primary for governor

Deb Haaland, who served as Interior Secretary under President Biden, won the Democratic primary for governor of New Mexico. The victory positions her as the party's nominee in the general election.

If elected in November, Haaland would make history as the first female Native American governor in the United States. She previously served as a U.S. Representative from New Mexico before joining the Biden Cabinet.

The sources instead focus on other Democratic primary races. In Illinois, Juliana Stratton, the state's lieutenant governor, won the Democratic primary to succeed retiring U.S. Senator Dick Durbin. Stratton defeated U.S. Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi in what observers characterized as an upset, leading by more than six percentage points with nearly 90 percent of votes counted.

Stratton, a progressive candidate backed by Illinois Governor J.B. Pritzker, had trailed Krishnamoorthi in earlier polling. Her campaign emphasized progressive causes including the abolishment of ICE, which gained traction as a central issue following immigration enforcement operations in Chicago. Stratton also faced competition from U.S. Representative Robin Kelly, who drew less than 20 percent of the vote.

"We did it," Stratton said at her election night event. "Tonight we showed what's possible when you listen to the people and give the people what they want."

Stratton highlighted her progressive platform, which includes Medicare for all, a $25 per hour minimum wage, and ICE abolishment. If elected in November, she would become the 15th Black U.S. senator in American history. Durbin said in a statement that he looked forward to passing the torch to Stratton at the end of his term.

The Democratic primary in Illinois drew unusually high turnout for a non-presidential year Senate race, with more than 1.14 million votes already tallied and total participation expected to surpass the 2004 Senate primary when Barack Obama won the Democratic nomination.
