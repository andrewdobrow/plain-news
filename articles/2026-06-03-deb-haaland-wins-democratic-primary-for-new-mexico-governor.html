Deb Haaland wins Democratic primary for New Mexico governor

Deb Haaland, who previously served as Interior Secretary in the Biden administration, won the Democratic primary for governor of New Mexico. She secured her party's nomination for the November general election by defeating other candidates in the race.

If Haaland prevails in November, she would make history as the first female Native American governor in the United States. Before joining the Biden cabinet, Haaland represented New Mexico in Congress.

Haaland's victory comes as primary elections continue across the country with significant implications for the fall general elections. In Illinois, Juliana Stratton, the state's lieutenant governor, won the Democratic primary to succeed retiring U.S. Senator Dick Durbin. Stratton defeated U.S. Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi in what was considered an upset victory.

With nearly 90 percent of votes counted, Stratton led Krishnamoorthi by more than six percentage points. She declared victory at her Chicago election night event, saying "tonight we showed what's possible when you listen to the people and give the people what they want."

Stratton, a progressive with backing from Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, had trailed Krishnamoorthi in earlier polling. However, an infusion of campaign cash from the governor and a shift in voter sentiment on immigration policy in recent weeks boosted her candidacy. Stratton had called directly for abolishing ICE, while Krishnamoorthi took a more moderate position, calling for abolishing "Trump's ICE."

Krishnamoorthi, a five-term congressman who raised more than 30 million dollars for his Senate campaign, conceded defeat at his election night event. "Obviously this is not the result we sought, but unlike Donald Trump I'm not going to question the outcome," he said.

If elected in November, Stratton would become the 15th Black U.S. senator in American history. Republicans have not won a statewide election in Illinois in more than a decade, making the Democratic primary winner the likely victor in the general election.

Turnout for the Illinois Democratic primary was unusually high for a Senate contest in a non-presidential election year. More than 1.14 million votes were tallied, with total participation expected to surpass the 2004 primary when Barack Obama won the Democratic Senate nomination.

Stratton also faced competition from Robin Kelly, who drew less than 20 percent of the vote. Both Stratton and Kelly are Black with strong connections to Illinois' Black community. Stratton will face Don Tracy, the former Republican state party chair, in November's general election.

In her victory speech, Stratton outlined progressive priorities including Medicare for all, a 25-dollar-per-hour minimum wage, and abolishing ICE. She referenced her late father, a civil rights activist, in calling on Illinois voters to make an "uncomfortable choice" and "choose to resist."
