US and Iran reach preliminary deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz and begin nuclear talks

The United States and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding over the weekend to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and begin 60 days of nuclear negotiations. The agreement, signed digitally, is set to be formalized in person on Friday. Senior US officials told reporters the memorandum is only the first step in a longer negotiation process.

The announcement lifted financial markets when they opened Monday morning. President Trump described the agreement as a victory in cutting off Iran's pathway to a nuclear weapon, while the deal also reopens the strategic waterway that had been blocked. Vice President Vance will take center stage in the implementation, with Trump saying Vance will play a key role as the vice president seeks to build credentials ahead of 2028.

The deal faces skepticism within the administration and from intelligence officials. CIA Director John Ratcliffe told President Trump and other senior officials that intelligence gathered by US agencies raised serious doubts about Iran's willingness to make the nuclear concessions the US is seeking in any final deal, according to three sources familiar with those discussions. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth have both expressed concerns and raised questions about the deal in internal discussions.

The political world has engaged in intense debate about a document virtually nobody has seen. A large vacuum has been left by the failure to disclose the specific terms of the memorandum. The agreement comes as tensions between Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have reached new heights over how to end the conflict they started together, threatening the viability of the Iran deal.

The deal represents a significant shift in US policy toward Iran. The reopening of the Strait of Hormuz addresses a critical chokepoint for global oil shipping, while the nuclear negotiations aim to prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons. The 60-day timeframe for negotiations suggests an urgent push to reach a comprehensive agreement, though internal administration doubts and external tensions with Israel may complicate the process.
