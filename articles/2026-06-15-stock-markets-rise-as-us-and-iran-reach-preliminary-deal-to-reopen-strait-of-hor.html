Iran Announces Nuclear Talks to Begin Friday After Initial US Agreement

Iran's foreign minister announced that new talks on nuclear arms and sanctions relief would begin Friday, following the signing of an initial agreement to end hostilities with the United States. President Trump stated he hopes to put the conflict in the rearview mirror.

The agreement marks a significant shift after a military conflict that began on February 28. According to Iranian officials, the deal includes provisions requiring Israel to withdraw from Lebanon. The framework emerged from negotiations conducted in Oman, where diplomatic efforts stretched across multiple rounds over the past year.

The talks leading up to this agreement faced significant challenges. Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and son-in-law Jared Kushner led the US negotiating team, though their approach drew criticism from regional observers. A Gulf diplomat with direct knowledge of the talks expressed frustration with their methods, describing them as unconventional and poorly resourced. The US team included Michael Anton, a political philosopher with no specialization in nuclear policy, and lacked the technical expertise that characterized previous diplomatic efforts. In comparison, the Obama administration sent 10 senior officials from four departments to Iran talks in Vienna in 2009.

The final round of talks occurred in Geneva on February 26, where Iran presented a seven-page written offer for a new agreement. Witkoff's request to keep a copy of the proposal was denied, a decision that later became a point of contention. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has indicated he hopes the truth about what occurred during final negotiations will eventually become public.

Iran bears some responsibility for the breakdown as well. The country has not published its written offer despite calls from within Iran to do so. According to some accounts, misunderstandings about Iran's nuclear program, including the purpose of the Tehran Research Reactor and the scope of Iran's planned nuclear capabilities, contributed to the deterioration of talks.

Kushner acknowledged that a deal could have been achieved that was superior to the 2015 Obama nuclear agreement. The compressed timeline for negotiations also hindered progress. On February 17, Witkoff allocated only three and a half hours for talks with Iran while simultaneously meeting with Ukrainian officials, leaving limited time for meaningful discussion given the indirect nature of the negotiations.

The upcoming talks face significant obstacles. Both countries must navigate domestic political pressures while addressing fundamental disagreements over Iran's nuclear capabilities and regional influence. Inside Iran, opposition to further negotiations with the United States has emerged, with protesters outside the foreign ministry demanding no return to talks. The initial agreement provides a foundation for further discussions, but reaching a comprehensive settlement will require substantial concessions from both sides on issues that have divided them for decades.
