OpenAI Delays Adult Mode for ChatGPT, Faces Internal Criticism Over Pentagon Deal

OpenAI has postponed the launch of "adult mode" for ChatGPT, shifting focus to what the company considers more urgent priorities. The delay comes as the startup manages competing demands from users, investors, and its own workforce.

Sam Altman announced last October that OpenAI would allow adult content on ChatGPT and implement age verification systems. The company has now reversed course on that timeline. "We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive," OpenAI said in a statement.

The company, which has more than 900 million ChatGPT users, acknowledged the principle behind the original plan. "We still believe in the principle of treating adults like adults, but getting the experience right will take more time," the statement continued.

Instead of rolling out adult mode, OpenAI is implementing age prediction tools that identify users under 18. When underage users are detected, stricter safety settings activate automatically, limiting exposure to graphic violence and sexual content. In the UK, if ChatGPT generates pornographic images deemed illegal under the Online Safety Act, OpenAI must use age verification to prevent minors from accessing that material.

The shift in priorities reflects pressure Altman has faced since declaring a "code red" to improve ChatGPT. The startup is competing intensely with Google, Anthropic, and other AI companies developing rival systems.

The delay announcement comes as OpenAI navigates internal dissent over a recently announced Pentagon contract. Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over the partnership, citing concerns about mass surveillance and autonomous weapons development.

"AI has an important role in national security. But surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got," Kalinowski wrote on X.

Kalinowski emphasized her objection centered on execution speed. "To be clear, my issue is that the announcement was rushed without the guardrails defined. It's a governance concern first and foremost. These are too important for deals or announcements to be rushed," she added.

OpenAI had moved quickly with the Pentagon contract after Anthropic, the previous AI contractor, was dropped from the position. Altman later acknowledged the deal appeared "opportunistic and sloppy."

Following the resignation and criticism, OpenAI amended its Pentagon agreement to explicitly exclude domestic mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. An OpenAI spokesperson stated the company believes the updated agreement creates a responsible path for national security applications of AI while maintaining clear boundaries.

"We recognise that people have strong views about these issues and we will continue to engage in discussion with employees, government, civil society and communities around the world," the company said.
