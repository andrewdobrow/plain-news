OpenAI Delays Adult Mode for ChatGPT as Security and Performance Take Priority

OpenAI has postponed the launch of "adult mode" for ChatGPT, shifting focus toward what the company considers more urgent development priorities. The decision comes after Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive, had announced last year that the platform would permit adult content alongside age verification systems.

The company stated: "We're pushing out the launch of adult mode so we can focus on work that is a higher priority for more users right now, including gains in intelligence, personality improvements, personalisation, and making the experience more proactive." With over 900 million ChatGPT users, OpenAI believes these enhancements warrant immediate attention.

Since Altman declared a "code red" last October to enhance the chatbot's capabilities, the company has intensified efforts to compete with rivals including Google and Anthropic. This acceleration has redirected resources away from the adult content feature, despite OpenAI's stated belief in treating adults like adults.

Rather than launching adult mode, OpenAI is rolling out age prediction tools that identify users under 18. When younger users are detected, the system activates enhanced safety settings that limit exposure to graphic violence and sexual role-play content. In the UK, the company must comply with the Online Safety Act by using age verification to shield pornographic images from minors.

The postponement reflects broader strategic choices at OpenAI as it navigates competitive pressures and regulatory requirements. The company continues developing safety measures alongside performance improvements, recognizing that responsible deployment requires careful implementation.

In separate developments, OpenAI has faced internal challenges regarding its recent Pentagon partnership. Caitlin Kalinowski, head of hardware in OpenAI's robotics division, resigned over concerns about the defense contract. She expressed worry about mass surveillance of US citizens and autonomous AI-guided weapons systems operating without human oversight.

Kalinowski wrote that while AI plays an important role in national security, "surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got." She emphasized her concern centered on governance, stating the announcement was rushed without proper guardrails.

OpenAI acknowledged the Pentagon deal appeared "opportunistic and sloppy" and has committed to amending the contract to explicitly exclude domestic surveillance applications and autonomous weapons systems. An OpenAI spokesperson emphasized the agreement creates "a workable path for responsible national security uses of AI while making clear our red lines."

The company indicated it would continue engaging with employees, government agencies, civil society groups, and global communities regarding these significant policy questions. The resignation and subsequent contract modifications underscore the tensions between commercial growth, national security interests, and internal employee values at major AI companies.
