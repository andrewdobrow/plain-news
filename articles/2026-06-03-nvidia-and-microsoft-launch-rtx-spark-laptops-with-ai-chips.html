Nvidia and Microsoft launch AI-powered Windows laptops with local processing chips

Nvidia has announced a partnership with Microsoft to introduce new laptop chips designed to run artificial intelligence models directly on personal computers without relying on cloud connectivity. The collaboration centers on the RTX Spark chip and the Vera central processing unit, paired with Microsoft's Windows software on devices like the Surface Laptop Ultra.

The RTX Spark represents Nvidia's bid to shift AI computing from remote data centers to consumer devices. Developed over three years with help from Taiwan's MediaTek, the chip combines microprocessor and graphics capabilities to run AI agents locally on laptops and desktops. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang described this as reinventing the PC for the AI era, noting the technology could eventually replace traditional mouse and keyboard interactions by allowing AI agents to navigate computers autonomously.

The move marks Nvidia's expansion beyond its dominant position in data center AI chips into consumer hardware. Huang positioned the announcement as part of a strategy to compete across every layer of the AI technology stack. Computer makers including Dell, Lenovo, Asus and HP plan to use the RTX Spark chip in their devices.

According to Neil Shah, co-founder of Counterpoint Research, the RTX Spark represents a transformational moment comparable to the iPhone or ChatGPT launch. He predicted the chip could transform traditional app-focused computers into useful AI personal computers with autonomous agents that would eventually become common in homes worldwide.

Despite the strategic significance, analysts suggest this venture represents a longer-term growth opportunity rather than an immediate earnings driver for Nvidia. The company's near-term fortunes remain heavily dependent on continued global demand for data center AI infrastructure and computing power.

Huang also announced plans for at least two additional generations of RTX Spark chips, signaling Nvidia's long-term commitment to the consumer PC market. The Vera CPU is already being evaluated by companies including OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX for early adoption.

The announcement intensifies competition in the AI hardware market. Intel plans to launch its own AI graphics chip, the Xe3P codenamed Crescent Island, later this year, positioning it as purpose-built for the upcoming generation of AI agents. The move pits Nvidia against established competitors including Intel, Apple, Qualcomm and AMD.

When asked about concerns that AI technology might eliminate software engineering jobs, Huang dismissed such worries as "complete nonsense," arguing that AI would increase productivity and lead to more hiring of software engineers rather than fewer.

The companies have not yet provided specific release dates or pricing for the new hardware. Nvidia will supply the specialized AI processing chips while Microsoft manufactures the Surface Laptop Ultra.
