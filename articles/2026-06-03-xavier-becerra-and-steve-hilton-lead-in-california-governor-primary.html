Becerra and Hilton advance to California governor general election

Xavier Becerra and Steve Hilton are projected to advance to California's November gubernatorial general election after the primary results, setting up a matchup between a Democrat and a Republican in a state where Democrats hold a significant advantage.

Becerra, the former California attorney general who later served as U.S. health secretary, secured one of the top two spots despite polling at just 3 percent months before the election. His campaign experienced a dramatic turnaround in the final weeks as Democratic voters consolidated behind him. "The underdog stayed in the fight," Becerra told supporters as results came in, calling his advance a "Hollywood ending" few had anticipated. If elected, he would become California's first Latino governor since 1875.

Hilton, a former Fox News host and UK political operative, claimed the other spot in the general election with backing from President Trump. His path to the general election comes despite running in a state where Republicans have faced significant challenges in statewide races. Democrats control nearly all government levels and hold nearly a two-to-one voter registration advantage over Republicans.

The race itself proved unusually competitive for California's Democratic stronghold. The primary field remained divided throughout the campaign, with environmental activist Tom Steyer and U.S. Representative Katie Porter also competing on the Democratic side. Steyer spent more than $200 million of his personal fortune on advertising but failed to secure a top-two finish.

The primary season saw dramatic shifts in momentum. Early in the contest, the race appeared it could produce two Republican nominees. However, Democrat Eric Swalwell's campaign collapsed following allegations of sexual assault and misconduct, which he has denied. He subsequently ended his gubernatorial bid and resigned from Congress.

Becerra faced significant headwinds during his campaign, including a viral video of him attempting to direct a TV reporter's interview and criticism from former Biden administration officials regarding his management style. He also faced scrutiny over his position on single-payer healthcare. Despite these challenges, he steadily gained support from Democratic voters as election day approached.

The general election winner will replace Governor Gavin Newsom, who is term-limited. A matchup between Becerra and Hilton sets up a contest where the Republican nominee must overcome substantial voter registration disadvantages and the fact that Trump remains deeply unpopular in California. Running with Trump's endorsement adds complexity to Hilton's path to victory.

As of the count's conclusion, election officials continued processing ballots to determine final placements. Many Democratic voters waited until the final days to cast ballots, causing late-arriving mail-in votes to still be counted as results were finalized.
