SpaceX prices shares at $135 in largest public offering on record

SpaceX has set the final price for its initial public offering at $135 per share, marking the largest stock market debut in history. The company plans to sell more than 555 million shares, raising approximately $75 billion in the public sale. Trading on the Nasdaq exchange begins Friday.

The pricing gives SpaceX a market valuation that exceeds any previous IPO. According to reports, BlackRock has placed an order worth at least $5 billion for shares in the offering. The strong investor demand reflects confidence in the company's position in commercial space launches and its expanding Starlink satellite internet business.

The public offering makes Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire on paper, based on his ownership stake in SpaceX combined with his holdings in other companies. Musk founded SpaceX in 2002 with the goal of reducing space transportation costs and enabling the colonization of Mars. The company now holds contracts with NASA and commercial clients for satellite launches and cargo deliveries to the International Space Station.

Up to a quarter of the shares will be reserved for individual investors rather than funds and banks, a larger share than is typically the case in major initial public offerings. In the UK, investment platforms including AJ Bell and Hargreaves Lansdown are offering clients the chance to bid for shares. In the US, buyers can purchase through Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi Technologies, and Morgan Stanley's E*Trade.

If the offering becomes oversubscribed, it remains unclear exactly how shares will be allocated among applicants. Minimum subscriptions are typically around 1,000 pounds or dollars, with applications closing before the pricing announcement.

Justin Fishner-Wolfson, a longtime investor in Musk's ventures, has played a central role in structuring the multibillion-dollar payout to existing shareholders as part of the IPO process. The offering allows early investors and employees to cash out portions of their stakes while providing SpaceX with capital for expansion.

Market observers note the IPO comes at a time when SpaceX has achieved technical milestones that competitors have not matched, including reusable rocket technology that significantly lowers launch costs. The Starlink satellite constellation now serves internet customers across multiple continents, creating a recurring revenue stream beyond traditional launch contracts.

However, potential risks remain for investors. These include possible launch failures, regulatory changes, increased competition, and the concentration of voting power. Musk is not selling any of his shares and will retain 82.4 percent of the company's voting power. The company's valuation reflects expectations that its business lines will continue growing as space-based services expand.
