SpaceX Sets Record IPO Price at $135 Per Share in Largest Public Offering

SpaceX finalized its initial public offering price at $135 per share, creating what will become the largest stock market debut in history. The space exploration, satellite broadband, and artificial intelligence company is selling more than 555 million shares, raising at least $75 billion through the public offering. Trading is set to begin on Friday.

The IPO values SpaceX at approximately $1.78 trillion. At this valuation, founder Elon Musk could become the world's first trillionaire on paper once shares begin trading publicly, though his net worth had declined by more than $50 billion in the period before the offering.

Investor demand for SpaceX shares has been exceptionally strong. Retail investor orders topped $100 billion, while institutional investors like BlackRock placed orders of at least $5 billion. Overall, the offering attracted between $250 billion and $300 billion in bids from investors, meaning it was oversubscribed by three to four times.

The $75 billion raise is nearly three times larger than the previous record IPO. Saudi Aramco's 2019 offering of $29.4 billion held the previous title.

However, not all analysts view the valuation favorably. Morningstar, an investment research firm, has calculated that SpaceX is worth only $63 per share, well below the IPO price. Michael Field, Morningstar's chief equity strategist, stated that the company has "real strengths, particularly in Starlink," but warned of concerns about valuation. "With so many unknown and untested technologies underpinning much of the valuation price, particularly within the AI business, we think the valuation is extremely speculative," Field said. He recommended investors wait for "a more attractive entry point down the line."

Morningstar estimates that Starlink's realistic global market opportunity is around $129 billion, significantly lower than SpaceX's claim of a $1.6 trillion addressable market for the division.

At $1.78 trillion, SpaceX trades at roughly 92 times its trailing sales, an exceptionally high valuation. The company reported a net loss of $4.9 billion in 2025.

U.S. Senator Elizabeth Warren called on the Securities and Exchange Commission to delay the IPO, citing concerns about investor protection and corporate governance given the offering's unprecedented scale.

SpaceX operates three main business segments: space exploration through its Falcon and Starship rockets, connectivity via its Starlink satellite constellation, and artificial intelligence through its xAI division. The company has established itself as a leader in commercial spaceflight while maintaining its satellite network.

Index providers have begun preparing for SpaceX's inclusion in major stock indices. MSCI confirmed it would apply existing rules to include the company in its Global Standard Indexes early. However, S&P Dow Jones Indices declined to relax its entry requirements, meaning SpaceX could take months to join the S&P 500.
