SpaceX sets share price at $135 for largest IPO on record

SpaceX has officially priced its shares at $135 each, marking the beginning of what will be the largest initial public offering in history. The space exploration company is expected to join the US stock market on Friday with a valuation of $1.78 trillion, offering at least $75 billion in shares to investors.

The offering has generated enormous investor demand, with more than $250 billion in bids according to Reuters, representing an oversubscription of three to four times. This $75 billion share offering is nearly three times larger than the previous IPO record set by Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion float in 2019.

If the offering succeeds as planned, founder Elon Musk could potentially become the world's first trillionaire. However, the valuation has drawn significant scrutiny from analysts and policymakers. Investment research group Morningstar has calculated that SpaceX is worth only $63 per share, well below the $135 IPO price. Michael Field, Morningstar's chief equity strategist, recommends that investors avoid the IPO and wait for "a more attractive entry point down the line."

"We believe the business has real strengths, particularly in Starlink, but with so many unknown and untested technologies underpinning much of the valuation price, particularly within the AI business, we think the valuation is extremely speculative," Field said in a statement.

The company comprises three business segments: space exploration through its Falcon and Starship rockets, connectivity via its Starlink satellite internet service, and artificial intelligence through its xAI division. At $1.78 trillion, the IPO values SpaceX at roughly 92 times its trailing sales, a valuation that assumes investors believe Musk can achieve ambitious goals including orbital datacenters in space, a moon base, and cities on other planets.

SpaceX claims Starlink has a total addressable market of $1.6 trillion, though Morningstar estimates the segment's realistic global opportunity at about $129 billion. The company posted a net loss of $4.9 billion in 2025, raising additional questions about the valuation.

US Senator Elizabeth Warren has called on the Securities and Exchange Commission to delay the IPO, citing concerns about investor protection and market integrity. "Given the unprecedented threats to investor protection and market integrity posed by the biggest IPO in history, you must delay any eventual acceleration of the registration statement's effectiveness accordingly," Warren wrote to the regulator.

Index provider MSCI has confirmed it will apply existing rules for early inclusion of large IPOs in its Global Standard Indexes, likely clearing the way for SpaceX to join. Meanwhile, S&P Dow Jones Indices has declined to relax its entry rules, which could delay SpaceX's inclusion in the S&P 500 by several months.
