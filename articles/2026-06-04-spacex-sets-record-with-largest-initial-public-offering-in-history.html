SpaceX sets IPO price at $135 per share in largest public offering ever

SpaceX has finalized its initial public offering price at $135 per share, positioning the company for what is expected to be the largest stock market debut in history. The aerospace company will sell more than 555 million shares, generating approximately $75 billion when trading begins Friday on the Nasdaq exchange. Retail investors have submitted orders exceeding $100 billion, demonstrating substantial appetite for shares in Elon Musk's space and satellite communications enterprise.

The company has built its business around multiple revenue streams, including launching satellites, providing internet service through its Starlink subsidiary, and developing rockets for commercial and government clients. The transition from private to public status marks a significant shift for SpaceX, which will now face new reporting requirements and shareholder oversight as a publicly traded company.

The IPO is expected to substantially increase Musk's personal wealth. As the world's richest person, Musk stands to become the first trillionaire on paper following the public offering, according to reporting from multiple financial outlets. His wealth calculations include existing stakes across his various companies and ventures.

SpaceX's valuation reflects the company's dominant position in the commercial space industry. The firm has secured extensive contracts with the U.S. government for defense and space operations, with NASA relying on SpaceX rockets for the majority of its launches. Beyond rocket launches, SpaceX's Starlink subsidiary has become a major satellite communications operator, generating more than half of the company's revenue. The satellite internet service has also expanded Musk's geopolitical influence, with international clients including the Ukrainian military depending on Starlink for operations.

In recent months, SpaceX has shifted strategic focus toward orbital datacenters and NASA's upcoming moon landing missions. The company acquired Musk's artificial intelligence firm xAI in February, citing plans to develop solar-powered datacenters in space to support AI computational demands. This acquisition valued xAI at $250 billion.

SpaceX is working with prominent international banks, including Barclays, to coordinate orders for the IPO. The company previously filed confidentially with regulators, allowing the Securities and Exchange Commission time to review financial disclosures before public investors could access them.

Financial details about SpaceX have remained closely guarded throughout its private history. The public offering will require the company to disclose previously confidential information about its operations and finances, subjecting it to the transparency standards required of publicly traded companies.

The debut represents a turning point for private space companies, which have historically depended on government contracts and private capital. Trading is scheduled to begin Friday morning, with market observers prepared to monitor how shares perform in their initial day of public trading.
