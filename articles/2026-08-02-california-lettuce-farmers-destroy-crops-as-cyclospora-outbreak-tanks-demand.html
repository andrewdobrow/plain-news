California Lettuce Farmers Destroy Crops as Cyclospora Outbreak Tanks Demand

California lettuce farmers are plowing under fields of healthy crops as demand for fresh greens collapses in response to a nationwide cyclospora outbreak. The parasite, which causes severe gastrointestinal illness, has been detected in at least 34 states, with Michigan reporting more than 5,000 confirmed cases alone.

The outbreak has been traced to Taylor Farms, a major produce supplier. The company recalled shredded iceberg lettuce grown at its facility in Guanajuato, Mexico, distributed across 27 states as recently as Thursday. Taylor Farms products reach major retailers including Costco, Target, and Walmart, as well as organic brands like Earthbound Farm. The FDA has also identified contaminated products served at Taco Bell locations in Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, and West Virginia, with more than 1,600 cases linked to the same product.

The scale of this outbreak far exceeds typical summer cyclospora cases. Last year, the entire US saw only 249 confirmed cases total. This year's numbers represent a dramatic spike, though health officials warn that actual infections may continue rising for at least another two weeks.

The destruction of ready-to-harvest lettuce represents a significant financial loss for growers who invested months of labor and resources into the crops. Fear of contamination has driven shoppers away from commercial lettuce, despite the outbreak being traced to a specific supplier and growing region.

Cyclospora is a parasite that contaminates food through contact with fecal matter, often via contaminated water used for irrigation or washing. The illness it causes includes severe diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea, and fatigue that can last for weeks without treatment.

The economic impact extends beyond individual farms. The crisis has disrupted the supply chain for fresh greens, affecting distributors, retailers, and food service operations. Taco Bell was the first restaurant to announce it would stop serving produce in several affected states. The outbreak arrives during peak growing season for lettuce in California, which produces the majority of fresh greens consumed in the United States.

The recall has created confusion among consumers and retailers. Taylor Farms did not specify the brand names of recalled products or where they were served and sold, complicating efforts to identify contaminated items. The FDA has warned that "additional implicated brands, restaurants, retailers, or distribution channels may be identified as the investigation continues."

This is not Taylor Farms' first serious food safety incident. In 2013, the company was linked to a cyclospora outbreak at restaurants including the Olive Garden and Red Lobster. In October 2024, an E. coli outbreak linked to McDonald's Quarter Pounders was traced to the producer's slivered onions.

Public health authorities continue investigating the source while the industry awaits clarity on which products remain safe.
