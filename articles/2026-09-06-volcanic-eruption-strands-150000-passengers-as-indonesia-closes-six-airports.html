Volcanic eruption strands 150,000 passengers as Indonesia closes six airports

More than 150,000 passengers have been stranded after Indonesian authorities temporarily suspended operations at six airports following a volcanic eruption at Anak Krakatau. Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta International Airport was the first to close Sunday morning after ash from the volcano was detected in nearby airspace. Transport Minister Dudy Purwagandhi said 467 flights have been affected, including routes to Singapore, Doha, Sydney, and Kuala Lumpur.

Anak Krakatau, located in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, began erupting late Friday night with continuous seismic activity, lava fountains, and booming sounds. Two additional eruptions occurred early Sunday. Indonesia's meteorological agency reported ash reached 20,000 feet on the east side toward Java and 50,000 feet west toward Sumatra. Purwagandhi said additional airports were closed after tests detected volcanic ash spreading toward West Java.

The health minister urged residents in affected areas to stay indoors and wear face masks to protect against respiratory, eye, and skin hazards. Several schools canceled activities Saturday after students and residents reported eye irritation from the ash. One resident told local newspaper Kompas the dust was so thick he had to sweep his yard every five minutes and needed eye drops for irritation that began Friday evening.

Authorities confirmed no tsunami warning has been issued. Anak Krakatau, which means Child of Krakatoa, has been sporadically active since emerging from the sea in the early 20th century in the caldera formed by the 1883 Mount Krakatoa eruption. The volcano collapsed in December 2018, triggering a tsunami that killed over 400 people and displaced thousands more on the coastlines of Sumatra and Java. Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, a zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.
