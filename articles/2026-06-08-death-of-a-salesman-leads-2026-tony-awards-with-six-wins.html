Death of a Salesman Leads 2026 Tony Awards With Six Wins

A stripped-back production of Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman dominated the 79th annual Tony Awards, winning six awards including best revival of a play. The reimagined classic claimed more honors than any other production at the ceremony, which was hosted by singer Pink at Radio City Music Hall.

The production made history as the most awarded play revival in Tony Awards history. Director Joe Mantello praised Miller's story as one that "still talks to us through time." Nathan Lane accepted the award on behalf of the cast, calling it a play that "continues to teach us who we are as humans and Americans."

Laurie Metcalf won best featured actress in a play for her role in the revival, marking her third Tony Award. She beat out June Squibb and Aya Cash for the honor. The production has now won multiple times at the Tonys, having previously won for best play in 1949, best revival in 1984, 1999, and 2012.

In other acting categories, Lesley Manville won best leading actress in a play for her performance in Oedipus, a Broadway transfer of the classical drama. She previously won an Olivier Award for the role. "I'm a bit overwhelmed, it was my first time on Broadway so this is such a big deal," Manville said on stage.

John Lithgow made Tony history by becoming the oldest man to win a competitive acting Tony at age 80 for his role as Roald Dahl in the antisemitism drama Giant. He beat out Nathan Lane, Mark Strong, and Daniel Radcliffe for the award. "It's an extraordinarily important play of this moment," Lithgow said on stage, winning his third Tony.

Schmigadoon!, the stage version of the cancelled Apple TV series, won best musical. Producer Lorne Michaels said in his speech, "Sometimes singing, dancing, a lot of jokes and a happy ending is really all you need." The show also picked up three additional awards.

Bess Wohl's Liberation, an intergenerational feminism epic that recently won the Pulitzer Prize for drama, was named best play. "This is the honour of a lifetime," Wohl said. It marks the first time a female playwright has won the award since 2009, with Wohl becoming only the fourth woman to win in this category.

A reimagined version of Ragtime won four Tonys, including best revival of a musical and leading actor and actress in a musical for Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy. It was Henry's first win after four previous nominations. The Lost Boys, an adaptation of the 1980s vampire film, also won four trophies including featured acting awards from a musical.

Pink opened the ceremony by flying in as Peter Pan before leading a star-packed version of "Lady Marmalade" with guests including Lea Michele and Megan Thee Stallion. She called herself "Broadway's biggest fan" and wanted to pay tribute to the hardest-working people in showbiz. The awards came after Broadway recorded nearly 1.91 billion dollars in ticket sales this season.
