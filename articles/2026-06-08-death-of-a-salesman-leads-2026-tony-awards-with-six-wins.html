Death of a Salesman Leads 2026 Tony Awards With Six Wins

Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman dominated the 79th annual Tony Awards, taking home six awards and more prizes than any other production at the ceremony. The revival, which stars Nathan Lane and Laurie Metcalf, captured the best revival of a play award along with several other major honors.

Metcalf won lead actress in a play for her role in the reimagining of Miller's exploration of the American Dream. The production also earned recognition in direction and additional categories. While the show achieved significant success overall, Lane did not win in the acting category.

Pink hosted the 2026 Tony Awards, opening the evening with a Broadway-scale performance inspired by her hit song "Lady Marmalade." The musical number featured numerous performers and set an energetic tone for the night.

Several other productions claimed major awards. The play Liberation won best play, continuing its recognition following a Pulitzer Prize win. Schmigadoon!, the musical, took home the best musical award. Ragtime also secured a top honor in its category.

John Lithgow made history at the ceremony, becoming the oldest man to win a competitive acting Tony at age 80 for his performance in Giant. He surpassed the previous record held by Roy Dotrice, who won at 77 for featured actor in a play in 2000.

The evening celebrated both new works and revivals across multiple categories. Broadway revivals demonstrated particular strength this awards season, with Death of a Salesman leading in total wins. The ceremony highlighted the diversity of theatrical productions that appeared on Broadway stages throughout the year, ranging from classic American plays to new musicals adapted from other media.

Other notable winners at the ceremony included Joshua Henry and Lesley Manville, who received competitive acting Tonys. The night recognized the breadth of talent and creativity currently on display across Broadway.

The Tony Awards represent one of Broadway's most prestigious annual celebrations, honoring excellence in theatre across acting, direction, design, and musical achievement. This year's ceremony demonstrated the continued vitality of Broadway as both a venue for classic revivals and innovative new productions.
