Death of a Salesman Dominates 2026 Tony Awards with Six Wins

Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman became the most awarded play revival in Tony Awards history, winning six awards at the 79th ceremony held at Radio City Music Hall and hosted by singer Pink.

The production claimed the award for best revival of a play. Director Joe Mantello praised Miller's work as a story that "still talks to us through time." Nathan Lane accepted on behalf of the cast, calling it a play that "continues to teach us who we are as humans and Americans." The six wins surpassed the previous record for a play revival, with the production having previously won for best play in 1949 and best revival in 1984, 1999 and 2012.

John Lithgow won best leading actor in a play for his role as Roald Dahl in the antisemitism drama Giant, making history at age 80 as the oldest man ever to win a competitive acting Tony. The previous record was held by Roy Dotrice, who won at 77 in 2000. This marked Lithgow's third Tony Award, coming 53 years after his first win. "It's an extraordinarily important play of this moment," he said on stage.

Lesley Manville took home the leading actress award for the Broadway transfer of Oedipus, defeating Rose Byrne and Carrie Coon. Making her Broadway debut, Manville said: "I'm a bit overwhelmed, it was my first time on Broadway so this is such a big deal."

Laurie Metcalf won best featured actress in a play for her role in Death of a Salesman, capturing her third Tony Award. She beat out June Squibb and Aya Cash for the honor.

Bess Wohl's Liberation was named best play after recently winning the Pulitzer Prize for drama. "This is the honour of a lifetime," Wohl said. It marked the first time a female playwright has won the award since 2009, making Wohl only the fourth woman to have won.

The musical Schmigadoon! won best musical, with producer Lorne Michaels noting: "Sometimes singing, dancing, a lot of jokes and a happy ending is really all you need." The show captured three additional awards.

Ragtime's new take on the classic musical won four Tonys, including best revival of a musical. Joshua Henry and Caissie Levy won for leading actor and leading actress respectively. This marked Henry's first win after four previous nominations. "Every artist in this room, every artist at home, fight, fight, fight to be heard," Henry said.

The Lost Boys, an adaptation of the 1980s vampire film, won four awards including featured actor and featured actress from a musical. Cats: The Jellicle Ball, a queer ballroom reinvention of Andrew Lloyd Webber's classic, won three awards including best direction of a musical.

Alden Ehrenreich won best featured actor in a play for his Broadway debut in a revival of the dating comedy Becky Shaw.

Pink opened the ceremony as Peter Pan, later leading a star-packed performance of her Moulin Rouge hit Lady Marmalade with guests including Lea Michele and Megan Thee Stallion.
