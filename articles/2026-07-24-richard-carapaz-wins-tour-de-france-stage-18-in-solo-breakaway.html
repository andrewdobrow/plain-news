Richard Carapaz wins Tour de France Stage 18 in solo breakaway

Richard Carapaz claimed a commanding victory on Stage 18 of the Tour de France, breaking clear of a six-rider breakaway group on the final climb to Orcières-Merlette. The EF Education-EasyPost cyclist timed his attack to perfection in the closing kilometers, eventually crossing the line ahead of Mauro Schmid of Jayco AlUla and Matteo Jorgenson of Visma-Lease a Bike.

Carapaz's winning time was 4 hours, 26 minutes, and 21 seconds, with Schmid finishing 45 seconds behind in second place. Jorgenson completed the top three, while Valentin Paret-Peintre of Soudal Quick-Step took fourth place, narrowing his deficit in the battle for the polka-dot jersey to just one point behind Tadej Pogacar.

The Ecuadorian rider had featured prominently in recent stages, and finally secured the breakthrough his team sought. After tracking attacks throughout the final climb, Carapaz launched his decisive move with approximately 3.5 kilometers remaining. When Paret-Peintre attempted to follow, other riders including Jorgenson and Schmid were unable to respond, leaving Carapaz to build a winning margin over the final kilometers.

Pogacar retained the yellow jersey despite the stage victory going to Carapaz. The Slovenian leader maintains his overall advantage, positioning him as the favorite heading into the remaining stages. His lead stands at 4 minutes and 32 seconds over Remco Evenepoel in second place, with Isaac Del Toro third overall.

The stage victory marks a significant achievement for Carapaz and EF Education-EasyPost. The team had been seeking a marquee win during this year's Tour, and Carapaz delivered exactly that with his display of climbing strength on the mountain stage.

Concerns emerged within Pogacar's team, however, as a virus swept through UAE Team Emirates XRG. Brandon McNulty abandoned the race with 21 kilometers remaining on Thursday's stage, having struggled with illness throughout the day. This represents another blow to Pogacar's support crew, following Adam Yates's difficult performance earlier in the week and ongoing health issues affecting other team members including Wellens and Vermeersch.

Despite the illness concerns affecting his team, Pogacar's position at the top of the general classification remains secure. The Tour continues with several stages still to come, though the mountain terrain has been particularly challenging. Carapaz's dominant performance serves as a reminder that individual stage victories remain fiercely contested even as the race heads toward its conclusion, with ambitious riders still capable of seizing their opportunities in the high mountains.
