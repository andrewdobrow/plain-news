Justice Department ends Trump anti-weaponization compensation fund

The Trump administration's nearly $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund will not continue, the Justice Department confirmed in a court filing on Friday. Two DOJ attorneys notified a federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia that the compensation program, established to pay individuals claiming they were targets of government overreach, has been terminated.

The filing came as the Justice Department simultaneously asked the court to dismiss a legal challenge to the fund brought by a former federal prosecutor and several other plaintiffs. The lawsuit had questioned how the fund would operate and who would be eligible for payments.

The fund faced swift legal and political opposition since its announcement last month. U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema had temporarily blocked the administration from transferring money from the fund after Democracy Forward filed a lawsuit seeking to dissolve it. The decision to end the program comes just two weeks after it was announced as part of a settlement in the president's lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service over the leak of his tax returns.

The compensation program drew intense scrutiny over concerns about lack of oversight and eligibility criteria. Administration officials indicated that payees could include pardoned January 6 rioters, sparking alarm among Republicans and Democrats alike.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune told reporters he believed the best approach would be for the administration to shut the fund down themselves. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called it "corruption in broad daylight" and vowed that Democrats would force Senate Republicans to vote on blocking any payments.

Former Vice President Mike Pence criticized the possibility of compensating Capitol rioters as "deeply offensive," telling NBC's Meet the Press that he believed most Republicans and Americans shared his concern. Several states, including California, Illinois, New York, and Connecticut, proposed tax measures targeting any fund distributions to their residents.

The program also faced criticism over its lack of transparency. The fund's terms did not require disclosure of who received payments or how much they were given. Critics viewed it as a secretive and loosely controlled mechanism that could be used for political favoritism.

The termination of the fund represents a significant reversal for the Trump administration, which had promoted it as a key initiative addressing what supporters characterized as politicized federal law enforcement. The decision appears to reflect the mounting political pressure from both parties concerned about the fund's structure and potential use.

The Justice Department's filing requesting dismissal of the lawsuit means the court has not yet ruled on whether to accept the government's request to end the case now that the program has been discontinued.
