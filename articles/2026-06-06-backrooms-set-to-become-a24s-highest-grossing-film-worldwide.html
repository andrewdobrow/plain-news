Backrooms Set to Become A24's Highest-Grossing Film Worldwide

The horror film Backrooms is on track to surpass Marty Supreme's $191 million worldwide total, marking a significant achievement for the independent studio A24. The movie's strong performance at the box office demonstrates the studio's ability to produce both critically respected and commercially viable films.

Director Kane Parsons, who is 20 years old, created the film by adapting a horror concept that originated in internet culture. His work on Backrooms represents a notable example of how online creative communities are now influencing major film productions.

The success of Backrooms, combined with another recent A24 release called Obsession, has drawn attention to a broader trend in the entertainment industry. Multiple sources have highlighted how YouTube creators and internet personalities are gaining increased influence in Hollywood. This shift reflects changing patterns in where filmmakers and talent are emerging from, as traditional industry pathways compete with digital platforms for recognition and opportunity.

Industry observers have also noted that Backrooms and similar recent releases signal what some are calling a horror renaissance aimed at younger audiences. This generation of viewers appears to have distinct preferences that differ from previous demographics, and studios are responding by developing content that appeals to their sensibilities and interests.

The film's production itself became part of its public narrative. According to reports, the filming process was so immersive and complex that even crew members found themselves disoriented on set, adding to the behind-the-scenes mystique surrounding the project.

Parsons' achievement at such a young age has generated considerable media interest. Profile pieces and retrospectives about the director have appeared across major entertainment publications, reflecting the novelty and appeal of his rapid rise in the industry.

A24's track record with Backrooms and Obsession suggests that the studio has successfully identified and capitalized on emerging audience tastes. Rather than relying solely on established franchises or star power, the studio backed projects rooted in contemporary internet culture and directed by young, digitally native creators.

This combination of factors—a young director adapting internet phenomena, content aimed at Gen Z audiences, and the involvement of YouTube creators—appears to represent a shift in how major studios are developing and greenighting films. The commercial success of Backrooms validates this approach and may influence how other studios approach their development strategies going forward.

The film's performance suggests that audiences are receptive to horror content that speaks directly to their cultural references and online experiences. As Backrooms moves closer to becoming A24's highest-grossing release, it serves as a marker of this changing landscape in filmmaking and entertainment production.
