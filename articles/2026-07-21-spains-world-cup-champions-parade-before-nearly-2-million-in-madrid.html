Spain's World Cup champions parade before nearly 2 million in Madrid

Spain's men's national soccer team returned home to a massive celebration in Madrid following their World Cup victory. Nearly 2 million people filled the streets of the Spanish capital to honor the champions during an open-top bus parade through the city on Monday.

The team captured the World Cup title after defeating Argentina 1-0 in the final held in New Jersey on Sunday. The players and coaching staff touched down at Madrid's Barajas airport at 2:30 p.m. local time before making their way through the city. The parade route centered around the Plaza de Cibeles in central Madrid, where crowds lined the streets to catch a glimpse of the trophy and celebrate with the squad.

The turnout represented one of the largest public gatherings in recent Spanish history. Many supporters had stayed out late into the night on Monday to welcome the team home, with some still recovering from the excitement of watching the final. The victory festivities drew an estimated crowd of almost 2 million Spaniards who descended on central Madrid.

The team's success came after Luis de la Fuente employed a team-focused approach throughout the tournament. Spain's ability to build a cohesive unit proved successful in securing the championship. The squad had not conceded a goal throughout their World Cup campaign, demonstrating their defensive strength and collective organization.

The squad was afforded official recognition beyond the public celebration. The players and coaching staff met with Spain's royal family and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez to mark the historic achievement. This reflected the national significance placed on the team's accomplishment for the country.

Following the final, Lionel Messi addressed Argentine supporters about their loss. The Argentine captain acknowledged the support from fans but noted that the disappointment would take time to process. For Argentina, the defeat marked a difficult conclusion to their World Cup campaign, despite entering the tournament with high expectations and strong performances in earlier rounds.

The final itself drew significant global attention. Donald Trump, the U.S. president, remained prominently positioned during the trophy ceremony in New Jersey, standing alongside FIFA President Gianni Infantino as Spain celebrated their victory. The moment marked the second consecutive major tournament in which Trump positioned himself centrally during another nation's championship celebration.

The World Cup victory represented a historic achievement for Spanish soccer and demonstrated the effectiveness of their possession-based approach and defensive organization that has defined the team for the past 15 to 20 years. The massive public response in Madrid reflected how deeply the achievement resonated with the Spanish population and cemented the team's place in the country's sporting history.
