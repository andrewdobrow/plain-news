12 people shot near street festival in Toledo, Ohio

Police in Toledo, Ohio are searching for suspects after a shooting near a community festival left at least 12 people wounded on Saturday.

Two of the injured remain in critical condition, according to Toledo deputy police chief Joe Heffernan. Authorities believe the incident involved at least two people firing weapons at each other rather than a random attack on festival attendees.

The shooting occurred near the Old West End Festival. When gunfire erupted, people attending the community event fled the area. Police described the level of violence as notable for the region, with Heffernan characterizing it as "over the top" compared to typical incidents in the city.

Investigators determined that the exchange of gunfire appeared to result from conflict between two individuals. Both suspects left the scene and remain at large. Police have not yet released descriptions of the wanted individuals or announced any arrests.

The victims were transported to nearby hospitals for treatment. Beyond the two in critical condition, the extent of injuries to the other ten people shot has not been detailed.

The incident adds to a broader pattern of gun violence at public gatherings across the country. Community festivals and outdoor events have increasingly become sites of shootings, raising concerns among authorities and residents about safety at such venues.

The investigation remains ongoing as police work to identify witnesses and collect evidence from the scene. Authorities are asking anyone with information about the shooting to contact law enforcement.

The White House was briefly locked down as a precaution following a separate shooting incident near the Washington Monument on Monday involving Secret Service officers, though that incident occurred in a different location and involved different circumstances from the Toledo shooting.
