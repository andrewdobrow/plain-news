Twelve wounded in shooting near Toledo street festival, suspects sought

Police are continuing their search for at least two suspects after a shooting near the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio left twelve people wounded on Saturday evening. The search entered its second day Sunday with no arrests made.

The gunfire erupted just after 5:30 p.m. near the historic neighborhood festival, which features live music and architectural home tours. Two victims remain in critical condition, according to Toledo police deputy chief Joseph Heffernan. The ages of those injured ranged from 14 to 61, with most victims in their early twenties.

Heffernan stated that the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the violence occurred. The incident took place in front of several hundred festival attendees. Despite the large police presence at the event, including extra officers and off-duty personnel working security, authorities have not apprehended any suspects.

"It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years," Heffernan said.

Law enforcement officials have appealed to the public for assistance in identifying the shooters. Authorities have asked witnesses to provide any photos or videos taken on their phones that might offer leads in the investigation.

Video footage captured the immediate aftermath of the shooting, showing festival attendees scrambling for cover as gunfire erupted. Additional video posted online showed a wounded young man walking down the street with blood visible from his injury before sitting down.

Festival organizers announced Sunday that remaining events would be cancelled following discussions with Toledo civic and police authorities. In a statement, organizers expressed their heartbreak over the incident and acknowledged the broader concern about violence at community celebrations.

"Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood," the statement said.

Ohio Governor Mike DeWine responded to the shooting with concern for public safety. "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence," DeWine said in a statement.

Fire Chief Allison Armstrong noted that closed roads and traffic congestion as people fled the festival created challenges in transporting victims to area hospitals.

According to the Gun Violence Archive, the Toledo shooting was one of more than 170 mass shootings in the United States so far this year. The archive defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more people are wounded or killed.

The investigation remains active as authorities work to locate the suspects.
