12 wounded in shooting near Toledo street festival, suspects still at large

At least 12 people were wounded in a shooting near the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio on Saturday evening, with law enforcement still searching for multiple suspects on Sunday.

The gunfire erupted just after 5:30 p.m. near the historic neighborhood festival, an annual event featuring live music and architectural home tours. Several hundred people were attending at the time. Toledo police deputy chief Joseph Heffernan said the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the violence occurred.

Two victims were reported in critical condition. The ages of those injured ranged from 14 to 61, with most in their early 20s. Victims were transported to area hospitals for treatment.

The search for suspects entered its second day on Sunday with no arrests announced. Law enforcement officials asked festival attendees to come forward with any photos or videos from their phones that might provide leads. Police said extra officers had been assigned to the festival, and a substantial number of off-duty officers were working the event itself.

"It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years," Heffernan said.

Festival organizers canceled all remaining events on Sunday following the shooting. In a statement, they expressed heartbreak over the injuries and said continuing the festival would be neither compassionate nor responsible given the circumstances.

"Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood," organizers said.

The Toledo fire chief noted that transporting victims to hospitals proved difficult because closed roads and traffic from people leaving the festival hindered emergency response.

Ohio governor Mike DeWine released a statement expressing deep concern about the incident. "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence," he said.

U.S. Senator Jon Husted also commented on the shooting, calling the violence "senseless" and thanking law enforcement for their quick response and ongoing efforts to locate suspects.

According to the Gun Violence Archive, the Toledo shooting was one of more than 170 mass shootings in the United States so far this year. The archive defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more people are wounded or killed.

The incident comes as the nation continues to grapple with recurring gun violence at public gatherings. Police are expected to provide additional details as the investigation progresses.
