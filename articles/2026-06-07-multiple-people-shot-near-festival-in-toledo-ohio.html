At Least 12 People Shot at Street Festival in Toledo, Ohio

Police are continuing their search for at least two gunmen who opened fire at the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio on Saturday evening, wounding 12 people. Festival organizers have cancelled Sunday's planned events as the investigation enters its second day.

The shooting erupted just after 5:30 p.m. near the Old West End festival, an annual community gathering featuring live music and architectural home tours. Toledo Police Deputy Chief Joseph Heffernan said the shooters were "probably shooting at each other" when the gunfire broke out. Several hundred people were attending the festival at the time.

Two victims are reported to be in critical condition. The ages of those shot ranged from 14 to 61 years old, with most in their early 20s. The fire chief reported that it was difficult to transport victims to area hospitals because closed roads and traffic from people leaving the festival hampered emergency response efforts.

With no suspects yet in custody, law enforcement officials have asked festival attendees to come forward with any photos or videos from their phones that might provide leads. Police noted that extra officers had been assigned to the festival, and a large number of off-duty officers were working the event itself.

Heffernan expressed frustration about the violence disrupting a longstanding community tradition. "It's just a shame when a few people, for whatever reasons going through their head, decide to disrupt something that has been a beloved community event for many, many, many years," he said.

Festival organizers released a statement expressing heartbreak over the incident. "Too often we turn on the news and learn of a celebration somewhere that turns into a tragedy. Now, that news comes from our own neighborhood," the statement read. After consulting with Toledo civic and police authorities, organizers determined that continuing the festival would not be "compassionate, responsible or possible."

The incident drew responses from state officials. Ohio Governor Mike DeWine said in a statement, "Summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence." State Senator Jon Husted called the violence "senseless" and thanked law enforcement for acting quickly and working to locate suspects.

This shooting is among more than 170 mass shooting incidents that have occurred in the United States so far this year, according to the Gun Violence Archive, which defines mass shootings as incidents in which four or more victims are wounded or killed. The Toledo incident reflects the ongoing challenge of gun violence at public gatherings across the country.
