Apple unveils Siri AI with new conversational features at WWDC 2026

Apple announced a major overhaul of its voice assistant at its annual Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday. The redesigned tool, now called Siri AI, represents a significant shift in how the assistant works. Rather than functioning as a web-based question-and-answer tool, the new Siri will operate more like AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini, integrating directly with Apple Intelligence across the company's ecosystem.

The announcement addresses years of user frustration with Siri's limitations and multiple delays in delivering promised improvements. Apple first announced an updated Siri in 2024 but postponed its release multiple times. The company even settled a 250 million dollar class-action lawsuit in May after consumers alleged that Apple had falsely advertised Siri's AI capabilities. Some affected customers received payouts of up to 95 dollars.

The new Siri will be powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a billion-dollar partnership between the two companies. According to Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice president of Siri engineering, the assistant has been fundamentally rebuilt with generative AI at its core. The tool will have its own dedicated app and will be more conversational, pulling from Apple's native app ecosystem on both mobile and laptop devices to help with daily planning, information retrieval, and navigation.

In a demonstration, Rockwell showed how the new Siri can handle complex tasks within a single interface. He displayed a sunset beach photo and asked Siri to identify the location. Once identified, he requested his friend's address, then had Siri provide directions to Santa Cruz, California with a stop at his friend's house, all completed without switching between multiple apps.

The assistant will initially launch in English only this fall. It will be capable of proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events, and using the camera to retrieve information like nutrition data by pointing at food. Apple is also integrating AI features into Safari, messaging, the Home app, camera tools, and photo editing capabilities, all developed in collaboration with Google.

The event marked CEO Tim Cook's final WWDC with the company. Cook, 65, is stepping down from his position in the fall after leading Apple for 15 years and transforming it into one of the world's most profitable publicly traded companies.

Apple executives acknowledged that the company had not invested as heavily or quickly in AI development compared to competitors. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, noted that some companies appear to be pursuing AI without clear regard for the people it ultimately serves.

The announcement comes as Apple faces regulatory pressure in the European Union. The company stated it is delaying Siri AI's release for iPhone users in the EU, citing regulators' unwillingness to engage with the company.
