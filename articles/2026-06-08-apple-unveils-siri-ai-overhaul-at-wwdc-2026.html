Apple updates Siri with voice activity detection and new foundation models

Apple unveiled a substantially redesigned Siri at its annual developer conference, marking a major shift in the voice assistant's capabilities. The new Siri, rebranded as "Siri AI," integrates with Apple Intelligence and will be widely available in the fall.

The revamped assistant represents a fundamental reconception of how Siri functions. Rather than operating as a web-based question-and-answer tool, the updated version mimics modern AI chatbots like ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, demonstrated the assistant's new conversational abilities during the conference, showing how it can work across Apple's native app ecosystem to handle tasks like planning, navigation and information retrieval.

A key advancement enables Siri to operate within a single interface rather than shuffling between multiple apps. In a demonstration, Rockwell showed Siri identifying a beach location from a photo, retrieving a friend's address from the contact app, and providing directions to the beach with a stop at the friend's house, all without leaving the Siri interface.

The new Siri will feature its own dedicated app and can handle expanded functionality including proofreading, shopping, calendar management and extracting information from images using the camera. Initially, English will be the only supported language.

Apple achieved these improvements through a partnership with Google, leveraging Google's Gemini AI model. According to Dipanjan Chatterjee, a principal analyst at Forrester, this represents a significant moment for Apple. He noted that "a fundamentally re-architected Siri inside iOS 27 with a little help from Google's Gemini would represent more than an update – it would signal Apple's readiness to make AI the primary interface layer across its ecosystem."

The update comes after years of delays and false starts with Siri improvements. Apple had previously postponed releasing an updated version first announced in 2024, as its AI development lagged competitors. The company faced additional pressure from a 250 million dollar settlement in May following a class-action lawsuit alleging false advertising of Siri's AI capabilities.

Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, acknowledged during the conference that some companies appear to be "racing forward, seemingly pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people." This framing positions Apple's approach as more deliberately integrated into existing products rather than pursuing AI as a standalone technology.

Beyond Siri, Apple announced new AI features across Safari, messaging and the Home app, alongside camera and photo tools developed with Google. The company also introduced expanded child safety features allowing parents greater control over content access and app usage through an updated setup assistant and monitoring tools.
