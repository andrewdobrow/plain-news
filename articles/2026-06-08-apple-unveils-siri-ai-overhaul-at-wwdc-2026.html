Apple updates Siri with AI features designed to reduce interruptions

Apple has unveiled a fundamentally redesigned Siri at its Worldwide Developers Conference, integrating the voice assistant with its new Apple Intelligence platform. The overhaul represents a major shift in Apple's approach to artificial intelligence after years of the company falling behind competitors in AI development.

The new Siri, branded as "Siri AI," will operate more like modern AI chatbots such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini rather than functioning as a traditional question-and-answer tool. Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, explained during the conference that the company had completely rebuilt the assistant with generative AI at its core. The update will launch this fall across iOS, iPadOS, and macOS.

A key aspect of the redesign involves making Siri more conversational and capable of drawing information from Apple's native app ecosystem. During a demonstration, Rockwell showed how Siri could identify a beach location from a photo, retrieve a friend's address, and provide directions to the beach with a stop at the friend's house, all within the Siri interface rather than jumping between multiple applications.

The revamped Siri will include capabilities for proofreading, shopping, adding calendar events, and retrieving information like nutrition data through the camera. Initially, the tool will be available only in English.

The update is powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a partnership between the two technology companies. This collaboration marks a significant step for Apple, which has historically developed its own AI systems but recognized the need to accelerate its AI capabilities. Dipanjan Chatterjee, a principal analyst at research firm Forrester, noted that integrating Siri as the primary interface across Apple's ecosystem would signal the company's readiness to prioritize AI in its products.

Apple's approach emphasizes on-device processing and privacy protections, distinguishing its strategy from competitors who have pursued broader generative AI deployment. The company has also announced partnerships with NVIDIA to expand its Private Cloud Compute infrastructure, which will handle complex requests requiring additional processing power while maintaining user privacy through confidential computing technology.

The Siri redesign comes after Apple repeatedly postponed updates to the assistant, with the previous announcement made in 2024. The company also faced legal challenges related to earlier Siri capabilities, agreeing to pay $250 million in May to settle a class-action lawsuit alleging false advertising of Siri's AI abilities.

Beyond Siri, Apple introduced new child safety features at the conference, including tools that monitor messages for harmful content and give parents greater control over app access. The company also integrated AI tools into Safari, messaging services, and the Home app, along with camera and photo features developed in collaboration with Google.
