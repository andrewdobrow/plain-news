Apple redesigns Siri to reduce unnecessary responses

Apple unveiled a fundamentally rebuilt version of Siri at its Worldwide Developers Conference, marking a major shift in how the voice assistant operates. The new system, powered by Google's Gemini AI model through a billion-dollar partnership, integrates with Apple Intelligence and will roll out to consumers in the fall.

Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice-president of Siri engineering, demonstrated how the redesigned assistant has become more conversational and capable of handling complex tasks across Apple's native app ecosystem. In one demonstration, Rockwell showed Siri identifying a beach location from a photo, retrieving a friend's address, and providing directions with a planned stop, all within a single interface rather than jumping between multiple apps.

A key improvement in the new Siri is its ability to determine when responses are necessary. Rather than providing verbose answers to every query, the assistant delivers more concise replies, avoiding unnecessary chatter that plagued earlier versions. This represents a departure from many AI chatbots that tend toward wordiness and excessive politeness.

The expanded capabilities will eventually include proofreading, shopping assistance, calendar management, and image analysis for tasks like retrieving nutritional information from photos of food. Initially, the tool will only support English. A dedicated Siri app will house these features alongside integration into Safari, text messaging, and the Home app.

Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice-president of software engineering, acknowledged the company's earlier lag in the AI race, noting that some competitors appear to be "pursuing AI for the sake of AI, without clear regard for the people that it's ultimately meant to serve." Apple has repeatedly delayed Siri updates since first announcing improvements in 2024, and the company faced legal consequences when it agreed to pay 250 million dollars in May to settle a class-action lawsuit alleging false advertising of Siri's capabilities.

Alongside the Siri overhaul, Apple announced enhanced child safety features. The company introduced new tools allowing parents to restrict content, control who children communicate with, and limit app access. An updated setup assistant helps parents gradually expand permissions as children age. New monitoring capabilities can blur violent or explicit messages, and parents can set daily time allowances for specific apps using their own preferences or recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics.

These safety features arrived amid increased scrutiny over image manipulation apps and government pressure to protect minors online. UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer urged Apple and Google to activate built-in features preventing children from accessing sexually explicit content.

The changes reflect Apple's strategy of positioning privacy and thoughtful AI integration as differentiators against competitors who rely more heavily on cloud-based processing. The new Siri will reach developers through beta versions and consumers later this year.
