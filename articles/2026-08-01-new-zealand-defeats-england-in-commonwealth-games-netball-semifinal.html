New Zealand defeats England in Commonwealth Games netball semifinal

England's bid to regain the Commonwealth Games netball title has ended following a 61-54 semifinal defeat to New Zealand in Glasgow. While the scoreline suggests a competitive contest, the result was far more decisive than the seven-point margin indicates, with the Silver Ferns dominating throughout the match.

The Roses, who won gold at the Gold Coast in 2018, will now compete for the bronze medal on Sunday morning rather than challenging for the title. England struggled to contain New Zealand's attack, particularly goal shooter Grace Nweke, who proved virtually unstoppable for much of the contest.

Nweke finished with 52 goals from 57 attempts, establishing control early in the match. After a nervous opening, New Zealand built a 16-11 lead by the end of the first quarter and quickly extended their advantage to nine points. Nweke was particularly clinical in the second quarter, scoring 23 consecutive goals before missing just before halftime. At that point, England had managed to narrow the gap to 29-24, offering some hope of a comeback similar to their earlier pool stage victory against South Africa.

However, changes introduced by England coach Anna Stembridge in the third quarter, including bringing on Eleanor Cardwell to provide additional physical presence, failed to alter the momentum. New Zealand's lead stretched to nine points by the end of the third quarter and reached 12 early in the fourth. Although England scored some late points, the result was decided well before the final whistle.

New Zealand reached the final despite missing defender Catherine Hall, who suffered a torn anterior cruciate ligament in an earlier match against Wales. Goal attack Georgia Heffernan played through an ankle injury sustained in the same fixture, contributing nine goals from 10 attempts and providing 15 goal assists.

The Silver Ferns will now face the winner of Saturday's other semifinal between Australia and Jamaica as they pursue their first Commonwealth Games gold medal since 2010. England will have the opportunity to finish on the podium in the third-place contest, providing some consolation after falling short of their primary objective.

The defeat represents a disappointing outcome for England, who had performed solidly throughout the preliminary rounds. Their inability to contain Nweke proved decisive, highlighting the quality of New Zealand's squad despite the absence of Hall.
