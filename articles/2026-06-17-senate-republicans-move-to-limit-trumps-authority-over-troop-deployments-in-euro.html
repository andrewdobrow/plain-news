Senate Republicans near deal to avert shutdown and limit White House budget authority

Senate Republicans have reached an agreement with Democrats to prevent a government shutdown and avoid a partial lapse in funding set to begin over the weekend. The deal represents a significant compromise after weeks of negotiation over spending priorities and immigration enforcement practices.

Under the agreement, Senate leadership will split the funding package by separating the Department of Homeland Security bill from other appropriations measures. The DHS will receive funding for two weeks at current levels, while the remaining government agencies would be funded through September. This approach allows lawmakers more time to negotiate over contentious immigration enforcement issues that Democrats have raised.

The deal emerged after a key Senate vote failed Thursday to advance a comprehensive spending package. All 47 Democratic senators voted against the measure, joined by seven Republican senators, preventing the bill from reaching the 60-vote threshold needed to overcome a filibuster. Senate Majority Leader John Thune voted no as well so he could bring the measure back for future consideration.

Democrats had refused to approve DHS funding without reforms to federal immigration enforcement following two deaths of U.S. citizens in Minneapolis involving ICE agents. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called for legislation to "rein ICE in" and demanded the DHS bill be set aside for further work. Schumer noted that Democrats were prepared to pass five bipartisan funding bills covering 96 percent of the federal government but needed substantial revisions to the DHS measure.

The deaths of residents Alex Pretti and Renee Good prompted Democrats to seek specific changes to agent conduct, including ending mask-wearing by federal agents, implementing a code of conduct, and establishing independent investigations into violations. Tom Homan, the Trump administration's border czar, acknowledged that "certain improvements could and should be made" but deferred specifics to Congress.

President Trump endorsed the spending agreement in a statement, calling it a bipartisan effort to fund most of the government while extending DHS funding. He urged both parties to vote yes on the revised package.

The House of Representatives approved the original six-bill package last week but remains out of session. Speaker Mike Johnson initially opposed splitting the funding package but indicated the House would move quickly if necessary. Johnson acknowledged a short shutdown could occur over the weekend before the House returns Monday.

A DHS funding lapse is unlikely to halt ICE deportation operations significantly, as the agency received tens of billions in funding under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act passed last year. The White House could also direct employees to continue working through a shutdown.

The agreement reflects compromise between both parties facing pressure to maintain government operations while addressing concerns about immigration enforcement practices under the Trump administration.
