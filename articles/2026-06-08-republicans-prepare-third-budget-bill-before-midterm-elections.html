House Republicans propose fourth budget reconciliation bill as third stalls

House Republican leaders have begun discussing the possibility of a fourth budget reconciliation bill, even as their third such measure faces significant uncertainty on its legislative path. The conversation about using this special parliamentary process again reflects growing dissatisfaction among many House Republicans with the current proposal under consideration.

Budget reconciliation bills allow legislation related to federal spending and revenues to pass with only Republican votes, bypassing the Senate filibuster and requiring just a simple majority. However, these bills must follow strict budgetary rules, and party unity becomes critical when Republicans hold narrow margins of control.

The uncertainty surrounding the current healthcare debate has prompted this discussion. With enhanced tax credits for Americans purchasing coverage through Affordable Care Act marketplaces set to expire at the end of the year, House Speaker Mike Johnson unveiled a Republican alternative on Friday. The proposal focuses on Republican priorities rather than extending the enhanced subsidies that help lower premiums for millions of Americans.

Johnson's package, described as more than 100 pages, emphasizes Republican proposals to expand access to association health plans and increase oversight of pharmacy benefit managers. Association health plans would allow small businesses and self-employed individuals to band together to purchase coverage, though critics argue such plans offer skimpier coverage than required under the Affordable Care Act. The proposal also includes requirements for more data collection from pharmacy benefit managers as a way to control drug costs.

The Republican plan does mention cost-sharing reductions for some lower-income people using ACA coverage, but these would not take effect until January 2027. Notably, the package does not extend the enhanced tax credit that currently helps millions of Americans afford marketplace insurance. That credit, implemented during the Biden administration in response to the pandemic, expires December 31, potentially doubling or more the out-of-pocket premiums for most families in the program.

President Donald Trump said Friday that Republicans will develop a better plan than the ACA, but he provided few specifics beyond his proposal to provide Americans with stipends to help purchase insurance rather than extending existing tax credits.

The Republican healthcare approach has put vulnerable House Republicans representing competitive districts in a difficult position. Some centrist Republican lawmakers have aligned with Democrats to push their own proposals for continuing the tax credits, at least temporarily. These lawmakers are co-sponsoring bills and signing discharge petitions that could force floor votes if they gather sufficient signatures.

Two discharge petitions targeting ACA subsidies had bipartisan support as of Friday. One petition by Pennsylvania Republican Brian Fitzpatrick proposed a two-year subsidy extension with fraud prevention measures. Another from New Jersey Democrat Josh Gottheimer supported a simpler one-year extension with new income caps. Both petitions had enough Republican backing to likely succeed if Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries encouraged his caucus to participate.
