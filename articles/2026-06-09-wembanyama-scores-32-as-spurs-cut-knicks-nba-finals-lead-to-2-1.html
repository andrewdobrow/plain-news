Wembanyama scores 32 as Spurs cut Knicks' NBA Finals lead to 2-1

Victor Wembanyama delivered a standout performance to lead the San Antonio Spurs past the New York Knicks 115-111 at Madison Square Garden, trimming the Knicks' NBA Finals lead to 2-1. The French center finished with 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks in a must-win game for San Antonio.

The Spurs snapped New York's 13-game postseason winning streak in a tightly contested matchup that went down to the final seconds. Stephon Castle contributed 23 points and made two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining in the third quarter to help secure the victory.

For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson scored 32 points while OG Anunoby added 28. However, their supporting cast struggled down the stretch. Karl-Anthony Towns managed just 11 points and eight rebounds after recording back-to-back double-doubles in San Antonio. The rest of New York's roster went cold in the fourth quarter, combining to shoot just 3 for 20 from the field.

Wembanyama had failed to fully break through in the first two games of the series before Monday night's performance. After Game 2, he threw an errant pass that bounced off a teammate's back and missed a potential game-winner. Reflecting on his turnaround, Wembanyama said he needed time to recover mentally. "Really tried to relax after Game 2. The playoffs, it's like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water," he explained. He started strong on Monday, scoring nine points in his first nine minutes.

The atmosphere at Madison Square Garden was dominated by the spectacle surrounding the game rather than the basketball itself. President Donald Trump attended as a guest of team owner James Dolan and received heavy boos when shown on the jumbotron during the national anthem. The crowd also included Mayor Zohran Mamdani and celebrities including Spike Lee, Timothée Chalamet, Ben Stiller, Tina Fey, Tracy Morgan and Larry David.

San Antonio jumped to an 11-point first-quarter lead before New York responded with 42 points in the second quarter to take a 64-57 halftime advantage. The third quarter remained competitive with neither team able to pull ahead by more than five points.

The Knicks' frustration boiled over in the fourth quarter regarding officiating decisions. New York was called for three fouls in the first 64 seconds of the period and took just eight free throws in the second half compared to San Antonio's 24. The Spurs benefited from 21 points off the Knicks' 13 turnovers.

Brunson kept the Knicks close with 12 fourth-quarter points, but his teammates couldn't provide enough support. Game 4 is scheduled for Wednesday in New York. Brunson acknowledged the loss but remained focused on improvement. "We've done our best to try to learn from wins over the past couple weeks. But now we have to learn from a loss," he said.
