Trump delivers address marking America's 250th birthday

President Donald Trump delivered a late-night speech in Washington DC on Saturday to commemorate the United States' 250th anniversary. The address, which began at 11:15 p.m., incorporated Trump's political messaging alongside recognition of American history and military service members.

Trump characterized the country as "a nation of winners" and stated that "today our country is winning again." He claimed the US was "just getting started" and vowed to take the nation "to new levels," invoking the concept of a "golden age of America." The president highlighted what he described as the country's "unmatched achievement and unlimited potential."

The celebrations were significantly disrupted by severe weather. An extreme heatwave swept across the US East Coast, with temperatures exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit in Washington. By 8 p.m. on Saturday, emergency services had treated 51 people for heat-related issues, with 12 requiring hospitalization. A passing thunderstorm forced evacuators to clear the event site for more than two hours due to lightning and rain concerns. Despite the evacuation, crowds returned to witness the speech and subsequent fireworks display.

The festivities featured what organizers billed as the largest fireworks display in world history. Trump introduced military veterans and the crew of the Artemis II lunar mission while discussing moments from American history. He described the country as "the hope, the promise, the light and the glory" of the world.

Throughout the weekend's celebrations, Trump delivered multiple addresses with partisan political content. On Friday evening at Mount Rushmore in South Dakota, he launched an attack on what he termed the "communist menace" in America, characterizing communism supporters as enemies of American independence. He reiterated these themes during his Washington speech, stating that communism "never worked, and it never will work."

The celebrations were marred by additional complications. The National Mall's reflecting pool, which Trump had ordered renovated for $14.7 million to turn "American Flag blue," remained green due to an algae bloom. Trump and his officials blamed vandals for the project's failure. The Independence Day parade in Washington was canceled due to extreme heat, as was a parade in Philadelphia, where the Declaration of Independence was signed in 1776.

Trump's allies have faced accusations of using the anniversary celebrations to intensify rather than bridge the country's divisions, transforming a traditionally nonpartisan patriotic moment into a vehicle for political messaging. His living predecessors were notably absent from the main events in Washington.

Despite the weather challenges, Trump pointed to the crowd's return after evacuation as an example of American resilience. "You heard it was over," he told attendees. "And what happened? You came back."
