Ukraine launches drone strikes on St. Petersburg after Putin rejects meeting

Ukraine conducted a large-scale drone attack on Russian military facilities in St. Petersburg, targeting a naval base and other installations in one of the most significant strikes on the city since the war began. The attack took place hours after Russian President Vladimir Putin concluded an appearance at an economic forum in the city and publicly rejected a proposed meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Multiple drones struck targets near St. Petersburg on the final day of a major Russian investment forum that Putin had been attending. The strikes hit a naval base used by the Russian fleet and other military infrastructure in the area. This marks the second long-range drone attack on the city in recent days, representing an escalation in Ukraine's campaign to strike deep inside Russian territory.

The timing of the attack followed Putin's dismissal of potential peace talks. Speaking at the economic forum in St. Petersburg, Putin rejected the possibility of meeting with Zelenskyy, effectively shutting down recent diplomatic overtures. Ukrainian officials have not officially commented on the strikes, maintaining their typical practice of neither confirming nor denying operations inside Russian territory.

The attack on St. Petersburg, Russia's second-largest city and a site of major symbolic importance, demonstrates Ukraine's expanding capability to conduct operations far beyond the front lines of the conflict. The city sits hundreds of miles from the Ukrainian border, requiring sophisticated planning and technology to reach with drone strikes. Military analysts note that such attacks serve both strategic purposes in degrading Russian military capabilities and psychological ones in bringing the war's impact to Russian population centers.

The escalation comes at a critical moment in the ongoing conflict, with both sides seeking military advantages while diplomatic channels appear increasingly strained. Russia has condemned Ukrainian strikes on its territory as terrorism, while Ukraine argues that targeting Russian military infrastructure is a legitimate response to Russia's invasion and continued attacks on Ukrainian cities and civilian areas.
