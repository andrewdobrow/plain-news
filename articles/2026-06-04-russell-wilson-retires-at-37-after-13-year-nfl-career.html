Russell Wilson retires at 37 after 13-year NFL career

Russell Wilson, the veteran quarterback who led the Seattle Seahawks to their first Super Bowl championship in 2014, has retired from professional football at age 37. The announcement ends a 13-year career that established Wilson as one of the most successful quarterbacks of his generation.

Wilson spent the first decade of his career with the Seahawks after being drafted in the third round of the 2012 NFL Draft. He quickly became the face of the franchise, leading Seattle to back-to-back Super Bowl appearances in the 2013 and 2014 seasons. The Seahawks defeated the Denver Broncos 43-8 in Super Bowl XLVIII, securing the franchise's first championship. Wilson's dual-threat ability as both a passer and scrambler revolutionized expectations for quarterback play in the modern NFL.

During his time in Seattle, Wilson earned nine Pro Bowl selections and was known for his ability to orchestrate dramatic fourth-quarter comebacks. His leadership helped establish one of the most dominant stretches in Seahawks history, with the team consistently competing for playoff berths throughout his tenure. After leaving Seattle, Wilson played for other NFL teams in the latter portion of his career.

Wilson's retirement comes at a time when the NFL continues to see generational shifts at the quarterback position. At 37, he joins a wave of veteran signal-callers from the 2010s era who have stepped away from the game in recent years. His career statistics and championship pedigree make him a likely candidate for future Hall of Fame consideration.

The retirement marks the end of an era for Seahawks fans and NFL observers who watched Wilson transform from a overlooked third-round pick into one of the league's most recognizable stars. His impact on the Seahawks franchise and his contributions to changing how teams evaluate mobile quarterbacks will be part of his lasting legacy in professional football.
