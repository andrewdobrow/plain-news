Spain defeats Argentina in extra time to win World Cup final

Spain claimed their second World Cup title after defeating Argentina 1-0 in extra time at the New York New Jersey Stadium. Barcelona winger Ferran Torres scored the decisive goal in the 106th minute, breaking through Argentina's stubborn defensive resistance to secure victory in a match that was dominated by the Spanish side throughout.

The final was marked by Argentina's cynical approach to the game. Chelsea midfielder Enzo Fernández was sent off for a second yellow card late in the 90 minutes, leaving Argentina to face the remainder of extra time with ten players. The match grew increasingly contentious as it progressed, culminating in a mass confrontation after the final whistle when Leandro Paredes pushed Gavi and Eric García to the ground, earning the Boca Juniors midfielder a red card.

Argentina's goalkeeper Emi Martínez kept his team competitive throughout the contest with a series of outstanding saves, preventing Spain from extending their lead. Spain had 20 shots on goal compared to Argentina's two, with Argentina's attempts not coming until the 115th minute when Lionel Messi struck the ball at defender Mikel Merino.

Messi made his final World Cup appearance in the match, later collecting his runners-up medal while visibly emotional. The entire stadium applauded the Argentine star as he received his award in tears. In response to Spain's victory, Argentina's squad turned their backs to face their own supporters as the Spanish players received their medals.

Donald Trump attended the trophy presentation alongside FIFA President Gianni Infantino. He received boos from the crowd when he walked out for the ceremony and appeared reluctant to step away from the players' celebrations after handing over the trophy to Spain's captain Rodri. King Felipe of Spain watched from Trump's private box alongside world leaders including the Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum.

This World Cup victory adds to Spain's European Championship triumph from two years earlier, making them only the second nation to hold both titles simultaneously since their previous World Cup win in 2010.

The final itself was overshadowed by extensive pre-match entertainment and a 27-minute-20-second halftime show, marking the first time pop stars were permitted to perform on the pitch during a World Cup final. The halftime performers included Madonna, Justin Bieber, and other international acts.

Spain's victory comes after they dismantled France in the semi-finals. Argentina manager Lionel Scaloni became emotional during his post-match news conference, leaving the event in tears while discussing his future with the national team.
