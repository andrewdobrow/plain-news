US Strikes Iranian Radar Sites After Intercepting Drones Fired at Bahrain and Kuwait

The United States military shot down four Iranian drones headed toward the Gulf and responded by striking Iranian coastal surveillance radar installations, prompting Iran to launch a fresh wave of attacks hours later. The exchange marks another serious test of the fragile ceasefire between the two countries.

On Saturday, air raid sirens sounded across Bahrain as residents were instructed to move to safe locations. Kuwait's military reported intercepting drones and missiles launched at the country. The Kuwaiti foreign ministry condemned the attacks as a "serious escalation" and "flagrant violation of its sovereignty," stating it reserved the right to defend itself.

US Central Command said the Iranian attack drones "posed an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic." The American strikes on radar installations at Sirik and Qeshm Island were intended to "defend against further attacks."

The Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it had targeted American bases in the region, specifically stating it was aiming at the Ali al-Salem airbase in Bahrain, where the US Navy's 5th Fleet is based. Central Command reported that Iran fired seven ballistic missiles toward Kuwait and Bahrain, with six intercepted and one failing to reach its target. "There are currently no reports of harm to US personnel, and Iranian claims of damaging US 5th fleet headquarters in Bahrain are false," the command said.

This latest confrontation continues a pattern of back-and-forth military actions that have strained ceasefire negotiations. US and Iranian representatives have been working for weeks to extend the existing ceasefire by 60 days and begin new talks on Iran's nuclear program, but both sides have demanded changes to any deal.

President Trump said on Friday that "the situation with Iran seems to be going quite well" and predicted a quick resolution. "We're going to come out of Iran very quickly and it's going to be very strong one way or the other, whether it's a piece of paper or the very tough way," he said at an event in Wisconsin.

However, when asked why negotiations were taking time, Trump told NBC News it was "a very hard thing" for Iran. He also stated that Iran still possessed approximately 21 to 22 percent of its missile stockpile, slightly higher than the 18 percent figure he had cited the previous month.

The fighting threatens broader regional stability. Israel has also been striking targets in Lebanon despite a ceasefire deal brokered by the US this week. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun condemned an Israeli airstrike that killed nine people, including three army soldiers, calling it a "flagrant violation" of sovereignty. Israel said the vehicle was moving suspiciously toward its forces in an area where the Iranian-backed militant group Hezbollah operates.

Iran has demanded that any lasting truce extend to include Lebanon, complicating efforts to end the broader conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial global shipping corridor.
