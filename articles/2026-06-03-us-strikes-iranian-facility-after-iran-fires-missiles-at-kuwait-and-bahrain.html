US strikes Iranian radar sites after intercepting drones fired at Bahrain and Kuwait

US and Iran Exchange Fire in Latest Test of Fragile Ceasefire

The United States and Iran exchanged military strikes in the Gulf on Saturday, marking another serious challenge to the tenuous ceasefire between the two countries. Air raid sirens sounded in Bahrain and Kuwait as the confrontation unfolded, with both nations' militaries scrambling to intercept incoming threats.

The US military said it shot down four Iranian attack drones launched toward the Strait of Hormuz and responded by striking Iranian coastal surveillance radar sites on the islands of Sirik and Qeshm Island. US Central Command stated the drones "posed an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic," while the radar strikes were intended to "defend against further attacks."

Hours later, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps retaliated, firing seven ballistic missiles toward Kuwait and Bahrain. The US military said six missiles were intercepted and one failed to reach its target. "There are currently no reports of harm to US personnel," Central Command stated, adding that Iranian claims of damaging US 5th Fleet headquarters in Bahrain were false.

The IRGC said it had targeted enemy bases in the Gulf following the American strikes. Kuwait's military confirmed it was intercepting drones and missiles launched at the country, while Bahrain issued air raid alerts instructing residents to move to safe locations.

Kuwait's foreign ministry condemned the attacks as a "serious escalation" and "flagrant violation of its sovereignty," saying the country reserved the right to defend itself. The latest exchange represents the continuing pattern of tit-for-tat military incidents that have strained diplomatic efforts.

The confrontation comes as the Trump administration pressures both countries to reach a permanent agreement. US and Iranian negotiators have spent weeks trying to extend the ceasefire by 60 days and begin new talks on Iran's nuclear program, but neither side appears ready to compromise. An Iranian official said the negotiations have reached a deadlock.

Trump told reporters on Friday that the situation with Iran "seems to be going quite well," but acknowledged the difficulty of negotiations. "It's a very hard thing for Iran," he said, adding that Iran had things it never expected to have to do.

The instability has extended beyond the US-Iran conflict. Fighting in Lebanon, where Israeli forces have seized large areas of the south, also threatens efforts to end the broader regional war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Iran has demanded that any lasting ceasefire extend to Lebanon, creating additional complications in negotiations.

The repeated military exchanges have created wider economic consequences. The World Food Programme reported that millions of people were being pushed into hunger due to the conflict's effects on energy and food prices.
