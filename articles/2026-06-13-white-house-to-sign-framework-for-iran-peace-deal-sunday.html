US and Iran plan to sign peace framework Sunday

President Trump announced on Saturday that the United States and Iran plan to sign a peace agreement on Sunday. In a post on Truth Social, Trump stated that the deal would prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping.

"Iran no longer want a Nuclear Weapon, nor will they have one, either through purchase, development, or any other form of procurement," Trump said in his announcement. He added that the strait would open to all shipping immediately after the agreement is signed and suggested that the US relationship with Iran under his administration would be "much different and better one than previous Administrations have had."

However, Iran's foreign ministry cast doubt on the timeline. Esmaeil Baghaei, an Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson, cautioned that an exact signing date had not been finalized. "We will have to wait and see about the exact date of the signing of the memorandum of understanding, although it will not be tomorrow," Baghaei said. He noted that signing could potentially happen in the coming days.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed optimism about the negotiations, saying Islamabad was preparing for an electronic signing within 24 hours to be followed by technical-level talks. "We are closer to a peace deal than ever before," Sharif wrote on social media.

The two countries and mediators have been discussing a preliminary peace agreement to end the three-month conflict in the Middle East. However, there are significant disagreements about what the deal contains. According to Iran's semi-official Mehr news agency, the agreement would end conflict on all fronts including Lebanon, release 24 billion dollars of Iran's frozen assets, allow Iran to charge fees for passage through the strait, and suspend sanctions on Iran's oil sales. The agreement would establish a 60-day period for nuclear negotiations.

US officials provided a different account, stating that the deal requires Iran's nuclear material to be destroyed and its nuclear program dismantled. They said Iran's frozen assets would not be released until it meets certain demands and that Iran must stop supporting militant movements in the Middle East.

Trump has repeatedly claimed over the past three months that a deal was imminent. He reserved what he called "the ultimate alternative" if Iran refuses to sign, suggesting continued military action remains possible.

The announcement marks a potential turning point in decades of hostility between Washington and Tehran. The framework addresses multiple sources of tension, including Iran's nuclear program and control of the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquid gas supplies previously passed before Iran closed it to most shipping.
