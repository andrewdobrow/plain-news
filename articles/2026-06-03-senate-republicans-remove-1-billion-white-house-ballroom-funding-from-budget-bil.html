Senate Republicans defeat proposal to block Trump anti-weaponization fund

Senate Republicans on Thursday blocked a Democratic attempt to prevent the creation of a $1.8 billion anti-weaponization fund, though the narrow vote revealed growing divisions within the president's own party.

Democratic Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer proposed language that would have barred the payouts as part of Republican-backed legislation to fund Trump's mass deportation campaign. The amendment failed 49-50 after a three-hour voting session that saw senators huddling on the chamber floor.

Three Republican senators broke ranks to support the amendment. Susan Collins of Maine, Dan Sullivan of Alaska, and Jon Husted of Ohio all voted with Democrats. All three represent seats that Democrats are targeting in the midterm elections. Husted faces particular pressure, trailing his Democratic challenger by eight points in a recent Fox News poll.

The fund proposal has sparked significant bipartisan concern. Trump's plan would issue financial settlements to people connected to the January 6 insurrection. Acting U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche told lawmakers earlier this week that the administration would not move forward with the fund. However, Schumer argued that a formal legal ban was necessary.

"Republicans are trusting the word of Todd Blanche, who built a career on lying," Schumer said. He pointed out that Trump had expressed his enthusiasm for the fund just the day before, calling it important. Schumer questioned whether Trump could resist using the fund for his own benefit.

Schumer proposed his amendment to a $70 billion bill funding immigration enforcement agencies through 2029. Republicans are using the budget reconciliation process to bypass Democratic filibuster rules and pass the legislation along party lines. This funding bill has been at the center of a major standoff between the parties since January, when Democrats refused to approve appropriations without reforms, leading to a 75-day partial shutdown of the Department of Homeland Security.

After Schumer's amendment failed, Republican Senator Thom Tillis proposed alternative language that would prevent payments from the anti-weaponization fund and redirect that money toward Justice Department fraud efforts. Tillis argued the move would eliminate an unpopular measure while funding important work.

"This bill is unpopular. This administration has said they're not moving forward with it. This is an opportunity for us to put it to bed and to also fund the fraud division, which I think is very important," Tillis said.

Democrats rejected Tillis's amendment, with Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon calling it insufficient. "Taking one slush fund and eliminating it and creating a new slush fund still under the control of the attorney general is not the way to go," Merkley said.

Tillis's proposal also failed, receiving support from only 11 Republicans and three Democrats.
