Waymo recalls 4,000 robotaxis after vehicles entered highway construction zones

Waymo has issued a recall affecting nearly 4,000 of its autonomous vehicles after identifying instances where the robotaxis drove into highway sections closed for construction. The company documented at least 13 separate incidents where its self-driving cars entered restricted construction areas on highways.

The recall addresses a software issue that prevented the vehicles from properly recognizing and responding to highway construction zone closures. Waymo's autonomous driving system failed to interpret the temporary road restrictions, allowing the robotaxis to proceed into areas where construction work was actively taking place. The company has not disclosed whether any of these incidents resulted in accidents, injuries, or property damage.

Waymo operates one of the largest commercial robotaxi fleets in the United States, providing ride-hailing services in several cities. The recall represents a significant portion of the company's active fleet and will require a software update to correct the navigation system's ability to detect and avoid construction zones. The fix aims to improve how the vehicles process temporary road closures and construction signage.

The recall comes as autonomous vehicle companies face increased scrutiny over safety performance. Self-driving technology relies on a combination of sensors, cameras, and mapping data to navigate roads, but temporary changes like construction zones can present unique challenges. Unlike permanent road features that can be pre-mapped, construction zones often appear suddenly and may not be reflected in existing digital maps.

Waymo has not provided a specific timeline for completing the software updates across its fleet. The company continues to operate its robotaxi service while implementing the fix. This recall follows other recent safety actions in the autonomous vehicle industry as regulators and companies work to address the challenges of deploying self-driving cars at scale on public roads.
