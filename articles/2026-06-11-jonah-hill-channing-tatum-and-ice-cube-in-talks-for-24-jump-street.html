Jonah Hill, Channing Tatum, and Ice Cube in Talks for 24 Jump Street

A third installment of the Jump Street comedy franchise is moving forward with the original stars set to return. Jonah Hill, Channing Tatum, and Ice Cube are in negotiations to reprise their roles in 24 Jump Street.

Rodney Rothman, known for directing Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse, will helm the project. Rothman co-wrote the script alongside Hill and Meghan Malloy, continuing Hill's involvement as both performer and writer on the franchise.

Producers Phil Lord and Chris Miller are also returning to work on the film. The pair produced the first two Jump Street movies, which transformed the 1980s television series into R-rated action comedies that earned strong box office results and critical appreciation for their self-aware approach to the genre.

The franchise follows two undercover police officers who infiltrate various institutions as part of a revived undercover program. Hill and Tatum portray mismatched partners navigating generational culture clashes while solving cases. Ice Cube plays their commanding officer, Captain Dickson, whose reactions to their chaos became a memorable trademark of the series.

The first Jump Street film arrived in 2012, followed by the sequel in 2014. Both movies found success through their chemistry between the leads and their ability to blend action sequences with comedy rooted in the relationship between the two main characters.

No release date or production timeline has been announced for the new installment. The involvement of the original creative team and cast suggests an effort to maintain the tone and appeal that made the earlier films popular with audiences. The franchise has been in intermittent development discussions since the second film's release more than a decade ago, with various concepts proposed but none advancing to actual production before now.

Hill's dual role as actor and writer continues a pattern from the previous films, where he collaborated on scripts with Michael Bacall. The combination of returning talent both on camera and behind the scenes positions the project as a continuation of the story rather than a complete restart, relying on audience familiarity with the characters and their dynamic.

The timing of this announcement comes as the entertainment industry continues to explore new installments of established franchises that demonstrated both critical and commercial success. The Jump Street series proved that R-rated comedies could achieve broad appeal when they balanced humor with genuine character development and chemistry between leads.
