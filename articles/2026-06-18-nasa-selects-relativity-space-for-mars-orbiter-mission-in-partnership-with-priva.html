NASA selects Relativity Space for Mars orbiter mission in partnership with private sector

NASA announced a public-private partnership with Relativity Space to develop a Mars orbiter mission, marking a significant step in the agency's approach to planetary exploration. The rocket manufacturer, acquired by former Google executive chair Eric Schmidt in 2025, will privately develop the Aeolus mission to Mars orbit.

The partnership represents a new model for NASA's Mars science program, combining government objectives with private sector resources and technology. Relativity Space will shoulder much of the development cost and operational responsibility for the orbiter, which is designed to advance Mars research. The mission structure mirrors approaches NASA has used for commercial cargo and crew transportation to the International Space Station.

Relativity Space faced setbacks in its initial business model before Schmidt's acquisition. The company originally focused on 3D-printed rockets for satellite launches but struggled to reach orbit on schedule. Under new ownership, the firm shifted strategy toward larger rockets and deep space missions. The Mars contract represents the company's most ambitious project to date.

The announcement sets up a competitive dynamic in Mars exploration. SpaceX, led by Elon Musk, has publicly stated Mars colonization as a core company goal and is developing the Starship vehicle partly for Mars missions. NASA has worked with SpaceX on multiple projects, including crew transportation and lunar lander development for the Artemis program. The agency now has partnerships with both companies pursuing Mars objectives through different approaches.

NASA did not announce a timeline for the Aeolus mission launch. Mars missions require precise launch windows when Earth and Mars are favorably aligned, which occur roughly every 26 months. The orbiter will conduct science observations from Mars orbit, though specific instruments and research priorities were not detailed in the announcement.
