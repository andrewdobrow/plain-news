Texas jury sentences teen to 35 years for murder at high school track meet

A Texas jury has convicted Karmelo Anthony, now 19, of murder in connection with the fatal stabbing of Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet. The conviction resulted in a 35-year prison sentence following a one-week trial that drew significant national attention.

The case centered on the death of Metcalf, a 17-year-old track athlete from a rival school, during a competitive meet in the Dallas suburb area. The trial proceeded from jury selection through verdict in just seven days, though the case generated considerable coverage beyond the local region.

The trial attracted national notice in part because of the racial composition of the case. Anthony is Black while Metcalf was white. The jury selection process resulted in 12 jurors and six alternates, with none of the jurors being Black, a detail that was highlighted in viral social media posts discussing the case.

Prosecutors presented evidence and testimony over multiple days before jurors reached their guilty verdict on the murder charge. Following the conviction, the same jury determined sentencing, ultimately deciding on the 35-year prison term. This sentence means Anthony will spend more than three decades in prison for the crime.

Under Texas law, juries have the responsibility of determining punishment in murder cases once a guilty verdict has been reached. The sentencing guidelines for murder convictions in Texas allow for parole consideration based on state regulations governing such sentences.

The stabbing took place at a high school athletic event, an environment typically filled with students, coaches, families, and spectators from multiple schools gathered for competition. Track meets are routine sporting events at Texas high schools that draw participants and audiences from surrounding communities.

The case proceeded through the Texas criminal justice system with prosecutors seeking a substantial prison term for the conviction. The verdict and sentencing concluded the closely watched trial that had drawn attention across and beyond the state due to its circumstances and the racial dynamics highlighted by observers and social media users following the proceedings.

The conviction and sentence reflect the severity of the murder charge under Texas criminal statutes. Anthony's case became one of several high-profile trials that drew broader public and media attention during the period, contributing to ongoing discussions about the criminal justice system and how cases involving young defendants are prosecuted and adjudicated in Texas courts.
