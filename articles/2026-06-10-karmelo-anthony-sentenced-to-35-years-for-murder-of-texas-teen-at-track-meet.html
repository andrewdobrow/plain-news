Karmelo Anthony sentenced to 35 years for murder of Texas teen at track meet

A Collin County jury found Karmelo Anthony guilty of murder for fatally stabbing Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet, resulting in a 35-year prison sentence.

The trial, which lasted one week from jury selection through verdict, drew national attention due to the racial demographics of the case. Anthony is Black while Metcalf was white. The jury consisted of 12 jurors and six alternates, none of whom were Black.

The incident occurred in April 2025 at a Frisco Independent School District track meet. Anthony, then 17 and attending Centennial High School, and Metcalf, also 17 and from Memorial High School, encountered each other when a rain shower caused confusion on the field. Some athletes remained competing while others sought shelter under team tents. When Centennial High School lacked a tent, Anthony moved toward Memorial's tent, where a confrontation broke out that resulted in Metcalf being stabbed. Metcalf died shortly after arriving at a local hospital.

Anthony's defense team argued he acted in self-defense. According to their account, Metcalf and his twin brother, both approximately 6 feet 1 inch tall and weighing around 213 pounds, confronted Anthony, who stands 5 feet 8 inches and weighs approximately 130 pounds. The defense characterized Anthony's actions as a reaction to "fear and chaos."

Prosecutors presented a different narrative. Prosecuting attorney Bill Wirskye argued that Metcalf was stabbed in a "sneak, surprise attack." He cited witness testimony indicating Anthony had told Metcalf, "Touch me and find out." Wirskye also noted that video evidence presented during trial showed other people in the tent did not turn on Anthony, suggesting the incident involved only the two teenagers.

Anthony, now 19, was tried as an adult under Texas law despite being a minor at the time of the stabbing. During the verdict reading, Metcalf's twin brother Hunter was present in the courtroom for the first time, as he had previously been excluded while listed as a witness. When the guilty verdict was announced, Hunter Metcalf leaned forward while Anthony's mother wept. Anthony broke down in tears, and his parents left the courtroom. Anthony has been remanded into custody of the Collin County Sheriff's Office.

The case highlighted broader concerns about safety at school athletic events and sparked discussions about the judicial treatment of minors charged with serious crimes.
