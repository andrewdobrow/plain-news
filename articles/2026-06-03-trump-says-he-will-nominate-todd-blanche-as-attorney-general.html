Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche faces confirmation hearing

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche will appear before the Senate Judiciary Committee on Wednesday for his confirmation hearing to become the permanent leader of the Justice Department. Blanche currently serves as the department's number two official after winning Senate confirmation earlier in President Trump's second term.

The hearing represents a critical test as Blanche seeks to advance from his deputy role to attorney general. Lawmakers are expected to question him on his qualifications to lead the nation's chief law enforcement agency and his vision for the department's future. Democratic Senator Adam Schiff plans to participate in the questioning.

Blanche's confirmation process comes at a particularly contentious moment for the Justice Department. The agency has faced significant scrutiny over its handling of the so-called Epstein files. In February, Democrats accused the department of a "full-blown cover up" after Blanche declared the Epstein investigation effectively over, releasing what he said was approximately 3 million previously unseen documents. However, critics have raised concerns about the completeness and redactions in those releases.

More recently, investigative journalist Katie Phang filed a lawsuit in federal court on Monday alleging what she described as a "brazen, shocking, and ongoing violation" of the Epstein Files Transparency Act. The law, passed by Congress in November with a December 19, 2025 deadline for full document release, requires the Justice Department to publish all documents it holds about Jeffrey Epstein. Phang's lawsuit names Blanche as the defendant and accuses him of missing statutory deadlines, making improper or excessive redactions, failing to explain redactions as required by law, and withholding or retracting documents after release.

Blanche has argued that many redactions are necessary to prevent inadvertent identification of Epstein's victims, many of whom are minors. However, some Epstein victims have complained that their sensitive personal information was improperly exposed in the releases.

Democratic House Judiciary Committee ranking member Jamie Raskin stated in February that there were "tons of completely unnecessary redactions" in addition to failures to redact victims' names.

The Justice Department's Office of Inspector General announced last week that it is launching its own audit into the department's compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act. The preliminary objective of this internal inquiry is to evaluate the Justice Department's processes for identifying, redacting, and releasing records.

Phang's lawsuit seeks a court declaration that the Justice Department violated the law, orders for the release of all required documents without unlawful redactions, and the appointment of an independent special master from outside the department to oversee compliance.

If confirmed, Blanche would assume leadership of the Justice Department as it navigates these high-profile matters alongside numerous other federal law enforcement and national security responsibilities.
