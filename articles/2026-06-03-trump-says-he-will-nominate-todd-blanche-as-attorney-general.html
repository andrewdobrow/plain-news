Trump nominates Todd Blanche as attorney general after two months as acting head

President Trump formally nominated Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche to the Senate on Monday, seeking to permanently install his former personal criminal defense attorney as head of the Department of Justice. Blanche has served in the acting role for two months following Trump's decision to fire former Attorney General Pam Bondi in April.

Trump had signaled the nomination earlier in the week during a podcast interview, describing Blanche as "a very talented guy." The position now moves to the Senate, where confirmation will require approval from the chamber.

Before his appointment as acting attorney general, Blanche held the position of deputy attorney general, the second-ranking official at the Justice Department. His background as Trump's personal criminal defense lawyer represents an unconventional path to leading the nation's top law enforcement agency.

The attorney general position carries significant authority over federal law enforcement, including oversight of criminal prosecutions, civil rights enforcement, and antitrust actions. The attorney general also supervises the FBI and other federal law enforcement agencies.

During his two months as acting attorney general, Blanche has overseen several controversial actions at the Justice Department. These include criminal charges against James Comey, the former FBI director, as part of an escalating investigation into ex-CIA director John Brennan. The department also removed press releases regarding prosecutions of individuals involved in the January 6 Capitol attack.

Blanche played a key role in developing a secretive 1.8 billion dollar fund designed to compensate Trump's allies. However, he abruptly announced the fund's cancellation on Tuesday following widespread public condemnation. Even Republican senators had pushed back against the proposed initiative.

The Senate will now review Blanche's qualifications and conduct confirmation hearings. Senators from both parties will have the opportunity to question him about his priorities for the department, his views on Justice Department independence, and his plans for managing the agency's broad responsibilities. The timeline for the confirmation process depends on Senate scheduling and the level of scrutiny the nomination receives.

Blanche's nomination marks the culmination of his alignment with Trump, having joined the president's legal team in 2023. Throughout his tenure as acting attorney general, he has demonstrated himself as what observers describe as a devoted Trump enforcer. His willingness to pursue the administration's priorities suggests he would continue carrying out the president's wishes if confirmed to the permanent position.

The Senate confirmation process will test whether lawmakers believe Blanche can maintain the independence traditionally expected of the attorney general role, or whether concerns about his loyalty to Trump will affect the vote.
