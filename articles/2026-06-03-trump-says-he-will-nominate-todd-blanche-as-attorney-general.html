Trump to nominate Todd Blanche as attorney general

President Trump announced at a White House dinner that he will nominate Todd Blanche to serve as attorney general. A video of the event was posted on social media by a White House aide, capturing Trump's statement that he plans to formally nominate Blanche.

Blanche, who has served as one of Trump's personal attorneys, would lead the Department of Justice if confirmed by the Senate. The attorney general oversees federal law enforcement, including the FBI, and directs the department's legal positions on behalf of the executive branch. The role has remained one of the most politically sensitive positions in the cabinet.

The announcement comes as Trump continues to shape his administration with loyalists in key positions. Blanche represented Trump in several high-profile legal matters before the president returned to office. His nomination will require Senate confirmation, where Republicans hold a majority but must still navigate the confirmation process.

The choice of Blanche signals Trump's continued preference for advisers with whom he has personal relationships and trust. The attorney general position has seen significant turnover during Trump's political career, with past occupants facing pressure over the independence of the Justice Department versus loyalty to the president.

However, Blanche faces immediate scrutiny over his handling of the Epstein Files Transparency Act. An investigative journalist and legal analyst filed a lawsuit in Washington DC federal court accusing Blanche of a "brazen, shocking, and ongoing violation" of the law requiring the Justice Department to release all documents related to Jeffrey Epstein. Congress passed the transparency act in November with a December 19, 2025 deadline for full document release.

The lawsuit alleges that Blanche, who served as deputy attorney general, has delayed the process and released only a fraction of available documents. It accuses him of missing statutory deadlines for document production, making improper redactions, failing to explain redactions as required by law, and withholding documents after release.

Blanche has argued that redactions were necessary to protect the identities of Epstein's victims, many of them minors, and acknowledged that "mistakes are inevitable." However, critics have questioned the department's process for determining which papers to withhold and which redactions to make. Some victims have complained that their sensitive personal information was improperly exposed in releases.

The Justice Department's office of the inspector general launched an audit into the department's compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act last week.

If confirmed, Blanche would take over the Justice Department at a time when it faces questions about prosecutorial priorities and its relationship with the White House. The formal nomination is expected to be submitted to the Senate in the coming days, beginning what could be a contentious confirmation battle where his record on transparency and document release will likely come under intense examination.
