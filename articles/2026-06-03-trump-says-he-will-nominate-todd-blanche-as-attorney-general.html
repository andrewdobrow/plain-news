Trump nominates Todd Blanche as attorney general

President Trump formally nominated Todd Blanche to serve as attorney general on Monday, positioning his former personal lawyer to become the nation's top law enforcement officer. The nomination will require Senate confirmation to become permanent.

Blanche has been serving in an acting capacity since April, when Trump fired Pam Bondi from the role. Bondi faced criticism for her handling of the Epstein files, an issue that has followed the president for years. Trump previously described Blanche as "a very talented guy" while discussing the nomination on a podcast.

Blanche previously represented Trump in several high-profile legal cases. He was Trump's attorney in the hush-money case involving adult film actor Stormy Daniels, which resulted in Trump's conviction on 34 felony counts for fraudulently falsifying business records. Blanche also represented Trump in the federal classified documents case and election obstruction cases, both of which were dismissed following Trump's electoral victory.

As acting attorney general, Blanche has pursued controversial actions under the banner of combating what the administration calls "weaponization" of the Justice Department. Federal prosecutors have filed criminal charges against James Comey, the former FBI director, and escalated investigations into former CIA director John Brennan. The department also removed press releases about prosecutions related to the January 6 Capitol attack.

Republican Senate Judiciary Committee Chair Chuck Grassley called Blanche "well-qualified," stating he has "worked well with acting attorney general Blanche for more than a year and appreciate his commitment to transparency and support for law enforcement."

Democratic opposition is expected to be significant. Senator Adam Schiff of California argued that "at every turn, Todd Blanche has been unable to put aside his role as Donald Trump's criminal defense lawyer and represent the American people instead." Schiff highlighted several controversial decisions, including support for the Comey indictment based on an Instagram photo and approval of a 1.8 billion dollar fund to compensate Trump supporters who claimed they faced political persecution.

Blanche also signed a Justice Department memo granting Trump, his sons, and the Trump Organization immunity from IRS audits and tax prosecutions. Representative Rosa DeLauro, a Connecticut Democrat, criticized this action, saying that "when you are issuing memos granting the president, his children, and their companies immunity from audits or prosecution for tax offenses, your previous role becomes relevant information."

The controversial compensation fund faced bipartisan criticism. Republican Brian Fitzpatrick from Pennsylvania drafted legislation with bipartisan support to block its creation. Under mounting pressure, Blanche announced on Tuesday that the fund had been terminated.

Republicans hold the Senate majority, which could facilitate confirmation, though some lawmakers have indicated they plan to review Blanche's record closely. The Senate Judiciary Committee typically schedules hearings within weeks of a formal nomination.
