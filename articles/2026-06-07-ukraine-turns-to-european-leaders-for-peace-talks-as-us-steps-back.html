Ukraine Turns to European Leaders for Peace Talks as US Steps Back

President Volodymyr Zelensky convened crisis talks in London with the leaders of Britain, Germany and France, signaling Ukraine's shift toward European-led diplomacy as the United States reduces its role in peace negotiations.

The Monday night meeting at Downing Street brought together Zelensky, French President Emmanuel Macron, and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, alongside leaders from seven other European countries, Turkish representation, and officials from NATO and the EU. The gathering reflected growing European determination to shape Ukraine's future as American diplomatic efforts face mounting skepticism.

The discussions centered on two critical issues: securing peace terms and unlocking frozen Russian assets to support Ukrainian reconstruction. European leaders "underscored the need for a just and lasting peace in Ukraine" including "robust security guarantees" against further Russian aggression, according to Downing Street. They committed to ramping up support for Kyiv and increasing economic pressure on Moscow.

The European show of unity came in response to sharp criticism from Donald Trump, who said he was "a little disappointed" in Zelensky and claimed the Ukrainian president had not properly reviewed a US-proposed peace deal. Trump asserted without evidence that Ukrainian people supported the proposal and that Putin was "fine with it."

A major focus of the London talks involved accessing more than 180 billion pounds of Russian assets frozen in European banks since Russia's 2022 invasion. The EU Commission has proposed funneling approximately 78 billion pounds into a "reparations loan" for Ukrainian reconstruction, based on the premise that Russia would eventually pay Kyiv reparations for war damage. However, Belgium has blocked the plan, citing concerns about liability for seizing assets held at Euroclear, a securities depository in Brussels.

The UK is prepared to contribute 8 billion pounds of frozen assets but wants to coordinate with other European nations. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer plans to discuss the matter with his Belgian counterpart on Friday.

Territorial disputes remain the primary obstacle to peace. Russia has demanded Ukraine cede large swathes of territory, a position Zelensky flatly rejected. "Russia is insisting that we give up territories, but we don't want to cede anything," Zelensky stated. "There are difficult problems concerning the territories and so far there has been no compromise."

Zelensky said the London talks produced "small progress towards peace" and indicated that Ukrainian and European negotiating plans should be ready to share with the United States by the following day. The meetings underscore how European nations are asserting themselves in shaping Ukraine's diplomatic future, particularly as American strategy shifts toward "re-establishing strategic stability with Russia," according to the Trump administration's recently released national security strategy.
