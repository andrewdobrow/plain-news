Stock Markets Surge After Trump Cancels Iran Strikes, Signals Peace Deal

Stock markets rallied sharply after President Trump called off planned military strikes on Iran and said a peace deal could be within reach. The Dow Jones Industrial Average surged 900 points, while oil prices retreated from recent highs as investors bet on reduced tensions in the Gulf region.

Trump announced the decision to halt strikes that had been planned following days of escalating military action between the United States and Iran. The president said negotiations were progressing and that the U.S. would soon sign a deal with Iran, though he did not provide specific details about the terms or timeline.

The shift in market sentiment was immediate and broad-based. Stock indexes across Asia and Europe posted gains, with U.S. futures pointing to continued strength. Oil prices fell to two-month lows as traders unwound positions that had anticipated prolonged conflict in a region critical to global energy supplies. The retreat in energy prices offered relief to consumers and businesses facing elevated costs.

The Iran conflict had weighed on economic activity in recent weeks. Data from the UK showed the economy contracted slightly in April as the war began affecting businesses. The prospect of reduced tensions could alleviate some of the economic pressure that has built up since hostilities began.

The market reaction underscored how deeply geopolitical uncertainty had affected investor confidence. Financial analysts noted that while the immediate crisis appeared to be easing, the durability of any agreement would determine whether the rally could be sustained. Energy markets in particular remained sensitive to any new developments, given the Gulf region's role in global oil production and shipping routes.
