Trump says Iran peace deal could be reached within days

President Trump announced he could approve an Iran peace deal on Friday that would require major concessions from Tehran, including opening the strait of Hormuz and eliminating the country's nuclear programme. However, top Iranian officials disputed his claims, signalling that no final agreement had been reached.

Trump spent more than two hours in the White House situation room with senior aides before making his announcement. On his Truth Social platform, he outlined the terms he said Iran must accept: never developing nuclear weapons, opening the strait of Hormuz to all traffic without tolls, removing mines from the waterway, and allowing the US to collect and destroy highly enriched uranium from an Iranian nuclear site. Trump also said he would lift the US naval blockade against Iran, though it remained unclear whether this would be contingent on the agreement being confirmed.

The list of demands presented by Trump as a completed agreement would indicate Iran had capitulated on key positions, including its historical right to collect tolls from ships using the strait, the release of frozen assets, and its insistence on maintaining its nuclear programme.

Iranian officials quickly rejected Trump's characterisation of the negotiations. The semi-official Tasnim news agency reported that no final understanding had been reached and described Trump's post as "in line with his usual pattern of making unilateral and egotistical statements." Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei told state media that "exchanges of messages are continuing, but no final agreement has been reached yet."

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said discussions about the strait of Hormuz had focused on management "in line with our sovereign responsibilities and international law," suggesting Iran would not accept Trump's proposed terms. Iran's Fars news agency characterised Trump's announcement as a "mixture of truth and lies" that did not reflect actual negotiations.

A White House official confirmed the situation room meeting had lasted approximately two hours but did not announce a deal. The official stated: "President Trump will only make a deal that is good for America and satisfies his red lines. Iran can never possess a nuclear weapon."

Iran's top negotiator Mohammad-Bagher Ghalibaf sent a defiant message, declaring that Iran had "no trust in guarantees or words, only actions are the measure." He suggested Iran was prepared to resume fighting if talks failed to extend the ceasefire and end the war.

A meeting in Washington between US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Pakistan's Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, who serves as a key mediator, ended without public comment on the negotiations. Trump reportedly needed more time to decide whether to accept a negotiated agreement that would defer difficult issues, including the fate of Iran's remaining nuclear material stockpile, into subsequent negotiations.
