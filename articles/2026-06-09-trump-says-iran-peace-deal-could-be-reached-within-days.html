Trump Says Iran Deal Near After Halting New Strikes, Tehran Denies Agreement Finalized

President Donald Trump announced on Thursday that a "great settlement" to end the conflict with Iran has been reached and that he is canceling fresh missile strikes. However, Iranian officials have rejected his characterization, saying no final agreement has been made and that reports of a deal remain speculative.

Trump's announcement came after two days of escalating military operations. The U.S. launched strikes against Iranian targets on Tuesday night in response to an Apache helicopter that went down near the Strait of Hormuz. While U.S. officials told Reuters that Iran had initially retaliated with long-range missiles and drones targeting locations in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan, nearly all of those weapons were intercepted.

The incident involving the helicopter remains contested. Trump initially stated on social media that Iran had shot down the aircraft, but later downplayed the event in an interview with the Wall Street Journal, calling it "not a big deal" and noting that the pilot was fine. A U.S. official told the Associated Press that the helicopter collided with an Iranian drone, though it remained unclear whether the collision was intentional. Both crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat.

Tensions escalated further as the Middle East dealt with fallout from recent Iran-Israel exchanges. Israeli attacks killed at least two members of Iran's air defense units, according to Iranian state television.

In his announcement about the potential deal, Trump did not provide details about the terms of any agreement. He said he canceled planned strikes because there had been "progress in peace negotiations." However, Iran's foreign ministry immediately disputed this claim, with officials insisting that no final decision on an agreement has been reached.

The conflicting statements highlight the fragile state of ceasefire efforts. An initial ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran was announced in April, but officials have been unable to convert it into a permanent deal to end the conflict. The situation has been further complicated by Israel's intensifying military campaign in Lebanon against the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militia.

Before the helicopter incident, Trump had expressed optimism about negotiations, telling the Wall Street Journal that there was "a good chance" of signing a deal in two or three days. He has made similar predictions repeatedly over the past two months, claiming the sides were "very close to having a very, very good, strong, powerful deal."

Iran's chief negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, responded to the latest developments by saying Iran prefers diplomatic language but "speak other languages far more fluently" if commitments are broken. Vice President JD Vance suggested the U.S.-Israel war with Iran could conclude within a week or several months, adding further uncertainty to the timeline.

The conflicting claims from Washington and Tehran leave the status of any potential agreement unclear.
