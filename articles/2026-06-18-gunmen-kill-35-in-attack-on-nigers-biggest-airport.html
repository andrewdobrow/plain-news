Gunmen Kill 35 in Attack on Niger's Biggest Airport

Islamic State in the Sahel has claimed responsibility for an assault on Niger's international airport and adjacent air force base in Niamey, the capital, according to the SITE Intelligence Group, which monitors jihadist communications worldwide. The attack began shortly after midnight on Thursday and involved motorcycle-riding militants who used heavy weaponry and drones in what the group described as a "surprise and coordinated" strike, according to statements released through the Islamic State's Amaq news agency.

The regional Islamic State affiliate has been linked to several high-profile attacks in Niger recently, killing more than 120 people in strikes targeting the Tillabéri region in September and abducting an American pilot in October. Gunfire and explosions at the airport, located about 6 miles from the presidential palace and Base Aérienne 101, a military base previously used by U.S. and Russian troops, damaged at least three aircraft. The damaged planes included one belonging to Ivorian carrier Air Côte d'Ivoire and two from the Togolese airline Asky.

According to Nigerien authorities, the attackers arrived on motorcycles and security forces quickly repelled their advance, killing 20 of the attackers and arresting 11 others. A stash of ammunition also caught fire during the assault. Staff from Asky remained in their hotel away from the airport, though their passports remained in the custody of authorities.

Niger has been fighting a militant Islamist insurgency for a decade, with violence concentrated in border regions. The country serves as a base for Western counterterrorism operations in the Sahel, a region stretching across Africa south of the Sahara Desert where jihadist groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State have expanded their reach.

The attack comes amid significant political upheaval in Niger. A military junta has led the country since deposing democratically elected President Mohamed Bazoum in July 2023. General Abdourahmane Tchiani, the head of the junta, said on national television following the attack: "We have heard them bark, they should be ready to hear us roar." He directed his comments at neighboring West African states and France.

Since the coup, Niger has exited the Economic Community of West African States and joined with fellow junta-run states Burkina Faso and Mali to form the Association of Sahel States. The junta has previously blamed security chaos on neighboring countries and France without presenting evidence.

The airport attack occurred around the same time as a drone assault by the Islamic State West Africa Province on a military base in Nigeria's northeastern Borno state, which killed at least nine soldiers and wounded several others.
