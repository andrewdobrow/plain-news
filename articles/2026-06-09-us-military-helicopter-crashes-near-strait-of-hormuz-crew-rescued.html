US and Iran to sign agreement ending war, reopening Strait of Hormuz

The United States and Iran are expected to hold a virtual meeting on Sunday to electronically sign a memorandum of understanding that will extend their ceasefire by 60 days, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and launch negotiations over Iran's nuclear program. The agreement comes after nearly three months of negotiations between the two nations, with mediation from Pakistan, Qatar, Egypt, and Turkey.

President Trump confirmed the signing on Saturday afternoon, writing on Truth Social that "The Deal is scheduled to get signed tomorrow." Trump described the current relationship with Iran as "a much different and better one than previous Administrations have had." The memorandum is intended to end the regional conflict and potentially stabilize global energy markets.

However, Iran's foreign ministry has cast doubt on the timeline. According to BBC News, Iranian officials said an exact date for signing has not been decided, stating it "will not be tomorrow."

The agreement has drawn criticism from some members of Congress. Democratic Representative Seth Moulton of Massachusetts told MS NOW on Saturday that the reported details constitute a terrible deal. "It's basically a surrender document from Donald Trump to the supreme leader of Iran," Moulton said, reflecting bipartisan concerns about the terms being negotiated with Tehran.

The blocked Strait of Hormuz has created significant global pressure for resolution. British Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper chaired a virtual summit attended by more than 40 countries to discuss measures to pressure Iran into reopening the strait. Cooper said Iran's actions were affecting nations worldwide through rising petrol prices, mortgage rates, jet fuel availability, fertiliser supplies to Africa, and gas shipments to Asia.

The World Bank has predicted that continued blockage could push 9 million people into food insecurity. The strait carries between 10 and 25 percent of the world's oil and gas supplies.

Australia's Foreign Minister Penny Wong participated in discussions about coordinated diplomatic efforts to reopen the route. She stated that "Iran is deliberately inflicting economic pain on communities worldwide, including the Indo-Pacific, with the costs borne disproportionately by the most vulnerable."

Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch said that Trump should not abandon "a mess that he's made" in the Middle East by leaving other countries to reopen the strait. She argued that if Trump started the war, he should help resolve it.

Meanwhile, President Trump has claimed the US has been conducting a "secret mission" in the strait of Hormuz to help bypass Iran's blockade, asserting that hundreds of tankers have escaped with their transmitters off.

Interior Secretary Doug Burgum attributed rising gasoline prices to state policies rather than disruptions in the strait, telling Fox News that gas price variations stem largely from state policy and taxes rather than underlying fundamentals.
