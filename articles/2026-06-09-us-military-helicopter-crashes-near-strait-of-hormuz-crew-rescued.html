U.S. and Iran sign agreement to end war and reopen Strait of Hormuz

The United States and Iran have signed a memorandum of understanding to end their conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump signed the document remotely, with the agreement now in effect. The signing happened ahead of a scheduled ceremony in Switzerland on Friday, after discussions among mediating countries about implementing the deal sooner.

The 14-paragraph memo includes an end to fighting, a commitment that Iran will never develop nuclear weapons, and a $300 billion redevelopment package for Iran. The deal aims to stabilize global energy markets after two months of military action that severely disrupted energy production and transportation across the Middle East.

Trump defended the agreement during an hour-long press conference, though he appeared to lower expectations for success. He warned he could authorize military strikes against Iran again if nuclear negotiations fail. Critics argue that making concessions simply to return to the pre-war situation demonstrates the conflict itself was a costly mistake. Before the war and as it began, Trump had outlined highly ambitious goals including Iran's total surrender and complete dismantling of its nuclear program.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has sharply criticized the deal, calling it a strategic and political disaster. Netanyahu had promised the Israeli public total victory but now faces Trump's diplomatic settlement and repeated public criticism from the president, occurring four months before an Israeli election. Netanyahu stands alone internationally in his opposition. Even the United Arab Emirates, previously the most hawkish Gulf state, supports the diplomatic resolution. Republican allies in Washington and the media have also been reluctant to fully reject a deal carrying Trump's approval.

The immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz faces significant delays. An estimated 5,000 mines that Iran placed in the waterway during the conflict must be swept before normal shipping traffic can resume. Trump said the U.S. military had already begun searching for mines, but the operation will require considerable time.

The conflict has created widespread economic damage globally. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships have been stranded in the Gulf. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent on Wednesday, while Greece announced 500 million euros in extra aid for households and farmers. Countries in Asia dependent on Gulf oil have suffered particularly severe shortages of fuel, fertilizer, and other raw materials.

Peace negotiations remain fragile. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf stated that reopening the strait would be impossible while the U.S. and Israel committed breaches of the ceasefire, including the U.S. naval blockade. He accused them of "hostage-taking of the world's economy." Iran's military seized two ships attempting to cross the strait on Wednesday, marking the first vessel seizures since the war began on February 28.
