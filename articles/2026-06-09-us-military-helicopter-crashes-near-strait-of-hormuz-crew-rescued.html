Iran and US near agreement to end fighting and reopen Strait of Hormuz

The United States and Iran say they are close to finalizing a deal to end hostilities, with both sides indicating that an agreement would lead to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Pakistan has been mediating the negotiations between Washington and Tehran. However, the situation remains volatile, with recent military actions raising questions about whether peace talks will ultimately succeed.

The strait is a critical global shipping route through which roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supply passes. Disruptions to this waterway have caused significant economic strain worldwide, with countries dependent on Gulf oil facing fuel shortages and price increases.

Recent developments have complicated negotiations. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said that reopening the strait would be "impossible" while the US and Israel committed what he called "flagrant" breaches of any ceasefire agreement. He specifically cited a US naval blockade that he described as "the hostage-taking of the world's economy."

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps seized two ships in the strait, claiming they were attempting to cross covertly. The Panama-flagged MSC Francesca and Liberia-flagged Epaminondas were brought to shore. Greece's foreign minister confirmed that an attack occurred against the Greek-operated Epaminondas. A maritime security monitor also reported that another vessel was fired upon by an Iranian gunboat, sustaining heavy damage.

The US has imposed its own blockade on Iran, which the White House said has cost Iran approximately $500 million daily. Trump announced he was extending a ceasefire unilaterally while simultaneously threatening military action, creating uncertainty about American intentions.

The economic consequences of the strait's closure have been severe. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in additional aid for households and farmers. The International Maritime Organization reported that approximately 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships have been stranded in the Gulf.

Planned peace talks in Islamabad were abandoned when Iran did not publicly accept the invitation and the US delegation led by Vice President JD Vance never departed Washington. A Pakistani official told Reuters that preparations had been completed but the cancelled talks represented an unexpected setback.

Prior attempts at diplomatic resolution have failed. Trump withdrew from Iran's nuclear agreement during his first term, a decision he was encouraged to make by Israel. Years of tension have now escalated into open conflict.

A secondary conflict between Israel and the Lebanese militant group Hezbollah has also complicated regional stability. A ceasefire between these parties is set to expire, with Israeli strikes continuing despite the temporary agreement. A journalist and photographer were killed in Lebanon on Wednesday during an Israeli airstrike.
