Oil Prices Rise as U.S. Reinstates Iran Blockade in Strait of Hormuz

Oil prices jumped sharply after the United States reinstated its blockade on Iranian shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, following the collapse of a ceasefire agreement between the two nations. The escalating tensions have sent ripples through global energy markets and beyond.

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, rose nearly 6 percent to more than $80 a barrel on Wednesday, marking the highest price since the U.S. and Iran had agreed to a ceasefire last month. The price increase came after Iran launched attacks on at least three tankers transiting the strait within 48 hours, including a vessel carrying liquified natural gas. In response, President Trump declared the ceasefire deal over and reinstated the blockade.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical maritime chokepoint where roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies pass through each day. The renewed tensions have essentially halted tanker traffic through the passage. According to ship-tracking data, at least four oil and gas tankers have turned back from attempting to transit the strait, disrupting efforts to restore normal flows after months of previous disruption.

The impact extends beyond oil markets. UK short-dated bonds suffered their worst day since March as markets priced in the likelihood of a Bank of England rate increase to combat renewed inflation pressures. The FTSE 100 index fell nearly 1.7 percent, its biggest single-day drop since May, as concerns grew about the economic fallout from escalating tensions. However, oil and gas companies bucked the trend, with BP rising 3.5 percent and Shell climbing 2.2 percent.

Energy prices spiked across the board. In Europe, natural gas market prices jumped 5 percent, with the benchmark Dutch contract rising more than 2.40 euros to 49 euros per megawatt hour. The UK gas equivalent rose by 6 pence to 116.75 pence per therm. These increases threaten to raise household costs, which have already faced the steepest summer energy bill increases in four years.

According to analysts at Rystad Energy, tanker traffic has essentially stopped, reflecting heightened risk perception rather than any single statement from either government. The critical test will come after Iran's supreme leader's burial ceremony later in the week, when both nations will signal whether they still have appetite for a diplomatic resolution.

Some analysts remain cautiously optimistic. Market experts note that global oil markets have demonstrated resilience to disruption, with alternative supply routes and emergency measures helping offset previous blockade impacts. However, the situation remains fluid, with shipping companies and governments assessing potential duration and alternative solutions to restore critical energy supplies through the strait.
