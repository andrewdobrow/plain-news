Gas Prices Fall Below $4 as U.S.-Iran Agreement Reopens Strait of Hormuz

Average gasoline prices across the United States have dropped below $4 per gallon following a U.S.-Iran peace agreement that will reopen the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping. The deal was signed by President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, with Pakistan's prime minister also party to the memorandum of understanding.

The agreement represents a significant development after more than 100 days of disruption to global energy supplies. Oil prices responded immediately to the news, with Brent crude tumbling to $82 a barrel, down from around $97 a barrel when peace negotiations were still uncertain. Wholesale gas prices fell approximately 6 percent.

The strait's closure during the recent conflict between the U.S., Israel, and Iran had severely disrupted energy markets and driven fuel costs higher. Two tankers carrying liquefied natural gas were already exiting the strait on the day of the announcement, heading to Pakistan and China, while a supertanker carrying Iraqi crude left the Gulf bound for China after being stranded for nearly three months.

Stock markets rose on the announcement, with investors responding positively to reduced geopolitical risk. Japan's Nikkei index rose nearly 3 percent and the pan-European Stoxx 600 index was up 1 percent. Treasury futures rallied, gold climbed 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce, and the pound gained almost 0.6 percent to $1.3506.

However, energy analysts cautioned that a return to normal oil flows will take months. Damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and elsewhere requires repair before supplies fully normalize. One analyst noted that markets should focus on physical oil flows through the strait, which remain restricted despite the agreement. Another warned that markets had been "at this stage before, only for talks to break down," suggesting caution about overreacting to the deal.

Oil prices remain significantly higher than before the war began in late February, when Brent crude traded at around $70 a barrel. Analysts expect continued declines as supply chains normalize and oil inventories replenish, though seasonal demand and refinery capacity could affect the pace of future price reductions.

The agreement also had broader economic effects. Mortgage rates declined this week as market optimism about reduced geopolitical uncertainty increased. The easing of tensions provided economic stability after weeks of market volatility related to energy supply concerns and potential impacts on inflation and consumer spending.

For consumers who faced elevated fuel costs during the conflict, the price decline offers relief from the energy shock that had driven inflation fears and hawkish interest rate expectations worldwide.
