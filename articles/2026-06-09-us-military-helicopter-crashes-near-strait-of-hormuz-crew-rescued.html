US and Iran sign agreement to end war and reopen Strait of Hormuz

The United States and Iran have signed a memorandum of understanding to end their conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with the agreement now in effect. President Trump signed the document remotely and later confirmed the signing. The agreement was originally scheduled to be signed in Switzerland on Friday, but officials accelerated the timeline to implement it sooner.

Trump presented the deal during an hour-long press conference at the Group of Seven summit in France. He appeared to lower expectations for what the agreement would accomplish, warning that he could authorize military strikes against Iran again if future nuclear negotiations fail. For two months, Trump had been pursuing a deal to end the war, reopen the critical shipping lane, and stabilize global energy markets.

The memorandum includes 14 paragraphs addressing an end to fighting and an agreement that Iran will never have a nuclear weapon. A 300 billion dollar redevelopment package for Iran is also part of the deal. However, critics argue that the agreement falls short of Trump's earlier promises regarding Iran's nuclear program. The president had previously demanded total Iranian surrender and full dismantlement of its nuclear capabilities, including no enrichment and no ballistic missile funding.

Despite the signed agreement, significant obstacles remain to reopening the waterway. Thousands of Iranian mines placed in the Strait of Hormuz during the conflict will delay the return of normal shipping traffic. Trump said the US military was already searching for an estimated 5,000 mines that must be cleared before commerce can fully resume.

Current tensions between the parties threaten to undermine the deal's implementation. Iran has seized two ships attempting to cross the strait, with Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, speaker of the Iranian parliament and lead negotiator, declaring that reopening the waterway would be "impossible" while the US and Israel committed what he called "flagrant" breaches of the ceasefire. He criticized the US naval blockade as "the hostage-taking of the world's economy."

The US has also seized Iranian vessels, with the White House press secretary stating that Trump was "satisfied" with the naval blockade and believed Iran was in a weak position, losing approximately 500 million dollars daily through economic strangulation.

The conflict has created severe global economic consequences. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships remain stranded in the Gulf. Countries dependent on Gulf oil have faced fuel shortages and price increases. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast, while Greece announced additional aid for households struggling with higher fuel and living costs.

Peace negotiations had been scheduled to continue in Pakistan, but talks never materialized. Iran did not publicly accept the invitation, and a US delegation led by Vice President JD Vance never departed Washington.
