SpaceX Sets $1.75 Trillion Valuation Target for Upcoming IPO

SpaceX has announced a target valuation of $1.75 trillion for its initial public offering, with shares priced at $135 each. The offering would become the largest IPO in history, exceeding the 2019 debut of Saudi Aramco in both valuation and total capital raised. The company, founded by Elon Musk, moved its IPO timeline forward sooner than many market observers had anticipated.

The timing of the public offering is noteworthy. According to recent reports, SpaceX plans to conduct the stock market float in early summer, coinciding with a planetary alignment and Musk's birthday on June 28.

The company's valuation reflects investor enthusiasm for space exploration and satellite internet capabilities. SpaceX operates a business that includes launching satellites and cargo for commercial and government clients, running the Starlink satellite internet network, and developing the Starship rocket system for missions to Mars. The aerospace company has secured contracts with NASA and the Department of Defense while expanding its commercial satellite launch business.

JPMorgan Chase is marketing the offering to ultra-wealthy clients, with CEO Jamie Dimon personally pitching the investment opportunity to select investors. The direct involvement of the bank's top executive underscores the significance Wall Street is placing on this historic public offering.

The acquisition of xAI by SpaceX for $1.25 trillion has also shaped the company's trajectory toward going public. The merger consolidates Musk's business empire and combines SpaceX's rocket and satellite capabilities with xAI's artificial intelligence assets, including the Grok chatbot and the social media platform X. The combined business is expected to be valued well in excess of $1 trillion following the merger.

In announcements about the deal, the companies highlighted plans for space-based datacenters powered by solar-powered satellites as a means of supporting artificial intelligence infrastructure. The statement noted that current AI advances depend on large terrestrial datacenters requiring immense amounts of power and cooling. "In the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale," the companies said.

However, not all analysts support SpaceX's valuation assessment. Investment research firm Morningstar stated the company is worth less than half the proposed $1.75 trillion IPO target, raising questions about whether the pricing reflects realistic expectations for the space industry and the broader market.

SpaceX has also expanded its terrestrial operations beyond rockets. The company recently secured tax exemptions from Grimes County, Texas for a $55 billion artificial intelligence chip manufacturing plant, despite opposition from local residents. The facility received approval for substantial tax breaks ahead of the public offering, signaling the company's broader technology ambitions beyond space exploration.
