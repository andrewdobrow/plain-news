SpaceX sets $1.75 trillion valuation as it prepares for largest-ever stock market debut

SpaceX has established a target share price valuing the company at $1.75 trillion as it prepares for what would be the largest initial public offering in stock market history. The aerospace company is seeking to raise approximately $75 billion through the offering, which is expected to launch in the coming weeks.

The valuation represents a significant increase from SpaceX's previous assessment. In December, the company had indicated an expected value of $800 billion to investors. The new $1.75 trillion figure reflects the company's expanding role in satellite launches, government contracts, and its Starlink internet service.

The timing of the IPO appears connected to a notable celestial event. According to reports, the stock market launch is primed for early summer, coinciding with a planetary alignment of Jupiter and Venus and founder Elon Musk's 55th birthday on June 28. The announcement of the IPO comes shortly after SpaceX completed a major acquisition of Musk's artificial intelligence company xAI.

Under the merger terms, SpaceX was valued at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion, with the combined entity expected to exceed $1 trillion in valuation at launch. The deal consolidates several of Musk's enterprises under one public company. SpaceX will gain access to xAI's Grok chatbot and the social media platform X through the acquisition.

The announcement cited space-based datacenters as a key rationale for combining the companies. According to the statement, current artificial intelligence advances depend on large terrestrial datacenters that require enormous amounts of power and cooling. The companies argued that global electricity demands for AI cannot be met through ground-based solutions without harming communities and the environment. SpaceX and xAI stated that space-based AI represents "the only way to scale" in the long term.

SpaceX has established itself as a dominant force in commercial spaceflight. The company operates an extensive satellite constellation for Starlink, conducts regular launches for government and commercial customers, and has transported astronauts to the International Space Station for NASA. The company is also developing Starship, its next-generation vehicle intended for future missions to Mars.

Some market observers have questioned whether the valuation is justified given the company's revenue and operational costs. SpaceX generates income from commercial satellite launches, government defense and space contracts, and Starlink subscriptions. However, the company faces substantial expenses for research, development, and manufacturing of rockets and spacecraft.

If the IPO proceeds at the proposed valuation, Musk, already the world's wealthiest person, could become positioned to make history as the first trillionaire. The offering also comes as several other major technology companies, including AI firm Anthropic, prepare for their own public market debuts.
