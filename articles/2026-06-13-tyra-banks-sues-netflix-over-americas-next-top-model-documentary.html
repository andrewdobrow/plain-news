Tyra Banks Sues Netflix Over Americas Next Top Model Documentary

Tyra Banks filed a defamation lawsuit against Netflix on Saturday, claiming the streaming service manipulated her interview for the documentary "Reality Check: Inside America's Next Top Model." Banks alleges her words were edited in ways that misrepresented her statements and presented her in a false light.

The lawsuit names Netflix, 89 Blocks Holdings, EverWonder Studio, Netflix Music, and co-directors Mor Loushy and Daniel Sivan as defendants. Banks is pursuing charges of false light, defamation by implication, breach of contract, and false endorsement.

According to co-director Mor Loushy, Banks was told the documentary would proceed with or without her participation. She agreed to participate in a four-hour interview to share her perspective, intending to express her commitment to making modeling more inclusive. However, Banks received no advance questions, had no approval over how she was presented, and could only view the final series alongside the general public.

The documentary has proven to be a major success for Netflix, drawing more than 14 million views in its first week and topping the platform's English-language charts. However, the release has intensified criticism of Banks and the reality show she created and hosted from 2003 to 2018. Former contestants and judges have expressed outrage at what they perceive as Banks's failure to take adequate responsibility for the show's documented problems, including body-shaming and inadequate duty of care.

Former contestant Brittany Brower called the series "triggering" and criticized Banks for not taking accountability for how contestants were "completely used." Another former contestant, Brittany Corinne Hatch, described Top Model as "a systemic labor violation and a psychological experiment."

Even Banks's former judges have been critical. Creative director Jay Manuel said he was disappointed by Banks's lack of vulnerability, noting what he observed was "Television Tyra." Catwalk coach J Alexander, who suffered a stroke in December 2022, has not received a visit from Banks, though she did send a text message.

During the documentary, Banks acknowledged going "too far" during a famous outburst at contestant Tiffany Richardson in cycle four, saying "You know, I lost it." However, Richardson has disputed Banks's characterization, accusing her of being a bully both on and off camera.

Some supporters have emerged, including Isis King, the show's first transgender contestant, who said she cannot downplay the positive impact Top Model had on her life.

The lawsuit represents a significant legal challenge for Netflix and the production companies involved. Defamation cases involving edited interviews often turn on whether the final product substantially altered the meaning of statements or created false implications. Banks is seeking damages and other relief through the court system.
