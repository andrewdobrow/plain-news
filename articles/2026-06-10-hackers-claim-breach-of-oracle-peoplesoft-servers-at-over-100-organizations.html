Hackers claim breach of Oracle PeopleSoft servers at over 100 organizations

A hacking group known as ShinyHunters has claimed responsibility for compromising Oracle PeopleSoft servers at more than 100 organizations, including numerous universities. The cybercriminals announced the breach, which targets enterprise resource planning software widely used for human resources, finance, and student information management.

Oracle PeopleSoft is deployed across thousands of institutions worldwide to manage sensitive employee and student data. The software handles payroll information, personal identification details, financial records, and academic information. A successful breach of these systems could expose confidential data for millions of individuals across affected organizations.

ShinyHunters has a history of high-profile data breaches and typically sells stolen information on underground forums. The group has previously claimed responsibility for breaches at major corporations and has established a reputation within cybercriminal circles for targeting large-scale databases. Universities represent particularly attractive targets due to the volume of personal information they store and often limited cybersecurity resources compared to private sector organizations.

The full scope of the breach remains under investigation. Organizations running PeopleSoft installations must determine whether their systems were compromised and what data may have been accessed. Many universities store decades of records in these systems, meaning a breach could affect current students and employees as well as alumni and former staff members.

The incident highlights ongoing vulnerabilities in enterprise software systems that serve as central repositories for sensitive information. Organizations relying on PeopleSoft for critical operations face potential disruption to payroll, benefits administration, and academic record management if systems must be taken offline for security assessment and remediation. Affected institutions will need to notify individuals whose information may have been compromised and implement enhanced security measures to prevent future intrusions.
