FBI seizes computers in search of Ohio voter registration group offices

Federal agents conducted a raid on the Cleveland office of the Ohio Organizing Collaborative, a grassroots organization focused on voter registration, prompting concerns about potential enforcement actions against voting rights groups ahead of the midterm elections.

According to Prentiss Haney, a board member of the group, FBI agents seized computers and phones during Thursday's operation. Agents also visited homes of people affiliated with the organization throughout Ohio and conducted interviews with them.

The investigation appears to focus on accusations related to the 2024 election, though specific details remain unclear. The FBI's Cleveland office did not respond to requests for comment about the raid or its underlying investigation.

Haney characterized the operation as part of a broader campaign. "This raid is a full-on coordinated assault weaponizing the justice department and DHS against people who are fighting for working-class voters and Black voters to make sure they have access to the ballot," he said.

The timing of the raid came approximately one month after Todd Blanche, a top official in the acting attorney general's office, instructed prosecutors to prioritize voter fraud cases. Additionally, Frank LaRose, Ohio's Republican secretary of state, referred over 1,000 individuals he identified as non-citizens who appeared to have registered in the state to the justice department last fall. Federal investigators have also gathered voter records from at least six Ohio counties.

The operation drew immediate criticism from Democratic elected officials and civil rights organizations. Shontel Brown, an Ohio congressional representative whose district includes Cleveland, called it "an unprecedented attack on democracy" and stated the raids must end. She characterized the action as part of a systematic effort to attack elections and promote voter fraud myths.

Sherrod Brown, a former senator now running for office, described reports of the raid as "deeply disturbing." Cleveland Mayor Justin Bibb called for transparency, saying the FBI should disclose the basis for its actions or face questions about whether it was unfairly targeting civic participation.

Michael Waldman, president of the Brennan Center for Justice, described the raid as "an outrageous fishing expedition" designed to intimidate people working for democratic participation. He characterized it as an abuse of law enforcement for political purposes that fits a pattern of federal inquiries targeting voting infrastructure before elections.

Ohio remains a significant swing state in national elections. The state delivered 17 electoral votes to Donald Trump in 2024, and Republican Bernie Moreno defeated longtime Democratic Senator Sherrod Brown in a competitive race that helped Republicans gain control of the Senate.

Trump has repeatedly claimed widespread voter fraud without providing evidence, most recently focusing on California's vote-counting procedures.
