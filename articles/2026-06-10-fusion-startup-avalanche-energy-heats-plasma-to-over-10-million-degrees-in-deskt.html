Fusion startup Avalanche Energy heats plasma to over 10 million degrees in desktop reactor

Avalanche Energy announced that its prototype fusion reactor achieved plasma temperatures exceeding 10 million degrees Celsius, marking progress in the company's pursuit of compact fusion power systems. The startup described the desktop-sized reactor as a significant step toward making fusion energy commercially viable.

Fusion reactions require extreme heat to force atomic nuclei together, which releases energy. The sun produces fusion at roughly 15 million degrees Celsius within its core. Avalanche Energy's strategy emphasizes creating small reactors that can fit on a desktop, a departure from the massive warehouse-scale facilities that most other fusion projects require.

The startup is one of several private companies now working on fusion energy technology using different approaches. These ventures are racing to achieve sustained fusion reactions that produce more energy than they consume. The sector has attracted significant investment as interest in carbon-free baseload power has increased substantially. Various companies have announced milestones, though no commercial fusion power plant has yet delivered electricity to the grid.

Avalanche Energy has not released specific details about how long the plasma remained heated, how much energy the reactor required as input, or whether the reaction achieved net energy gain. These measurements are crucial for determining whether a fusion approach can eventually become commercially practical. The company's desktop design represents a fundamentally different strategy from the larger tokamak reactors being developed by other companies such as Commonwealth Fusion Systems or government-backed projects like ITER in France.

The announcement arrives as demand for electricity from artificial intelligence data centers has sparked renewed focus on innovative power generation technologies. Companies across the energy sector are exploring how to satisfy the enormous electricity consumption required by computing infrastructure, which traditional renewable energy and fossil fuels may struggle to provide at the necessary scale and reliability.

The timing also coincides with broader momentum in the fusion sector. Trump Media and Technology Group announced a merger with TAE Technologies, an established fusion energy company, creating a combined entity valued at 6 billion dollars. The deal includes 300 million dollars in funding for TAE to continue developing its technology. TAE has stated it plans to begin construction on a utility-scale fusion power plant with a goal of generating commercial power by 2031. Major technology companies, including Alphabet and investment from OpenAI's leadership, have also backed fusion energy startups as the race to commercialize the technology intensifies. The US Department of Energy has established a new Office of Fusion dedicated to accelerating the commercial readiness of fusion technology, signaling growing government support for the sector's development.
