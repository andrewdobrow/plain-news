Anthony Head, Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Ted Lasso Actor, Dies at 72

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. His daughters Emily and Daisy Head announced that he "passed away peacefully of complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family."

Head gained widespread recognition for his role as Giles, the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character throughout the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. The character became a fan favorite for his dry wit and fatherly relationship with Buffy. More recently, Head appeared in Apple TV's Ted Lasso, where he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, and was praised for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actor. Head began his professional career on stage, starring in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in numerous stage productions including Chess, where he played lead role Freddie Trumper, and The Rocky Horror Show as Dr. Frank-N-Furter.

Before becoming famous for Buffy, Head gained recognition in Britain for his role in Nescafé Gold Blend television advertisements. He also appeared in films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie, and had a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain. Additionally, Head sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and was featured on their 1986 debut album.

During Buffy's production, Head commuted between Los Angeles and England, where his partner Sarah and two young daughters lived. "I'd try to go home to them every three or four weeks," he told the Guardian, explaining that the production team worked around his schedule.

Sarah Michelle Gellar paid tribute to Head on Instagram, opening with a quote from her character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

His daughters stated that Head "loved his job very much and he always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people, in such wonderful productions, across a career that spanned several decades."

The actors further expressed that "our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of and in the audiences that love them."

Head's partner, Sarah Fisher, died in December 2025 at age 61.
