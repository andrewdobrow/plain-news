Actor Anthony Head Dies at 72

British actor Anthony Head has died at age 72 from complications related to pneumonia. His daughters Emily and Daisy confirmed the news in a statement, saying he "passed away peacefully of complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family."

Head became widely recognized for playing Rupert Giles, the school librarian and mentor figure in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, which ran from 1997 to 2003. His daughters paid tribute to his impact, saying "It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many."

Sarah Michelle Gellar, who played the title character, shared an emotional tribute on Instagram. She quoted her character from the show: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok," then added, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Beyond Buffy, Head had a diverse career spanning stage and screen. Born in Camden, London, he came from an artistic family. His father founded Verity Films, a documentary production company, while his mother was an actor who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor.

Head began his professional career in theatre, starring in a 1978 West End production of Godspell. He took roles in films including the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and the 1987 film A Prayer for the Dying alongside Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson. In the late 1980s, he performed in the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess and played Dr Frank-N-Furter in a 1990 production of The Rocky Horror Show.

Head also achieved prominence in the United Kingdom for appearing in Nescafé Gold Blend television advertisements during the 1980s. He sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album.

Following Buffy, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain, where he played a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant. More recently, he appeared in the BBC production of Merlin and had a regular role in the Apple TV+ series Ted Lasso, where he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, earning praise for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

His daughters noted that Head "loved his job very much and he always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people." They emphasized that his legacy would live on through the shows he appeared in and the audiences who loved them.
