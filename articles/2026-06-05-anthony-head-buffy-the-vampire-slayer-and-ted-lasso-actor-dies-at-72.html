Anthony Head, Buffy the Vampire Slayer Star, Dies at 72

Anthony Head, the British actor renowned for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72 from complications related to pneumonia. He passed away peacefully, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said.

Head's career encompassed multiple decades working across theater, television, and film. Beyond his iconic role as Buffy's mentor and librarian, he appeared in the fantasy series Merlin as King Uther Pendragon and took on a recurring role in the sketch comedy series Little Britain, playing a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in the Apple TV Plus series Ted Lasso, where he developed praised on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother also pursued acting. Head began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell alongside Su Pollard. He subsequently appeared in the 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and the 1987 film A Prayer for the Dying with Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson.

In the 1980s, Head also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and was featured on their 1986 debut album. He became well known in the UK and US for his role in Nescafe's Gold Blend television advertisements, which depicted a slow-burning romance between a British couple. The stage remained central to his career, with a lead role in the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess in 1988 and a starring role as Dr Frank-N-Furter in the 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show.

During his five years filming Buffy in Los Angeles while his family remained in England, Head managed the demanding schedule by flying home every three or four weeks whenever the production schedule allowed.

His daughters reflected on his legacy, saying that Head "loved his job very much and he always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people, in such wonderful productions, across a career that spanned several decades."

Tributes poured in from colleagues. Little Britain co-creator Matt Lucas wrote on X that Head was "unfailingly brilliant, and always so kind."
