Anthony Head, Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Ted Lasso Actor, Dies at 72

British actor Anthony Head has died at age 72 from complications due to pneumonia. His daughters Emily and Daisy Head confirmed the news in a statement, saying he "passed away peacefully, surrounded by his family."

Head was best known for playing Rupert Giles, the school librarian and mentor figure in Buffy the Vampire Slayer. The role made him a recognizable face across British and American audiences during the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in the Apple TV+ series Ted Lasso, where he developed notable on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father founded Verity Films as a documentary filmmaker, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the BBC crime drama Maigret during the 1960s. His brother also pursued acting. Head's parents encouraged his creative pursuits from childhood, with his mother handmaking elaborate dressing-up outfits for him.

Before finding fame in television, Head established himself as a stage actor. He performed in the 1978 West End revival of Godspell and earned a lead role as Freddie Trumper in the original West End production of the musical Chess in 1988. He also starred as Dr. Frank-N-Furter in a 1990 West End production of The Rocky Horror Show. Beyond theatre, Head appeared in films including The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie.

Head became widely recognized in the UK and US through television commercials for Nescafe Gold Blend, which featured a slow-burn romantic storyline between two characters. In the early 1980s, he also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album.

After landing the role of Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Head relocated to Los Angeles for five years while his partner Sarah and two young daughters remained in England. He managed the long-distance arrangement by returning home every three or four weeks whenever production schedules allowed. Despite his rising profile, Head remained grounded, telling The Guardian that he avoided getting caught up in the competitive nature of celebrity.

Following Buffy's conclusion, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain and appeared in the BBC production of Merlin. His daughters said their father "always considered himself incredibly lucky to have been able to work alongside such exceptionally talented people in such wonderful productions across a career that spanned several decades."

Head's partner Sarah Fisher died in December 2025 at age 61. His legacy continues through the television shows and films he appeared in throughout his extensive career.
