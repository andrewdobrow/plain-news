Anthony Head, Buffy the Vampire Slayer and Ted Lasso Actor, Dies at 72

British actor Anthony Head, best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72 from complications due to pneumonia. His daughters Emily and Daisy Head confirmed he passed away peacefully, surrounded by family.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said in a statement.

Head's most iconic role was as the mentor and father figure to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character throughout Buffy's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. In a tribute posted on Instagram, Gellar opened with a quote from her character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you."

Born in Camden, London, Head came from an artistic family. His father was a documentary filmmaker who founded Verity Films, while his mother was an actor who appeared in the BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor, best known for his lead role in the 1971 film Sunday Bloody Sunday.

Head began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell alongside Su Pollard. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he appeared in various productions including the original West End production of the Abba musical Chess in 1988 and a 1990 production of The Rocky Horror Show as Dr. Frank-N-Furter. He also took roles in films including a 1981 adaptation of Lady Chatterley's Lover and 1987's A Prayer for the Dying with Bob Hoskins and Liam Neeson.

In the early 1980s, Head sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and was featured on their 1986 debut album. He became widely recognized in the UK and US for his role in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements, which portrayed a slow-burn courtship between a British couple.

While filming Buffy in Los Angeles, Head remained committed to his family in England. He told The Guardian that he would return home every three or four weeks whenever the production schedule allowed, spending considerable money on airfares to maintain those connections.

After Buffy ended, Head took a recurring role in the BBC sketch show Little Britain and appeared in the BBC production of Merlin. In recent years, he played former football club owner Rupert Mannion in the Apple TV series Ted Lasso, which introduced him to new audiences and earned praise for his on-screen chemistry with co-creator Jason Sudeikis.

Head's daughters noted that his legacy will live on through the shows he worked on and the audiences who loved them.
