British Actor Anthony Head Dies at 72

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72 from complications due to pneumonia. His daughters Emily and Daisy Head confirmed his passing in a statement, saying he "passed away peacefully, surrounded by his family."

Head's most iconic role was as the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's character throughout Buffy the Vampire Slayer's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. The character of Giles, known as a "Watcher," became a beloved figure among fans of the supernatural series. Creator Joss Whedon had long planned a spinoff called Ripper that would have explored Giles' younger years, though the project never came to fruition.

Beyond his work on Buffy, Head had a remarkably diverse career spanning decades across theatre, film, and television. He appeared in the BBC sketch comedy show Little Britain, where he played a British prime minister constantly fawned over by a flirtatious assistant. He also featured in the BBC's Merlin series and took film roles in The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie.

More recently, Head played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in the Apple TV+ series Ted Lasso, where he worked alongside co-creator Jason Sudeikis and was praised for their on-screen chemistry.

Head was born in Camden, London, to artistic parents. His father founded the documentary production company Verity Films, while his mother was an actor who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor known for his lead role in the 1971 film Sunday Bloody Sunday. This creative environment shaped Head's early life, with his mother making elaborate dressing-up costumes that completely immersed the young actor in character play.

He began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell. His theatre work became a cornerstone of his career, including roles in productions of Chess, where he played Freddie Trumper, and The Rocky Horror Show, where he starred as Dr. Frank-N-Furter in 1990. In the 1980s, he also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box, appearing on their 1986 debut album.

Head gained significant recognition in Britain and America through Nescafé Gold Blend television advertisements that featured a slow-burn romance between two neighbours. When he took the Buffy role, he relocated to Los Angeles while his partner Sarah and two young daughters remained in England. He made frequent trips home, arranging his filming schedule to allow visits every three to four weeks.

His daughters remembered him as someone who loved his work and felt fortunate to work with talented colleagues across productions spanning several decades. They noted that his legacy would continue through the shows he appeared in and the audiences who cherish them.
