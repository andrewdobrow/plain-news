Anthony Head, Buffy the Vampire Slayer Actor, Dies at 72

Anthony Head, the British actor best known for playing Rupert Giles in Buffy the Vampire Slayer, has died at age 72. He passed away peacefully from complications due to pneumonia, surrounded by his family, according to a statement from his daughters Emily and Daisy Head.

"It has been, and forever will be, an honour and a privilege to be his daughters, and to have witnessed first-hand the impact both he and his work have had on so many," they said.

Head played Giles, the school librarian and mentor to Sarah Michelle Gellar's vampire slayer, throughout the show's seven-season run from 1997 to 2003. The character became iconic as the tweedy father figure guiding Buffy and her friends through supernatural threats. Cast members from Buffy paid tribute following his death, with references to show themes reflecting the lasting bonds formed during production.

Sarah Michelle Gellar shared her tribute on Instagram, beginning with a quote from her Buffy character: "Tell Giles I figured it out and I'm ok." She continued, "Well I don't have it figured out and I'm not ok. But I know I'm the lucky one because I knew you. Thank you to Daisy and Emily who not only shared their dad with me, but with the world."

Beyond Buffy, Head's career spanned decades across British and American television and film. He appeared in the BBC production of Merlin and took a recurring role in the sketch comedy show Little Britain. His film credits included roles in The Iron Lady and The Inbetweeners Movie. More recently, he played Rupert Mannion, a former football club owner, in Ted Lasso, where he worked alongside co-creator Jason Sudeikis and received praise for their on-screen chemistry.

Head was born in Camden, London, to artistic parents. His father founded the documentary production company Verity Films, while his mother was an actress who appeared in the 1960s BBC crime drama Maigret. His brother was also an actor known for the 1971 film Sunday Bloody Sunday.

Head began his professional career on stage, starring in a 1978 West End revival of Godspell. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, he performed in various stage productions including Chess and The Rocky Horror Show. He also sang backing vocals for the British pop group Red Box and appeared on their 1986 debut album The Circle & the Square.

Head became widely recognized in the UK and US for appearing in Nescafe Gold Blend television advertisements that featured a slow-burn romance between a British couple.

During Buffy's filming, Head spent five years in Los Angeles while his partner Sarah and their two young daughters remained in England. He traveled home every three or four weeks whenever possible, working with the production team to arrange his schedule around these visits.

His daughters noted that Head loved his work and considered himself fortunate to collaborate with talented people throughout his long career. "Our grief is far greater than the hole he has left behind but we know his legacy will live on in the shows he was a part of," they said.
