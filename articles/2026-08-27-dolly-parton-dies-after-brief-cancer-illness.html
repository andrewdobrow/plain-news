Dolly Parton dies after brief cancer illness

Dolly Parton, one of the greatest singer-songwriters in country music history, has died at age 80 following a cancer diagnosis. Her nephew Brian Seaver announced her death on Instagram, saying "It is an honour, an honour that is mixed with absolute pride and great sadness."

Parton rose from poverty in rural Tennessee to become a global entertainment icon whose influence extended far beyond music. Born in Pittman Center and raised in the isolated Smoky Mountains hamlet of Locust Ridge, she transformed her humble beginnings into the foundation of her artistic vision. Songs like "Coat of Many Colors" and "My Tennessee Mountain Home" drew directly from her early years and resonated with audiences across generations.

Her career spanned more than six decades and encompassed music, film, business, and philanthropy. As a soprano with a distinctive vibrato and east Tennessee twang, her voice became one of the most recognizable in music. She achieved remarkable commercial success with hits including "Jolene," "I Will Always Love You," and "9 to 5." The last of these was written for the 1980 film in which she also starred.

Parton's business acumen matched her creative talents. When Elvis Presley wanted to record "I Will Always Love You" but demanded 50 percent of the publishing rights, she refused. That decision proved wise when Whitney Houston's rendition for "The Bodyguard" soundtrack in 1992 became one of the best-selling singles of all time, earning Parton substantial royalties.

Known for her wit and self-deprecating humor, Parton crafted a distinctive persona that balanced authenticity with showmanship. She famously said, "It takes a lot of money to look this cheap," capturing her approach to her signature glamorous style. At Glastonbury in 2014, she attracted what was reportedly the largest crowd in the festival's history, performing in a crystal-studded white suit.

Beyond entertainment, Parton became celebrated for her charitable work. She established her Imagination Library, a book-reading program launched in 1995 that distributed millions of free books to children internationally. She also invested in medical research, supported communities affected by wildfires and hurricanes, and contributed to COVID-19 vaccine development.

Her awards reflected her broad impact. She earned 10 Grammy Awards, 11 from the Academy of Country Music, and numerous other honors. She was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame and played a major role in bringing country music into the mainstream.

Tributes poured in from across music, film, and politics. Her goddaughter Miley Cyrus described her as an angel, while others acknowledged the depth of her influence on popular culture and her lasting legacy as both an artist and humanitarian.
