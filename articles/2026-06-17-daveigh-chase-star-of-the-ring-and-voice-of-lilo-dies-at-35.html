Daveigh Chase, Voice of Lilo and Star of The Ring, Dies at 35

Daveigh Chase, the actress and voice performer known for bringing Lilo to life in Disney's 2002 animated film Lilo & Stitch and for playing the ghostly Samara Morgan in the horror film The Ring, has died at age 35. Her boyfriend, Roy Hernandez, confirmed that she died from meningitis and blood infections that led to sepsis.

Chase had been hospitalized in Los Angeles earlier this month after being admitted for malnutrition. Her condition deteriorated as the infections took hold, and she passed away on Tuesday. Before her death, Hernandez had set up a GoFundMe page seeking assistance, describing Chase's struggles and his desire to provide her with a safe home during her final days.

Born on July 24, 1990, in Las Vegas, Nevada, Chase began her entertainment career as a child actor. Her dual roles in 2002 demonstrated her range as a performer, moving seamlessly between family-friendly animation and dark horror. Her voice work as Lilo Pelekai became iconic in animation, while her portrayal of the long-haired ghost who emerges from a television screen in The Ring became one of the most memorable images in modern horror cinema.

Beyond these two most recognizable roles, Chase built a substantial career in film and television. She voiced Chihiro Ogino in the English dub of Studio Ghibli's Spirited Away and appeared in several other notable projects including the cult film Donnie Darko and the television series Sabrina the Teenage Witch. Her voice work connected to Lilo & Stitch extended to related projects and sequels throughout her career.

According to her longtime manager, Chase had faced significant hardships behind the scenes. In statements to media outlets, the manager noted that despite her successful film work, she had struggled with personal difficulties. Hernandez's GoFundMe message described how Chase had endured a challenging childhood and a painful estrangement from her family. He spoke of her experiencing bullying and struggling to find stability while living in downtown Los Angeles.

Hernandez characterized Chase as someone who "has always been a light" in his life, and expressed his commitment to providing her with the love and comfort she deserved during their time together. Before her condition became critical, they had found moments of happiness and hope.

Chase's death marks the early end of a career that began with significant promise and delivered performances that continue to reach audiences across generations and genres. Her work as Lilo brought warmth and humor to a story about family and belonging, while her contribution to The Ring helped define horror cinema for a new generation of viewers. The entertainment world has lost a performer whose roles became embedded in popular culture.
