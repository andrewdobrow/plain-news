Former National Security Advisor John Bolton to plead guilty in classified documents case

John Bolton, the former national security adviser who became a vocal critic of Donald Trump, has agreed to plead guilty to charges related to mishandling classified documents. The plea agreement is subject to court approval, according to sources familiar with the matter.

Bolton will plead guilty to one count of illegal retention of sensitive national security information. The case represents part of what observers describe as an escalating series of prosecutions against Trump's critics.

The investigation came to public attention when FBI agents conducted early morning raids at Bolton's Bethesda, Maryland home and his Washington office on Friday. Bolton was not present at his home when agents arrived, though he was later spotted in the lobby of his office building speaking with FBI personnel. His wife, Gretchen, was seen leaving their home during the search.

Bolton served as Trump's third national security adviser for 17 months, from 2018 to 2019. He departed the administration following disagreements with Trump over Iran, Afghanistan, and North Korea policies. After leaving government service, Bolton wrote a political memoir titled "The Room Where It Happened," which detailed what he characterized as impeachable conduct by Trump. Trump's administration unsuccessfully attempted to block the book's publication, but a judge ruled in June 2020 that it could be released.

The classified documents controversy centers on Bolton's retention of sensitive materials after departing his position. Earlier legal disputes surrounding his memoir similarly involved questions about whether it contained classified information, though those previous actions ultimately proved unsuccessful for the government.

Shortly after the raids became public, FBI Director Kash Patel posted a message on social media stating "NO ONE is above the law." Attorney General Pam Bondi subsequently posted that "America's safety isn't negotiable. Justice will be pursued. Always."

Trump told reporters he was not aware the raid was planned and claimed he did not want to know about such operations. He made critical comments about Bolton, calling him "a real sort of a lowlife" and suggesting he could be "very unpatriotic."

Some observers questioned the timing and motivation behind the investigation. Meghan Hays, a former White House special adviser under Joe Biden, characterized the raid as "extremely political" and "pure revenge." Congressman Raja Krishnamoorthi suggested the action may have been designed as a distraction from other ongoing matters.

Bolton had not been detained or charged with crimes at the time of the raids. Trump had previously revoked Bolton's security clearance and canceled his security detail in the early days of his administration this year.

The plea agreement marks a conclusion to the investigation and will require approval from a federal judge before becoming final.
