John Bolton to plead guilty to retaining classified information

Former Trump Adviser John Bolton Reaches Plea Deal Over Classified Documents

John Bolton, the former national security adviser who became a vocal critic of Donald Trump after leaving his administration, has agreed to plead guilty to illegally retaining classified national security documents.

The Department of Justice filed charges against Bolton in October 2025 as part of a broader wave of prosecutions against Trump critics that occurred over several weeks. Bolton intends to plead guilty to one count of illegal retention of sensitive national security documents. As part of the agreement, he has also agreed to pay a fine exceeding $2 million.

Bolton served as national security adviser under Trump from April 2018 to September 2019 before departing amid disagreements over foreign policy matters, including the administration's approach to North Korea and Afghanistan. After leaving the White House, he became known for his public criticism of the former president.

Court records indicate that Bolton is scheduled to appear in court to enter his plea on June 26. Multiple news outlets, including CNN, the Associated Press, and the New York Times, reported the provisional plea agreement after sources familiar with the case confirmed the details.

Bolton's legal troubles extend beyond the current classified documents case. After leaving the Trump administration, he published a memoir that sparked a separate legal battle. The Justice Department sought to block publication, arguing that Bolton had not completed the required pre-publication review process for works by former officials with access to classified material. Despite government objections, the book was ultimately published, though legal proceedings continued at the time.

The plea agreement resolves the classified documents investigation that had been underway for an unspecified period. Federal charges related to mishandling classified information typically carry significant penalties, though plea agreements often result in reduced consequences in exchange for cooperation and admission of wrongdoing.

Trump claimed when the charges were announced that he was not aware of the prosecution, though he characterized Bolton as a "bad guy" at the time. Bolton's case adds to a growing list of high-level officials who have faced legal consequences for their handling of sensitive government documents.

Bolton has been a prominent foreign policy voice in conservative circles throughout his career, serving in various Republican administrations before his tenure under Trump. His departure from the White House and subsequent emergence as a Trump critic marked a notable shift in his public profile.

The formal guilty plea hearing will allow Bolton to officially enter his plea before a federal judge, completing the agreement he has reached with prosecutors. Details regarding sentencing recommendations and other specific terms of the plea deal were not immediately disclosed.
