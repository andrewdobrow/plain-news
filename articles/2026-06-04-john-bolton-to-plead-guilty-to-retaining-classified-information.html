Former National Security Advisor John Bolton to plead guilty in classified documents case

John Bolton, who served as National Security Advisor under President Trump, has reached a plea agreement with federal prosecutors over charges related to the mishandling of classified national security information. The former diplomat is expected to enter a guilty plea to illegal retention of sensitive national security information, according to sources familiar with the matter.

The agreement, subject to court approval, marks a significant legal development for Bolton, who held one of the most sensitive positions in the U.S. government from April 2018 to September 2019. During his tenure, he had access to some of the nation's most closely guarded secrets regarding national security, foreign policy, and military operations.

The Justice Department filed charges against Bolton in 2025 as part of what sources describe as an onslaught against the president's critics. The case underscores ongoing concerns about the handling of classified materials by senior government officials and enforcement of laws designed to protect national security information from unauthorized disclosure or retention.

Bolton's case adds to a growing list of high-profile national security officials who have faced legal consequences for improper handling of classified information. Charges of retaining classified documents carry potential penalties including fines and imprisonment, though plea agreements typically involve reduced sentences in exchange for a guilty plea.

Before his time in the Trump administration, Bolton served in various government roles spanning several decades, including as U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations under President George W. Bush. After leaving the White House in 2019, he published a memoir that itself became the subject of legal disputes over whether it contained classified information. The Trump administration unsuccessfully sought to block the book's publication.

Bolton fell out with Trump during his first administration and was ousted from his national security adviser position in 2019. The two have maintained a political feud ever since. Bolton has also been critical of Trump's recent handling of the Russia-Ukraine war. After a Trump-Putin meeting in Alaska failed to yield progress on ending the war, Bolton wrote on social media that it appeared "Putin got to Trump."

Early in 2025, Trump revoked the security clearances of more than four dozen former intelligence officials, including Bolton. Bolton was also among a trio of former Trump officials whose security details were canceled by the president.

Bolton's book, The Room Where It Happened, became a bestseller despite the Trump administration's attempts to ban its publication. In it, he detailed what he described as impeachable conduct and accused Trump of asking China to help secure his re-election.

The Justice Department has not publicly released details about the specific classified materials involved in the case. Bolton has not been detained or charged with additional crimes beyond those covered by the plea agreement.
