Flyers tender offer sheet to Ducks center Leo Carlsson

The Philadelphia Flyers tendered an offer sheet to Anaheim Ducks star center Leo Carlsson on Friday, proposing a five-year contract worth $18 million annually. The move represents one of the boldest plays in this offseason's NHL free agency period and could reshape the competitive landscape in both the Eastern and Western conferences.

Offer sheets are rare in the NHL because they allow teams to poach restricted free agents from other franchises, often at the cost of multiple draft picks as compensation. The $90 million total value of the Flyers' proposal to Carlsson signals Philadelphia's intent to accelerate their rebuild around a young cornerstone player. Carlsson, who has emerged as a premier two-way center, would immediately become the highest-paid player on the Flyers roster.

The Ducks now face a critical decision. Anaheim has seven days to match the offer and retain Carlsson, or accept compensation from Philadelphia in the form of draft picks. The compensation structure is determined by the average annual value of the contract. At $18 million per year, the Ducks would receive four first-round picks if they decline to match, a substantial haul that could fuel their own rebuilding efforts.

Carlsson's addition would give the Flyers a franchise center to build around as they continue to reshape their roster following several seasons of inconsistency. Philadelphia has been actively retooling through the draft and free agency, and landing a player of Carlsson's caliber would represent a significant step forward in their competitive timeline. The 22-year-old center posted strong numbers last season and is viewed as one of the league's rising stars.

The Ducks must now weigh whether to commit significant long-term salary to Carlsson or accept the draft capital and redirect those resources elsewhere. The decision carries major implications for both franchises as they navigate different stages of their competitive cycles. The league will be watching closely to see whether Anaheim matches the offer or allows one of their cornerstone players to depart for a historic draft pick return.
