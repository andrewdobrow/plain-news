Lyft and Baidu begin robotaxi testing in London

Lyft and Baidu have launched autonomous vehicle testing in London, marking the entry of two major technology companies into the city's emerging robotaxi market. Baidu's Apollo Go self-driving vehicles will be available through Freenow, the European mobility network that Lyft acquired in 2025.

The partnership combines Baidu's autonomous driving technology with Lyft's established ride-hailing infrastructure in Europe. Apollo Go has been operating autonomous taxi services in several Chinese cities, and this expansion represents the company's first deployment in the United Kingdom. Freenow operates across multiple European markets and provides Baidu with an existing customer base and operational framework.

London has become a testing ground for autonomous vehicle companies seeking to demonstrate their technology in dense urban environments. The city's complex traffic patterns, narrow streets, and mix of pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles present technical challenges that differ from the suburban and highway settings where many robotaxi services first launched. Multiple companies are now competing to establish autonomous ride-hailing services in the capital.

The testing phase will allow Baidu to collect data on how its vehicles perform in London conditions and refine the technology before any potential commercial launch. Lyft's acquisition of Freenow in 2025 positioned the company to expand beyond its traditional North American market and compete with other ride-hailing services in Europe. The addition of autonomous vehicles could differentiate Freenow from competitors and reduce long-term operational costs.

Regulatory frameworks for autonomous vehicles in the UK continue to develop as technology companies push forward with testing and deployment plans. The collaboration between Lyft and Baidu demonstrates how established ride-hailing companies are partnering with autonomous vehicle developers rather than building the technology entirely in-house. The outcome of testing in London could influence how quickly robotaxis become available to the general public in the UK and other European cities.
