Former South Korean President Yoon Sentenced to 30 Years for Drone Flights Over North Korea

A South Korean court has sentenced ousted President Yoon Suk Yeol to 30 years in prison for authorizing drone flights over Pyongyang in October 2024. The Seoul Central District Court found Yoon guilty of abuse of power and aiding the enemy, concluding that he had conspired in the drone operation from the beginning.

Prosecutors argued that Yoon ordered the flights to fabricate what they called "wartime conditions" that would justify his declaration of martial law. The operation, they contended, was designed to heighten tensions with North Korea and create a pretext for domestic political action. Special prosecutors stated in April that the drone incursions undermined state security.

Yoon has denied all wrongdoing. His legal team argued that he neither ordered nor approved the operation, which they characterized as unrelated to his martial law declaration. Instead, they said the flights were a response to months of North Korean balloon launches that dropped rubbish across the border.

In October 2024, North Korea accused Seoul of flying drones over Pyongyang to drop propaganda leaflets on three separate occasions. South Korea's defense ministry initially issued a vague denial before saying it could neither confirm nor deny the allegations. While the incursions raised tensions between the two countries, they did not result in military clashes.

Prosecutors also argued that the operation led to the leak of classified information about South Korean military capabilities after the drones crashed. This security breach further complicated an already tense situation on the peninsula.

This latest conviction adds to mounting legal troubles for Yoon, who already faces severe penalties from previous cases. In February, he received a life sentence for leading an insurrection related to his martial law declaration, which he claimed was declared "solely for the sake of the nation." He has appealed that conviction.

Yoon was removed from office last year after South Korea's Constitutional Court upheld his impeachment. A snap election followed, won by liberal President Lee Jae Myung. Lee himself faced diplomatic complications when an investigation revealed that government officials had sent drones into North Korea in January. Lee expressed regret over the operation, which North Korea's leader Kim Jong-un's sister initially praised as "wise behaviour."

Drone flights remain a significant source of tension between the two Koreas, which technically remain at war. The fragile military situation on the peninsula means that airspace violations can quickly escalate into serious confrontations.

Yoon, who is currently in custody, has the right to appeal Friday's ruling. Legal experts note that his accumulated sentences could result in lengthy imprisonment.
