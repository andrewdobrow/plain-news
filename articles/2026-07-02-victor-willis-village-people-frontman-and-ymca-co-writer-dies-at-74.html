Victor Willis, Village People Frontman and Y.M.C.A. Co-Writer, Dies at 74

Victor Willis, the original lead singer of the Village People and co-writer of the disco anthem Y.M.C.A., has died at 74. The group announced that Willis passed away on Monday, June 30, 2026, following "a short but aggressive illness."

Willis joined the Village People in 1977 after being discovered by French producer Jacques Morali while performing on Broadway. Morali had conceived the group as a concept band based on American stereotypes, telling his lawyer: "We have an idea to put together a very special group, very American and very happy." Willis performed as a naval officer or policeman as part of the band's distinctive costume concept.

As the group's frontman during its peak years, Willis helped create some of disco's most enduring hits. The 1978 single Y.M.C.A. became a global phenomenon, reaching number one in 17 countries. The band followed with other major successes including Macho Man and In the Navy. Despite widespread interpretation of these songs as gay anthems, Willis refuted this characterization and threatened legal action against news organizations making such claims. "That is a false assumption based on the fact that my writing partner was gay, and some (not all) of Village People were gay," he stated in 2024.

Willis left the Village People in 1980 as the group prepared for the poorly received feature film Can't Stop the Music. He briefly returned for the 1982 album Fox on the Box before departing again the following year. A solo album he recorded in 1979 remained unreleased until 2015.

After leaving the music industry, Willis struggled with substance abuse and legal troubles for decades. In 1993, he was charged with rape and assault but was acquitted. He faced multiple drug and firearms arrests beginning in 2005, including possession of cocaine and a handgun. Following probation violations and a string of offenses, a judge granted him leniency in 2006, citing his "potential which remains untapped," and sent him to the Betty Ford rehabilitation clinic in California.

Willis maintained a complicated relationship with Y.M.C.A.'s adoption at political rallies. He initially approved its use for Donald Trump's 2020 presidential campaign but retracted his support as the Black Lives Matter movement gained prominence. He later reversed his position again, acknowledging that Trump's use of the track had "greatly benefited the song," even as he stated his ideological opposition to the president and his support for Kamala Harris in 2024. The Village People performed at Trump's second inauguration events in 2025.

Willis was born on July 1, 1951, in Texas and grew up singing in his father's Baptist church. He met his future wife, actress Phylicia Rashad, while both starred in the Broadway musical The Wiz. They were married from 1978 to the early 1980s.
