Scientists find evidence of fire use by early humans 1.8 million years ago in South African cave

Researchers have discovered evidence suggesting early humans used fire nearly 1.8 million years ago in a South African cave, pushing back the timeline for controlled fire use by hundreds of thousands of years. The findings come from a site called Wonderwerk Cave, where an international team of scientists including Israeli researchers identified signs of ancient fire use that predate previous estimates by a significant margin.

Previous archaeological evidence had placed the earliest confirmed use of fire by humans at around 780,000 years ago. This new discovery extends that timeline by more than a million years, potentially rewriting our understanding of human technological development and evolution. The cave site has provided material evidence that researchers say demonstrates early human ancestors had the ability to harness and control fire far earlier than previously thought.

The ability to control fire represented a major technological advancement for early humans, providing warmth, protection from predators, a means to cook food, and light during dark hours. These capabilities would have offered significant survival advantages and may have played a role in human brain development and social organization. The discovery suggests that our ancestors developed this critical skill much earlier in their evolutionary history than scientists had established.

The research team analyzed materials from the cave using modern scientific techniques to identify traces of ancient fires. The South African cave site has been an important location for studying early human history and technological development. The findings add to growing evidence about the capabilities of early human ancestors and their ability to adapt to their environments through technological innovation.

This discovery has implications for understanding human evolution and the development of early human societies. The timeline shift requires researchers to reconsider theories about when and how early humans developed the cognitive abilities necessary to control fire, as well as how this technology may have influenced human migration patterns and settlement choices. The research contributes to ongoing efforts to piece together the complex history of human technological advancement during the earliest periods of human existence.
