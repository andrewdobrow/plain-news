Ukraine Launches Largest Drone Strike on Moscow Since War Began

Ukraine struck Moscow with nearly 200 drones in what officials on both sides described as the largest aerial attack on the Russian capital since the full-scale war began in February 2022. The assault targeted an oil refinery and a shopping center in the southeastern part of the city, setting both facilities ablaze and forcing the closure of Moscow's airports for several hours.

The barrage focused on a major oil refinery in the Kapotno area, with the aim of shutting down its operations. A shopping center in the same region also caught fire during the attack. As drones moved toward their targets during the early morning hours, Russian air defense systems engaged the incoming aircraft. Explosions and fires became visible across the southeastern districts of the capital as the strike unfolded. The scale of the operation marked a significant escalation in Ukraine's campaign to strike deep inside Russian territory.

The attack disrupted transportation in the capital. Moscow's major airports suspended operations for several hours as authorities responded to the drone barrage, affecting both domestic and international flights. Russian officials activated air defense protocols across the capital region while emergency crews worked to contain the refinery fire. The damage to civilian infrastructure at the shopping center added to concerns about the scope of strikes being directed at Moscow.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the attack as a response to Russian strikes on a historic monastery complex in Kyiv earlier in the week. The assault appeared to surprise most Muscovites, as the city does not typically issue air raid warnings to residents, prompting panicked messages across social media.

Since the war began, Ukraine has increasingly targeted Russian energy infrastructure and military facilities far from the front lines as part of its strategy to degrade Russia's capacity to wage war. Earlier drone attacks on Moscow have been smaller in scale, typically involving dozens rather than hundreds of aircraft. The Kremlin has condemned such strikes as acts of terrorism, while Ukrainian officials argue they represent legitimate military operations targeting infrastructure that supports Russia's invasion.

The Moscow refinery represents a key piece of energy infrastructure serving the capital region. Its temporary shutdown could affect fuel supplies throughout the area. Ukraine has significantly expanded its domestic drone production capacity since the war began, allowing it to launch more frequent and larger strikes on targets inside Russian territory.

The attack occurred as fighting continued along a largely static front line in eastern and southern Ukraine. Both countries have faced mounting casualties and infrastructure damage as the conflict has persisted, with no clear end in sight despite ongoing diplomatic discussions about potential peace negotiations.
