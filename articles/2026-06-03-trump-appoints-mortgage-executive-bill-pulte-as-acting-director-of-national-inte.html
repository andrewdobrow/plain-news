Republican lawmaker urges Trump to reverse acting intelligence director pick to preserve surveillance powers

Rep. Don Bacon of Nebraska called on President Trump on Monday to cancel Bill Pulte's appointment as acting Director of National Intelligence, warning that the move could jeopardize surveillance authorities under the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act.

Pulte currently serves as Director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency. Bacon argued that maintaining FISA's surveillance capabilities should take priority over the personnel change, stating that the law provides more than half of the nation's most sensitive intelligence needed for national security operations.

The Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act authorizes the government to conduct electronic surveillance and gather intelligence on foreign powers and their agents. The law requires periodic congressional reauthorization, and lawmakers are currently debating whether to extend its provisions as they approach expiration.

Bacon's public call for Trump to reconsider the appointment reflects divisions within the Republican Party regarding intelligence gathering authorities. Some GOP members have expressed doubts about FISA's implementation, particularly after previous revelations about surveillance applications that targeted American citizens. The Nebraska congressman appears concerned that Pulte's appointment could complicate efforts to secure the necessary votes for reauthorization.

The position of acting Director of National Intelligence carries significant responsibility, overseeing the 18 agencies that comprise the intelligence community and serving as the principal intelligence advisor to the president. Leadership changes at intelligence agencies typically occur during periods of congressional debate over surveillance programs, creating potential complications for legislative outcomes.

Bacon's intervention suggests he believes the appointment could influence how Congress votes on extending FISA authorities. Intelligence officials maintain that these surveillance powers remain essential to counterterrorism efforts and national security operations. However, the reauthorization debate has intensified as concerns about government surveillance and civil liberties continue to draw scrutiny from various quarters.

The timing of Bacon's statement highlights the tension between those who view FISA as necessary for protecting the nation and those who question its scope and implementation. As the administration considers changes to intelligence agency leadership, lawmakers must balance security priorities against the ongoing debate over surveillance authorities and their potential impact on American citizens.

The congressional reauthorization process for FISA has historically sparked disagreement between those prioritizing national security and those emphasizing privacy protections. Bacon's public appeal to Trump demonstrates that this debate remains central to discussions about intelligence policy, even as the administration pursues organizational changes within the intelligence community. Whether Trump will heed Bacon's recommendation remains unclear, but the congressman's statement underscores the stakes involved in both the personnel decision and the broader legislative battle over surveillance powers.
