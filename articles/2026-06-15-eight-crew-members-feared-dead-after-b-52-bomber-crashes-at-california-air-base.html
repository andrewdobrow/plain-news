Air Force identifies eight crew members killed in B-52 crash at California base

The Air Force released the names of eight crew members who died when a B-52 Stratofortress crashed at Edwards Air Force Base in California. The victims included service members, Boeing employees, and a base engineer who had been marking his wedding anniversary.

The crash occurred at Edwards Air Force Base, a major testing and development facility in the Mojave Desert. The B-52 Stratofortress is a long-range strategic bomber that has been in service since the 1950s and remains a key component of the Air Force fleet. The aircraft was operating at the base when the fatal incident took place.

Among those killed was an engineer described by his wife as a leader and hero. The victims represented a mix of military personnel and civilian contractors working with the aircraft. Boeing employees were aboard the aircraft as part of testing or maintenance operations typical at the base, which serves as a center for aircraft development and evaluation.

Edwards Air Force Base is located in southern California and has long served as a testing ground for military and experimental aircraft. The facility has been the site of numerous aviation milestones and continues to play a role in aircraft certification and evaluation. The B-52 fleet undergoes regular maintenance and testing to keep the aging bombers operational.

The Pentagon released the identities of all eight victims following standard notification procedures for next of kin. Families of those killed received official confirmation before the public announcement. The crash represents one of the deadliest incidents at Edwards Air Force Base in recent years and highlights the risks associated with military aviation testing and operations.
