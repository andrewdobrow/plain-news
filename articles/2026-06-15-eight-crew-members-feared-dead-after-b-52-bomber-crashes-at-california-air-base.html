Eight killed in B-52 crash at Edwards Air Force Base in California

A Boeing B-52 Stratofortress bomber carrying eight people crashed at Edwards Air Force Base in the Mojave Desert during a test flight on Monday, killing everyone on board. The aircraft went down in a fiery explosion that sent thick plumes of smoke into the air at the base, located about 100 miles northeast of Los Angeles.

Flight tracking data shows the bomber made a sharp right turn after takeoff, followed by a near 180-degree turn before descending at approximately 5,056 feet per minute, nearly ten times the normal descent rate for landing preparation. The aircraft was supporting a radar modernization program when the crash occurred.

The eight victims included Boeing employees, government workers, military personnel, and civilian contractors. Two have been publicly identified as Lt. Col. Miles Middleton and Jeromy Smith, an engineer at the base. According to Smith's wife, he was "a leader" and "a hero."

According to Col James Hayes, the deputy commander for the 412th Test Wing at Edwards, the B-52 was equipped with a modernized radar system designed to keep the bomber operational through at least 2050, nearly a century after the aircraft first entered service in the 1950s.

Aviation safety expert Jeff Guzzetti, who previously investigated crashes for the Federal Aviation Administration and the National Transportation Safety Board, suggested the crash may have resulted from a flight control problem. "I think it was definitely a controllability issue," Guzzetti said. "Now, whether that was tied to an engine failure, a flight control failure, or some new testing device failure, I'm not sure."

He noted that issues could include controls rigged incorrectly after maintenance work, a catastrophic engine problem, or failure of equipment being tested.

The investigation into the cause could take up to six months to complete, officials said. The airfield remained closed as crews made the crash site safe for search and recovery teams. Fires flared up overnight at the scene, according to Mike Paoli, a spokesperson for the 412 Test Wing.

Edwards Air Force Base serves as a major testing facility for military aircraft. The 412th Test Wing conducts developmental testing of all Air Force aircraft, weapons systems, software, and components before purchase and throughout their operational lifespan.

This crash represents one of the more serious military aviation accidents in recent years. It comes as the Air Force continues to rely on its aging B-52 fleet, which remains a key component of American strategic military capabilities despite its decades of service. The military has maintained and upgraded the bombers over the years, though the fleet's advanced age presents ongoing maintenance and safety considerations.
