Eight killed in B-52 bomber crash at Edwards Air Force Base in California

Eight people died when a B-52 Stratofortress bomber crashed at Edwards Air Force Base in California. An Air Force colonel confirmed the loss of eight Americans in the fiery crash at the base.

The Boeing B-52 Stratofortress went down at the base located in the Mojave Desert. Edwards Air Force Base, situated in southern California, serves as a key testing facility for military aircraft. The crash occurred involving one of the long-range strategic bombers that has been in service with the US military since the 1950s.

The B-52 has been a mainstay of American air power for over seven decades. The aircraft typically carries a crew of five, though the bomber can accommodate additional personnel for training missions or specialized operations. The specific circumstances of the crash have not been detailed in official statements.

Edwards Air Force Base confirmed the incident through its official channels. The base has historically served as a testing ground for experimental and operational military aircraft. Military officials have acknowledged the fatalities but have not released the identities of those killed pending notification of their families.

The crash represents one of the deadliest incidents involving a B-52 in recent years. The Air Force will conduct an investigation to determine the cause of the accident. The Boeing-manufactured bomber fleet continues to serve as part of the US strategic nuclear and conventional strike capabilities.
