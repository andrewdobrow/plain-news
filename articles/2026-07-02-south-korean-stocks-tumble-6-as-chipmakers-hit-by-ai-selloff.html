South Korean stocks tumble 6% as chipmakers hit by AI selloff

South Korean stock markets experienced a sharp decline on Thursday, with the main index dropping 6% as chipmakers faced losses in a global retreat from artificial intelligence-related investments. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, two of the world's largest memory chip producers, saw their shares fall more than 7% during the trading session.

The Seoul market weakness followed declines on Wall Street, where technology stocks had pulled major indexes lower. Investor sentiment has shifted as concerns grew about whether valuations in the AI sector have become stretched. This apprehension triggered a broad pullback from semiconductor stocks that have performed strongly over the past year due to surging demand for AI hardware.

The selloff rippled across Asian markets on Thursday, with chipmakers bearing the largest losses. Stock futures in the United States also signaled further potential weakness ahead, suggesting that volatility in technology shares could persist as traders awaited key employment data scheduled for later in the day.

South Korean chipmakers have been among the biggest winners from the AI boom, with Samsung and SK Hynix supplying high-bandwidth memory chips essential for data centers and AI training systems. The sharp decline in their stock prices marks a significant reversal from the robust gains these companies had achieved earlier in the year.

The broader regional market response highlighted growing skepticism about whether the recent AI-driven rallies have pushed stock valuations beyond sustainable levels. Technology investors across Asia retreated from their positions, with the movement spreading outward from initial Wall Street losses through exchanges in Japan, South Korea, and other major regional markets.

The timing of the selloff reflects multiple pressures on investor confidence. Market participants have grown increasingly cautious about whether the current levels of AI-related stock valuations can be justified. Additionally, concerns about potential interest rate increases from the Federal Reserve have added to the uncertainty weighing on technology stocks globally.

The losses represent a stark contrast to the enthusiasm that characterized the technology sector in earlier months. Companies whose valuations are heavily tied to artificial intelligence developments have faced particular pressure as investors reassess risk and potential returns in the sector.

The volatility underscores the sensitivity of markets to shifting sentiment around emerging technologies. Semiconductor companies, which sit at the center of AI infrastructure development, have become key indicators of investor confidence in the broader AI investment thesis. As traders worldwide reassess their positions, the movements in South Korean chipmaker stocks serve as a barometer for the health of global technology investments and the sustainability of valuations that have grown rapidly since AI applications gained widespread attention.
