Trump to appear on Meet the Press as approval ratings hit new lows

President Trump is scheduled to sit down for an interview Sunday on NBC's Meet the Press following a turbulent week marked by new lows in his approval ratings. The appearance comes as tensions with Senate Republicans have spilled into public view.

The president faces mounting political pressure on key issues including the economy as midterm elections approach. Growing friction with members of his own party has created additional headwinds for the administration heading into the campaign season.

Trump's second-term approval rating fell to 36 percent in December, among the lowest ever recorded by Gallup, the public opinion polling agency. He began his second term with an approval rating of 47 percent, marking a significant decline over the course of several months.

The timing of this interview comes as Gallup announced it will stop tracking presidential approval ratings after almost nine decades. A spokesperson for the company confirmed the decision was "solely based on Gallup's research goals and priorities" and reflects an evolution in how the agency focuses its public research efforts. The Gallup Presidential Approval Rating has been a widely cited barometer of public opinion on presidential performance since Harry Truman's administration.

Trump has been aggressive in pursuing action against polling organizations and media companies that report unfavorable numbers about his popularity. In January, after the New York Times released a poll showing his approval rating at 40 percent, Trump threatened to expand his pre-existing lawsuit for defamation against the newspaper. He posted on Truth Social that the Times poll, which he described as "tremendously negative," would be added to his litigation.

The New York Times defended its polling methodology in response to Trump's criticism. Spokesperson Charlie Stadtlander stated in a comment posted on X that "President Trump likes polls that appear favorable to him and dislikes polls that do not. But whether a poll is good or bad for the president has no bearing on our methodology."

The president has consistently criticized media outlets for what he characterizes as biased reporting on his approval numbers. His public complaints about unfavorable polling have become a regular feature of his communications with supporters and the media.

As Trump prepares for his Meet the Press appearance, he faces questions about his economic policies and his relationship with Republican senators. The tensions within the party have become increasingly visible in recent weeks, potentially affecting party unity heading into the midterm elections.

The Sunday interview represents an opportunity for Trump to address concerns about his approval ratings and explain his administration's policies directly to voters. The appearance will likely touch on the economic challenges and political dynamics that have contributed to his declining favorability numbers in recent polling.
