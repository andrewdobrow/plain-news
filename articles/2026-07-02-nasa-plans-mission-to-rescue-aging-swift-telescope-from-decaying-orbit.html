NASA Launches Rescue Mission to Save Falling Swift Space Telescope

A rescue mission launched Friday to save a NASA space observatory that has been falling back toward Earth. The Swift telescope, which weighs 3,200 pounds, faces an untimely end after solar storms degraded its orbit over time.

Northrop Grumman's Pegasus rocket carried the rescue spacecraft into orbit to rendezvous with the Swift observatory. The mission aims to boost the telescope back to a stable orbital altitude, extending its operational life. Teams have successfully made contact with the spacecraft designed to perform the orbital boost maneuver.

The Swift observatory has served NASA for years, but increased solar activity has accelerated the natural decay of its orbit. Solar storms create atmospheric drag that pulls satellites gradually closer to Earth. Without intervention, Swift would eventually reenter the atmosphere and be destroyed.

The rescue represents a novel approach to satellite servicing in orbit. Rather than allowing the aging telescope to fall and burn up, NASA chose to launch a dedicated mission to extend its usefulness. The operation requires precise navigation to match orbits with Swift and execute the boost safely.

NASA officials have not specified how much additional operational time the orbital boost will provide. The success of this mission could establish a model for rescuing other valuable scientific instruments threatened by orbital decay. The Swift telescope continues scientific observations while the rescue spacecraft approaches for the planned rendezvous and boost operation.
