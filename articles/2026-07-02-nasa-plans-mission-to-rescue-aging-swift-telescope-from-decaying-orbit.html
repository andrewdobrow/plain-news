NASA launches mission to rescue falling Swift space telescope

A rescue mission launched to save a NASA observatory that has been falling toward Earth after years of solar storm activity degraded its orbit. The Swift spacecraft, which weighs 3,200 pounds, has been studying gamma-ray bursts and other cosmic phenomena since its launch in 2004.

The spacecraft has been losing altitude due to increased atmospheric drag caused by solar storms expanding Earth's atmosphere. Without intervention, Swift would have reentered the atmosphere and been destroyed. The rescue mission aims to boost the telescope back to a stable orbit where it can continue its scientific work.

Teams have established contact with the spacecraft sent to rendezvous with Swift. The mission represents a new approach to extending the life of valuable scientific instruments in orbit. Swift has made significant contributions to astronomy over its two decades in space, observing transient cosmic events that provide insights into the universe's most energetic processes.

The observatory studies gamma-ray bursts, which are brief flashes of high-energy radiation from distant galaxies. These events help scientists understand stellar explosions and the formation of black holes. Swift also monitors other cosmic phenomena, including supernovae and asteroid activity.

The rescue effort highlights both the value of long-running space missions and the challenges of maintaining aging spacecraft. Solar activity follows an 11-year cycle, with periods of increased storms affecting satellites in low Earth orbit. The successful boost would allow Swift to continue operations and demonstrate techniques that could be used to save other scientific satellites facing similar orbital decay.
