Russian Strike on Kyiv Kills at Least 27 in Largest Assault on Capital

Russia launched one of its most extensive attacks on Kyiv, killing at least 27 people in what officials described as the deadliest night raid on the Ukrainian capital in recent months. The overnight bombardment deployed the largest number of weapons yet used against the city, striking more than 20 locations across residential and infrastructure targets.

Ukrainian air force data showed that Russia fired 629 missiles and drones in the assault, including 11 ballistic missiles, 20 cruise missiles, and 598 Shahed drones. Air defense systems intercepted or jammed a large proportion of the incoming weapons, including 563 of the drones, but several high-speed ballistic missiles and cruise missiles struck their targets.

The heaviest toll came when a five-story residential building in the eastern Darnytskyi district was hit in the middle of the night, killing 17 people including at least four children. Rescue services said 10 more people were missing from the building, though not all may have been inside at the time. Another person died in a separate strike near Kyiv's central railway station, where attacks damaged buildings housing offices of the EU delegation to Ukraine and the British Council. Both organizations confirmed their offices sustained severe damage.

Waves of explosions shook the capital starting after 3am and continuing after 5:30am. Damage was recorded at around 30 locations throughout the city, with most being ordinary residential buildings. A railway siding in eastern Kyiv was also hit, leaving some intercity train carriages burned out. A total of 38 people were wounded in the attacks.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had previously warned that Moscow was preparing a major strike in response to Ukrainian forces hitting targets inside Russian territory. He said the bombardment demonstrated Russia's rejection of peace efforts. "These Russian missiles and attack drones today are a clear response to everyone in the world who has been calling for a ceasefire and for real diplomacy," Zelenskyy said on social media.

The assault marked a return to intensive bombing of Kyiv after a relatively quieter August period. Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko called it a barbaric attack and declared Friday a day of mourning for the capital.

Zelenskyy called for an international response, including further sanctions against Russia. He specifically appealed to China, Russia's ally, to respond to the strikes. The Kremlin said it remained interested in diplomacy but would continue launching strikes on Ukraine.

The attack represents a significant escalation in the conflict, with both sides demonstrating willingness to strike deeper into enemy territory and target civilian areas.
