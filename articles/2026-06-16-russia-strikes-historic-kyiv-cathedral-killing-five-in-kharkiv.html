Russia launches major missile and drone strikes on Kyiv, killing at least eight

Russia struck Kyiv with a large-scale missile and drone attack early Thursday, killing at least eight people and injuring five others. The strikes set residential buildings ablaze across the Ukrainian capital in what officials described as the biggest assault on the city in weeks. Strong explosions rocked Kyiv hours after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Moscow was planning a massive attack.

Multiple residential buildings were hit in the strikes, which involved both missiles and drones. Emergency crews worked to extinguish fires and search for survivors amid the damaged structures. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed that residential areas bore the brunt of the assault, which caused widespread damage across several districts of the capital.

The timing of the attack followed Zelensky's public warning about an impending major Russian offensive. Ukrainian air defense systems engaged the incoming missiles and drones, though several projectiles reached their targets. The scale of the assault marked a significant escalation in attacks on the capital, which had seen reduced strike activity in recent weeks compared to earlier phases of the conflict.

The strike comes as Russia continues its military campaign in Ukraine, with periodic intensification of attacks on major urban centers. Kyiv has been targeted repeatedly throughout the war, though the frequency and intensity of strikes on the capital have varied. Thursday's attack represented one of the most significant assaults on the city in recent months, both in terms of casualties and the volume of missiles and drones deployed.

Ukrainian authorities continue to assess the full extent of the damage from the strikes. Rescue operations were ongoing as emergency services worked to ensure no additional casualties remained trapped in damaged buildings. The attack underscores the continued vulnerability of Ukrainian cities to Russian missile and drone strikes, despite air defense systems that have intercepted many incoming threats throughout the conflict.
