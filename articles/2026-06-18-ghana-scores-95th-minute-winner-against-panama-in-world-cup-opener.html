Ghana scores 95th-minute winner against Panama in World Cup opener

Ghana secured a 1-0 victory over Panama with a stoppage-time goal at the 2026 FIFA World Cup in Toronto, Canada. Caleb Yirenkyi netted the decisive strike in the 95th minute, steering in Brandon Thomas-Asante's cross to claim three points in their Group L opener.

The match remained tightly contested throughout, with few clear-cut opportunities for either side. Panama's Cecilio Waterman came closest to breaking the deadlock early in the second minute when he met a cross from the right, but Ghana goalkeeper Lawrence Ati-Zigi made a good save to deny him. The rainy conditions in Toronto appeared to make the contest even more cautious, with both teams struggling to create meaningful chances in what proved to be a cagey affair.

Throughout regulation time, neither team managed to find the back of the net. The match remained scoreless as it progressed into the closing stages, setting up a dramatic finale. Yirenkyi's late intervention proved decisive, denying Panama a first-ever point at a World Cup finals tournament. The Central American nation was making their second World Cup appearance after competing in Russia in 2018, where they failed to advance from the group stage.

With this victory, Ghana joined England at the top of Group L. The West African nation is competing in their fourth World Cup finals, having previously reached the quarterfinals in 2010. The win demonstrates Ghana's experience in high-pressure situations and represents a valuable foundation for their tournament campaign.

Panama will need to respond quickly as they continue their Group L fixtures. The team earned qualification for the expanded 48-team tournament format but faced a difficult opening assignment against more experienced World Cup competition. Despite their disappointing start, the Central American side still has opportunities to build momentum in the group stage.

The match took place amid ongoing discussion regarding mid-half hydration breaks implemented at the tournament. The breaks, designed to help players manage the physical demands of competition, drew mixed reactions from spectators at St. James' Park. Supporters expressed frustration with the interruptions to play, while coaches have utilized the stoppages for tactical adjustments and strategic discussions with their teams.

Ghana's victory showcases the importance of clinical finishing in knockout-style moments. While Panama maintained defensive discipline for large portions of the match, they were ultimately undone by a lapse in the closing minutes. The result sends a clear message about Ghana's intentions in the tournament as they look to advance deeper into the competition than their previous World Cup appearances.
