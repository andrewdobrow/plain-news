Kennedy Center board to appeal ruling requiring removal of Trump's name

The Kennedy Center's board of trustees voted to appeal a federal judge's ruling that requires President Trump's name to be removed from the performing arts center in Washington, D.C. The board, which Trump appointed, decided to seek the appeal before U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper's Friday deadline for removal of the signage.

Judge Cooper sided with a lawsuit challenging the addition of Trump's name to the building. The judge ruled last month that the board had "overstepped its statutory bounds by unilaterally renaming the Kennedy Center after President Trump." The lawsuit was brought by Rep. Joyce Beatty, a Democrat from Ohio who serves as an ex officio member of the Kennedy Center Board of Trustees.

The appeal could extend the ongoing dispute over Trump's name and shape the future direction of the renowned arts institution. The board voted on Thursday to seek a stay of the judge's ruling, according to multiple reports.

The naming controversy emerged after Trump returned to office and significantly changed the composition of the Kennedy Center's board. The board voted to rename the facility "The Donald J Trump and the John F Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts." Trump himself weighed in on the decision, suggesting the center pursue a comprehensive renovation project.

The renaming sparked significant backlash from the arts community. Major performing groups and artists canceled bookings in protest. The musical Hamilton withdrew its scheduled run. Jean Davidson, the executive director of the National Symphony Orchestra, left her position to lead the Los Angeles-based Wallis Annenberg Center for the Performing Arts.

Since the renaming, the Kennedy Center's operations shifted to align more closely with the White House's cultural agenda. Trump hosted the World Cup draw at the venue in December and used the facility for other administration events, including addressing House Republicans and premiering a documentary about First Lady Melania Trump. He also hosted the Kennedy Center Honors there.

Last week, Ric Grenell, a Trump appointee who played a key role in the effort to overhaul the center, announced he was stepping down as president. Matt Floca, the operations chief, was confirmed as his replacement on Monday.

The board also voted to approve a comprehensive two-year renovation project at the facility. The center announced it would temporarily close after an Independence Day celebration to complete the work, with a grand reopening planned to follow. Trump stated that closing the center temporarily would allow for the most efficient renovation effort. The project, initiated during Trump's first term, represents one of several significant civic improvements planned for Washington.

The legal dispute continues to unfold as the appeal process moves forward.
