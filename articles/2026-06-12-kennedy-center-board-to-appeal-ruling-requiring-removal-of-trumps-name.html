Workers remove Trump's name from Kennedy Center following court order

Workers began removing Donald Trump's name from the Kennedy Center's facade early Saturday morning, hours after a federal court deadline passed on Friday night. The removal work started in the pre-dawn hours after an emergency appeal to block the removal was rejected by an appeals court.

The Kennedy Center had missed a two-week court-ordered deadline to remove the words "The Donald J Trump and" from its exterior by Friday at 11:59pm. U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper had previously ruled that Trump's name had been illegally added to the building and ordered its removal.

Trump's name was added to the Kennedy Center last December after his hand-picked board of trustees voted unanimously to rename the venue. The board consisted of trustees Trump selected after removing the 18 members appointed by former President Joe Biden. Trump also made himself chair of the board and seized control of the venue in February.

The Kennedy Center was originally designated by Congress as a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy and opened in 1971. Judge Cooper wrote in his 94-page opinion that "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it." The legal challenge was brought by Joyce Beatty, a Democratic representative from Ohio and ex-officio member of the Kennedy Center's board.

Workers erected scaffolding Friday evening and draped tarpaulin over the temporary structure at 2am to block public views. Despite the covering, workers were observed removing the letters around 3am. A crowd of more than 100 people gathered at a "Hands Off the Arts" rally in front of the center to witness the removal, with attendees chanting "Shame!" and "Take it down."

Trump and his legal team mounted a last-minute effort to keep his name on the building less than 36 hours before the deadline. At 1pm Friday, Judge Cooper ruled that Trump's lawyers had failed to demonstrate they were likely to win an appeal or that the Kennedy Center would suffer "irreparable harm" if the name was removed.

Justice Department lawyers then appealed Cooper's decision at 3:46pm, arguing to the appeals court that it made no sense to alter the signage only to potentially revert the name again after a successful appeal. This second emergency appeal was denied shortly after 7pm.

The Kennedy Center had already removed Trump's name from its website and sent emails offering tickets to events at "the Kennedy Center" without reference to Trump. The venue continued operating its regular programming schedule during the removal process.

Judge Cooper also issued a temporary block on Trump's demand to close the Kennedy Center for two years of renovations, which was scheduled to begin in July. Trump subsequently announced he was giving up control of the venue.
