Kennedy Center board appeals order to remove Trump's name from building

The Kennedy Center's board of trustees appealed a federal judge's order requiring the removal of President Trump's name from the performing arts venue, filing court documents Thursday just before the judge's deadline. The board also voted to seek a stay of the ruling, according to a person familiar with the private meeting.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper issued his decision on May 29, finding that Trump's name was illegally added to the building. The judge sided with a lawsuit brought by Rep. Jamie Raskin and other plaintiffs seeking removal of the signage from the Washington facility.

Cooper's 94-page ruling determined that the Kennedy Center's founding statute requires the venue to be named for President John F. Kennedy and prohibits other formal names or public memorials without congressional action. "Congress gave the Kennedy Center its name, and only Congress can change it," Cooper wrote.

The building's exterior still displayed "The Donald J Trump and The John F Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts" as of Monday afternoon, though the Kennedy Center had begun removing Trump's name from its website. The center's general counsel issued a memo instructing employees to change email signatures, letterheads, and other documents to refer to the venue simply as "The John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts" or "Kennedy Center." The deadline for completing changes to templates, forms, signage, brochures, and website pages was set for June 12, 2026.

The appeal could extend the legal dispute over Trump's name on the building, with no timeline established for resolution.

Cooper also temporarily blocked the Kennedy Center from closing for renovations this summer. The board, composed of Trump's appointees, had approved a $257 million "revitalization project" that would have shuttered the center for two years. The judge halted this plan as part of his ruling.

Trump responded to Cooper's decision with a lengthy statement on social media, expressing his lack of interest in pursuing what he characterized as a "hopeless journey."

The dispute reflects broader tensions surrounding Trump's second term and his influence on institutions where his name was added during his presidency. The Kennedy Center case represents one of the legal challenges now confronting decisions made by his appointed board members. The outcome will likely depend on the appeals court's interpretation of the center's statutory requirements and whether Congress might need to take action to formally resolve the naming dispute.
