Zelensky Proposes Direct Talks With Putin to End War

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has proposed direct face-to-face negotiations with Russian President Vladimir Putin in an open letter, arguing that only bilateral engagement between the two countries can resolve the conflict. Zelensky called for a full ceasefire and suggested meeting in a neutral country to restart peace discussions.

The proposal comes as diplomatic efforts around the Ukraine conflict remain fragmented. The EU has grown concerned about being sidelined from substantive negotiations, with European leaders increasingly worried that discussions between the US, Russia, and Ukraine have stalled while leaving the bloc vulnerable to accepting a deal it does not support. European capitals have long insisted that any talks affecting Ukraine must include Kyiv's participation.

Russia has continued to set preconditions for negotiations. The Kremlin demands that Ukraine withdraw its troops from the eastern Donbas region before serious talks can begin. Putin's advisers have indicated that he remains determined to seize remaining parts of the region by force this year before any meaningful discussions commence.

Ukraine has flatly rejected any suggestion that it would unilaterally withdraw from its own territory. This position has been strengthened by recent developments on the battlefield, where Russian advances have largely stalled. Both armies show mounting signs of exhaustion and continue to sustain heavy casualties while conducting strikes against each other's energy infrastructure.

The conflict shows few signs of diplomatic resolution. Analysts assess that a breakthrough remains unlikely given the current military stalemate and entrenched positions of both sides. German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius has suggested that Putin's recent peace remarks may be a tactical maneuver rather than a genuine move toward ending the war. "If he sees the end of this war approaching, couldn't he simply end it himself?" Pistorius said during a visit to Kyiv, suggesting that Putin's conditions indicate continued deception rather than serious intent to negotiate.

The diplomatic landscape has also been complicated by disputes over who should represent various parties in peace talks. The EU rejected Putin's suggestion that Gerhard Schröder, a former German chancellor with longstanding ties to Moscow, serve as a European mediator. EU top diplomat Kaja Kallas stated that allowing Russia to appoint a negotiator for Europe would be unwise, noting that Schröder's past work as a high-level lobbyist for Russian state-owned companies, including Nord Stream gas pipelines and Rosneft, made him unsuitable. "It is clear why Putin wants him to be the person, so that actually he would be sitting on both sides of the table," Kallas said.

A US-brokered ceasefire was scheduled to expire, with Moscow rejecting efforts to extend the truce. The path toward resolving the more than four-year conflict remains uncertain as all parties maintain competing demands and strategic positions.
