Zelensky Proposes Direct Talks With Putin to End War

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has called for face-to-face negotiations with Russian President Vladimir Putin in a public letter addressed directly to the Russian leader. The letter marks the first time Zelensky has written publicly to Putin since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022, and represents a significant diplomatic overture.

In the correspondence, Zelensky proposes a meeting in a neutral third country and calls for a full ceasefire. The letter serves as a sweeping criticism of Putin's 26 years in power while simultaneously arguing that only direct engagement between the two nations could resolve the conflict.

The proposal comes as diplomatic efforts continue on multiple fronts. Trump has stated that both sides will need to "make compromises" to reach a resolution. However, the Kremlin has not yet publicly responded to Zelensky's direct appeal for negotiations.

The path to peace remains complicated by fundamental disagreements between the parties. Russian officials continue to demand that Ukraine withdraw from the eastern Donbas region as a precondition for serious talks. Ukraine has firmly rejected any suggestion that it would unilaterally abandon its own territory, a position reinforced by recent battlefield developments where Russian advances have largely stalled.

Analysts suggest that a diplomatic breakthrough appears unlikely in the near term. Both armies show signs of mounting exhaustion while sustaining heavy casualties. The conflict has also shifted to strikes on energy infrastructure, indicating the grinding nature of the current military situation.

Russia has also proposed mediators to facilitate discussions. Putin recently suggested that Gerhard Schröder, the former German chancellor and longtime Putin ally, could serve as a European mediator. However, the European Union has dismissed this idea. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas stated that allowing Russia to appoint a negotiator on Europe's behalf would be unwise. She noted that Schröder's extensive work as a "high-level lobbyist for Russian state-owned companies," including roles with Nord Stream gas pipelines and Russian oil company Rosneft, disqualified him from serving as an impartial mediator.

German officials echoed these concerns. Defense Minister Boris Pistolius suggested that Putin's recent peace remarks may be a strategic deception aimed at diverting attention from Russian military weaknesses. Germany's Europe Minister Gunther Krichbaum questioned whether Schröder could act as an honest broker given his close relationship with Putin.

European leaders have increasingly insisted that any substantive peace negotiations must include Ukraine and that no decisions about the country should occur without Kyiv's involvement. Many European capitals fear that discussions between the US, Russia, and Ukraine have made limited progress while leaving the EU sidelined and vulnerable to accepting agreements it opposes.

A US-brokered ceasefire was scheduled to expire recently, with Moscow rejecting proposals to extend the truce.
