OPEC+ approves fourth oil output increase since Strait of Hormuz closure

OPEC and its allies agreed to raise their collective oil production quota by 188,000 barrels per day for July, marking the fourth increase since the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. The decision came during meetings held as the Middle East conflict continues to disrupt global oil flows.

The output increase represents a largely symbolic gesture, according to industry observers and cartel delegates. Vast quantities of the world's oil supply remain stranded due to the effective shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which much of the global oil supply typically flows. The ongoing conflict in Iran has prevented a ceasefire that would reopen the waterway.

The production adjustments come as OPEC+ attempts to manage global oil markets amid unprecedented supply disruptions. The cartel has now approved four separate quota increases since the strait's closure, though the actual ability to deliver additional barrels to global markets remains severely constrained by the blocked shipping route. The Strait of Hormuz typically serves as a vital passage for oil tankers moving crude from Middle Eastern producers to international buyers.

The decision reflects the cartel's efforts to signal its willingness to support global oil supply even as geopolitical circumstances limit the practical impact of production increases. With the strait closed and no immediate ceasefire in sight, the gap between OPEC+ production quotas and actual deliverable supply continues to widen. Industry analysts note that the quota adjustments may have minimal effect on global oil availability until the Strait of Hormuz reopens to commercial shipping.

OPEC+ delegates characterized the move as part of ongoing efforts to stabilize energy markets during the crisis. The cartel's ability to influence global oil prices and supply has been significantly hampered by the infrastructure disruption caused by the conflict, leaving producers with limited tools to respond to market conditions beyond symbolic quota adjustments.
