OPEC Plus to increase oil production despite Strait of Hormuz shutdown

OPEC Plus announced plans to increase oil production by 188,000 barrels per day amid ongoing instability in the Middle East. The decision comes as the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global energy transport, faces continued disruption from regional tensions.

The production increase appears largely symbolic given the current transportation constraints. Approximately 20 percent of global oil and liquefied natural gas typically flows through the strait, but that flow has nearly stopped since a conflict involving the US, Israel, and Iran began on February 28. About 850 vessels remain trapped in the Gulf region.

President Trump announced "Project Freedom," a plan to have the US Navy guide stranded ships out of the waterway. He framed the operation as a humanitarian gesture benefiting the United States, Middle Eastern countries, and Iran itself. However, the plan faced immediate skepticism from industry experts and those directly affected.

Within hours of Trump's announcement, Iran's Fars news agency reported that a US warship had been hit by two missiles after ignoring Iranian warnings. The US military denied the claim. Meanwhile, Brent crude oil prices rose more than 5 percent to $114.45 per barrel following reports of a missile threat to the United Arab Emirates and a fire aboard a South Korean vessel in the Gulf.

The US Central Command confirmed that two US-flagged merchant vessels successfully transited the strait, but shipping industry leaders questioned whether this operation could be sustained or represented only a limited effort. Trump provided no details on how more than 850 trapped vessels would be freed.

Seafarers union leader Sascha Meijer expressed cautious concerns about the proposal. "Seafarers stuck in the strait would really appreciate protection to get ships sailing out. But is this protection sure? How about mines? Are the ships insured?" An oil tanker captain currently in the strait, Raman Kapoor, said he would not personally attempt the crossing without certainty about crew safety.

Jakob Larsen, chief safety officer at the shipping industry group Bimco, emphasized that Iran's consent remained unclear. "Without consent from Iran to let commercial ships transit safely through the strait of Hormuz, it is currently not clear whether the Iranian threat to ships can be degraded or suppressed," he stated. He warned that "Project Freedom" could trigger new hostilities.

The US military indicated it would deploy guided-missile destroyers, over 100 land and sea-based aircraft, unmanned platforms, and 15,000 service members to support the operation.

The broader crisis threatens global economic stability. Oil prices currently sit approximately 50 percent higher than pre-conflict levels, intensifying concerns about demand destruction as consumers and businesses reduce consumption in response to sustained price increases.
