Chinese operatives use LinkedIn to target people with access to sensitive information

Chinese Intelligence Officers Target Western Professionals on Job Platforms

Chinese military intelligence services are conducting an aggressive recruitment campaign on LinkedIn and other job platforms, targeting government and military personnel across Five Eyes nations, according to a joint security bulletin from intelligence agencies in the UK, US, Australia, Canada and New Zealand.

The operatives pose as human resources consultants or employees of private companies and thinktanks, posting advertisements for nonexistent positions such as foreign policy analysts or defence experts. After reviewing applicants' CVs to identify those with access to sensitive information, they conduct virtual interviews while concealing their true identities.

During these conversations, the recruiters question candidates about their government contacts and military knowledge. They then ask applicants to write trial reports on topics including China's international relations, defence capabilities, or trade policy. Once candidates have demonstrated their access to useful information, the conversation typically moves to encrypted messaging platforms where they are pressed for more sensitive material.

Those who provide information receive payment ranging from hundreds to several thousand dollars per report through various financial platforms including PayPal, Payoneer, Zelle, Skrill, Wise, Western Union, e-transfer services and cryptocurrency.

The security agencies warn that several categories of workers face particular risk. These include people holding security clearances in defence, foreign affairs and security sectors; military personnel stationed in the Indo-Pacific region; and individuals with indirect or peripheral access to government information, such as academics, journalists, thinktank employees and those working in defence and policy sectors.

The bulletin emphasises that even individuals without direct access to classified information remain targets. Details about government policy, military strategies and capabilities could compromise national security if disclosed, the agencies stated.

MI5 has previously warned that Chinese spies seeking to recruit Britons in sensitive positions have used LinkedIn, with reports suggesting that at least 20,000 Britons have been approached through such recruitment schemes.

The security agencies caution that anyone involved in unauthorised disclosure of information faces potential prosecution for espionage. They recommend that professionals contacted by unknown individuals on job platforms exercise caution, particularly when outreach appears focused on their access to specific information. Security experts suggest verifying job offers through independent research and remaining alert to requests seeking details about workplace security practices or sensitive projects.

A Chinese embassy spokesperson rejected the allegations as "entirely fabricated" and "malicious slander," characterising the Five Eyes intelligence gathering activities as "the real threat to peace-loving countries."
