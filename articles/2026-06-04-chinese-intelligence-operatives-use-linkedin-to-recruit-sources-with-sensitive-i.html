Chinese intelligence operatives use LinkedIn to recruit sources with sensitive information

Chinese Military Intelligence Targets Five Eyes Personnel Through Fake Job Postings

Intelligence agencies from five allied nations have issued a joint warning about an escalating recruitment campaign by Chinese military intelligence services targeting government and military personnel through professional networking sites and job boards.

The bulletin, released by MI5, the FBI, Australia's ASIO, Canada's CSIS, and New Zealand's NZSIS, describes what they characterize as an "aggressive" strategy where Chinese intelligence officers pose as human resources consultants or employees of legitimate-appearing private companies. They advertise nonexistent positions such as foreign policy analysts or defence specialists on platforms including LinkedIn, Indeed, and Upwork.

The targeted recruitment focuses on individuals with direct or indirect access to classified or sensitive government information. The scope extends beyond those with formal security clearances to include academics, journalists, freelance writers, and think tank employees with connections to defence, security, policy, and economic sectors. Military personnel with knowledge of regional capabilities, particularly those stationed in the Indo-Pacific region, are also targeted.

The recruitment process follows a deliberate pattern. After posting job advertisements, the operatives review applicant credentials to identify individuals with potential access to useful information. Virtual interviews, conducted by recruiters who conceal their true identities, focus on extracting details about government contacts and military activities. Candidates are then asked to write sample reports on topics including China's international relations, defence matters, or trade issues.

As conversations progress, operatives shift to encrypted messaging platforms and escalate requests for increasingly sensitive information. Recruits receive payment ranging from hundreds to several thousand dollars per report through various financial channels, including PayPal, cryptocurrency, Wise, Western Union, and other platforms.

According to the advisory, China's military intelligence services seek to obtain privileged military, political, and economic intelligence that could provide strategic and tactical advantages. The bulletin notes that even information that does not directly involve classified material, such as details about government policy or military capabilities, could compromise national security.

The warning emphasizes that individuals involved in unauthorized disclosure of information face potential prosecution for espionage. Security training programs are increasingly incorporating guidance on recognizing recruitment attempts through social media and professional platforms.

This advisory represents a continuation of warnings about Chinese intelligence activities. MI5 previously reported that Chinese agents posing as recruitment consultants had attempted to target at least 20,000 British citizens working in sensitive positions.

A Chinese embassy spokesperson rejected the allegations, stating: "The allegation of so-called Chinese espionage threat is entirely fabricated and constitutes malicious slander." The spokesperson characterized intelligence gathering by Five Eyes countries as "the real threat to peace-loving countries."
