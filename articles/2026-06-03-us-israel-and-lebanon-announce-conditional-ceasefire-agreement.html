Israel Strikes Hezbollah Targets Hours After New Lebanon Cease-Fire Begins

Israel launched intensified military operations against Hezbollah positions shortly after a new cease-fire agreement with Lebanon took effect, according to Israeli officials and reports from the region. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered the escalation, stating he had instructed the military to "crush" Hezbollah through expanded offensive operations.

The U.S.-brokered cease-fire agreement, announced following negotiations in Washington, required a complete cessation of fire from Hezbollah and the evacuation of its fighters south of the Litani River in southern Lebanon. However, the truce quickly fractured as both sides resumed military operations within hours of the announcement.

Hezbollah responded to what it characterized as Israeli violations of the cease-fire by launching several attacks on Monday. The Iranian-backed group claimed responsibility for at least four drone strikes on Shomera barracks in northern Israel, attacks on two additional military barracks in the region, and another strike on a military post in Misgav Am. These operations occurred at short intervals throughout the day.

Netanyahu justified the military escalation in a video statement posted on his messaging channel. "I have ordered an even greater acceleration of our operations," he said. "It is true that they are attacking us with drones, including fiber-optic drones, but we have teams working on countermeasures and we will solve this issue. We will intensify our blows, increase our firepower, and we will crush them."

The Israeli air force conducted successive strikes across southern Lebanon and the Bekaa Valley in eastern Lebanon on Monday evening. According to Lebanon's National News Agency, these operations killed three people and prompted evacuation orders for ten villages. Israel accused Hezbollah of breaching the truce agreement, citing continued drone attacks across the border.

The escalation reflects deep skepticism about the cease-fire's viability. The original negotiations that produced the agreement did not include direct participation from Hezbollah, raising questions about whether the group would honor terms to which it was not party. The military force has operated with significant autonomy within Lebanon, maintaining capabilities separate from the Lebanese government.

According to Lebanese authorities, Israeli strikes since early March have killed more than 3,100 people. The Israeli military reported that one soldier was killed on Sunday in southern Lebanon, bringing the total number of Israeli military deaths to 23 since hostilities erupted on March 2. One civilian contractor has also been killed.

Two far-right Israeli ministers called for further expansion of military operations. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich and National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir separately urged intensified warfare and territorial control expansion.

The conflict between Israel and Hezbollah has persisted since March 2, with the April 17 cease-fire proving ineffective at stopping near-daily exchanges of fire between the two sides.
