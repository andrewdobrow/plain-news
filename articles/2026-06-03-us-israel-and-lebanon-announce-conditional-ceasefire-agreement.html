Israel and Lebanon Agree to Renew Cease-Fire

Israel and Lebanon have agreed to implement a renewed cease-fire arrangement brokered by the United States. The Trump administration announced the agreement following negotiations in Washington, with the deal requiring a complete cessation of fire from Hezbollah, an Iran-aligned armed group operating in southern Lebanon.

Under the terms of the agreement, Hezbollah must stop all attacks and evacuate its fighters from the area south of the Litani River in southern Lebanon. Both countries have rejected "any attempt, by any state or non-state actor, to hold Lebanon's future hostage," according to a joint statement released by the U.S. State Department.

While the agreement was signed between the governments of Lebanon and Israel, the actual fighting has been between Hezbollah and Israel. The Lebanese army has not been a direct party to the conflict. However, the Lebanese government has been negotiating with Israel in an effort to reassert government control over the country and disarm the armed group.

The two countries also agreed to create "pilot zones" in which the Lebanese armed forces will take exclusive control of territory to the exclusion of all non-state actors. The joint statement indicated that ongoing meetings in Washington would continue to flesh out the cease-fire details and plan its phased implementation.

However, the agreement faces immediate credibility questions. Despite the announced accord, Israel carried out multiple drone strikes in southern Lebanon on Thursday morning, raising doubts about commitment to the arrangement. Additionally, a previous cease-fire scheduled to take hold on April 17 failed, with both sides continuing hostilities and accusing each other of violations.

A Hezbollah official told media outlets that the group would not accept a partial cease-fire and had not been party to the talks. The group signaled privately, however, that it is ready for a complete cease-fire, meaning it would refrain from attacks if Israel also stopped strikes. Hezbollah has stated it will not return to the pre-war status quo, under which Israel conducted frequent strikes despite the existence of a previous cease-fire.

The cease-fire agreement resembles a 2024 arrangement in which Hezbollah agreed to pull back from south of the Litani River so Lebanese armed forces could deploy there. That earlier accord failed to achieve Hezbollah's full disarmament, and Israel carried out more than 10,000 strikes in violation of the cease-fire during the 15 months that followed.

The negotiations come as the U.S. seeks to resolve broader regional conflicts, including the war with Iran. President Trump has indicated he wants to separate talks on the Lebanon conflict from negotiations with Iran, though Tehran insists the conflicts are linked. The fate of Hezbollah is understood to be connected to outcomes of negotiations with Iran and potentially a broader political settlement in the region.
