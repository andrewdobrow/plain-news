SpaceX Shares Rise 11% in Largest US IPO on Record

SpaceX opened at $150 per share on the Nasdaq, an 11% jump from its initial public offering price of $135, completing the largest stock market debut in US history. The rocket company founded by Elon Musk raised $75 billion ahead of the public sale, valuing the company at approximately $1.77 trillion at its opening price.

The IPO marks a watershed moment for private space companies and could pave the way for other high-profile technology firms to go public. Sources indicate that artificial intelligence companies OpenAI and Anthropic are preparing their own initial public offerings in the coming months, following SpaceX's market reception.

Broader stock indexes climbed alongside the SpaceX debut. The S&P 500 posted gains while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 525 points. The positive market sentiment came as oil prices fell on reports of potential progress in US-Iran negotiations, with President Trump indicating that a peace deal could be within reach after calling off planned strikes on Iran.

On the day of the IPO, SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell raised the possibility of a merger between SpaceX and Tesla, another Musk-led company. The speculation drove activity on prediction markets, where bets increased on the likelihood of the two companies combining operations. Analysts have offered conflicting views on whether such a merger would create value for shareholders or pose regulatory and operational challenges.

The successful debut could push Musk's personal wealth past the trillion-dollar mark, making him the world's first trillionaire. However, some investors have questioned whether SpaceX's $1.77 trillion valuation is sustainable, describing the figure as overly optimistic given the company's current revenue streams and the long-term uncertainties in the commercial space industry.
