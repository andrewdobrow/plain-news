SpaceX shares rise 20% in largest IPO ever as Musk becomes first trillionaire

SpaceX completed the largest initial public offering in history on Friday, with shares jumping 20% during their Wall Street debut. The company's valuation exceeded $2 trillion, making CEO Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire with a net worth estimated at $1.1 trillion.

Trading began around midday with an opening price of $150 per share on the Nasdaq exchange. The company had sought to raise $75 billion through the offering, but investor demand reportedly ran up to four times oversubscribed, potentially bringing total investment to $250 billion. SpaceX used an unusual approach to the IPO, setting a fixed share price of $135 rather than offering investors a customary price range.

The offering marks a watershed moment for SpaceX, which had remained private for nearly two and a half decades since its founding in a warehouse in El Segundo, California. Musk, who controls approximately 85% of SpaceX's voting shares, has stated that going public will provide the capital necessary for the company's ambitious plans to make life multiplanetary and establish human colonies on other planets.

The IPO comes at a time when SpaceX encompasses multiple business ventures, including Starlink, the satellite internet service provider, and xAI, Musk's artificial intelligence startup. However, the conglomerate is not currently profitable. Last year, SpaceX generated $18.7 billion in revenue while recording an operating loss of $4.3 billion, partly due to significant spending on xAI's data center buildout.

The company's IPO structure has drawn scrutiny from critics, including Massachusetts Senator Elizabeth Warren, who asked the Securities and Exchange Commission to examine whether accounting and valuations were accurate. Some analysts have flagged concerns about a company with such high valuations while burning cash and governed predominantly by one individual.

SpaceX shares will be quickly distributed into index funds following the IPO, though notably not into the S&P 500. This means millions of Americans with retirement accounts like 401(k) plans may automatically own SpaceX shares through their index funds, exposing ordinary investors to potential volatility if the company's stock price declines.

For SpaceX employees, the valuation creates significant wealth. More than 4,400 current and former employees are expected to become millionaires through the IPO, with approximately 400 of them each securing $100 million or more.

The SpaceX debut is expected to open the door for other major technology companies. OpenAI and Anthropic, two leading artificial intelligence firms, have filed to go public and are predicted to raise record sums. Economist Gabriel Zucman warned that the concentrated wealth being created through these mega IPOs could have profound effects on democratic societies and economic inequality.
