Hulk Hogan's Cause of Death Revealed as Florida Investigation Closes

Florida police have closed their investigation into the death of wrestling legend Hulk Hogan, revealing the cause of death nearly a year after he died. Hogan, whose real name was Terry Gene Bollea, died on July 24, 2025, at the age of 71. The investigation has now concluded, though specific details about the cause of death were not disclosed in available reports.

Hogan became one of the most recognizable figures in professional wrestling during the 1980s and 1990s, helping to bring the sport into mainstream American culture. His career spanned several decades, during which he became synonymous with World Wrestling Entertainment and later appeared in various entertainment projects beyond wrestling. The wrestler's death last summer marked the end of an era for many fans who grew up watching his performances.

The Florida police investigation remained open for months following Hogan's death, a timeline that can be standard for high-profile cases or when authorities need to conduct thorough examinations and await test results. Law enforcement agencies typically close such investigations once all necessary examinations have been completed and officials have determined the manner and cause of death to their satisfaction.

The closure of the investigation brings a measure of finality to questions surrounding the circumstances of Hogan's death. While the wrestling icon had largely stepped back from active competition in his later years, he remained a visible figure in wrestling circles and popular culture. His influence on professional wrestling and entertainment continues to be recognized by fans and industry figures alike.

The announcement comes as the one-year anniversary of Hogan's death approaches. Florida authorities have concluded their work on the case, allowing Hogan's family and fans to move forward with a clearer understanding of the circumstances surrounding his passing. The wrestler's legacy in professional wrestling remains secure, with many considering him one of the defining figures of the sport's golden age.
