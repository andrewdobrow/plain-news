States Prepare Antitrust Lawsuit to Block Paramount Acquisition of Warner Bros.

Multiple U.S. states are preparing to file an antitrust lawsuit aimed at blocking Paramount's proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, according to sources familiar with the matter. The effort represents a significant challenge to what would be one of the largest media consolidations in recent years.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta signaled the state's intent to challenge the deal, stating on social media that "Paramount/Warner Bros is not a done deal" and that the California Department of Justice has an open investigation. Bonta indicated he was in conversation with fellow state attorneys general about coordinating action against the $110 billion transaction.

Legal experts suggest state-level challenges pose a genuine threat. Alvaro Bedoya, who served on the Federal Trade Commission between 2022 and 2025, said he believed state attorneys general "have a shot at stopping it if they pool their resources." Bill Baer, former assistant attorney general in charge of the antitrust division at the Department of Justice, told the Guardian that "a combined lawsuit by state attorneys general presents a real threat," though he acknowledged it would be harder for states to succeed if federal regulators approve the deal.

The proposed merger has drawn opposition from entertainment industry workers and major organizations. The Writers Guild of America issued a statement opposing the deal, arguing that the loss of competition "would be a disaster for writers, consumers and the entire entertainment industry." Several U.S. senators, including Elizabeth Warren and Richard Blumenthal, have raised concerns about the consolidated power the deal would create, warning that one family would control Warner Bros., Paramount Plus, HBO, CBS News, CNN, and numerous other networks and channels.

Antitrust experts point to specific concerns. Bedoya noted there is "an even more compelling case that a whole lot of people are going to be put out of work," given that the combined company carries $79 billion in debt and will likely undertake significant cost-cutting measures including layoffs. Competition experts have also highlighted how merging HBO Max and Paramount Plus could reduce competition in the streaming market, though Paramount says it will maintain both the Warner Bros. and Paramount film studios.

Paramount's chief executive David Ellison has expressed confidence that the merger will pass regulatory review, claiming the combination "does not come close to hitting any of the metrics that would be problematic." Paramount has already completed at least two information requests from the Department of Justice, and the mandatory 10-day waiting period under federal antitrust law expired in February.

The state-level legal action would add another layer of complexity to an already complicated approval process. If multiple states file suit, the litigation could significantly delay or derail the transaction even if federal regulators ultimately allow it to proceed.
