Justice Department Approves Paramount Acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery

The Justice Department has cleared Paramount's $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, moving forward one of the largest media consolidations in recent history. The antitrust division completed its eight-month investigation and determined the merger would not harm competition or consumers.

"The Division has completed its analysis of the proposed merger of Paramount and Warner Bros and determined based on the evidence received in its investigation that the transaction is not likely to result in harm to competition or American consumers, including with respect to streaming video on demand, linear television, and studio development, production, or distribution of films for theatrical release," the agency stated.

The merger will unite Paramount, which owns CBS and the Paramount+ streaming service, with Warner Bros. Discovery, parent company of HBO, Discovery Channel, Max streaming platform, and CNN. The combined company will control extensive film and television production assets alongside major news operations. The Justice Department's eight-month investigation involved reviewing over two million documents from more than eighty custodians and substantial data from third parties across the media and entertainment sectors.

Despite the federal approval, significant hurdles remain. The UK Competition and Markets Authority opened an investigation into the merger this week with an August 7 deadline to determine whether a more thorough review is needed. European regulators are also investigating the funding behind the deal, including $24 billion in commitments from three Gulf sovereign-wealth funds. Australia has already approved the merger, determining it would not substantially lessen competition in theatrical film distribution.

State-level challenges may also emerge. California's attorney general, Rob Bonta, indicated on social media that the merger remains under investigation by his office and is not yet a done deal. Legal experts have suggested a coalition of state attorneys general could file a lawsuit to block the merger in coming weeks.

The decision has drawn criticism from media advocacy groups and politicians. Craig Aaron, co-chief executive of Free Press, argued that the Trump Justice Department approved the deal without adequate scrutiny, calling on state attorneys general to file suit. Democratic senator Elizabeth Warren characterized the approval as "terrible news for every American who doesn't want Trump-aligned billionaires to control what they watch and how much they pay."

Concerns focus on potential job losses and editorial changes. The companies have promised $6 billion in synergies from the deal, which industry observers expect could result in significant layoffs. Journalists at CBS News and CNN have expressed concerns about a possible merger of the two news networks. Some CNN staff members worry that Paramount's owner, David Ellison, and his father Larry, a longtime Trump associate, could influence the network's editorial direction, though David Ellison pledged in March that CNN's editorial independence would be protected.

The combined company will position itself to compete against dominant streaming platforms in an increasingly competitive media landscape where companies vie for audiences, advertising revenue, and subscription income.
