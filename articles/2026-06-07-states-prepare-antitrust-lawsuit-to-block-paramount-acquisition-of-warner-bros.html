Justice Department Approves Paramount-Warner Bros. Merger

The Department of Justice has cleared the way for Paramount Skydance to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery in a deal valued at $111 billion, concluding an eight-month antitrust investigation into one of the entertainment industry's largest consolidations. The approval allows the two media giants to combine their streaming services, film studios, and television networks.

The Justice Department's antitrust division determined that the merger would not harm competition or consumers across streaming video, linear television, and theatrical film distribution. In a statement, the agency noted it had received over two million documents from more than 80 sources during its investigation. The deal combines major content libraries including Star Trek, DC Comics properties, and HBO's programming alongside streaming platforms Paramount Plus and Max, along with cable networks including CNN and HBO.

Paramount celebrated the approval as a pro-competitive move that would strengthen its position against larger technology platforms. "This deal is pro-competitive, resulting in a stronger company better positioned to compete against dominant technology platforms in an industry increasingly defined by intense competition for audiences, talent, technology, and investment," the company said in a statement.

However, significant obstacles remain. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened its own investigation and set an August 7 deadline to determine whether a more detailed review is needed. European regulators are also scrutinizing the funding behind the merger, which includes $24 billion in commitments from Gulf sovereign wealth funds. Australia has already approved the deal, and several other countries have provided clearance.

Critics have raised concerns about the merger's impact on competition and news independence. The combination would likely merge CNN and Paramount's CBS News, potentially resulting in significant job cuts as the companies pursue $6 billion in promised synergies. Some CNN staff have expressed worry that David Ellison, who would control the combined company through his family's Skydance investment, could alter editorial direction despite pledges to protect independence.

"The merger of Warner Bros and Paramount is not a done deal and remains under investigation by my office," California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on Friday, signaling that state-level legal challenges may follow.

Craig Aaron, co-chief executive of Free Press, criticized the approval, stating that "Paramount Skydance has feted, flattered and promised sweeping changes to news coverage to win the administration's approval." Senator Elizabeth Warren called the decision "terrible news for every American who doesn't want Trump-aligned billionaires to control what they watch and how much they pay."

The merger still requires shareholder votes at both companies and faces potential legal action from state attorneys general in coming weeks. The deal's completion timeline remains uncertain pending these additional reviews and approvals.
