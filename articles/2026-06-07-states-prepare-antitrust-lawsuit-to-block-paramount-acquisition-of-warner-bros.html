Justice Department Approves Warner Bros Sale to Paramount in $111 Billion Deal

The Trump administration's Justice Department has approved Paramount Skydance's $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros Discovery, clearing a major regulatory hurdle for the merger. The antitrust division completed its review after eight months of investigation, determining the deal would not harm competition or consumers.

The Justice Department stated it examined the merger's potential effects across three areas: streaming video on demand, linear television, and studio development and distribution of theatrical films. "The Division has completed its analysis of the proposed merger of Paramount and Warner Bros and determined based on the evidence received in its investigation that the transaction is not likely to result in harm to competition or American consumers," the agency said in its Friday announcement.

The Ellison family controls Paramount Skydance, which owns CBS News. Warner Bros Discovery operates CNN, HBO, and other major media properties. The combined company will control a significant portion of American media assets, including multiple film studios, television networks, streaming platforms, and news operations.

Paramount welcomed the approval, stating the merger would create a stronger company capable of competing against dominant technology platforms. "This deal is pro-competitive, resulting in a stronger company better positioned to compete against dominant technology platforms in an industry increasingly defined by intense competition for audiences, talent, technology, and investment," the company said.

However, significant hurdles remain. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened its own investigation, setting an August 7 deadline to determine whether further review is needed. European regulators are also investigating the funding behind the deal, as three Gulf sovereign-wealth funds have committed a combined $24 billion.

Australia has already approved the merger, determining it would not substantially lessen competition in theatrical film distribution.

Several groups have opposed the deal. Journalists at both CBS News and CNN have expressed concerns about potential network consolidation, which could trigger significant job cuts. The companies have projected $6 billion in synergies from the merger. Some CNN staff have worried that Larry Ellison, a longtime Trump associate, and his son David could push the network in a different editorial direction, though David Ellison pledged in March that CNN's editorial independence would be protected.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta indicated his office continues investigating the merger. "The merger of Warner Bros and Paramount is not a done deal and remains under investigation by my office," he posted on social media.

Critics have also questioned the approval's legitimacy. Free Press co-chief Craig Aaron stated the decision showed bias, saying Paramount had "fêted, flattered and promised sweeping changes to news coverage to win the administration's approval." Democrat Senator Elizabeth Warren called the approval "terrible news for every American who doesn't want Trump-aligned billionaires to control what they watch."

A coalition of state attorneys general could potentially file a lawsuit to block the merger in coming weeks.
