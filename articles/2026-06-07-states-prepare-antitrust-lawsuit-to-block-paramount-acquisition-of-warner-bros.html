Justice Department Approves Paramount-Warner Bros. Merger

The Justice Department has cleared the way for Paramount Skydance to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery in an $111 billion deal that will reshape the media landscape. The approval came after an eight-month antitrust investigation, concluding that the merger poses no threat to competition or consumers.

The combined company will control major film studios, streaming platforms, cable networks, and news operations. Paramount brings CBS, CBS News, Paramount Pictures, and the Paramount+ streaming service to the transaction. Warner Bros. Discovery contributes HBO, Max, the Discovery Channel, and Warner Bros. Studios. The merger will place CNN and CBS News under the same corporate ownership.

The Justice Department's antitrust division completed its analysis after reviewing over two million documents from more than 80 custodians and receiving extensive input from third parties across the media and entertainment sector. In a statement, the agency said it found the transaction unlikely to harm competition in streaming video on demand, linear television, or studio development and distribution of theatrical films.

Paramount responded positively to the approval, calling the deal "pro-competitive" and saying it will create "a stronger company better positioned to compete against dominant technology platforms." The company characterized the merger as beneficial for consumers, creators, and the entertainment industry.

However, obstacles remain. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened an investigation into the merger, setting a deadline of August 7 to decide whether a more detailed review is needed. European regulators are also examining the funding behind the deal, which includes $24 billion in commitments from Gulf sovereign-wealth funds. Australia has already approved the merger, determining it is unlikely to substantially lessen competition in theatrical film distribution.

Staff at CNN and CBS News have raised concerns about the combination, worried about potential job cuts and editorial changes. Paramount has promised $6 billion in cost savings from the merger. There is also speculation about potential changes to CNN's editorial direction under Paramount Skydance leadership, though David Ellison pledged in March that the network's editorial independence would be protected.

State-level opposition could pose additional challenges. California Attorney General Rob Bonta stated that the merger "remains under investigation" by his office and is "not a done deal." Multiple state attorneys general could potentially file a lawsuit to block the transaction in coming weeks.

Critics of the deal have expressed concerns about media consolidation. Craig Aaron, co-chief executive of Free Press, called the approval troubling, saying it concentrates too much media power in one corporation. Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren also criticized the decision, arguing the merger allows aligned billionaires to control media consumption and pricing.

The deal now moves toward final closing procedures, including shareholder votes and remaining regulatory requirements across jurisdictions where both companies operate.
