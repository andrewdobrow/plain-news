Microsoft AI chief calls Anthropic models too expensive

Microsoft Supports Anthropic's Legal Challenge Against Pentagon

Microsoft has filed a court brief backing Anthropic's legal challenge against a Pentagon designation that has effectively blocked the AI company from government work. The filing, submitted to federal court in San Francisco this week, argues that a temporary restraining order is necessary to prevent disruption to suppliers whose products depend on Anthropic's technology.

Google, Amazon, Apple and OpenAI have also signed onto a brief supporting Anthropic. In a statement, Microsoft said: "The Department of War needs reliable access to the country's best technology. And everyone wants to ensure AI is not used for mass domestic surveillance or to start a war without human control. The government, the entire tech sector, and the American public need a path to achieve all these goals together."

Anthropic launched two lawsuits Monday, one in federal court in California and one in the DC circuit court of appeals, challenging the Pentagon's decision to label the company a supply-chain risk. This designation has never before been applied to a US company. The dispute emerged after contract negotiations collapsed last month over a $200 million deal to deploy Anthropic's AI on classified military systems. Anthropic insisted its technology should not be used for mass surveillance of US citizens or to power autonomous lethal weapons. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth then designated the company a supply-chain risk, and Anthropic says its government contracts have already begun to be cancelled.

Pentagon chief technology officer Emil Michael told CNBC on Thursday that "there's no chance" the agency will renegotiate with Anthropic after the designation.

In its legal complaint, Anthropic explained the reasoning behind its restrictions. "Anthropic currently does not have confidence, for example, that Claude would function reliably or safely if used to support lethal autonomous warfare," the filing stated. "These usage restrictions are therefore rooted in Anthropic's unique understanding of Claude's risks and limitations."

The company also argued that its First Amendment rights were under attack, contending that the Pentagon used the supply-chain risk designation, typically reserved for firms with ties to foreign adversaries like China, as ideological punishment for its public stance on AI safety.

Microsoft is one of the Pentagon's most deeply embedded tech partners. The company holds a share of the military's $9 billion Joint Warfighting Cloud Capability contract alongside Amazon, Google and Oracle, plus separate software and enterprise services deals worth several billion dollars more. Under the Trump administration in September, Microsoft struck another multibillion-dollar deal to help advance cloud services and AI in the federal government.

Meanwhile, House Democrats have pressed the Pentagon for information about whether artificial intelligence, including the Maven Smart System, was used to identify targets in recent military strikes in Iran that have resulted in civilian casualties.
