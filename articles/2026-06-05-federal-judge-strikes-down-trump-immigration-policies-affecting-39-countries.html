Federal judge orders Trump administration to restore national park signs on slavery and climate change

A federal judge has ordered the Trump administration to restore historical exhibits and educational materials that were removed from national parks, finding the administration's actions constitute censorship and set a dangerous precedent.

The ruling requires officials to reinstate any history or science materials removed from public monuments and national parks. One case involved a slavery exhibit at Independence National Historical Park in Philadelphia, where the National Park Service had dismantled a display about enslaved people who lived at the President's House, the former residence of Presidents George Washington and John Adams.

The city of Philadelphia sued the federal government over the removal, and U.S. District Judge Cynthia Rufe granted the city's request to temporarily block the removal and order restoration of the exhibit. Rufe, who was appointed by President George W. Bush, opened her ruling with a quote from George Orwell's 1984 about erasing uncomfortable facts from history.

"As if the Ministry of Truth in George Orwell's 1984 now existed, with its motto 'Ignorance is Strength,' this Court is now asked to determine whether the federal government has the power it claims to dissemble and disassemble historical truths," Rufe wrote, concluding: "It does not."

The judge ordered the National Park Service to restore the site to its physical status as of January 21, 2026, the day before the exhibit was removed. During a court hearing, Rufe described the Trump administration's argument that a president could unilaterally order changes to exhibits as both "horrifying" and "dangerous."

In her ruling, Rufe explained the historical significance of the exhibit. The display documented nine enslaved individuals owned by Washington who were brought to the presidential residence and rotated in and out of Pennsylvania to prevent them from petitioning for freedom under Pennsylvania law. The names of those nine people were etched into a wall at the exhibit.

The order came after Trump signed an executive order in March 2025 titled "Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History," directing the secretary of interior to examine monuments, memorials, and statues altered after January 2020 to represent what the administration called a "false construction of American history."

Another federal judge also blocked the national parks order, characterizing the administration's actions as censorship and calling the removals a "dangerous precedent of censorship and sanitization." Officials were given 21 days to comply with restoration orders.

The rulings represent significant legal setbacks for the administration's efforts to control the content displayed at federally managed sites. The judges' decisions underscore the role national parks play as educational resources for millions of annual visitors seeking to understand American history.
