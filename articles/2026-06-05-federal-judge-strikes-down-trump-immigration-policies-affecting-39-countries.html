Federal judge strikes down Trump immigration freeze affecting 39 countries

A federal judge has ruled that the Trump administration unlawfully barred applicants from 39 travel-ban countries from receiving decisions on asylum claims, work permits, green cards and citizenship applications.

The ruling represents a significant legal setback for the administration's immigration policies. The judge determined that the freeze on processing applications from these nations exceeded the government's authority and violated applicants' rights to have their cases reviewed.

The decision came on the same day the US Senate voted to pass legislation funding Trump's immigration enforcement agencies. The $70 billion bill passed 52-47 with no Democratic support after a marathon voting session that knocked down numerous proposed amendments. The funding supports US Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Department of Homeland Security for three years, ending a partial government shutdown that had lasted since February.

The immigration freeze had prevented tens of thousands of applicants from advancing their cases through normal channels. The Trump administration had defended the restrictions as necessary for national security purposes. However, critics argued the policy lacked clear justification and created humanitarian hardships for people seeking legal entry to or residence in the United States.

The ruling requires the government to resume processing applications from the 39 designated countries under standard procedures. The decision does not automatically grant immigration benefits to affected applicants but allows their cases to proceed through normal review processes. The practical impact will unfold as immigration agencies work through the backlog of cases that accumulated during the freeze period.

Separately, a federal judge in New York issued an order restricting immigration enforcement actions in federal courthouses. US District Judge P. Kevin Castel ruled that ICE agents cannot arrest immigrants in or around three federal courthouses in lower Manhattan except under exceptional circumstances. The ruling came in response to a lawsuit brought by civil liberties organizations including the ACLU and Make the Road NY.

Castel noted that while there is "a strong governmental interest in enforcing immigration laws," there is also a serious interest in allowing individuals to attend removal proceedings and pursue asylum claims "without fear of arrest." The order applies to immigration courts at 26 Federal Plaza and two other Manhattan locations. Castel indicated that a court case before him would likely result in a ruling that it was "arbitrary and capricious" for the Trump administration to withdraw 2021 policies regarding enforcement actions inside courthouses.

The Justice Department has not announced whether it will appeal the ruling on the 39 countries. Immigration advocates have welcomed the decision as a restoration of legal processes for thousands of people whose cases had been frozen.
