Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and Netherlands to open migrant deportation hub in Rwanda in 2027

Five European Union countries announced plans to open a migrant deportation center in Rwanda next year, sparking criticism from Spain and human rights organizations over the legality and ethics of the arrangement. Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands said the return hub will be ready to accept transfers in 2027 and will apply to migrants staying illegally in their nations who are subject to deportation. The Dutch government said the facility will provide an alternative when deportees cannot be returned to their country of origin.

The five countries said the scheme is in line with EU and international law, but human rights groups disputed that claim. Human Rights Watch senior Africa researcher Clementine de Montjoye said Rwanda has a well-established record of repression of dissent, arbitrary detention, ill-treatment in detention and support for armed rebels in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The independent EU Agency for Fundamental Rights said any third country hosting a return hub should respect fundamental rights, including that people should not be sent to a country where they face serious risk of persecution.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez told Spanish broadcaster Telecinco that the plan had no future or humanity and was not the solution, according to AFP news agency. The arrangement follows a recent EU reform that allowed for the creation of hubs outside the bloc. At a joint press conference in Vienna, Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker said the five countries wanted to relieve European asylum systems and stop traffickers arranging deadly trips across the Mediterranean.

The plan differs from a British deal scrapped in 2024 that envisaged sending people to Rwanda to have asylum claims processed. The new scheme will apply only to people who have exhausted their legal avenues to stay in the five nations. Diplomatic sources told AFP the hub is expected to initially run as a pilot hosting about 100 people, with each nation possibly sending about 20 migrants. The Dutch government said the first phase will only return single men, with a focus on people from countries of origin in the same region as Rwanda. Rwandan Interior Minister Vincent Biruta said the country is committed to working with European partners to provide a safe, legal and dignified solution for migrants.
