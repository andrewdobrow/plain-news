Federal Reserve prepares rate decision as new chair Warsh takes helm

The Federal Reserve is set to announce its interest rate decision as new chair Kevin Warsh leads his first policy meeting. Stock futures are higher ahead of the announcement, with the Dow recently crossing 52,000 for the first time. The decision arrives as markets weigh mixed signals about the economic outlook and inflation trends.

Warsh, appointed by President Trump, is expected to withhold his individual rate projection from the central bank's interest rate outlook. The move would mark an unusual approach for a Fed chair during a closely watched policy decision. Trump selected Warsh with expectations of rate cuts, but members of Warsh's committee have discussed the possibility of rate increases instead, creating uncertainty about the Fed's direction.

The rate decision comes as inflation remains elevated at a three-year high, complicating the Fed's policy choices. Oil prices have extended their decline, providing some relief on energy costs, while chip stocks have led technology shares lower in recent trading sessions. The Dow has hit record levels even as broader market indexes showed more mixed performance.

The timing of Warsh's first meeting carries significant weight given ongoing tensions between the Trump administration and the Federal Reserve over monetary policy independence. Former Fed chair Jerome Powell recently warned that political interference in monetary policy could permanently damage public trust in the institution. Powell, who stepped down last month and remains on the Fed's board of governors, argued that legal protections separating the Fed from political pressure "have served the public well" across administrations of both parties.

Powell's remarks come as the Supreme Court weighs the constitutionality of Trump's attempt to remove Fed governor Lisa Cook last August. A federal judge blocked the firing in September, and both conservative and liberal justices signaled skepticism toward the administration's position during oral arguments in January. A final ruling is expected before summer recess, typically in late June.

Powell emphasized that Fed decisions are made "based only on our best economic analysis of what would most benefit the people we serve" and that officials do not consider "the fortunes of any political party or politician." He noted that Fed officials serve long terms unrelated to four-year presidential election cycles and hold legal protections against removal.

Market strategists are closely watching how Warsh will navigate competing pressures between the administration's expectations for lower rates and persistent inflation concerns that typically warrant tighter monetary policy. The outcome of this meeting will set the tone for Warsh's tenure as Fed chair and could significantly affect borrowing costs for consumers and businesses. Markets have priced in various scenarios, with traders positioning themselves for potential volatility following the announcement.
