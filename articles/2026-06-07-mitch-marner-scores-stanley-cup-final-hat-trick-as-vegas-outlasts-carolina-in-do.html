Mitch Marner scores Stanley Cup Final hat trick as Vegas outlasts Carolina in double overtime

Vegas Holds Off Carolina's Comeback to Take 2-1 Series Lead

Shea Theodore scored at 5:38 of the second overtime period to give the Vegas Golden Knights a 5-4 victory over the Carolina Hurricanes in Game 3 of the Stanley Cup Final on Saturday night. The goal, which deflected off goalie Brandon Bussi's skate, secured a 2-1 series lead for Vegas after the Hurricanes mounted a dramatic fourth-period rally.

The Golden Knights appeared to be cruising to victory thanks to a dominant second period from Mitch Marner, who recorded the fastest hat trick in Stanley Cup Final history. Marner's three goals gave Vegas a commanding four-goal advantage heading into the final period, seemingly putting the game out of reach.

Carolina had other ideas. The Hurricanes stormed back in the third period, scoring four consecutive goals to erase Vegas's lead and force the game into overtime. The comeback shifted momentum entirely, transforming what looked like a comfortable Golden Knights victory into one of the most dramatic playoff contests in recent memory.

Both teams created chances during the first overtime period but failed to convert. The game remained deadlocked until Theodore's heroic finish in the second overtime, ending one of the most unpredictable Stanley Cup Final games in recent years.

Theodore's winning goal prevented what could have been a devastating collapse for Vegas. The Golden Knights had been in complete control for much of the game, with Marner's historic performance appearing to have settled the outcome. Instead, the Hurricanes proved their resilience and staying power, nearly completing an improbable comeback before Theodore's late heroics.

The victory gave Vegas the advantage in the series as the two teams prepared to continue their Finals matchup. The combination of Marner's record-setting achievement and Carolina's furious comeback created a contest that showcased the intensity and unpredictability characteristic of championship-level hockey. The game took place in front of a capacity crowd that witnessed playoff hockey at its most dramatic.

Marner's hat trick stands as a milestone in Stanley Cup Final history. His three goals in the second period represented the fastest such performance in the Finals' long history, cementing his place in the record books regardless of the game's ultimate outcome.

The Hurricanes' ability to score four consecutive goals demonstrated their determination and offensive capability, even as they fell short in the end. The loss was particularly difficult given how close they came to completing an extraordinary comeback and potentially shifting the series momentum entirely in their favor.

With the series now favoring Vegas 2-1, both teams faced the prospect of continuing their battle for the Stanley Cup. The dramatic nature of Game 3 promised that subsequent games would be contested with similar intensity and urgency.
