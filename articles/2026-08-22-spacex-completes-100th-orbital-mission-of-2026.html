SpaceX completes 100th orbital mission of 2026

SpaceX launched its 100th orbital mission of 2026, marking a new milestone in commercial spaceflight operations. The company deployed 29 Starlink satellites and successfully landed its rocket on a ship at sea. The achievement represents a significant increase in launch cadence for the private aerospace manufacturer.

The milestone comes as SpaceX continues expanding its Starlink satellite internet constellation. As of August 19, the company pushed its Starlink network past 11,000 satellites in orbit. This single network now accounts for roughly two-thirds of all active satellites currently circling Earth.

SpaceX experienced at least one launch delay during recent operations, halting a Starlink launch countdown with just 28 seconds remaining before liftoff. The company routinely performs last-minute countdown holds as part of its safety protocols before clearing rockets for launch.

The rapid expansion of SpaceX's satellite network has drawn attention to the growing concentration of orbital infrastructure under single operators. Elon Musk highlighted the disparity between SpaceX and competitors, noting that Amazon has not yet reached 1,000 satellites in orbit despite its own planned constellation.

The 100 orbital missions milestone demonstrates SpaceX's dominant position in the commercial launch market. The company maintains this pace through reusable rocket technology and frequent launches from facilities in Florida and California. Each successful landing of a rocket booster allows SpaceX to refurbish and refly hardware, reducing costs and enabling higher launch frequencies than traditional expendable rocket systems.
