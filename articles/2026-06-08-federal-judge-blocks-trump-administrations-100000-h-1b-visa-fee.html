Federal judge blocks Trump administration's $100,000 H-1B visa fee

A federal judge has invalidated Donald Trump's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, ruling that the policy violates federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page ruling in a lawsuit brought by 20 Democratic state attorneys general who challenged the fee Trump announced in September. The decision vacates the controversial charge, which represented a 20-to-50 fold increase over existing H-1B application fees.

The H-1B visa program allows US companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring technical or theoretical expertise. The program has been widely used by technology companies, healthcare providers, and other industries to fill positions demanding specialized skills.

Judge Sorokin found that the Trump administration lacked legal authority to impose such a substantial fee. The ruling represents a significant setback for the policy, which supporters had argued would generate revenue and incentivize companies to hire American workers instead.

The $100,000 charge would have applied to each H-1B visa petition filed by an employer. This dwarfed the existing application and processing fees, which typically total several thousand dollars. Business groups and immigration advocates had challenged the policy before it took effect, contending it would harm American competitiveness and make it difficult for companies to access needed talent from abroad.

The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the decision. The outcome of that appeal remains uncertain as the case moves through the court system.

The ruling does not affect other aspects of H-1B visa processing or eligibility requirements, which continue under existing regulations. Employers can continue to apply for H-1B visas under the previous fee structure during the appeals process.

Immigration policy has remained a central focus of the Trump administration, with various proposals and changes implemented since taking office. The H-1B program itself has been subject to ongoing debate, with critics arguing it can depress wages for American workers and supporters maintaining it addresses genuine skills shortages in critical industries.

The fee proposal had generated substantial opposition from business communities that depend on accessing international talent. Technology companies, in particular, had raised concerns about how the increased cost would affect their ability to recruit specialists for positions they argued could not be filled with American workers.

The decision comes as courts continue to review other Trump administration policies implemented through executive orders and regulatory changes. Legal challenges to administration actions have resulted in mixed outcomes across different federal courts.
