Federal judge strikes down Trump administration's $100,000 H-1B visa fee

A federal judge has invalidated Donald Trump's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, determining the charge constitutes an unlawful tax that violates federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page ruling in a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general who challenged the fee Trump announced in September. The decision vacates the sweeping charge, which represented a 20-to-50 fold increase from existing application rates.

The H-1B visa program enables US companies to hire foreign workers for specialty occupations, particularly in technology, engineering, and healthcare sectors. The Trump administration had positioned the fee as a mechanism to prioritize American workers and generate revenue, but opponents argued it would severely restrict companies' ability to recruit international talent.

The judge's ruling determined that the administration lacked legal authority to implement such a substantial fee without congressional approval. Industry groups and technology companies that had challenged the fee welcomed the decision, noting it would have harmed innovation and competitiveness in sectors dependent on specialized international expertise.

Indian diaspora groups also welcomed the court's decision, as workers from India represent a significant portion of H-1B visa recipients. The ruling preserves the existing H-1B visa application process and fee structure. Companies that had been preparing for higher costs or reconsidering hiring plans can now proceed under the previous system.

The decision does not address other aspects of immigration policy or separate visa categories. The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the ruling.

The H-1B program operates under an annual cap of 85,000 visas: 65,000 available for workers holding bachelor's degrees and 20,000 for those with advanced degrees from US institutions. Demand consistently exceeds available slots, with the government employing a lottery system to allocate visas among eligible applicants.

Previously, Trump had announced the fee through an executive order aimed at raising the cost of obtaining H-1B visas. The proposed charge would have applied to each H-1B visa application filed by employers, significantly increasing what had been several thousand dollars in existing application costs.

The ruling represents a setback for the administration's efforts to restrict access to temporary work visas for foreign nationals. Companies seeking to maintain their international workforce recruitment strategies can now resume operations without facing the proposed fee increase.

The case reflects ongoing debate about immigration policy and the balance between protecting American workers and maintaining the ability of US businesses to access specialized talent from abroad. The Trump administration has not announced a timeline for any potential appeal.
