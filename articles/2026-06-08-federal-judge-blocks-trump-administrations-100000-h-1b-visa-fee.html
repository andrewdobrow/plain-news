Federal judge blocks Trump administration's $100,000 H-1B visa fee

A federal judge has invalidated the Trump administration's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, ruling that the policy violated federal administrative law and the Constitution.

U.S. District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the 42-page ruling in response to a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general. The decision struck down a fee that represented a dramatic increase from previous H-1B visa costs, amounting to a 20-to-50 fold increase on existing rates. The Trump administration announced the fee in September as a way to prevent foreign workers from taking American jobs.

The H-1B visa program allows U.S. companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring theoretical or technical expertise. The program is widely used by technology companies and other industries facing skilled worker shortages. By imposing such a substantial financial burden on companies seeking to hire foreign talent, the administration aimed to discourage the practice and create more opportunities for domestic workers.

Critics of the H-1B program have long argued that it allows companies to bypass qualified American workers in favor of cheaper foreign labor. Supporters of the administration's policy saw the fee as a tool to reshape hiring practices and prioritize American workers in the labor market.

However, the court found the policy legally insufficient to stand. Judge Sorokin determined that the fee functioned as an unlawful tax that violated federal administrative procedures. The ruling vacates the sweeping fee entirely, removing a major obstacle for companies that rely on foreign talent to fill specialized positions.

The decision affects how businesses can staff roles requiring specific technical skills and expertise that may be in short supply among the domestic workforce. It also has implications for foreign workers who had been seeking employment opportunities in the United States through the H-1B program.

The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the judge's decision. The legal challenge underscores ongoing tensions between immigration policy and business interests. While the administration sought to use the fee as a tool to restrict various immigration pathways and reshape the labor market through executive action, the court blocked the approach.

The ruling represents a setback for the broader administration efforts to restrict immigration. It demonstrates how executive actions on immigration policy face legal scrutiny when they conflict with established administrative law and constitutional principles. The case illustrates the continuing debate over how to balance business needs for specialized foreign workers with concerns about protecting domestic employment opportunities.
