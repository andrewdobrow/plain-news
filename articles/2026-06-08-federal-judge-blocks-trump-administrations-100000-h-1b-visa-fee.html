Judge blocks Trump administration's $100,000 H-1B visa fee

A federal judge has invalidated Donald Trump's $100,000 annual fee on H-1B visa applications, determining it violates federal administrative law and the Constitution.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston issued the ruling in response to a lawsuit filed by 20 Democratic state attorneys general challenging the fee Trump announced in September. The decision came in a 42-page ruling that vacated the sweeping increase, which represented a 20-to-50 fold jump from existing H-1B visa rates.

The H-1B visa program allows U.S. companies to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring technical or theoretical expertise. The program has been widely used by technology firms, healthcare organizations, and other sectors facing shortages of specialized workers.

The judge determined that the administration lacked the legal authority to impose such a substantial fee increase through executive action. Business groups and immigration advocates had challenged the fee structure as both economically damaging and beyond the scope of executive authority. Critics argued the dramatic cost increase would effectively price out many legitimate employers from using the program to access global talent pools.

The decision applies immediately, preventing the fee from taking effect while legal challenges continue. The Trump administration is widely expected to appeal the ruling or attempt to implement a modified fee structure that might withstand judicial scrutiny.

The case reflects broader tensions over immigration policy and the H-1B program itself. Supporters argue the visa program helps American companies remain competitive by accessing skilled international workers, particularly in technology and specialized fields. Critics contend the program can displace domestic workers or suppress wages in certain industries.

This ruling represents a significant setback for the administration's efforts to reshape immigration policy through regulatory changes. The fee had been a centerpiece of Trump's approach to restricting immigration pathways, particularly for employment-based visas that bring foreign workers to the United States.

The decision also comes amid broader legal challenges to various Trump administration policies. Other recent court rulings have blocked different aspects of the administration's immigration agenda, with judges frequently finding that executive actions exceeded constitutional or statutory authority.

Business organizations across multiple sectors that rely on foreign talent for positions requiring advanced degrees and specialized skills have largely supported legal challenges to the fee. Technology companies in particular depend heavily on the H-1B program to fill technical positions.

The outcome of any appeal could significantly impact how many foreign workers enter the United States through the H-1B program in coming years. Until the appeals process concludes, employers can proceed with H-1B visa applications at the previous fee levels.
