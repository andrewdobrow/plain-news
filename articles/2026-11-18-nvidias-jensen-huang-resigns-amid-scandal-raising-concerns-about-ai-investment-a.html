Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigns amid scandal, raising concerns about AI investment and US economic impact

Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned following revelations of extramarital affairs, one allegedly involving a Chinese spy, triggering questions about the chip manufacturer's role in the American economy and the broader artificial intelligence spending boom. The scandal wiped hundreds of billions of dollars off Nvidia's stock market value and created uncertainty around the company's future direction. Nvidia, valued at $3.8 trillion, reported Tuesday that quarterly sales doubled from a year earlier.

Huang led Nvidia's pivot from graphics chips for computer games to chips used in AI systems, where it now dominates the market. He maintained direct oversight of up to 200 people and played a central role in promoting investment across the AI industry. Analysts warn his departure poses a risk that Nvidia could change strategy and reduce spending, potentially triggering a wider slowdown in AI investment with economic consequences for companies relying on Nvidia as a key source of finance.

Nvidia has created what analysts call a spider's web of circular financing, not only selling microchips to customers including Meta, SpaceX and OpenAI but also providing them loans or direct investments. This practice has prompted investor concerns about what would happen if Nvidia reduced spending. Tech executives including Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney have called for Huang's reinstatement, with Musk claiming critical deals involving Nvidia technology would be lost. A shareholder campaign dubbed Bring Jensen Back has emerged, and Huang said he is open to returning days after resigning.

Investment in AI is expected to exceed $1 trillion in 2026, according to Goldman Sachs. Nvidia interim chief Colette Kress said it was business as usual and the company's growth remains on track. When Nvidia's share price plunged after the revelations, rival semiconductor makers including AMD, Broadcom and Marvell Technology saw their stock jump. Nvidia's board said it is not considering bringing Huang back despite his expressed willingness to return.
