Violence Erupts in Belfast After Stabbing Attack Triggers Anti-Immigrant Riots

Anti-immigrant violence swept through Belfast for a second consecutive night after a stabbing attack sparked widespread unrest. Far-right activists, including prominent agitators, called for demonstrations in response to a graphic video of the incident that circulated on social media. Crowds of masked men burned vehicles and houses, blocked roads, and looted shops across the city and surrounding areas.

The disorder began hours after a 30-year-old asylum seeker from Sudan was charged with attempted murder in connection with a stabbing attack on Monday night in north Belfast. The victim, a man in his 40s, suffered serious injuries to his eyes, face and back. Video footage showed the assault occurring outside a block of flats, with bystanders intervening to stop the attack.

On Tuesday night, protesters hijacked and burned a bus on Newtownards Road in east Belfast. Fires erupted near Shankill Road and in Newtownabbey as groups stormed homes they claimed were occupied by families from ethnic minority backgrounds. Two phone shops were looted and an African shop was set ablaze. Groups launched fireworks and started fires in bins and buses while police helicopters hovered overhead.

Police Assistant Chief Constable Ryan Henderson appealed for calm as sporadic disorder broke out across multiple locations. "We are urging everyone to remain calm, act responsibly, and avoid any activity that could place themselves or others at risk," he said. He called on "all voices of influence" within communities to discourage violence and encourage peaceful protest.

Political leaders condemned the rioting. Northern Ireland's First Minister Michelle O'Neill called the actions "disgusting cowardice," saying "Groups of masked men burning families out of their homes has nothing to do with community. This is outright thuggery." She urged the public not to be persuaded by social media accounts that stoke tension. Other MPs described the scenes as shameful and said demonstrators were "intent on wreaking destruction on the very communities they claim they are trying to protect."

The suspect in the stabbing was also charged with possession of an article with a blade in a public place and threats to kill. Police said he was granted leave to remain in the UK on September 28, 2023. He had traveled from Sudan to Paris, then to Dublin before claiming asylum in Belfast on February 10, 2023.

Chief Constable Jon Boutcher stated the suspect had no trace on national security databases and was not previously known to police. "At this stage, we have no information to suggest" the attack was motivated by terrorism, Boutcher said.

Smaller protests also occurred in London, Antrim, Bangor, and Ballymena. The stabbing victim's family called for calm and expressed disgust at the rioting that followed the attack.
