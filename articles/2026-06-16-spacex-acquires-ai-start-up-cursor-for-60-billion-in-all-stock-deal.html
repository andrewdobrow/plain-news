SpaceX Acquires AI Startup for $60 Billion in All-Stock Deal

SpaceX has exercised an option to acquire Anysphere, the startup behind the AI-powered coding app Cursor, for $60 billion in an all-stock transaction, the company announced days after its record-breaking initial public offering.

The deal represents one of the largest technology acquisitions in recent years and expands SpaceX's artificial intelligence capabilities alongside its core aerospace business. By using stock rather than cash, SpaceX preserves its balance sheet while deploying its newly public equity as currency for growth.

SpaceX's IPO on Friday marked a historic milestone. The company began trading at $150 per share and climbed to $160 by market close, a gain of more than 19 percent. The offering valued SpaceX at $2.1 trillion, making it one of the world's most valuable companies. The company raised $75 billion from the public offering after nearly 25 years as a private enterprise.

The stock surge has dramatically increased Elon Musk's personal wealth. As SpaceX's majority shareholder controlling approximately 85 percent of voting shares, Musk became the world's first trillionaire, with his net worth reaching $1.1 trillion according to Forbes. His stake in Tesla, valued at around $300 billion, contributes to his overall fortune.

SpaceX's expansion into artificial intelligence reflects a broader strategy by Musk. The company told IPO investors it sees a $26 trillion addressable market in artificial intelligence. The Anysphere acquisition builds on SpaceX's existing AI division, xAI, which the company incorporated into its public structure. The technology could enhance autonomous rocket landing systems, satellite network operations, and future space exploration missions.

The company's rapid market ascent has positioned it ahead of several tech giants by valuation. SpaceX has surpassed Amazon and Meta in market capitalization, approaching Microsoft's standing.

However, SpaceX's financial picture presents complexities. Last year the company generated $18.7 billion in revenue while recording an operating loss of $4.3 billion. Analysts note that the company is burning cash on its AI infrastructure buildout, with xAI spending substantially on datacenters. Some market watchers have expressed caution about the company's significant valuation given its lack of profitability and concentration of control under Musk.

SpaceX's IPO included unconventional terms. Rather than offering a price range to investors, the company set a fixed pre-opening price of $135, with investor demand reportedly reaching four times oversubscription levels.

The acquisition reflects Musk's stated mission for SpaceX to "make humanity multiplanetary" and advance space exploration. Company president Gwynne Shotwell highlighted the company's accomplishments, noting that SpaceX launched a Falcon 9 rocket on IPO morning carrying 29 Starlink satellites to low-Earth orbit.
