SpaceX Acquires AI Start-Up Cursor for $60 Billion in All-Stock Deal

SpaceX has agreed to purchase Cursor, an AI coding company, in an all-stock transaction valued at $60 billion. The acquisition occurred just days after SpaceX completed its historic initial public offering, marking a significant expansion of Elon Musk's artificial intelligence portfolio.

The deal was completed through an option that SpaceX had previously arranged with Cursor, suggesting the two companies had negotiated terms before the start-up's public debut. Under the all-stock structure, Cursor shareholders will receive SpaceX shares rather than cash compensation, directly linking their financial interests to the aerospace company's future performance.

SpaceX's stock price surged following the acquisition announcement. The company's shares climbed 14 percent, pushing its overall market value beyond $2.9 trillion. This stock performance allowed SpaceX to surpass Amazon and become the world's fifth-most valuable company. The strong trading activity reflected continued investor enthusiasm following the company's record-breaking IPO, which valued SpaceX at approximately $2.1 trillion on its opening day.

SpaceX's initial public offering represented a historic moment for the company. Trading began at $150 per share, well above the pre-opening price of $135, and reached a high of $176 during the day's session. By market close, shares had settled at $160, representing a 19 percent increase from the opening price.

The acquisition strengthens SpaceX's capabilities in artificial intelligence technology. The company told IPO investors that it identified a $26 trillion addressable market in AI. Access to Cursor's coding technology could support SpaceX's operations across multiple divisions, including rocket development and its Starlink satellite internet services.

Musk's personal wealth has grown substantially from these developments. According to the Bloomberg rich list, his net worth now stands at approximately $1.11 trillion, making him the world's wealthiest individual. The combination of SpaceX's successful public market debut and the major acquisition represents a significant period for the company and Musk's broader business interests.

The Cursor acquisition signals SpaceX's commitment to competing directly in the artificial intelligence sector, an area where major technology companies have substantially increased their investments. This move complements Musk's existing AI ventures and could accelerate development of autonomous systems and software tools integrated throughout SpaceX's operations.

SpaceX operates as a conglomeration of several Musk-led businesses, including the Starlink satellite and internet provider, the xAI artificial intelligence company, the X social media platform, and the core SpaceX rocket business. The company generated $18.7 billion in revenue during the previous year, though it recorded an operating loss of $4.3 billion, reflecting its significant ongoing investment in research and development across its various divisions.
