Nvidia board launches independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang after reports of Chinese intelligence targeting

Nvidia's board of directors announced Sunday that it is opening an independent investigation following reports that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. The investigation comes after The New York Times and Financial Times reported that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman the newspapers said had been recruited to spy for China. The two are not related. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will oversee the investigation, which Nvidia said will examine whether national security or company secrets were compromised. The company stated it has no reason to believe either was compromised but called national and economic security paramount to its interests.

In a memo to employees that Nvidia shared publicly, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife. The Financial Times reported Friday that the U.S. government believed Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, whose Singapore-based company was buying Nvidia's advanced chips. U.S. intelligence agencies found she had been recruited by China's top intelligence agency, according to the reports. Huang had previously described his interactions with Alice Huang as business-related and said they were related to her company Megaspeed.

Nvidia has been at the center of the artificial intelligence boom, producing the world's most sought-after chips. Its top-tier products are mostly restricted from sale to China due to national security concerns, while China has set a 2030 deadline to surpass American AI capabilities. Nvidia is the world's second-largest publicly traded company, worth nearly $4.5 trillion as of Friday.

Before Sunday's announcement, congressional Republicans had already requested a briefing from Huang. Representative John Moolenaar, chairman of the House Select Committee on China, said the reports raise significant national security concerns regarding China's access to sensitive U.S. technology and information. A Nvidia spokesperson said the news would not slow the company and that Huang is committed to continuing as CEO without interruption.
