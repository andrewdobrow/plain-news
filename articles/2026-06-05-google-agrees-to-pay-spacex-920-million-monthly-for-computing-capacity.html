Google Agrees to Pay SpaceX Nearly $1 Billion Monthly for Cloud Computing

Google has agreed to pay SpaceX approximately $920 million each month for access to computing capacity, according to multiple reports. The deal represents a $30 billion arrangement over its duration and reflects growing demand for the infrastructure needed to power artificial intelligence applications.

Under the agreement, Google will gain access to computing resources at data centers operated by xAI, the artificial intelligence startup founded by Elon Musk. A Google representative stated that the deal resulted from unexpected demand for the company's recently launched AI products.

The computing power Google is purchasing will help support the development and operation of large language models and other AI applications. This deal underscores the massive scale of investment required to build and maintain the computational infrastructure that modern AI systems demand.

SpaceX has positioned itself as a major player in the AI computing space. The company recently completed construction of the Colossus data center, which represents a significant capital investment. According to SpaceX's IPO prospectus filed ahead of its anticipated stock market debut, the xAI unit accounted for $12.7 billion of the company's $20.7 billion capital expenditure, primarily due to data center construction costs.

The Google deal follows a similar arrangement SpaceX previously secured with Anthropic for AI compute capacity. These agreements highlight how artificial intelligence companies and major tech firms are increasingly willing to spend enormous sums to access the computing infrastructure necessary for AI development.

SpaceX's xAI unit has become a significant cost center for the company. The AI division lost $6.4 billion last year, driven largely by higher computing expenses needed to build and operate AI models, including Grok, Musk's artificial intelligence tool. However, major contracts like the Google deal suggest SpaceX is working to offset these losses through revenue from other companies seeking computing power.

The timing of the announcement is significant as SpaceX prepares for what is expected to be one of the largest initial public offerings in history. The company is aiming to raise $75 billion through its stock market listing, which is scheduled for early June. The IPO will value SpaceX at approximately $1.77 trillion.

The company's three business segments include space operations, which handles rocket launches for clients including NASA; connectivity, which operates Starlink satellite broadband service; and AI, which encompasses xAI and the X platform formerly known as Twitter. The AI segment generated $3.2 billion in revenue last year, though it remains heavily loss-making. Connectivity was the only segment to turn a profit in the first quarter of 2026.
