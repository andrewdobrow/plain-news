SpaceX Signs $30 Billion Deal to Lease Computing Capacity to Google

SpaceX has agreed to a $30 billion contract to provide computing capacity to Google, according to multiple reports from financial news outlets. Under the agreement, Google will pay SpaceX approximately $920 million per month for access to computing resources at xAI data centers.

A Google representative stated that the deal resulted from unexpected demand for the company's recently launched artificial intelligence products. The arrangement marks one of the largest cloud computing agreements in the technology industry and reflects the intense competition among major tech companies to secure computing infrastructure for AI development.

The timing of the SpaceX deal is significant as the rocket company prepares for a major initial public offering. SpaceX is targeting a stock market debut that could raise up to $75 billion, which would represent the largest IPO in history. If the listing succeeds as planned, SpaceX founder Elon Musk could become the world's first trillionaire.

This computing agreement follows similar infrastructure deals announced by other AI companies. OpenAI recently signed a $38 billion agreement with Amazon Web Services to operate its artificial intelligence products using AWS datacenters and Nvidia chips. OpenAI's chief executive Sam Altman has committed his company to spending $1.4 trillion on AI infrastructure development overall.

The computing power deals highlight the enormous resource demands of modern artificial intelligence systems. These datacenters serve as critical infrastructure for training and running AI models like ChatGPT. OpenAI is committed to developing 30 gigawatts of computing resources, enough to power approximately 25 million US homes.

Market analysts project that global spending on datacenters will reach nearly $3 trillion between now and 2028. Major US technology companies are expected to cover half of this spending, while the remainder will likely come from private credit markets and other financial sources.

The Google-SpaceX deal also comes as SpaceX prepares for its public market debut next week. The company's IPO announcement has generated substantial investor interest, with the potential valuation reaching $1.77 trillion based on the planned fundraising target.

These major computing infrastructure agreements underscore the growing competition among technology companies to secure the resources necessary for advancing artificial intelligence development. As AI capabilities expand and demand increases, companies are committing unprecedented capital to build the physical infrastructure required to support these technologies at scale.
