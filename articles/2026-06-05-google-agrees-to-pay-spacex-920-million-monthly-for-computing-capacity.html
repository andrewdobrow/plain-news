Google Agrees to Pay SpaceX Nearly $1 Billion Monthly for Cloud Computing

Google has reached an agreement to pay SpaceX approximately $920 million per month for computing capacity at xAI data centers, according to multiple reports. The arrangement represents a $30 billion total commitment and comes as SpaceX prepares for its initial public offering.

The deal provides Google with artificial intelligence computing power to support recently launched AI products. A Google representative attributed the contract to unexpected demand for these new offerings. The agreement positions SpaceX as a major provider of computing infrastructure for technology companies developing AI systems.

This follows a similar arrangement SpaceX previously signed with Anthropic for AI compute capacity. The contracts highlight growing demand from tech firms for dedicated computing resources as they scale artificial intelligence applications.

SpaceX is preparing for what could become the largest initial public offering in history. The company is targeting a $75 billion fundraising, which would value it at approximately $1.77 trillion. The IPO is scheduled for mid-June.

SpaceX operates as a sprawling conglomerate encompassing multiple divisions. Its space segment handles rocket launches for clients including NASA. The connectivity division manages Starlink, its satellite broadband service. The AI division oversees xAI and the social media platform X, formerly Twitter.

The company faces significant financial challenges despite revenue growth. SpaceX reported a $4.9 billion loss in 2025 on revenues of $18.7 billion, though revenue increased by one-third compared to 2024. Losses have accelerated this year, with a $4.3 billion loss in the first quarter compared to a $528 million loss in the same period last year.

The connectivity segment generates the most revenue at $11.4 billion, followed by the space division at $4.1 billion and the AI unit at $3.2 billion. However, only the connectivity division was profitable during the first three months of this year. The AI unit is heavily loss-making, having lost $6.4 billion last year due to higher computing expenses required to build and operate AI models.

Capital expenditure totaled $20.7 billion, with the xAI unit accounting for $12.7 billion, primarily for constructing massive data centers. SpaceX has built a facility called the Colossus data center to support these operations.

Founder and CEO Elon Musk will maintain approximately 85 percent voting control of SpaceX through majority ownership of Class B shares, which carry 10 votes per share compared to one vote for Class A shares held by other investors. Musk's base salary remains unchanged at $54,000 annually since 2019.
