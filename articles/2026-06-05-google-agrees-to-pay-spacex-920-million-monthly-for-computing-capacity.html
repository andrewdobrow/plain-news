Google agrees to pay SpaceX $920 million monthly for computing capacity

Google and SpaceX announced a deal on Friday under which Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month for computing services. The agreement comes one week before SpaceX's scheduled initial public offering, marking a significant corporate partnership between two major technology companies.

The monthly payment represents one of the largest cloud computing contracts announced in recent years. The deal positions SpaceX to provide substantial computing infrastructure to Google, though the companies did not disclose the specific services or duration of the agreement. The timing suggests both companies see strategic value in finalizing the arrangement before SpaceX becomes a publicly traded entity.

SpaceX has been building out computing capabilities alongside its satellite internet service Starlink, which operates thousands of satellites in low Earth orbit. The company has invested heavily in ground infrastructure and data centers to support its space operations and communications network. This agreement with Google represents a new revenue stream for SpaceX beyond its traditional launch services and Starlink subscriptions.

The announcement comes as SpaceX prepares for what is expected to be one of the most closely watched public offerings in recent memory. The company, founded by Elon Musk, has grown from a startup rocket manufacturer into a dominant force in the space industry, with contracts from NASA, the Department of Defense, and commercial customers worldwide. The Google deal adds to SpaceX's revenue base ahead of its debut on public markets.

For Google, the agreement secures access to computing capacity at a time when demand for cloud infrastructure continues to grow, driven in part by artificial intelligence applications. The deal also represents a bet on SpaceX's infrastructure capabilities beyond its core space transportation business. Both companies have significant operations and investments in advanced technology, making the partnership a convergence of space, communications, and computing infrastructure.
