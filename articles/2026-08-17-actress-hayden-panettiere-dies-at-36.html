Actress Hayden Panettiere Dies at 36

Hayden Panettiere, the actress known for her roles in the television series Heroes and Nashville, died at age 36. Her father, Skip Panettiere, confirmed her death in a statement, describing her as "an incredible light and a force of nature who brought immeasurable love and joy to all who knew her." No cause of death was released.

Panettiere began her career as a child actor at just 11 months old, appearing in commercials. She made her film debut at age nine as the voice of Dot in A Bug's Life. In 2000, she appeared in Remember the Titans. Her breakthrough came in 2006 when she landed the role of invulnerable cheerleader Claire Bennet in the NBC science fiction series Heroes, which ran for four seasons and made her famous worldwide.

Later, Panettiere earned two Golden Globe nominations for her performance as Juliette Barnes, a troubled country music star, on the musical drama Nashville. During the show's run, eleven songs she recorded for the series appeared on Billboard's Hot Country Songs charts, including two collaborations with co-star Connie Britton. She also appeared as Kirby Reed in Scream 4 in 2011 and returned to the franchise in Scream VI in 2023.

In 2014, Panettiere gave birth to her daughter Kaya Klitschko with her ex-fiance, Ukrainian heavyweight boxing champion Wladimir Klitschko. In 2018, she gave full custody to Klitschko to focus on her recovery from what she described as a "horrible cycle for years of battling depression and anxiety and alcoholism and substance abuse."

Panettiere had been open about her mental health struggles. She entered rehab in 2015 while filming Nashville, an experience that influenced some of the show's storylines. In a 2022 interview, she reflected on her challenges, saying "I didn't know where the alcoholism was ending and the postpartum was beginning. And I ran myself pretty ragged."

By 2022, Panettiere spoke to People magazine about her recovery: "This hasn't been easy and there were a lot of ups and downs. But I don't regret even the ugliest things that have happened to me. I feel incredibly accomplished. And I feel like I have a second chance."

Earlier this year, Panettiere published a memoir titled This is Me: A Reckoning, which addressed her struggles with fame, postpartum depression, addiction, and recovery. In May, she gave an emotional interview to podcaster Jay Shetty about her daughter, expressing her hopes for their future relationship.

Panettiere is survived by her father Skip Panettiere and her mother Lesley Vogel. Her brother Jansen died suddenly in 2023 at age 28 from cardiomegaly and aortic valve complications.

Her death prompted widespread tributes from fellow actors and colleagues across the entertainment industry.
