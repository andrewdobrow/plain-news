Trump directs $700 million to coal industry under wartime authority

President Trump has announced a major investment in the coal industry, framing it as a national security priority during the ongoing conflict with Iran. The funding represents a significant federal intervention in energy markets at a time when fuel prices are affecting American consumers.

During a White House ceremony on Wednesday, Trump signed an executive order directing the Department of Defense to purchase long-term power from coal plants for military installations and other critical facilities. "We're going to be buying a lot of coal through the military now," Trump said at the event, which included coal executives, miners, and Republican lawmakers.

The announcement included an additional allocation of $175 million from the Department of Energy to modernize and extend the operation of coal-fired power plants serving rural communities in West Virginia, Ohio, North Carolina, and Kentucky. The administration framed both initiatives as part of a broader strategy to increase domestic energy production and reduce reliance on foreign oil markets during wartime.

The coal industry has welcomed the support. James Grech, CEO of Peabody Energy, the largest coal company in the United States, praised Trump's actions. "We stand here today representing the thousands of coalminers across the country to express our deep gratitude to you, sir, for the actions you've taken to support our industry," Grech said.

This latest move follows other administration efforts to revive coal. In September, the White House announced $625 million for coal-fired power plants and opened 13.1 million acres of public land to coal mining.

The investment marks a significant policy shift toward fossil fuel production despite long-term industry trends. Coal has experienced sharp decline in recent decades. According to the Energy Information Administration, U.S. coal production in 2023 was less than half its 2008 level, and coal plants generated only about 15 percent of American electricity in 2024, compared to roughly 50 percent in 2000.

Energy analysts note that coal production faces structural challenges that extend beyond current market conditions. Aging infrastructure and competition from cheaper natural gas plants have created persistent headwinds for the industry. The federal investment could provide short-term support to coal producers, but economists question whether it can address the longer-term economic pressures reshaping the energy sector.

Trump stated that the administration aims to position the United States as "a massive energy exporter" and said the government is "lifting up our hard-working American miners like nobody has ever done before." The funding relies on presidential authority typically reserved for national security emergencies, with the administration citing energy supply disruptions from the Iran conflict as justification for the intervention.
