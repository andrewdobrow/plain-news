Senate Intelligence Chair calls Trump intelligence pick a national security risk

Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Mark Warner described Bill Pulte, named by President Trump as acting director of national intelligence, as a national security risk. Warner made the statement as scrutiny intensifies over Pulte's appointment to oversee the nation's intelligence agencies.

Representative Jim Himes joined the criticism, calling Pulte in an intelligence role Trump's worst and most dangerous appointment. The concerns from senior lawmakers on intelligence matters highlight opposition to Pulte's qualifications for the position. Trump has stated he wants Pulte to reduce the size of the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.

The position of director of national intelligence oversees 18 intelligence agencies including the CIA and NSA. The role was created after the September 11 attacks to coordinate intelligence gathering and sharing across the government. Pulte's appointment comes as Trump moves to reshape the intelligence community.

Separately, uncertainty has emerged around the Trump administration's plan to spin off mortgage giants Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The two government-sponsored enterprises have been under federal conservatorship since the 2008 financial crisis. Any restructuring would have significant implications for the housing finance system and mortgage markets.

The intelligence appointment requires Senate confirmation, where Warner's committee will play a central role in evaluating Pulte's background and qualifications. The public criticism from the committee chair signals potential difficulty in securing confirmation votes. Trump has made overhauling intelligence agencies a priority, arguing they need restructuring and size reduction.
