Former CBS News Anchor Scott Pelley Calls for Removal of Network News Chief Bari Weiss

Scott Pelley, a veteran journalist who spent decades at CBS News, was terminated by the network following a heated confrontation with management over recent staff cuts at 60 Minutes, according to reporting from the Guardian.

The conflict centered on changes made to the long-running news program last week, when CBS News fired its executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents, Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi, without providing specific reasons for the terminations.

During a Monday morning meeting with Nick Bilton, the newly appointed executive editor, Pelley criticized Bari Weiss, the former opinion commentator who joined CBS News in October as editor-in-chief. According to the Guardian, Pelley said Weiss was "murdering 60 Minutes" and accused her of not caring about the organization. "She was brought in to kill it and is doing exactly that," he told the group.

After the meeting, Weiss informed Pelley that his behavior was inappropriate. Pelley subsequently told staff members he expected to be terminated. On Tuesday evening, Bilton sent Pelley a message stating he had been "terminated for cause effective immediately." In the message, Bilton pointed to Pelley's conduct during their meeting, writing that Pelley had "hijacked" the conversation to "disparage me, my qualifications, and my intentions with remarkable incivility and contempt."

Bilton then notified the 60 Minutes staff of Pelley's departure in an email, stating the two had "parted ways." He noted his attempts to have conversations with Pelley over the weekend and that afternoon, though Pelley "was not" willing to find common ground.

Pelley's firing comes as the program faces significant upheaval. Earlier this year, journalist Anderson Cooper announced he was leaving the show. The departure leaves 60 Minutes with only three full-time correspondents ahead of its 59th season launch: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Norah O'Donnell contributes to the program as well.

In response to his termination, Pelley issued a public statement accusing CBS News executives of silencing employees and instructing him to "inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. He emphasized that 60 Minutes has remained America's top program "because our beloved audience finds integrity, quality, and humanity in our stories."

Rome Hartman, a longtime 60 Minutes producer who retired last year, praised Pelley as "among the all-time greats of CBS News." He criticized Weiss and Bilton for firing Pelley for pushing back during a staff meeting, noting the irony given Weiss's company the Free Press positions itself against cancel culture.

Pelley joined CBS News in 1989 and served as anchor and managing editor of the CBS Evening News from 2011 to 2017 before joining 60 Minutes as a correspondent.
