Scott Pelley Says Bari Weiss Wanted 60 Minutes to Misrepresent Police Shooting

Scott Pelley, a veteran correspondent at 60 Minutes, publicly criticized CBS News leadership during a staff meeting on Monday morning, targeting the network's recent personnel decisions and the editorial direction set by editor-in-chief Bari Weiss.

The confrontation followed CBS News's Thursday announcement that it had fired the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents, Sharyn Alfonsi and Cecilia Vega. During the Monday meeting with newly appointed executive producer Nick Bilton and managing editor Charles Forelle, Pelley launched a direct attack on Weiss's management of the program.

"She's murdering 60 Minutes," Pelley said, according to sources familiar with the situation. "She does not love this place. She was brought in to kill it and is doing exactly that." Pelley also criticized how the network handled the firing of executive producer Tanya Simon. When managing editor Forelle accused Pelley of being rude, Pelley responded that the network itself had been rude in its treatment of Simon.

The staff meeting revealed significant internal discord at the network. Bilton told Pelley that he would not be "intimidated" by his remarks, while staff members present gave Pelley a standing ovation in apparent support of his position. CBS News declined to comment on the meeting.

The tensions at 60 Minutes reflect broader concerns about editorial independence and journalistic integrity. In a statement released Wednesday, Alfonsi, who had reported a segment about a prison in El Salvador that was shelved by Weiss, stated: "The wall between editorial independence and corporate interest at CBS is being methodically torn down." She added that "journalists willing to challenge authority are being pushed aside in favor of those who will not."

Vega suggested that political bias was influencing editorial decisions. "In recent months, my producing teams and I have experienced efforts to insert political bias into our stories," she said. "Reporting teams have held back on submitting story pitches about important news topics out of fear of the internal repercussions." She called this "censorship, both imposed and self-driven" that is "dangerous for the show and dangerous for democracy." The network disputed Vega's characterization.

Despite the turmoil, some veteran correspondents have indicated they plan to remain with the show. Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim have said they will stay, though questions persist about the program's future composition and direction.

Pelley's public criticism represents an unusual departure from typical network news protocol, where such disputes are usually handled privately. According to sources, CBS News has made overtures to Pelley, recognizing his importance to the show, though the exchange raises questions about his long-term future at 60 Minutes.
