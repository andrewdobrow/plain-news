Scott Pelley Calls for Removal of CBS News Chief Bari Weiss

Former 60 Minutes correspondent Scott Pelley has publicly called for the removal of Bari Weiss as head of the CBS News division following his firing from the long-running news program. Pelley's departure came after an explosive confrontation with Nick Bilton, the show's newly appointed executive producer.

The conflict escalated during a heated Monday morning meeting where Pelley criticized Weiss's leadership. According to reports, Pelley stated that Weiss "does not love this place" and suggested she "was brought in to kill it." He also told colleagues that she was "murdering 60 Minutes."

CBS News management had terminated the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents, Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi, without providing specific reasons for their dismissals. Executives attempted to meet with Pelley following these terminations, but he initially declined. When he finally met with Weiss and other executives on Tuesday, they conveyed that his conduct in the previous day's meeting was inappropriate.

Bilton informed Pelley by message on Tuesday evening that he had been "terminated for cause effective immediately." In his communication, Bilton referenced Pelley's behavior, stating that "you hijacked my first meeting with staff to disparage me, my qualifications, and my intentions with remarkable incivility and contempt."

Pelley, who joined CBS News in 1989 and had worked at 60 Minutes since 2004, later issued a public statement accusing the network's new executives of silencing employees. He claimed they instructed him "to inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. In his statement shared on social media, Pelley emphasized that 60 Minutes has remained the top-rated program in America "because our beloved audience finds integrity, quality, and humanity in our stories."

Weiss, a former New York Times opinion editor and founder of the independent news site The Free Press, was appointed as editor-in-chief in October as part of a restructuring under parent company Paramount Skydance. Her appointment marked a departure from traditional network news leadership, with her background primarily in print and digital media rather than television production.

With Pelley's departure and Anderson Cooper's earlier announcement that he would be leaving the program, 60 Minutes is left with only three full-time correspondents: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and L Jon Wertheim. CBS News journalist and former evening news anchor Norah O'Donnell contributes to the show.

The situation represents one of the most visible leadership conflicts in recent CBS News history, with veteran journalists openly challenging executive decisions in major publications. Weiss has not publicly responded to Pelley's comments or accusations.
