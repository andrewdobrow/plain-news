Scott Pelley Calls for Removal of CBS News Chief Bari Weiss

Scott Pelley, a veteran correspondent who spent decades at CBS News, was terminated by the network after a heated confrontation with executives over significant staff reductions at 60 Minutes, according to reporting by The Guardian.

The conflict emerged following CBS News management's decision to fire the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents, Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi, without providing specific reasons for the terminations. During a Monday morning meeting with newly appointed executive editor Nick Bilton and other CBS News leadership, Pelley expressed his frustration with Bari Weiss, the former opinion commentator who joined the network in October as editor-in-chief.

"She's murdering 60 Minutes," Pelley said during the meeting, according to The Guardian. "She does not love this place. She was brought in to kill it and is doing exactly that."

Weiss conveyed to Pelley that his behavior during the meeting was inappropriate. Pelley subsequently told staff members he expected to be fired. On Tuesday evening, Bilton sent Pelley a termination message, citing his conduct in the meeting.

"Yesterday, you hijacked my first meeting with staff to disparage me, my qualifications, and my intentions with remarkable incivility and contempt," Bilton wrote in the message obtained by Puck's Dylan Byers.

Bilton also notified 60 Minutes staff of Pelley's departure, stating he had made repeated attempts to have direct conversations with Pelley over the weekend. "I made repeated attempts to have direct conversations with him over the weekend, and this afternoon I tried to find common ground. That was not the path Scott chose," Bilton wrote.

Pelley later issued a public statement accusing the network's new executives of silencing employees and claiming they instructed him "to inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. He emphasized that 60 Minutes had maintained its position as America's top program "because our beloved audience finds integrity, quality, and humanity in our stories."

The termination represents a significant shift at the storied news program. With Anderson Cooper announcing his departure earlier this year and Pelley now fired, 60 Minutes is left with only three full-time correspondents: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Norah O'Donnell contributes to the show as well.

The controversy comes months after David Ellison's Skydance acquired CBS's parent company Paramount. In December, Pelley had publicly praised the new management's approach to 60 Minutes, stating the show experienced "no corporate interference of any kind." Nine days later, Alfonsi revealed that Weiss had spiked her investigation into an El Salvador prison for "political" reasons, triggering one of the biggest controversies in the network's history.
