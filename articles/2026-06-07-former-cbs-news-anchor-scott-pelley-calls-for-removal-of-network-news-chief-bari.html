Former CBS Correspondent Scott Pelley Calls for News Chief Bari Weiss to Be Removed

Scott Pelley, a veteran 60 Minutes correspondent, was terminated by CBS News after a heated confrontation with network management over recent staff cuts and the direction of the program. The firing marked a dramatic escalation in tensions at the news division under new leadership.

The conflict emerged following a round of layoffs at 60 Minutes that shocked staff members. CBS News management fired the show's executive producer, executive editor, and two correspondents, Cecilia Vega and Sharyn Alfonsi, without providing specific reasons for the terminations. During a tense meeting on Monday morning with newly appointed executive editor Nick Bilton and another CBS News executive, Pelley directly criticized Bari Weiss, the opinion commentator who joined CBS News in October as editor-in-chief.

"She's murdering 60 Minutes," Pelley said at the meeting. "She does not love this place. She was brought in to kill it and is doing exactly that."

When Weiss later conveyed that Pelley's behavior was inappropriate, he told staff members he expected to be fired. On Tuesday evening, Bilton sent Pelley a message stating he had been "terminated for cause effective immediately." In the message, Bilton referenced Pelley's conduct during the meeting, saying Pelley had "hijacked" the discussion "to disparage me, my qualifications, and my intentions with remarkable incivility and contempt."

Pelley subsequently issued a public statement accusing the network's new executives of instructing him "to inject falsehoods and bias" into his reporting. He argued that 60 Minutes had succeeded for decades through its commitment to "integrity, quality, and humanity."

Pelley joined CBS News in 1989 and had worked on 60 Minutes since 2004. He previously served as chief White House correspondent and anchored the CBS Evening News from 2011 to 2017. His departure leaves the program with significant staffing challenges heading into its 59th season this fall.

The show now retains only three full-time correspondents: Lesley Stahl, Bill Whitaker, and Jon Wertheim. Anderson Cooper announced earlier this year that he was leaving to focus on his CNN responsibilities. The departures have raised concerns among CBS News employees about whether the program can maintain its position as the most-watched show in news.

Retired 60 Minutes producer Rome Hartman criticized Weiss and Bilton for terminating Pelley for pushing back aggressively during a staff meeting. He noted the irony that Weiss's company, the Free Press, positions itself against cancel culture while the network was firing someone for speaking his mind.

The situation represents one of the most significant upheavals in 60 Minutes' history since David Ellison's Skydance acquired Paramount and Weiss took over as editor-in-chief.
