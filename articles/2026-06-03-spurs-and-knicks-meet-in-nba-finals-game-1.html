Knicks defeat Spurs in Game 1 of NBA Finals

The New York Knicks won Game 1 of the NBA Finals against the San Antonio Spurs with a 105-95 victory in San Antonio on Wednesday night. The win extended the Knicks' remarkable playoff streak to 11 consecutive victories and moved them three wins away from their first championship since 1973.

The Knicks entered the game as heavy favorites despite facing Victor Wembanyama, described as the most exciting player in the world. The Spurs had earned their Finals berth by defeating the defending champion Oklahoma City Thunder in the previous round. However, the Knicks kept Wembanyama quiet for long stretches throughout the contest.

The game was tightly contested, with the Spurs leading by 14 points in the third quarter. The Knicks, demonstrating the same resilience that defined their playoff run, mounted a comeback similar to their Game 1 victory against the Cavaliers in the Eastern Conference Finals, when they overcame a 22-point deficit. New York took a 78-76 lead early in the fourth quarter before putting together an 11-0 run to close out the game.

Jalen Brunson was instrumental in the victory, particularly down the stretch. He finished with 30 points, including 13 in the fourth quarter. "Just sticking together, it wasn't really our night and wasn't really my night most of the night but we kept finding a way, kept chipping away," Brunson said after the game. "Just knowing we have each other's back, there's a lot of things we could have done better, but I think our togetherness was really the biggest difference."

OG Anunoby also stepped up when it mattered, scoring 12 of his 17 points in the fourth quarter. The Knicks demonstrated excellent execution down the stretch, recording no turnovers in the final quarter.

Wembanyama led the Spurs with 26 points, 12 rebounds, and three blocks, but he was far from his dominant best. He shot just 6-for-21 from the field and acknowledged his poor performance. "I was bad tonight, it's not more complicated than that," Wembanyama said. He denied that nerves or the occasion affected the team's performance.

The Spurs struggled in the fourth quarter, committing five turnovers while shooting just 28.6 percent from the field.

Brunson had an injury scare of his own, colliding with Harrison Barnes at the end of the first quarter. He briefly left the game with a knee injury but returned in the second quarter and appeared to aggravate an ankle injury but remained in the game.

Game 2 takes place Friday night in San Antonio before the series shifts to Madison Square Garden on Monday for what is expected to be a raucous Game 3.
