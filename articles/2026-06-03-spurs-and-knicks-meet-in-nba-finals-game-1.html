Knicks rally from 29 points down to win Game 4, move within one win of title

The New York Knicks completed a historic comeback against the San Antonio Spurs in Game 4 of the NBA Finals on Wednesday night, erasing a 29-point second-half deficit to secure a dramatic victory. The win represents the largest comeback in NBA Finals history and puts the Knicks just one game away from clinching their first championship in more than half a century.

OG Anunoby delivered the decisive play in the closing seconds, hitting a tip-in that sealed the improbable victory. The shot capped off a stunning fourth-quarter performance that energized both the team and the city of New York. With the win, the Knicks moved to a 3-1 series advantage over the Spurs.

The victory sparked jubilant celebrations throughout New York City, with thousands of fans gathering outside Madison Square Garden to mark the occasion. Celebrities joined fans in celebrating the momentous achievement, which has become etched in Knicks franchise history. New Yorkers woke Thursday morning still celebrating the team's remarkable performance.

The comeback showcased the Knicks' resilience and determination after facing what appeared to be an insurmountable deficit during the second half. The team's ability to mount such a significant comeback demonstrates the depth of their roster and their mental toughness in crucial moments.

However, the celebrations were not without incident. Security was heightened around Madison Square Garden for Game 4 after disorder erupted at a watch party in Bryant Park following Monday's Game 3 loss. That event resulted in 21 arrests, with five police officers injured. Videos circulated online showing confrontations between fans, including some targeting supporters wearing Spurs apparel.

In response to the earlier chaos, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani approved a ticketed watch party for Game 4 with a capacity limit of 1,000 people. Entry into the restricted area surrounding the arena was limited to those with game tickets, train tickets, proof of residence, or business in the area. All attendees underwent screening at a designated access point.

Following Wednesday's game, tensions continued outside the arena. Victor Wembanyama, the Spurs' star player, was jeered by Knicks fans as he entered his hotel, and video showed an egg thrown in his direction as he arrived with security.

Despite the surrounding incidents, the Knicks' historic achievement remains the focal point. The team's extraordinary comeback from a 29-point deficit has galvanized the city and positioned them one victory away from ending a championship drought that has lasted over 50 years. Game 5 will offer the Knicks an opportunity to complete their remarkable Finals run.
