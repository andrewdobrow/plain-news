Knicks rally from 29 points down to beat Spurs, take 3-1 NBA Finals lead

The New York Knicks achieved the largest comeback in NBA Finals history on Wednesday night, erasing a 29-point deficit to defeat the San Antonio Spurs 107-106 in Game 4. OG Anunoby's tip-in with 1.2 seconds remaining provided the winning margin, moving the Knicks within one victory of their first championship in 53 years.

The comeback unfolded before a shell-shocked Madison Square Garden crowd that had witnessed the Spurs build their commanding advantage earlier in the contest. The Knicks methodically chipped away at the deficit through disciplined offensive execution and defensive intensity, ultimately seizing control in the final moments.

Jalen Brunson led the Knicks' charge with 32 points, including crucial scoring in the final period. Anunoby contributed 28 points throughout the game before delivering the decisive tip-in off Brunson's missed three-pointer. The pair's combined performance proved instrumental in completing the historic rally.

The Game 3 matchup at Madison Square Garden had drawn significant attention beyond the basketball itself. The atmosphere included high-profile attendees such as Donald Trump, who watched from a suite and received heavy boos when displayed on the arena's jumbotron during the national anthem. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani attended separately, having paid approximately $1,000 for a standing-room-only ticket. Celebrity attendees included Spike Lee, Timothée Chalamet, Ben Stiller, Tina Fey, Tracy Morgan, and Larry David.

Physical play characterized the series, particularly involving Spurs center Victor Wembanyama. The 7-foot-4 center avoided a flagrant foul after shoving Brunson during Game 3, which the Spurs won 115-111. Wembanyama had recorded 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists, and three blocks in that contest. During Game 4, Wembanyama engaged in physical exchanges with Knicks center Mitchell Robinson, reportedly telling Robinson repeatedly that he was in his head after taking a forearm to the face.

The Knicks' comeback in Game 4 represented a dramatic turnaround from the previous game's frustration. In that contest, New York had shot just eight free throws in the second half compared to 24 for San Antonio, sparking fan complaints about officiating.

With the series now at 3-1 in New York's favor, the Knicks have the opportunity to clinch the championship at home in their next game. The Spurs face elimination and must win to extend the series after squandering their substantial first-half advantage in Game 4.

The historic comeback surpassed all previous NBA Finals records for largest deficit overcome, cementing the Knicks' place in basketball history while positioning them on the threshold of ending their five-decade championship drought.
