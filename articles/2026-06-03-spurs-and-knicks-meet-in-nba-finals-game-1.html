Knicks beat Spurs in Game 1 of NBA Finals behind Jalen Brunson

The New York Knicks defeated the San Antonio Spurs 105-95 in Game 1 of the NBA Finals on Wednesday night in San Antonio, taking a crucial 1-0 series lead. The victory was particularly impressive given that the Knicks arrived at the Finals riding an 11-game winning streak, only to face a Spurs team fresh off eliminating the defending champion Oklahoma City Thunder.

Jalen Brunson led the Knicks to victory with 30 points, proving decisive in what was a defensive, low-scoring affair. The Knicks kept Victor Wembanyama relatively quiet for stretches throughout the game, limiting the impact of the Spurs' star player in his Finals debut. Wembanyama struggled with his shot, finishing 6-for-21 from the field while committing six turnovers.

The game was marked by poor shooting from both teams. The Knicks shot just 31 percent from three-point range, while the Spurs managed only 26 percent. Both squads struggled to establish offensive rhythm in what became a grind-it-out contest typical of championship basketball. The opening quarter was particularly ragged, with neither team able to gain early separation.

Rather than dominating from start to finish, the Knicks had to fight back. The Spurs built a 14-point lead in the third quarter, but New York mounted a late surge to overcome the deficit and secure the win. The ability to battle back and close out the game demonstrated the Knicks' resilience and determination.

The victory brings the Knicks tantalizingly close to their first championship since 1973, with the team now needing just three more wins. For a franchise that has endured a 53-year title drought, this opening game victory carries enormous significance. The last time the Knicks reached the Finals was in 1999, when they faced these same Spurs and lost the series 4-1.

San Antonio will need to make adjustments as the series continues. The Spurs' defense must find ways to better contain Brunson's playmaking, while their offensive consistency requires improvement. Wembanyama, despite his struggles shooting, remained defensively engaged throughout the contest.

The Spurs can take some encouragement from the historical precedent of their 1999 Finals matchup against New York, but they face the challenge of bouncing back on their home court in Game 2. The Knicks, meanwhile, will look to extend their series advantage before the Finals shift to New York, where they hope to close out the series on their home floor.
