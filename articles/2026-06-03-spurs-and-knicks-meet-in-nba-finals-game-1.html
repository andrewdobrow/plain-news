Spurs Beat Knicks 115-111 in NBA Finals Game 3 Behind Wembanyama

The San Antonio Spurs defeated the New York Knicks 115-111 at Madison Square Garden on Monday night, snapping New York's 13-game postseason winning streak and cutting the Knicks' series lead to 2-1.

Victor Wembanyama delivered his best performance of the Finals, recording 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks. Stephon Castle contributed 23 points, including two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining in the third quarter. The Spurs' victory came in a tightly contested game that remained competitive throughout.

The Knicks were led by Jalen Brunson, who finished with 32 points, and OG Anunoby, who added 28. However, the rest of New York's roster struggled in the fourth quarter as the game went back and forth between both teams.

The game took place against an extraordinary backdrop of celebrity and political attention. President Donald Trump attended as a guest of Knicks owner James Dolan, watching from a suite. When he appeared on the jumbotron during the national anthem, he received heavy boos from the crowd. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani also attended, purchasing a standing-room-only ticket for approximately 1,000 dollars rather than premium seating. Celebrities including Spike Lee, Timothée Chalamet, Ben Stiller, Tina Fey, Tracy Morgan and Larry David occupied celebrity row.

The matchup generated intense anticipation leading up to tipoff. Ticket prices on the secondary market drew significant discussion, with some described as "ridiculous" by Knicks player Josh Hart. The Knicks reported that the winning auction bid for two celebrity row seats reached 1 million dollars. The game marked New York's first NBA Finals home game since June 25, 1999.

Trump's attendance prompted heightened security measures around Madison Square Garden, with attendees facing airport-style security screening. A planned watch party was cancelled due to the increased security requirements. The city designated an additional watch party location at Bryant Park to accommodate fans unable to secure tickets.

The atmosphere reflected the significance of the moment for the Knicks franchise, which had not reached the Finals in over 25 years. Supporters traveled from around the world to witness the historic games.

Despite the loss, the Spurs remain competitive in the series. The Knicks still hold the advantage, but San Antonio demonstrated it could win at Madison Square Garden. No NBA team has lost the first two games of the Finals on its home floor and gone on to win the championship, though the Spurs' hopes of making history remain alive.

Game 4 was scheduled for Wednesday in New York. The Knicks continued their pursuit of their first championship since 1973.
