Knicks take 2-0 NBA Finals lead after holding off Spurs in Game 2

The New York Knicks defeated the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 of the NBA Finals on Friday night, securing a commanding 2-0 series lead before returning home to Madison Square Garden. The victory moves the Knicks within two wins of their first championship since 1973.

Jalen Brunson delivered the decisive moment when it mattered most, sinking a go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining after Victor Wembanyama committed a costly turnover. Moments later, Wembanyama's clean look from the elbow at the buzzer rimmed out, sealing the heartbreaking loss for San Antonio.

The win extended the Knicks' postseason winning streak to 13 consecutive games, matching one of the longest single-season playoff winning streaks in NBA history. New York also became only the third team to win the first two games of an NBA Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets.

Brunson finished with 20 points on 7-for-25 shooting despite battling a sore knee and ankle. He provided the composure the Knicks needed in crucial moments, much as he did in Game 1's furious fourth-quarter comeback. Karl-Anthony Towns led the team with 21 points while adding 13 rebounds.

Wembanyama responded strongly after a frustrating Finals debut, scoring 29 points on 11-for-21 shooting in 40 minutes. However, his late-game mistakes ultimately defined the outcome. His wayward pass off the back of teammate Stephon Castle while attempting to initiate the final possession proved pivotal, as did his missed jumper at the buzzer.

The Spurs mounted a remarkable comeback from a 14-point deficit midway through the fourth quarter. San Antonio engineered a 14-0 run behind Wembanyama and De'Aaron Fox to tie the game at 97-97 with three minutes remaining, then took their first lead of the half with less than a minute to play.

Brunson quickly answered with a driving basket to tie the game at 104-104. After Wembanyama came up empty on the Spurs' next possession, the Knicks called timeout. San Antonio forced a miss but Wembanyama threw the ball away on the ensuing possession, allowing Brunson to draw a foul and convert the winning free throw.

"Of course I liked the shot," Wembanyama said afterward. "In moments like this, results matter more than process. We just needed to score. I just needed to score. That's the whole point."

The series now shifts to New York for Games 3 and 4. San Antonio faces an uphill battle, as no team in NBA history has won the championship after losing the first two games at home. Game 3 is scheduled for Monday night at Madison Square Garden.
