Knicks take 2-0 NBA Finals lead after Wembanyama turnover in closing seconds

The New York Knicks moved within two wins of their first NBA championship since 1973, defeating the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 to take a commanding 2-0 series lead in the NBA Finals. The victory came down to a crucial turnover by Spurs star Victor Wembanyama in the final seconds.

Jalen Brunson, the Knicks' All-NBA guard, delivered the decisive moment by sinking a go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining following Wembanyama's costly error. Moments later, Wembanyama's clean look from the elbow at the buzzer rimmed off the back of the rim, sealing the Knicks' win and extending their postseason dominance.

The result marks a historic achievement for New York. The Knicks became only the third team to win the first two games of an NBA Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets. After stealing Game 1 with a furious fourth-quarter comeback, the Knicks demonstrated remarkable resilience by closing out another thriller away from home.

The Spurs mounted a late rally to pull within striking distance, but Wembanyama's turnover and missed final attempt proved decisive. The young center, who has been a cornerstone of San Antonio's surprising Finals run, acknowledged the difficult stretch after the game.

New York's offensive firepower has been on full display throughout the postseason. The Knicks' dynamic ball movement and off-the-ball action helped them dismantle three opponents on the way to the Finals. Role players have been making shots consistently, and Brunson has risen to the occasion when the game is on the line, creating what many observers describe as Team of Destiny energy.

The series now shifts to Madison Square Garden for Game 3, where the Knicks will look to put themselves in position to clinch the championship. No team has ever come back from a 3-0 deficit in NBA playoff history, meaning the Spurs face an increasingly difficult path to winning the Finals.

San Antonio's youth and resilience have been impressive throughout the postseason, particularly in a bruising Game 7 victory over Oklahoma City in the previous round. However, the exhaustion from that series combined with the Knicks' fresher roster may continue to take its toll.

The Knicks have been playing at an extraordinary level, with their best-in-the-playoffs defensive net rating complementing their high-powered offense. For Wembanyama and the Spurs, Game 3 at Madison Square Garden represents a critical opportunity to avoid falling into an insurmountable hole. The 20-year-old generational talent will need to bounce back quickly as San Antonio attempts to prevent the Knicks from claiming their first championship in over fifty years.
