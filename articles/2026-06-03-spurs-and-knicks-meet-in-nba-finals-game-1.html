Knicks take 2-0 lead in NBA Finals after holding off Spurs

The New York Knicks defeated the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 to take a commanding 2-0 series lead, moving within two wins of their first NBA championship since 1973. The victory capped an remarkable postseason run, extending the Knicks' winning streak to 13 consecutive games.

The game came down to the final seconds. With 9.5 seconds remaining, Jalen Brunson drew a foul and made the go-ahead free throw after Victor Wembanyama turned the ball over. Wembanyama's attempt at the buzzer from near the elbow bounced off the back rim, sealing the Knicks' second road victory of the Finals. New York became only the third team in NBA history to win the first two games of the Finals on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets.

Brunson finished with 20 points on 7-for-25 shooting despite battling injuries. Karl-Anthony Towns led the Knicks with 21 points and added 13 rebounds, highlighting the team's balanced offensive approach. "We've gotten leads and we've held the leads," Towns said after the game. "Today, the Spurs fought. They got themselves back into the lead and put themselves in a position to win, but we found a way to get it done."

The Spurs mounted a furious fourth-quarter comeback. After trailing by 14 points midway through the final quarter, San Antonio used a 14-0 run to tie the game at 97-97 with three minutes remaining. Wembanyama, looking to bounce back from shooting 6-for-21 in Game 1, finished with 29 points on 11-for-21 shooting while playing 40 minutes. However, his crucial turnover in the final moments proved costly.

"Of course I liked the shot," Wembanyama said after the game. "In moments like this, results matter more than process. We just needed to score. I just needed to score."

The Spurs face a historical mountain to climb. No team in NBA history has won the championship after losing the first two games of the Finals at home. San Antonio must now travel to Madison Square Garden, where the Knicks have the opportunity to take a commanding 3-0 series lead in Game 3 on Monday night.

The return to New York has generated intense excitement throughout the city. Secondary-market ticket prices for Game 3 have approached $9,000 for some seats, reflecting the historic nature of the moment. The Knicks' Finals appearance has captured the imagination of New Yorkers waiting more than five decades for a championship.
