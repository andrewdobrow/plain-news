Knicks take 2-0 NBA Finals lead with one-point victory over Spurs

The New York Knicks defeated the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in Game 2 of the NBA Finals on Friday night, securing a commanding 2-0 series lead as the matchup shifts to Madison Square Garden. The Knicks became only the third team in NBA history to win the first two Finals games on the road, joining the 1993 Chicago Bulls and 1995 Houston Rockets.

Playing in San Antonio, the Knicks relied on their star point guard Jalen Brunson to deliver in the final moments. Brunson sank the go-ahead free throw with 9.5 seconds remaining after a crucial turnover by Spurs star Victor Wembanyama. On the final possession, Wembanyama's clean look from the elbow at the buzzer caromed off the back rim, sealing the Knicks' one-point victory.

This marks the second consecutive close game in the series. The Knicks also won Game 1 in a fourth-quarter comeback, overcoming a 14-point deficit in that contest. Brunson has emerged as a key performer in clutch moments throughout New York's playoff run, consistently delivering when games hang in the balance.

The Knicks' success extends beyond these Finals games. New York entered this championship matchup on an 11-game winning streak, dominating opponents throughout their playoff journey. The team now stands just two wins away from capturing their first NBA championship since 1973.

For the Spurs, the loss represents a significant setback. San Antonio had knocked out the defending champion Oklahoma City Thunder in the previous round and entered the Finals with considerable momentum. However, the team struggled to maintain that form on their home court. In Game 1, Wembanyama, widely considered the most exciting player in the NBA, was kept quiet for stretches and shot just 6-of-21 from the field while finishing with 26 points, 12 rebounds and three blocks.

Following Game 2, Wembanyama acknowledged his performance fell short. "I was bad tonight, it's not more complicated than that," he said, dismissing suggestions that the occasion had affected the team's play. "It definitely felt special for sure, but nothing close that could be an excuse."

The Spurs now face an uphill climb. No team in NBA Finals history has overcome a three-game deficit, so winning at least one game in New York is essential to keep their championship hopes alive. The series continues at Madison Square Garden, where the Knicks will look to extend their lead while playing in front of their home crowd. The Spurs must find a way to steal a victory in that hostile environment and bring the series back to San Antonio with momentum on their side.
