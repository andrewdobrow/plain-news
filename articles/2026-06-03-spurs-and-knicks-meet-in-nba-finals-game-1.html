Spurs defeat Knicks 115-111 in Game 3, trail Finals 2-1

Victor Wembanyama delivered a breakout performance to lead the San Antonio Spurs past the New York Knicks 115-111 in Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden, snapping New York's 13-game postseason winning streak and cutting the series deficit to 2-1.

Wembanyama finished with 32 points, eight rebounds, six assists and three blocks, his best effort of the series so far. Stephon Castle contributed 23 points and hit two crucial free throws with 6.8 seconds remaining in the third quarter. For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson scored 32 points and OG Anunoby added 28, but the rest of the roster struggled in the fourth quarter, going just 3 for 20 from the field.

The game unfolded against an extraordinary backdrop at Madison Square Garden, with the first NBA Finals game in New York since 1999 drawing high-profile attendees including the sitting U.S. president, Mayor Zohran Mamdani, and numerous celebrities. The atmosphere created an intense environment that tested both teams' focus.

After a difficult Game 2 in which he missed a potential game-winner, Wembanyama entered Game 3 with renewed energy. "Really tried to relax," he said of his approach after the previous loss. "The playoffs, it's like a whirlwind. It's hard to put your head out of the water." He started strong, scoring nine points in his opening nine minutes.

The Spurs built an 11-point first-quarter lead before the Knicks responded aggressively, outscoring San Antonio 42-24 in the second quarter to take a 64-57 halftime advantage. The third quarter remained competitive, with neither team establishing a significant cushion.

The fourth quarter proved decisive. While Brunson contributed 12 points in the final period to keep New York close, his teammates couldn't provide enough support. The Knicks' frustration grew over foul call discrepancies, with chants of "refs, you suck!" breaking out multiple times. New York took just eight free throws in the second half compared to 24 for San Antonio.

Outside of Wembanyama and Castle, the Spurs benefited from the Knicks' 13 turnovers, which resulted in 21 points. Karl-Anthony Towns finished with just 11 points and eight rebounds after consecutive double-doubles in San Antonio, while the rest of the Knicks' supporting cast struggled.

Brunson acknowledged the setback but remained focused on improvement. "We've done our best to try to learn from wins over the past couple weeks. But now we have to learn from a loss," he said.

The series continues with Game 4 on Wednesday in New York. The Knicks need two more wins to clinch the championship, while the Spurs must win consecutive games to force the series back to San Antonio.
