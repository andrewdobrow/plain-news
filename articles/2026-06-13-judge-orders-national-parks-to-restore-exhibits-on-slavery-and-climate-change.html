Judge orders national parks to restore exhibits on slavery and climate change

A federal judge has ordered the Trump administration to restore signs and exhibits at national parks that address topics including slavery and climate change. The ruling blocks the removal of educational materials that had been targeted under a recent executive directive.

The order requires officials to re-install displays that had been taken down from parks across the country. The affected exhibits include historical information about slavery and environmental content related to climate change. Multiple national park sites had begun removing or covering such materials following the administration's directive.

The judge's decision halts what the court characterized as censorship of park exhibits. The ruling comes after challenges were filed against the removal policy, with plaintiffs arguing that the directive violated educational and historical preservation mandates. The order applies to materials that had been removed or were scheduled for removal under the executive action.

National parks serve as educational institutions that preserve and interpret American history and natural resources for millions of visitors annually. The affected exhibits provide context about historical events and scientific information that parks have traditionally presented as part of their mission. The removal of such materials had drawn objections from historians, scientists, and park advocacy groups.

The Trump administration has not yet indicated whether it will appeal the ruling. Park officials must now work to restore the removed materials to their original locations. The decision represents a significant check on the executive directive and affirms the role of national parks in presenting comprehensive historical and scientific information to the public.
