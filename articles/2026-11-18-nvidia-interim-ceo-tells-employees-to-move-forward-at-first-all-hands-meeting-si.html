Nvidia interim CEO tells employees to move forward at first all-hands meeting since Huang resignation

Nvidia interim chief executive Colette Kress told employees to move forward on Wednesday during the company's first all-hands meeting since founder Jensen Huang resigned, according to an attendee who spoke to CNBC. Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones and hyperscale and high-performance computing head Ian Buck, along with several other senior executives. The meeting lasted about an hour and was held in person and streamed on Microsoft Teams.

Kress opened with the message about moving forward and announced that the yearly review process would be moved up from March to December, when employees typically receive pay raises and stock grants. Jones discussed the search for the next chief executive. Nvidia shares are down 37 percent from their 52-week high.

Employees have spoken up on social media in recent days to call for the return of Huang as chief executive, and one senior employee reportedly sent a letter to the board warning Nvidia would lose irreplaceable talent if it does not consider reinstating Huang. One attendee said the general sentiment in the room was that employees want Huang to come back. Another person familiar with the matter disagreed, saying that employees welcomed the remarks from leadership and the visibility into company leadership.

Jones said during the earnings call on Tuesday that the Nvidia board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive. Some executives disagreed with the statement, with one employee questioning why Jones would make such a definitive statement while the investigation is still playing out. The meeting came one day after Huang said he was ready to return as chief executive should the board want him. Multiple employees said Huang's letter fired employees up. The executives did not answer questions during Wednesday's all-hands meeting.
