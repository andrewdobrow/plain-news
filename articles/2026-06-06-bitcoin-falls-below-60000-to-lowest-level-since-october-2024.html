Bitcoin Falls Below $60,000 in Worst Week Since FTX Collapse

Bitcoin dropped below $60,000 this week, marking the worst weekly performance for major cryptocurrencies since the November 2022 collapse of the FTX exchange. Ether also experienced sharp declines as digital assets erased all gains accumulated since the November 2024 presidential election.

The cryptocurrency fell to its lowest level since October 2024, wiping out months of price appreciation. Both Bitcoin and Ether suffered comparable percentage losses during the current downturn, reflecting broad weakness rather than isolated pressure on individual tokens.

The sell-off erased what had been a period of strength for cryptocurrencies following the 2024 election. Bitcoin had climbed steadily in the months after November, buoyed by expectations of favorable regulatory treatment and institutional adoption. Those gains have now been completely reversed, with prices falling below pre-election levels.

The magnitude of this week's decline places it among the most severe sell-offs in recent cryptocurrency history. The last comparable weekly decline occurred during the FTX crisis, when the major exchange's sudden bankruptcy triggered widespread panic selling across digital asset markets.

Market analysts pointed to several factors weighing on digital assets, though no single catalyst appeared to trigger the decline. The sharp reversal highlights ongoing volatility in cryptocurrency markets, where rapid price swings remain common despite growing institutional participation. Trading volumes increased during the sell-off as investors sought to exit positions, adding to downward pressure on prices.

The broader economic environment has also created headwinds for digital assets. Rising inflation and economic uncertainty stemming from geopolitical tensions have rattled financial markets more broadly. US stock markets experienced their own significant declines this week, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq index closing down 4 percent in a single day, the largest drop in over a year. The S&P 500 and Dow Jones also fell sharply.

Despite some recent positive economic indicators, including steady job growth in the labor market, investor sentiment remains fragile across asset classes. The combination of persistent inflation concerns and geopolitical risk has prompted investors to reassess their portfolios and reduce exposure to riskier investments, including cryptocurrencies.

This week's downturn demonstrates that cryptocurrency markets remain highly sensitive to broader economic shifts and investor sentiment. While institutional participation in digital assets has grown significantly over the past few years, the sector continues to experience sharp reversals that can wipe out substantial gains in relatively short timeframes. The speed at which Bitcoin erased nearly a year's worth of gains underscores the continued volatility that characterizes cryptocurrency investing, even as the market has matured considerably since the days of the FTX collapse.
