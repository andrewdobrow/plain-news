Bitcoin drops below $60,000 in worst weekly decline since FTX collapse

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 this week, marking the cryptocurrency's steepest weekly decline since the FTX exchange collapsed in late 2022. The drop erased all gains Bitcoin had made since the November 2024 presidential election and pushed prices to their lowest levels since October 2024.

Ether, the second-largest cryptocurrency, also experienced sharp losses during the week. Both digital assets suffered significant sell-offs as investors pulled back from cryptocurrency markets. The decline represents a reversal of fortune for Bitcoin, which had rallied following political developments in late 2024.

The cryptocurrency market downturn comes amid broader volatility in digital asset trading. Bitcoin's fall below the $60,000 threshold marks a psychological barrier for traders who had seen the currency climb substantially higher in previous months. The severity of the weekly drop has drawn comparisons to the market turmoil that followed the collapse of FTX, one of the largest cryptocurrency exchanges, which filed for bankruptcy in November 2022 after revelations of mismanaged customer funds.

The sustained price decline has affected cryptocurrency investors across the board. Industry observers note that the current market conditions reflect heightened uncertainty among traders. The drop has wiped out months of gains and returned Bitcoin to price levels not seen since the autumn of 2024, when the cryptocurrency was trading at significantly lower valuations before its election-related surge.
