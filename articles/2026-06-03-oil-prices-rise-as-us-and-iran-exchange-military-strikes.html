Israel and Iran halt strikes after Trump intervention

Israel and Iran have announced a pause in military operations following pressure from President Donald Trump to "immediately stop shooting." The halt marks a temporary end to a dramatic escalation that threatened to destabilize the entire Middle East region.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu confirmed the ceasefire in a televised address, stating that Israel's "fire on this front has been halted" after Iran's initial strike was repelled. However, Netanyahu made clear the pause is conditional, warning that Israel would "respond with force" if Iran attacks again. "If that terrorist regime makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force," he said.

The recent violence represented the most direct confrontation between the two countries since an April ceasefire. Iranian ballistic missiles targeted Israeli territory, prompting Israeli warplane strikes on Iran in response. The exchange sent shockwaves through global markets, with oil prices rising 5% as investors worried about broader regional instability.

Trump claimed credit for de-escalating the situation, saying both countries wanted "an immediate ceasefire" and that "final negotiations on peace are proceeding." The president has reportedly warned Netanyahu that Israel would be "on your own very soon" if attacks continued, according to reports of their phone conversation.

Yet analysts caution that this ceasefire is extremely fragile. Multiple flashpoints could trigger fresh exchanges of missiles and strikes at any moment. The situation remains complicated by broader conflicts in the region, particularly Israel's military operations in Lebanon against Hezbollah, an Iranian-backed militant group.

Israeli officials have rejected Iranian attempts to link a permanent ceasefire to Israel halting its Lebanon offensive. Israel's defense minister stated Monday that Israel would continue operating against Hezbollah and would strike Beirut if the group attacked Israeli territory. Recent Israeli attacks on the southern suburbs of Beirut, a Hezbollah stronghold, triggered Iran's missile barrages on Sunday.

Iran signaled it remains ready to fight back. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker and top negotiator, posted on social media that Tehran would not tolerate what it termed "repeated violations." He said Iran would respond similarly unless there was genuine willingness to build trust.

Meanwhile, the Houthis, Yemen-based militia forces aligned with Iran, pledged to prevent Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea and claimed they had fired missiles at Israel in recent days. This adds another layer of complexity to an already volatile situation.

The ceasefire provides temporary breathing room as Trump pushes toward broader peace negotiations, but deep mistrust between the parties and competing strategic interests suggest tensions could reignite quickly. Netanyahu faces domestic political pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities, while Iran shows no signs of backing down from its regional ambitions.
