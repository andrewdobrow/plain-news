Israel and Iran Pause Strikes After Trump Claims Progress on Nuclear Talks

Israel and Iran have halted military operations following an exchange of strikes, with President Trump claiming progress toward nuclear negotiations has influenced the decision to stand down.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced that Israel's military actions are temporarily suspended after speaking with Trump. Iran's armed forces also confirmed they have stopped their attacks. Both nations, however, signaled they remain prepared to resume hostilities if the ceasefire is violated.

The pause follows what sources described as a significant 15-hour exchange of strikes between the two countries. Trump had urged Netanyahu not to respond to Iran's attacks, telling reporters that he did not want escalation to derail ongoing negotiations. "I'm about to call Bibi right now and tell him not to respond. Both of them have already done their part. Israel had its strike and Iran had its strike. We don't need another one," Trump said.

Initially, Netanyahu continued military action despite Trump's calls for restraint. The Israeli military confirmed it struck military targets in western and central Iran, though it provided no further details. Trump subsequently warned Netanyahu that Israel would be "on your own" if attacks on Iran continued. The White House did not immediately confirm whether the Israeli strikes were coordinated with the United States.

Trump has repeatedly claimed progress toward a deal with Iran, telling Axios that he is concerned escalation could jeopardize negotiations. "I don't want it to blow up because of what's happening now," Trump said.

The escalating strikes had raised concerns about a wider regional conflict engulfing the Middle East. The temporary pause creates space for potential diplomatic progress on nuclear talks, though the fragility of the ceasefire remains evident.

Iran appears emboldened by the outcome of the exchange. According to BBC analysis, Iran's decision to proceed with strikes despite potential damage to peace talks may reflect how its leaders view their current position. The missile exchange demonstrated the constraints facing both sides and exposed the challenges Trump faces in managing his relationship with Netanyahu while pursuing negotiations with Tehran.

Iran's parliament speaker and top negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, stated that US bases and assets in the region had become "legitimate targets" following the Israeli strike on southern Beirut in Lebanon.

Both countries have maintained their military capability and expressed willingness to resume operations if they determine violations have occurred. Netanyahu specifically said strikes were on hold "at the moment," emphasizing the conditional nature of the ceasefire.

The situation continues to evolve as Trump pursues negotiations with Iran while attempting to prevent further escalation in the region.
