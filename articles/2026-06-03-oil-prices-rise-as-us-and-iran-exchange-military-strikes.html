Israel and Iran halt strikes after Trump intervention

Israel and Iran have agreed to pause military operations following calls from President Donald Trump, with both nations indicating they are ready to resume attacks if the ceasefire breaks down.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced the halt in a televised address, stating that Israel's "fire on this front has been halted" after Iran was struck and stopped attacking. He warned that if Iran attacks again, Israel will "respond with force." Iran's armed forces similarly confirmed they had stopped military action.

The pause follows the most direct confrontation between the two countries since an April ceasefire. The recent escalation began when Israel struck targets in Iran, including a petrochemical complex and defence systems across multiple Iranian cities. Iran responded with ballistic missile attacks on Israel, which then launched retaliatory airstrikes.

Trump claimed the ceasefire represents progress toward resolving the broader conflict, though he did not specify a timeline for any agreement. He posted on social media that both sides wanted "an immediate CEASEFIRE" and that "final negotiations on 'Peace' are proceeding."

However, analysts warn the situation remains extremely fragile. Multiple flashpoints could trigger fresh exchanges of strikes at any moment. Israeli officials have rejected Iranian attempts to link any ceasefire to Israel halting its military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon. Israel's Defence Minister said Israel would continue operating against the militant group and would strike Beirut if Hezbollah attacked.

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator, said Tehran would not tolerate what it called "repeated violation" and that Iran's response would remain unchanged until genuine trust is built.

According to reports, Trump has pressured Netanyahu to stop attacks in Lebanon to create space for negotiations. Netanyahu, however, faces domestic pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities ahead of elections later this year.

The violence has extended beyond direct Israeli-Iranian exchanges. Yemen's Houthi rebels, which are aligned with Iran, have fired at Israel and warned they would target Israeli-affiliated ships in the Red Sea. The group controls shipping routes near the Strait of Hormuz, making their involvement significant for global oil supplies.

The escalation disrupted financial markets, with oil prices rising 5 percent before stocks recovered when both sides announced the halt.

The broader conflict began in February with strikes that killed Iran's supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. The April ceasefire had held for over a month before the recent flare-up, though sporadic clashes continued in the Gulf region.

Trump said a U.S. blockade of Iranian ports would remain in place until a final deal is reached. Iran's foreign ministry accused Israel of sabotaging diplomacy, claiming Israel could not act without U.S. coordination.
