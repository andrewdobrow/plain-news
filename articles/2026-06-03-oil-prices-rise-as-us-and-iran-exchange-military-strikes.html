Iran and Israel halt strikes after exchange of fire, both warn readiness to resume

Israel and Iran have agreed to pause their military operations following the first direct exchange of fire between the two countries since a ceasefire was established in April. However, both nations have signaled they remain prepared to resume attacks if circumstances change.

The escalation began when Iran launched approximately 30 missiles at Israel, apparently in response to an Israeli strike in Lebanon. Israel then conducted two waves of airstrikes inside Iran, targeting military installations and a petrochemical complex. The Israeli military said it struck and dismantled Iranian defense systems deployed across multiple areas. Explosions were reported in Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz, and Tehran, though Iranian officials provided no details on damage sustained.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced that Israel's military operations are on hold following a phone call with President Trump. Iran's leadership similarly declared it would cease attacks but maintained its right to respond if provoked further.

Trump's role in de-escalating the situation appears significant. A senior US official told the Associated Press that Trump had urged Netanyahu not to retaliate immediately after the Iranian missile attack, and that "Trump got Bibi to hold off for the time being." Trump also posted on Truth Social calling for both nations to stop military operations immediately.

However, Trump's exact position regarding the Israeli strikes remains unclear. Speaking to the Financial Times before Israel struck Iran, Trump stated he "called all the shots" in directing how the war should proceed, suggesting he exercises significant control over Israeli military decisions. The White House did not respond to inquiries about whether the Israeli strikes were coordinated with the United States.

Iranian officials expressed skepticism about American claims of non-involvement. A foreign ministry spokesperson stated that "no one believes that the Zionist regime would carry out any action without prior coordination and cooperation with the United States." Some Iranian analysts argued that videos of the strikes provided evidence of direct American participation.

The conditional nature of the ceasefire leaves the region in a precarious state. Both Israel and Iran emphasized their readiness to resume military operations, suggesting the current halt may be temporary. The exchange demonstrates how rapidly tensions between the two adversaries can escalate into direct military confrontation.

The situation also threatens ongoing diplomatic efforts. Iranian hardliners have been pressuring negotiators to abandon peace talks with the United States, and if Tehran concludes the White House covertly endorsed the Israeli attack, consequences for stalled negotiations could follow.

Beyond Israel and Iran, regional tensions intensified as Yemen's Houthi rebels launched missiles at Israel and declared a complete ban on Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea, raising concerns about disruption to critical trade routes. Oil markets reacted to the escalation, with Brent crude jumping 3.50 dollars per barrel.
