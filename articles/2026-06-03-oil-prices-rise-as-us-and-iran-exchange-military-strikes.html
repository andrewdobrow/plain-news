Israel and Iran halt strikes after Trump claims progress on nuclear talks

Tensions in the Middle East eased on Monday after Israel and Iran agreed to stop attacking each other following an appeal from Donald Trump to "immediately stop shooting." The pause came after an intense 15-hour exchange of strikes between the two nations, marking the most direct confrontation since an April ceasefire.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu acknowledged the halt in his country's military operations in a televised speech, though he made clear that restraint was conditional. "At present, the fire on this front has been halted, because after the terrorist regime in Tehran was struck, it stopped attacking us," Netanyahu said. "If that terrorist regime makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force."

The escalation began when Israel launched strikes on Iranian targets, including a petrochemical complex and defense systems across several areas. Iran responded with ballistic missile attacks on Sunday, with explosions reported in Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz and Tehran. Iranian military officials said they had targeted two Israeli military bases. The exchanges sent shock waves through financial markets, with oil prices rising 5 percent.

However, analysts warn that any ceasefire remains extremely fragile. Multiple flashpoints exist that could trigger fresh waves of strikes and missile barrages at any moment. Both Israel and Iran have reserved the right to resume military action if provoked.

A key complication involves Israel's ongoing operations in Lebanon against Hezbollah, the Iran-backed militant group. Iranian officials have attempted to link any lasting ceasefire to an Israeli halt in Lebanon, but Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz rejected this approach. "Any Iranian attempt to link Lebanon and Iran and attack Israel will be met with great force," Katz said.

Netanyahu faces domestic political pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities, as elections approach. Trump has pushed Israel to halt the Lebanon offensive to create space for broader negotiations with Iran, reportedly delivering strong criticism to Netanyahu in recent phone calls.

Trump claimed Monday that final peace negotiations were proceeding and that both Israel and Iran wanted "an immediate CEASEFIRE." He stated that a U.S. blockade on Iranian ports would remain until a final agreement was reached.

Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf took a more defiant stance, warning that "so long as you lack a genuine willingness to build trust, Iran's response will remain the same." A foreign ministry spokesperson said Tehran was exchanging messages with Washington amid "extreme suspicion," and accused Israel of sabotaging diplomacy through its Lebanon operations.

The Houthis, Yemen-based rebels aligned with Iran, also intensified threats, pledging to block Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea and warning they had fired missiles at Israel. The temporary halt leaves the region in a precarious state, with military forces on both sides positioned to resume operations.
