Israel and Iran halt attacks after Trump intervention

Israel and Iran have agreed to pause military strikes following an appeal from President Trump to "immediately stop shooting." Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu confirmed the halt in a televised speech, stating that "at present, the fire on this front has been halted" after Iran was struck and stopped attacking. However, Netanyahu vowed to respond "with force" if Iran attacks again.

The temporary ceasefire comes after a significant escalation in which Iran launched approximately 30 ballistic missiles at Israel, prompting Israeli retaliatory air strikes on Iranian targets including defense systems and a petrochemical complex. This marked the most direct confrontation between the two nations since an April ceasefire.

Both countries have emphasized their readiness to resume hostilities if necessary. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Tehran would not tolerate what it called "repeated violations," warning that "Iran's response will remain the same" without genuine efforts to build trust. Israel's defense minister stated that Israel would continue operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon and strike Beirut if the militant group attacked Israel.

Analysts describe the new ceasefire as extremely fragile, with multiple potential flashpoints for renewed escalation. The agreement does not address underlying disputes between the nations, and significant complications remain unresolved.

A key tension point involves Lebanon, where Israel has been conducting operations against Hezbollah, an Iranian ally. Israel has rejected Iranian attempts to link any permanent ceasefire to halting its Lebanon offensive. Netanyahu faces domestic pressure and an election later this year to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities. Reports indicate Hezbollah continued firing rockets into northern Israel even as the ceasefire was announced.

The escalation has had broader consequences. Oil prices surged five percent during the violence, raising concerns about global fuel costs and economic impacts. Stock markets rose when both sides announced the halt.

Trump has pressured Israel to stop attacks in Lebanon to create space for diplomatic negotiations. In recent communications with Netanyahu, Trump has been explicit about his demands for de-escalation. The president posted on social media that both countries wanted an "immediate CEASEFIRE" and that "final negotiations on 'Peace' are proceeding."

Iran has signaled skepticism about American intentions, with a foreign ministry spokesperson saying Tehran was exchanging messages with Washington in an atmosphere of "extreme suspicion." Iran also accused Israel of sabotaging diplomacy through its Lebanon operations.

The Houthis, Yemen-based militia allies of Iran, have pledged to continue targeting Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea, suggesting the broader regional conflict remains volatile. Their control of strategic shipping routes adds economic dimensions to the security concerns.

The situation continues to require active diplomatic management to prevent renewed hostilities between Israel and Iran.
