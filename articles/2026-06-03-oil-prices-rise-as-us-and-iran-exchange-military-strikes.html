Israel and Iran halt strikes after Trump calls Netanyahu

Fears of widening regional conflict in the Middle East eased Monday as Israel and Iran announced they had stopped attacking each other following an appeal from President Trump to "immediately stop shooting."

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu confirmed the pause in a televised speech, though he made clear Israel remained ready for confrontation. "At present, the fire on this front has been halted, because after the terrorist regime in Tehran was struck, it stopped attacking us," Netanyahu said. "If that terrorist regime makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force."

The halt came after the most direct confrontation between the two countries since an April ceasefire. Iran fired ballistic missiles at Israel, and Israeli warplanes responded with retaliatory strikes on Iranian targets, including a petrochemical complex. The Israeli military also struck Iranian defense systems across several areas. The Iranian military said it had "delivered a painful response" to Israel for strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs, a Hezbollah stronghold.

Both countries issued similar warnings about resuming attacks if the other side breached the arrangement. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf posted on social media that Tehran would not tolerate what it called "repeated violation," stating "So long as you lack a genuine willingness to build trust, Iran's response will remain the same."

Analysts describe the ceasefire as extremely fragile, with multiple flashpoints that could trigger fresh exchanges at any moment. Israeli officials have rejected Iranian attempts to link any permanent ceasefire to Israel halting its military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon, which Tehran backs. Israel's defense minister said the country would continue striking Hezbollah and would target Beirut if the militant group attacked Israel.

Trump claimed progress toward nuclear negotiations with Iran and said a US blockade of Iranian ports would remain until a final deal was reached. In social media posts, he stated both countries wanted "an immediate CEASEFIRE" and that "Final negotiations on 'Peace' are proceeding."

However, the violence has complicated Trump's diplomatic efforts. Reports indicate he pressured Netanyahu to halt Lebanon operations to create space for negotiations, though the Israeli prime minister faces domestic pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities ahead of elections later this year.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said Tehran was exchanging messages with Washington in an atmosphere of "extreme suspicion," accusing Israel of sabotaging diplomacy through its Lebanon operations.

The violence also shook financial markets, sending oil prices up five percent. The situation remains unstable, with reports of continued rocket launches from Hezbollah into northern Israel and Israeli strikes near southern Lebanon even after the announced ceasefire.
