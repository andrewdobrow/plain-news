Israel and Iran Halt Strikes After Trump Reports Progress on Nuclear Talks

Israel and Iran agreed to pause their military operations following a 15-hour exchange of fire that marked their most direct confrontation since an April ceasefire. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced the halt in a televised speech, saying "the fire on this front has been halted" after Iran was struck and stopped attacking. However, Netanyahu warned that Israel would "respond with force" if Iran attacked again.

President Trump claimed credit for the pause, appealing to both sides to "immediately stop shooting." He posted on social media that Israel and Iran both wanted "an immediate CEASEFIRE" and that "final negotiations on 'Peace' are proceeding, subject to ignorance or stupidity getting in its way." Trump also stated he would maintain a U.S. blockade of Iranian ports until a final deal is reached.

Despite the temporary truce, analysts describe the ceasefire as extremely fragile, with multiple flashpoints that could trigger fresh exchanges at any moment. Both governments emphasized their pause is tactical rather than a fundamental shift, with each warning it remains ready to resume military operations.

The latest escalation began after Israeli strikes on the southern suburbs of Beirut, a Hezbollah stronghold. Iran responded with a barrage of ballistic missiles targeting Israeli military bases. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had "delivered a painful response" to Israel for its attacks on Lebanon and previous strikes on Iranian targets, including a petrochemical complex. Iranian state television reported explosions in Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz, and Tehran.

Israel's defense minister said the country would continue operating against Hezbollah in Lebanon despite the pause. "Any Iranian attempt to link Lebanon and Iran and attack Israel will be met with great force," Defense Minister Israel Katz said. Iran rejected Israeli efforts to separate the Lebanon conflict from broader negotiations, with Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker, warning that "Iran's response will remain the same" without genuine willingness to build trust.

Trump has pressured Netanyahu to halt Lebanon operations to create space for diplomatic negotiations. According to reports, Trump used harsh language in a recent call with the Israeli prime minister, warning he would be "on your own very soon" if he did not exercise caution. Netanyahu, however, faces domestic pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities ahead of upcoming elections.

The violence sent shockwaves through global markets, with oil prices rising 5 percent before stocks recovered when both sides agreed to the pause. The escalation has complicated Trump's broader push to end the wider regional war with Iran, which began in February with strikes that killed Iran's then supreme leader.

Iran's foreign ministry indicated it was exchanging messages with Washington in an atmosphere of "extreme suspicion," suggesting diplomatic channels remain open despite the military exchanges.
