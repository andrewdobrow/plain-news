Oil Prices Fall After Israel and Iran Agree to Halt Strikes

Oil prices declined after Israel and Iran announced they had stopped launching attacks against each other, easing concerns about a wider regional conflict that had pushed crude higher in recent days. The agreement temporarily reduces tensions in the Persian Gulf, a vital shipping corridor for global oil supplies.

The halt in military exchanges brought immediate relief to energy markets, which had been trading on heightened uncertainty. Crude oil futures fell as traders reassessed the risk of supply disruptions in the region. The Persian Gulf accounts for a substantial portion of global oil production and transport, making any conflict there a significant concern for energy markets worldwide.

The announcement came after a period of escalating military strikes between the two nations. Iran launched ballistic missile attacks on Israel, and Israeli warplanes responded with retaliatory strikes on Iran, marking the most direct confrontation since an April ceasefire. The escalation had prompted oil prices to spike as market participants factored in the possibility of disrupted shipping lanes or damaged energy infrastructure. The conflict also involved Yemen-based Houthi rebels aligned with Iran, who fired at Israel and warned they would target Israeli-affiliated ships in the Red Sea.

Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel's prime minister, acknowledged the halt in fighting in a televised speech but vowed to respond "with force" to future attacks. "At present, the fire on this front has been halted, because after the terrorist regime in Tehran was struck, it stopped attacking us," he said. "If that terrorist regime makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force."

Energy analysts noted that while the halt in strikes reduces immediate supply concerns, the underlying tensions remain unresolved. The agreement represents a pause rather than a comprehensive resolution to the longstanding disputes between the two nations. According to analysts, the ceasefire is fragile, with multiple flashpoints that could trigger fresh exchanges of strikes at any moment.

Israel has rejected Iranian efforts to link any definitive ceasefire to Israel stopping its military operations in Lebanon against Hezbollah, which has close ties with Tehran. Israel's defence minister stated that Israel would continue operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon and strike Beirut if the militant group attacked Israel.

Iran remained defiant, with Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker, saying Tehran would not tolerate what it called "repeated violation." He stated that Iran's response would remain unchanged without a genuine willingness to build trust.

The impact of the reduced tensions extended beyond energy markets. Stock markets showed gains as investors grew more confident about regional stability. The easing of oil prices offers potential relief for consumers and businesses dealing with energy costs, though analysts caution that the geopolitical landscape in the region remains fragile and could shift quickly if hostilities resume.
