Oil Prices Jump as Iran and Israel Exchange Strikes

Oil prices surged after Israel launched airstrikes on Iran in response to Iranian missile attacks, marking the first direct military exchange between the two countries since a ceasefire was established on April 8. The escalation raised immediate concerns about potential disruptions to energy supplies in a region that supplies much of the world's oil.

Brent crude jumped $3.50 to $96.59 a barrel following the strikes. The price increase reflected market anxiety over threats to oil infrastructure and shipping routes in the Middle East. Yemen's Houthi rebels, aligned with Iran, also fired missiles at Israel and declared a complete ban on Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea, further threatening critical trade routes.

The Iranian military said it had targeted two military bases in Israel as part of Operation Nasr, or "Victory." Israel's attacks included strikes on an Iranian petrochemical complex and Iranian defense systems. Explosions were reported in Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz, and Tehran, though Iranian officials offered no details about damage sustained.

The conflict escalated despite apparent pressure from Donald Trump, who told Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu that Israel did not need to respond and that he called "all the shots," not Netanyahu. A senior U.S. official said Trump had urged Netanyahu not to retaliate immediately after the Iranian attack, believing he had convinced the Israeli leader to wait. However, Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei suggested that no one believed Israel would act without U.S. approval and coordination.

The exchange threatened ongoing diplomatic efforts. Stalled peace talks between Iran and the United States faced potential consequences if Tehran concluded the White House had covertly endorsed the Israeli attack. Iranian hardliners in parliament had already been pressuring negotiators to abandon the talks, and some claimed specific deal aspects needed tightening.

Stock markets initially reacted sharply to the developments. Asian markets, heavily dependent on oil imports, fell sharply in early trading. However, markets recovered somewhat after Iran announced an end to its military operations against Israel, allowing oil prices to pare some of their earlier gains.

The incident underscored how quickly geopolitical events in major oil-producing regions can affect global energy and financial markets. The escalation between Iran and Israel has reinforced concerns among energy-importing nations about their vulnerability to regional conflicts. Many countries are increasingly focused on securing domestic energy sources to reduce exposure to volatile international markets and geopolitical risks.

Analysts expect markets will remain sensitive to any further developments in the Middle East, particularly regarding the stability of oil production and shipping through critical waterways.
