Iran and Israel Exchange Strikes as Two-Month Truce Collapses

Iran launched missiles at northern Israel on Monday, prompting Israeli airstrikes on Iranian military targets and a petrochemical complex. The exchange marked the first direct fire between the two nations since a ceasefire took effect in April, raising concerns about a return to wider regional conflict in the Middle East.

The Iranian military, through the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, said it targeted two Israeli military bases as part of Operation Nasr, or "Victory." The strikes came in response to earlier Israeli airstrikes on the Beirut area in Lebanon. Israeli forces worked to intercept the incoming Iranian missiles, and explosions were heard across Jerusalem and other areas, with some damage reported in West Bank settlements but no injuries.

Israel responded with strikes aimed at dismantling Iranian defense systems across multiple areas, including Isfahan, Karaj, Tabriz, and Tehran. The Israeli military used air-launched ballistic missiles in its attack. Officials provided no details on damage from the Iranian strikes.

President Trump called on both nations to cease fire immediately, posting on his Truth Social platform: "Israel and Iran must immediately stop 'shooting'. President DONALD J. TRUMP." According to a senior US official, Trump had urged Netanyahu not to retaliate after the Iranian attack, believing he had convinced the Israeli leader to wait. However, Israel proceeded with strikes despite this appeal.

Iranian officials claimed that Israel would not have launched attacks without US approval and coordination, rejecting suggestions that Netanyahu had defied Trump's instructions. An international relations specialist in Tehran pointed to videos he said showed the US involvement in the Israeli strikes, noting that Israel lacks the capability to launch certain long-range cruise missiles from the eastern Mediterranean.

The White House did not respond to questions about whether the Israeli strikes were coordinated with the US.

The resumption of hostilities also drew involvement from Yemen's Houthi rebels, who announced a missile attack on Israel on Monday, their first since early April. The group declared a complete ban on Israeli maritime navigation in the Red Sea, threatening disruption to a critical trade route.

The breakdown of the two-month truce creates complications for diplomatic efforts. Trump has claimed peace talks could produce a deal within days, but the escalation may undermine negotiations. Iranian hardliners in parliament have pressured negotiators to abandon talks, while others have questioned specific aspects of potential agreements.

The market reaction reflected investor concerns about regional stability. Brent crude oil jumped 3.50 dollars to 96.59 dollars per barrel, while Asian stock markets, heavily dependent on oil imports, fell sharply in early trading.
