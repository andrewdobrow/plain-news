Stock futures drop and Asian markets slide as U.S. launches strikes against Iran

Stock futures declined and Asian markets fell after the United States launched strikes against Iran, escalating tensions in the Middle East following the downing of a U.S. Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump confirmed that Iran shot down the military aircraft and said the U.S. "must" respond to the attack. The military action prompted investors to pull back from risk assets, with stock futures slipping in early trading and Asian equities registering losses across the region.

The helicopter's crew was rescued by a drone before the U.S. launched what officials described as "self-defense strikes" against Iran. Iran responded with retaliatory strikes on Wednesday morning, targeting Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan. This exchange of fire marks a sharp escalation in the conflict and has imperiled a ceasefire that the two countries announced in April.

The geopolitical tension coincided with weakness in technology stocks on Wall Street. The Nasdaq fell 3 percent in the previous session as chip stocks sold off and hopes for an Iran deal faded. Technology stocks led the decline, with the broader S&P 500 also closing lower as investors rotated out of high-growth sectors. The tech selling followed a period of strong gains in semiconductor and artificial intelligence-related stocks.

Oil prices gained on concerns about potential supply disruptions. The military confrontation has intensified focus on the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies. The strait's closure would severely impact oil flows to major markets including China and Pakistan.

Investors are watching closely for further developments in the U.S.-Iran confrontation and its potential impact on global supply chains and energy markets. The military strikes add to uncertainty that has already weighed on risk appetite, with geopolitical risk now combining with concerns about elevated technology stock valuations to pressure equities.

The recent conflict has driven inflation concerns worldwide, as higher costs for oil, gas and other commodities including fertilizer could push food prices sharply higher in the coming months. These inflation worries have shifted market expectations away from interest rate cuts and toward predictions of rate increases from central banks.

The military confrontation between the two countries has ended hopes for a diplomatic resolution that had briefly supported market sentiment earlier this week. This latest escalation represents a significant shift from just days earlier, when there had been optimism about negotiations to end the conflict between the U.S. and Iran. The uncertainty surrounding both the military situation and its economic consequences has left investors cautious about risk assets heading forward.
