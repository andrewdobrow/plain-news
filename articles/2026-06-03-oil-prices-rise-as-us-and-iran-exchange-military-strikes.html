Israel and Iran Halt Strikes After Trump Claims Progress on Nuclear Talks

Israel and Iran have suspended military strikes following pressure from President Trump, with both nations agreeing to pause hostilities but warning they remain prepared to resume at any moment.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced the halt in a televised speech, stating that "the fire on this front has been halted" after Iran stopped attacking following Israeli strikes. However, he vowed to respond with force to any future Iranian attacks. "If that terrorist regime makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force," Netanyahu said.

The pause comes after a 15-hour exchange of strikes that revealed the difficult position Israel faces. Iran demonstrated both the capability and willingness to strike Israeli territory directly with ballistic missiles in response to Israeli military actions. This confrontation marked the most direct exchange between the two countries since an April ceasefire.

Trump urged both sides to "immediately stop shooting" and cited progress in diplomatic negotiations toward a nuclear agreement as justification for the pause. According to reports, Trump warned Netanyahu that Israel would be "on your own very soon" if it continued military operations, particularly against Hezbollah in Lebanon.

Despite the ceasefire, tensions remain extremely fragile. Israel has rejected Iranian efforts to link any definitive agreement to an Israeli halt of its offensive operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon. Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz stated that Israel would continue operations against Hezbollah and strike Beirut if the militant group attacked Israel.

Iran's parliamentary speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, remained defiant, saying that "so long as you lack a genuine willingness to build trust, Iran's response will remain the same." He accused Israel of repeated violations and suggested that diplomatic efforts had been sabotaged by Israeli military actions.

The exchange of strikes shook financial markets, sending oil prices up 5 percent and raising concerns about further fuel price increases globally. Stocks rose when both sides appeared to agree to the halt.

Multiple flashpoints could trigger renewed violence at any moment. Yemen-based Houthi rebels, aligned with Iran, have pledged to target Israeli maritime traffic in the Red Sea and fired missiles at Israel during recent days. These actions potentially threaten a critical shipping route for Middle Eastern oil.

Israeli officials acknowledge that the recent military exchanges have shifted their strategic calculations. Any significant response to Iranian proxy forces now risks direct Iranian missile strikes, potentially drawing Israel into a wider regional conflict. Netanyahu faces domestic pressure to continue degrading Hezbollah's capabilities ahead of elections later this year, complicating Trump's diplomatic efforts.

Both nations maintain they will resume military action if they determine threats warrant response, leaving the region in a precarious standoff dependent on whether diplomatic negotiations produce meaningful progress.
