Progressive Randy Villegas advances to face Rep. David Valadao in California House race

Randy Villegas, a progressive educator and community college professor, has advanced to the general election in California's 22nd Congressional District, where he will face off against incumbent Republican Rep. David Valadao. Decision Desk HQ called the race after Villegas emerged as the top Democrat in the primary.

Villegas defeated California State Assemblymember Jasmeet Bains in a competitive Democratic primary contest. Neither candidate secured the 60 percent threshold required to obtain the California Democratic Party endorsement outright during the party's nomination process.

The 22nd District is considered a battleground seat, with both parties viewing it as competitive heading into the November general election. The district is 75 percent Latino with a median voter age of just 30 years old.

The two Democrats engaged in a fierce primary battle that reflected broader ideological divisions within the party. Villegas, a 31-year-old second-generation Mexican immigrant and community organizer with a doctoral thesis on voter turnout among young Latinos, built his campaign around criticisms of centrist politics. He argued that Democrats needed to expand their electorate rather than run "Republican-lite campaigns" that fail to inspire turnout.

"We need to work to expand the electorate," Villegas said during the campaign. "For too long, Democrats have run Republican-lite campaigns. We can't just offer that we're not Trump. We have to stand for something."

Villegas refused corporate campaign donations, a stance he believed was particularly important in a region where politicians from both parties have traditionally sided with major donors on issues like water policy, pesticide regulation and air pollution that affect public health.

Despite these unconventional positions, Villegas surprised political professionals by outperforming Bains in fundraising and winning endorsements from party chairs in all four counties within the district. Political observers credited him with running an energetic campaign that resonated with voters dealing with rising costs of living, a sluggish job market and cuts to government-provided healthcare.

Bains, a physician who continued working weekend shifts at a community clinic while serving in the state legislature, had initially been viewed favorably by Democratic power brokers. As the daughter of Sikh refugees and the first Asian American to serve in the California assembly, she brought a compelling personal narrative. The Service Employees International Union endorsed her candidacy, citing her fight for healthcare and services for seniors.

However, Bains was less visible on the campaign trail during the primary, maintaining focus on talking points about championing healthcare and serving as an independent voice.

The race highlighted ongoing tensions between the Democratic Party's moderate and progressive wings, with candidates disagreeing fundamentally about strategy, funding sources and how to energize the district's young, Latino-majority electorate.
