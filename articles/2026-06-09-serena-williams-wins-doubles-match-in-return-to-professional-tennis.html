Serena Williams wins doubles match in return to professional tennis

Serena Williams made her competitive comeback after nearly four years away, winning her opening doubles match at Queen's Club alongside Canadian teenager Victoria Mboko. The match marked Williams' first competitive outing since stepping away from professional tennis in 2022.

The 44-year-old former champion emphasized that her return is motivated by enjoyment rather than competitive achievement. "I don't need to win. I've won more than most people have in their whole lives, so for me it's not important," Williams said. She stressed that the experience is about sharing her love of tennis with her children, particularly her eight-year-old daughter Olympia and two-year-old daughter Adira.

Williams selected Mboko, currently ranked ninth in singles, as her doubles partner after being impressed by the teenager's performance at the Canadian Open. "She reminded me a lot of myself," Williams explained, noting that she appreciated Mboko's ability to maintain momentum after victories. For Mboko, the partnership holds special significance. The 19-year-old was only 10 years old when Williams won her final Grand Slam title. "Growing up, Serena has always been my idol," Mboko said. "Seeing someone on TV versus actually talking to them in person, of course, it's very different. I think she's really nice and very personable."

When asked about returning to singles competition, Williams remained noncommittal. "I can't say yeah, I can't say no. Right now, no," she responded. She noted that if she did pursue singles play, she would need additional training. Williams has committed to playing doubles in Berlin following her Queen's appearance, though her plans beyond that remain uncertain.

The comeback has generated widespread interest across professional tennis. Emma Raducanu, the British number one who faced Williams in her final competitive match in 2022, praised her return. "It's really great to see her back. I think it's an amazing inspiration," Raducanu said, describing Williams as "the greatest female tennis player of all time."

Williams emphasized that she has nothing to prove at this stage of her career. "I don't have anything to lose and everything here is just a gain," she said. "This whole journey is like I'm putting no pressure on myself." She also highlighted the timing of her return, noting that summer provided the ideal opportunity for her children to experience her competing at the professional level while they were not in school.

The victory against third-seeded opponents Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe demonstrated that Williams' competitive abilities remain sharp despite her extended absence. Her return adds significant star power to Queen's Club and raises questions about the potential scope of her comeback as the grass-court season progresses.
