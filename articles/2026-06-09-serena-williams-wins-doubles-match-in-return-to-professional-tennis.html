Serena Williams wins first match in comeback after four-year absence

Serena Williams returned to professional tennis after 1,375 days away, securing a doubles victory at Queen's Club in west London. The 44-year-old and partner Victoria Mboko defeated third seeds Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe 7-6 (2), 6-2 to advance to the quarter-finals.

Williams' comeback marks her first competitive appearance since the US Open in 2022. She had previously described her departure from the sport as "evolving away" from tennis rather than a formal retirement, though she remained on the retired players' list until last year.

The American announced her return through a Nike video with the tagline "Guess everybody heard the news." In a statement, Williams explained her choice of venue, saying: "Queen's Club feels like the perfect place to begin this next chapter. Grass has given me some of the most meaningful moments of my career, and I'm excited to be back competing on one of the sport's most iconic stages."

Williams' return to the drug-testing pool last December signaled her intentions to compete again. She had trained in Florida in preparation for her comeback, with rising player Alycia Parks posting videos of their training sessions in March.

Mboko, a Canadian ranked number 9 in singles, expressed enthusiasm about partnering with Williams. "I'm very happy," Mboko said. "Me and Serena have stayed in touch, which is really, really nice, because I really look up to her. I think for me I want to let the moment be for her. I feel like if she's ready to come back on her own terms, then I feel like it's up to her to announce that."

The decision to begin with doubles allows Williams to ease back into competitive play while managing court time with her partner. Williams has the option to progress to singles in future events, though a potential Wimbledon appearance would require rapid preparation given the short grass-court season. Wimbledon begins three weeks after Queen's, leaving limited time for singles competition before the championships.

Williams is widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes of all time. Her 23 Grand Slam singles titles represent a women's open era record, and she holds 14 Grand Slam doubles titles alongside her sister Venus, who remains active on the tour at 45 years old. Williams is the only player to win the career Golden Slam in both singles and doubles.

The crowd at Queen's Club gave Williams an enthusiastic reception as she warmed up on the grass. Her victory demonstrates that she retains competitive ability despite the extended absence. Her presence adds a significant storyline to the current tennis season, as the women's tour has evolved during her time away, with new champions emerging and younger players establishing themselves.
