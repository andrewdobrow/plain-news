Serena Williams wins doubles match in return to tennis after nearly four years

Serena Williams made a winning professional tennis comeback at age 44, partnering with Canadian teenager Victoria Mboko to defeat third seeds Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe 7-6 (7-2), 6-2 in the first round of doubles at Queen's Club.

The victory marked Williams' return to competition after 1,375 days away from the sport. She had stepped back from professional tennis in 2022, having won 23 Grand Slam singles titles throughout her career.

The first set proved competitive, with the unseeded pair needing a tiebreak to secure the opening set. They controlled the tiebreak decisively before dominating the second set to claim the match in straight sets. The win came against seeded opponents, demonstrating that Williams retains the competitive skills that defined her legendary career.

Williams chose to return through doubles rather than singles, allowing her to ease back into competitive tennis with a partner. When assessing her performance after the match, she gave herself a modest grade, acknowledging room for improvement as she rebuilds her competitive fitness.

In discussing her comeback motivation, Williams emphasized that winning is no longer her primary concern. "I don't need to win. I've won more than most people have in their whole lives, so for me it's not important," she said. She explained that the return is largely about her children experiencing her as a competitor. She gave birth to a daughter in 2023 and has an eight-year-old daughter, Olympia.

Williams selected Mboko, the world number 9 in singles, as her partner after being impressed by the teenager's attitude and drive. "I remember seeing her play in Montreal. I was impressed with her attitude, and what I liked the most about her was that the next time she played she still kept winning," Williams said. She noted that Mboko "reminded me a lot of myself."

For Mboko, the partnership holds special significance. Growing up, Williams had been her idol. "It was really hard not to like her because she won every tournament she played," Mboko said. She added that meeting Williams in person revealed her to be "really nice and very personable, very relatable. And I think she's really funny."

Williams has committed to playing doubles in Berlin following Queen's but said her future in singles remains uncertain. When asked about returning to singles competition, she stated: "I can't say yeah, I can't say no. Right now, no." She suggested she would need additional training to compete in singles and emphasized that her current focus is on enjoying the experience.

The Queen's Club tournament serves as a grass-court warmup event before Wimbledon. Williams and Mboko will compete in the second round of the doubles draw.
