Serena Williams wins doubles match on return to tennis after three years

Serena Williams returned to competitive tennis after 1,375 days away from the sport, securing a victory in doubles at Queen's Club in London. The 44-year-old partnered with Canadian teenager Victoria Mboko to defeat third seeds Nicole Melichar-Martinez and Erin Routliffe in the opening round.

The unseeded pair won in straight sets, taking the first set in a tiebreak 7-6 (7-2) before dominating the second set 6-2. Williams' comeback marks her first competitive match since stepping away from professional tennis in 2022.

Williams has described her return as being driven by personal rather than competitive motivations. She emphasized that winning is not important to her, having already achieved 23 Grand Slam singles titles during her career. "I don't need to win. I've won more than most people have in their whole lives, so for me it's not important," she said. "This whole journey is like I'm putting no pressure on myself."

A key motivation for her comeback involves her children. Williams gave birth to daughters in 2017 and 2023, and she expressed that the opportunity for them to see her play was meaningful. "It's really about my kids getting to see me play," she explained.

Williams selected Mboko as her partner after being impressed by the teenager's attitude and drive during previous tournaments. Mboko, the world number nine in singles, was only 10 years old when Williams won her last Grand Slam title. The Canadian expressed her excitement about the partnership, saying she has always admired Williams and found her "really nice and very relatable."

When asked about her performance during the match, Williams was self-critical, grading herself as a "C-minus." Despite her modest assessment, she demonstrated the skill and competitive instinct needed to secure the win.

Williams' future in tennis remains uncertain beyond her current commitments. She has committed to playing doubles in Berlin following Queen's, but she has not confirmed plans to return to singles competition. When asked about potentially playing singles at Wimbledon, she said: "I can't say yeah, I can't say no. Right now, no." She suggested she would need more training if she were to pursue singles play.

The grass court season at Queen's provides a traditional warm-up for Wimbledon, and Williams' decision to return at this prestigious tournament has drawn significant attention from fans and fellow players. Her comeback represents a notable moment in professional tennis, with players including Emma Raducanu welcoming her return to the sport.

Williams and Mboko will face opponents in the second round of the doubles tournament.
