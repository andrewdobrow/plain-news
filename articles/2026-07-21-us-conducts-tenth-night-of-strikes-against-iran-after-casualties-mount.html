US conducts tenth night of strikes against Iran after casualties mount

The United States has launched its tenth consecutive round of airstrikes against Iranian targets as the military campaign intensifies following the deaths of three American service members and injuries to nearly 100 others in recent weeks.

President Trump has vowed retaliation for the deaths of American soldiers as US forces continue their aerial campaign. In response, Iran's Revolutionary Guards have struck American military installations and vessels in the region, including targets in Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Qatar. Iran claims to have hit two oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, though the US military disputes these reports.

The escalating conflict centers on control of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway through which approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and gas supply passed before the fighting resumed. The current round of violence follows the collapse of a memorandum of understanding signed by both countries in June that was intended to keep the strait open for shipping and allow room for negotiations.

Both countries have interpreted the shipping agreement differently, with Iran attacking vessels that followed US-designated transit routes. Shipping traffic in the waterway has dropped significantly as violence has intensified, though most ships that continue to transit use the Iranian route.

US strikes have targeted Iranian military infrastructure, including bridges in the Hormozgan province, a tower at Chabahar port, and electrical infrastructure. Iranian state media reported that at least 38 people were killed and more than 400 wounded by Friday morning. Iran's energy ministry has urged citizens to reduce electricity and air conditioning use after power plants sustained damage from US strikes.

The IRGC has threatened severe consequences for countries hosting US military bases if American attacks on civilian infrastructure continue. According to Iranian state media, the Revolutionary Guards targeted US combat aircraft at Sheikh Isa air base in Bahrain, an intelligence center in Bahrain, a naval fuel pier in Kuwait, and a communications center in Kuwait.

Iran has closed the Strait of Hormuz while the United States has reimposed a blockade on Iranian ports and ships. The US military said it boarded one ship and redirected three others attempting to break the blockade since it began on Tuesday.

The conflict has had economic ripple effects across the region and globally. Average US diesel prices have climbed above five dollars per gallon, with gasoline reaching nearly four dollars. These prices represent significant increases from a year ago, reflecting the broader economic impact of the renewed hostilities.

Even as military operations continue, diplomats report that de-escalation talks are ongoing. However, with neither side showing signs of backing down, questions remain about when or how this cycle of escalating military exchanges might be reversed.
