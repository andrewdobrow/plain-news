House to vote on $69.5 billion immigration enforcement bill as Congress faces FISA deadline

Republicans are preparing to advance two major legislative priorities this week as the House schedules a vote on a $69.5 billion budget reconciliation package for immigration enforcement and Congress faces a deadline to extend warrantless surveillance powers under the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act.

The budget reconciliation bill would provide funding for Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Border Patrol operations. Republicans are using the budget reconciliation process, which allows legislation to pass with a simple majority in the Senate and avoid a filibuster. The House is expected to take up the measure in the coming days.

Congress is also working to extend government surveillance powers under FISA before current authorization expires. The law allows intelligence agencies to conduct warrantless surveillance of foreign targets, though it has drawn criticism from privacy advocates and some lawmakers who argue it can sweep up communications of American citizens. Republicans have made the extension a priority, though the timeline for action remains tight.

The dual agenda comes as Republicans control both chambers of Congress and are working to advance legislative items ahead of the August recess. With only weeks remaining before the month-long break, the party faces pressure to deliver on key policy goals before attention shifts to midterm election campaigns.

The reconciliation bill represents one of potentially three party-line packages Republicans hope to pass this year. Party leaders are already discussing a third reconciliation measure that would need to clear both chambers before the November midterm elections. The tight legislative calendar and narrow margins in both the House and Senate mean Republicans will need to maintain near-unanimous support within their caucus to advance their agenda.
