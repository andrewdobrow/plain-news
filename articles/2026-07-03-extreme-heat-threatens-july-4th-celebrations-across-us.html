Extreme heat threatens July 4th celebrations across US

A severe heat wave is disrupting July 4th celebrations across the United States as the country marks its 250th birthday. The extreme temperatures have forced changes to outdoor events and raised public safety concerns in multiple cities.

Washington DC has altered its July Fourth weekend event plans in response to the oppressive conditions. The heat dome has trapped approximately 260 million people under scorching temperatures that are expected to last through the holiday. Major metropolitan areas including New York City, Philadelphia, Boston, and Washington DC are experiencing the most intense effects.

The Mid-Atlantic region has reached peak heat levels, with numerous temperature records falling across the eastern United States. Maryland faces extreme heat through July 4th, with forecasters warning of an increased risk of storms as the weather system continues. The combination of record-breaking temperatures and high humidity has created dangerous conditions for outdoor gatherings traditionally held during the Independence Day holiday.

Authorities are urging residents to take precautions during outdoor activities and to monitor vulnerable populations. The heat wave's timing coincides with one of the busiest travel and celebration periods of the year, when millions of Americans typically gather for parades, barbecues, and fireworks displays. Event organizers across affected regions have been forced to reassess safety protocols and make last-minute adjustments to scheduled activities.

The extreme weather pattern represents a significant disruption to the milestone anniversary celebrations. Public health officials continue to monitor the situation as the heat dome shows little sign of weakening through the holiday weekend.
