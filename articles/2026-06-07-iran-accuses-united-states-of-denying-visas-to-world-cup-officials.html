Iran accuses United States of denying visas to World Cup officials

Iran's soccer federation has accused the United States of "vindictive behavior" after visas were refused for key members of its World Cup delegation, creating tension just days before the tournament begins.

The dispute centers on administrative and support staff who were denied entry despite players and coaching staff receiving approval. The Iranian football federation chief Mehdi Taj was among those reportedly blocked from entering the country.

On June 5, the United States approved visas for Iran's players and some staff members ahead of the competition. However, Iranian state media and officials reported that same day that several support staff were left out. The US State Department has stated that "necessary" visas were issued, but the disagreement over which staff members should have received access has sparked a diplomatic dispute.

Iran's team has been preparing in Turkey for the past three weeks, training and playing closed-door matches while diplomats worked to secure visa approvals. The team departed for Mexico on June 6 to establish its base camp before the tournament.

Despite the visa complications, FIFA has confirmed that Iran will compete as scheduled. FIFA President Gianni Infantino stated that Iran "will be at the World Cup" and that their matches will be played where scheduled according to the draw. He emphasized that he had spoken with the team's players and coaching staff and that everything was in order.

Iran's participation in the tournament had faced uncertainty due to broader geopolitical tensions. The dispute over visas comes amid heightened tensions between the US and Iran following military conflicts in the region. Earlier, former President Donald Trump suggested Iran's team should not travel "for their own life and safety," a statement Iran rejected, saying that "no one can exclude Iran's national team from the World Cup."

The Iranian football federation had previously requested that FIFA relocate Iran's group-stage matches from the United States to Mexico due to these security concerns. Mexico's president indicated willingness to host the games if necessary, but FIFA committed to proceeding with the original schedule.

Iran qualified for the 2026 World Cup after finishing atop their group in Asian confederation qualifying. The team will face New Zealand on June 15 in Los Angeles, followed by matches against Belgium on June 21 in Los Angeles and Egypt on June 26 in Seattle, all in the United States as originally scheduled.

The visa denial for administrative staff represents the latest complication in what has been a diplomatically tense road to the tournament for Iran's participation.
