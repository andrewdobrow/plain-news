US Restricts Foreign Access to Anthropic's Most Advanced AI Models

Anthropic has disabled access to its most powerful artificial intelligence models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, following a US government directive restricting foreign nationals from using the technology. The company announced it will "abruptly disable" the models for all users in compliance with the order, citing national security concerns.

The government issued an export control directive targeting these advanced systems without providing Anthropic with specific details about the security threat. Anthropic expressed frustration with the decision, stating in a blog post: "We disagree that the finding of a narrow potential jailbreak should be cause for recalling a commercial model deployed to hundreds of millions of people."

The restrictions stem from concerns about the models' cybersecurity capabilities. The UK's AI Security Institute warned that Mythos represents a significant step up from previous models in terms of the cyber threat it poses. The system can identify flaws in IT systems and conduct attacks requiring multiple actions without human intervention, tasks that typically take cybersecurity professionals days to complete. During testing, Mythos successfully completed a 32-step simulated cyber attack three out of ten times, marking the first AI model to accomplish this benchmark.

These concerns intensified after an unauthorized access incident. Bloomberg reported that a small group of people gained access to the Mythos model through a third-party contractor environment before the official limited release. The individuals accessed the system using methods employed by cybersecurity researchers. While they reportedly have not conducted cybersecurity tests on the model and are primarily interested in exploring the technology rather than causing harm, the breach raised alarms among government officials.

UK AI Minister Kanishka Narayan warned that UK businesses "should be worried" about Mythos's ability to identify IT system vulnerabilities that hackers could exploit. The unauthorized access demonstrated that safeguards protecting the model could potentially be bypassed, prompting the government action.

The decision significantly impacts Anthropic's international business operations. Companies outside the United States that had integrated these models into their products and services now face service interruption. The move also reflects a broader shift in how governments approach advanced artificial intelligence, applying regulatory frameworks similar to those governing weapons technology and strategic semiconductors.

Industry observers note this represents a new phase in AI regulation, where the most capable systems face government oversight linked to national security. Anthropic, which competes with OpenAI and other major AI developers, had previously made these models available internationally. The restrictions may accelerate development of alternative AI capabilities in other countries as organizations seek systems not subject to US export controls.

The full economic impact on Anthropic and the broader AI industry remains uncertain as companies navigate the intersection of technological advancement, national security requirements, and international commerce.
