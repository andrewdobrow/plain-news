US Government Restricts Foreign Access to Anthropic's Most Advanced AI Models

Anthropic has announced it will disable its most advanced artificial intelligence models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, following a US government directive that bars foreign nationals from accessing the systems. The company received an export control order from the government but was not given specific details about the national security concerns behind the decision.

The restriction marks a significant intervention in the commercial AI sector. The government's action came after concerns emerged about the models' potential to identify software vulnerabilities and enable cyberattacks. The UK's AI Security Institute had previously warned that Mythos represented a "step up" in cyber-threat capability compared to earlier models, noting it could complete complex multi-step attacks that would normally require days of work by human professionals.

News of the ban followed reports of unauthorized access to the Mythos model. A small group of people gained access through a third-party vendor environment where one user worked as a contractor for Anthropic. According to Bloomberg, the group accessed the model using methods employed by cybersecurity researchers, though they had not attempted to run cyberattacks with the system.

Anthropic has expressed frustration with the government's decision. "We disagree that the finding of a narrow potential jailbreak should be cause for recalling a commercial model deployed to hundreds of millions of people," the company stated in a blog post. The company indicated it had received the directive without being given specific details of the security concerns that prompted it.

The move reflects growing US government focus on preventing advanced AI capabilities from reaching foreign adversaries. Officials have increasingly treated cutting-edge AI technology similarly to sensitive military or dual-use technologies subject to export controls. The restrictions align with broader efforts to control access to technology that could have national security implications.

The ban creates uncertainty for international businesses and researchers who had access to or were planning to use these advanced AI tools. Anthropic has not indicated whether foreign access might be restored in the future or what timeline might govern any potential reversal of the restrictions.

The decision could establish a precedent for how the US regulates access to advanced AI systems. Other companies developing similar technologies are likely watching to determine whether this represents a one-time intervention or signals the beginning of broader regulatory frameworks governing AI exports.

Industry observers have noted the irony that Anthropic's own safety research and transparency about the models' capabilities may have contributed to the government's decision to restrict them. The company's willingness to highlight security risks appears to have prompted official action rather than leading to collaborative solutions with regulators.
