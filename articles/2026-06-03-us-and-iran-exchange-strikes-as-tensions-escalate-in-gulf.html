US and Iran exchange strikes across Gulf region

The United States military struck Iranian drone and radar positions after Iran launched attacks targeting American forces in Kuwait and Bahrain, marking the latest escalation in a confrontation that threatens a fragile regional ceasefire.

US Central Command reported intercepting Iranian drones near the Strait of Hormuz, shooting down at least four unmanned aircraft. American forces then conducted strikes against Iranian radar installations and drone launch sites. Tehran responded by firing missiles and drones at US military bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, significantly widening the scope of the conflict.

The Iranian attacks extended beyond military installations to civilian and energy infrastructure across the Gulf. Qatar's defense ministry reported that drones struck energy facilities in the industrial city of Ras Laffan, prompting Doha to shut down its liquefied natural gas production. Oman reported an attack on an oil tanker off Muscat. The UAE, which withdrew its ambassador from Tehran in protest, reported detecting 174 ballistic missiles, 689 drones, and eight cruise missiles. The country estimates the cost of intercepting these weapons at approximately two billion dollars, since each interception costs five times more than launching the weapons.

According to Ali Larijani, secretary of Iran's supreme national security council, Tehran justified its actions as targeting US military bases. However, Arab leaders expressed alarm at the scale and scope of the attacks, which they characterized as disproportionate and reckless.

The strikes have shifted sentiment among Gulf states regarding Iran. Qatar's former prime minister Hamad bin Jassim bin Jaber al-Thani warned that Iran had "lost through this action the Gulf sympathy that was pushing with every possible effort towards de-escalation." Many Gulf nations had invested significant diplomatic effort in maintaining neutral relations with Iran while hosting American military bases.

Eight Gulf states and Iraq issued a joint statement warning that "targeting civilians and countries not engaged in hostilities is reckless and destabilising." The attacks even appeared to suspend tensions between rival Gulf monarchies Saudi Arabia and the UAE, which had previously divided over conflicts in Sudan and Yemen.

Some Arab leaders expressed frustration with US priorities. A Saudi official complained that "the United States abandoned the Gulf states and redirected its air defence to protect Israel," leaving Gulf nations hosting American bases vulnerable to Iranian strikes.

The escalation comes as ongoing tensions in Lebanon persist despite ceasefire efforts. Iranian officials have warned of a wider war if attacks continue. The exchange represents one of the most serious direct military confrontations between US and Iranian forces in recent months, raising serious questions about whether the fragile regional ceasefire can survive further escalation.
