US and Iran exchange strikes in Gulf as ceasefire shows signs of strain

The United States and Iran clashed in the most serious test yet of their month-long ceasefire, with both sides accusing each other of violations as military exchanges erupted over the Persian Gulf on Thursday.

The US military said it intercepted multiple Iranian missiles, drones, and small boats targeting three American destroyers, the USS Truxtun, USS Rafael Peralta, and USS Mason, as they transited the Strait of Hormuz. American forces responded by striking Iranian military facilities, including missile and drone launch sites, command and control locations, and surveillance nodes. The Pentagon stated that no US assets were damaged in the attacks.

Iran's military disputed the American account, claiming the US violated the ceasefire by targeting an Iranian oil tanker and another vessel in the strait. Iranian officials said American airstrikes hit civilian areas along the coasts of Bandar Khamir, Sirik, and the island of Qeshm, which is home to approximately 150,000 people and a water desalination plant. Iran's armed forces responded by attacking US military vessels, with military officials claiming they inflicted significant damage, though the US denied this.

The strikes marked a troubling escalation for a ceasefire that has held for roughly a month. President Donald Trump downplayed the significance of the exchanges, describing the strikes as a "love tap" during remarks to reporters. He insisted the ceasefire remained intact despite the military action, though he acknowledged that a negotiated settlement "might not happen, but it could happen any day." Trump stated he believed Iran wanted a deal more than he did.

The confrontation came amid reports that the US and Iran had been approaching a temporary agreement to halt hostilities, with a one-page memorandum being shared between the two nations through Pakistan. However, senior Iranian officials have rejected recent concessions, with some analysts suggesting Iran may prefer prolonged negotiations closer to the November US midterm elections, when the Trump administration could face greater pressure to reach a settlement.

The exchanges occurred as the US has been enforcing a naval blockade to pressure Iran to reopen the strait. Earlier in the week, Trump sent warships to escort stranded tankers through the waterway in what he called "Project Freedom," during which the US military said it destroyed six Iranian boats along with cruise missiles and drones.

The latest flare-up raised questions about whether the ceasefire can withstand continued military provocations. Regional diplomats believe Iran risks overplaying its hand, as the current moment could offer an opportunity to end the conflict and claim victory, whereas resuming full-scale fighting might prove more costly. Meanwhile, crude oil prices climbed to approximately $101 per barrel following news of the attacks.
