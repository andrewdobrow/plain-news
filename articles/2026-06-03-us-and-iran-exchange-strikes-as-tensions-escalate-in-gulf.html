US and Iran exchange military strikes across Gulf after ceasefire breaks down

The US military struck Iranian drone and radar installations after Tehran launched attacks across the Persian Gulf region, marking another escalation in an already fragile ceasefire between the two nations.

The exchange began when Iran fired drones toward the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping lane through which roughly a fifth of global oil passes. US forces intercepted and destroyed at least four of these drones. In response, the US conducted strikes against Iranian military facilities. Tehran then launched seven ballistic missiles targeting American bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, signaling a willingness to expand military operations beyond Iran's immediate borders.

The escalation came as tensions intensified across multiple fronts in the region. A fresh wave of Iranian strikes hit Gulf states including Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted 15 drones, while strikes in Bahrain damaged an important desalination plant. Two people were killed in Saudi Arabia after a projectile struck a residential area, and one US service member died from injuries sustained in an Iranian attack on troops in Saudi Arabia.

The confrontation represents the latest breakdown in efforts to maintain a ceasefire that began on April 8. Earlier flare-ups over the past two months were quickly contained, but analysts suggest this round of escalation reflects deeper strains within the region's political leadership.

The tensions were further complicated by significant political developments. Iran's clerical assembly announced the appointment of Mojtaba Khamenei, the late supreme leader's son, as his successor. This marked the first time since the 1979 Islamic revolution that Iran's supreme leadership passed from father to son, a development likely to spark internal debate about hereditary rule in a state founded explicitly to reject it.

The strikes also targeted energy infrastructure. Israel conducted airstrikes hitting at least five energy sites around Tehran, creating plumes of black smoke across the capital and nearby Karaj. Iran's oil distribution company reported four employees killed. A spokesman for Iran's Revolutionary Guards warned that oil prices could exceed $200 per barrel if the attacks on energy infrastructure continued. However, the US energy secretary stated that Washington would not target Iran's energy infrastructure and claimed supply disruptions would last only a few weeks.

The ceasefire has held enough to prevent a return to all-out war, but neither side appears close to achieving lasting peace. The situation risks becoming another Middle East stalemate with significant consequences for the global economy and international energy markets.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a focal point of military tension between Washington and Tehran, with any sustained conflict in the area potentially disrupting global trade and energy supplies.
