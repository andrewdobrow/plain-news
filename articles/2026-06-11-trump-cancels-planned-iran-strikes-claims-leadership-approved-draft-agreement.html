Trump cancels planned Iran strikes, claims leadership approved draft agreement

President Trump announced Thursday that he was canceling scheduled military strikes against Iran, stating that discussions with Iranian leadership had progressed to the highest levels and been approved by the country's top officials.

The decision came after a volatile 24-hour period of military escalation. The U.S. had launched 49 Tomahawk missiles at Iranian targets, and Trump had threatened that morning to hit Iran "very hard tonight" and seize the country's Kharg Island, a critical oil export hub. By afternoon, he reversed course following what he described as talks with Tehran's leadership.

Trump posted on Truth Social that discussions and final points had been "approved by all parties involved," including the United States, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and numerous Gulf states. He claimed that Iran's leadership had approved a draft agreement extending the ceasefire, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and launching 60 days of negotiations on Iran's nuclear program.

However, Iran has not publicly confirmed Trump's claims about an approved agreement. According to sources briefed on the negotiations, representatives from Iran and Qatar as mediators had resolved key gaps during Wednesday talks, but Tehran's formal position remains unclear.

The announcement prompted immediate market reactions. Brent crude futures fell 3.6 percent to $89.73 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude dropped 3.6 percent to $86.83 a barrel.

Trump stated that a naval blockade would remain in effect until the agreement was finalized, and he promised to announce the time and place of signing shortly. He previously claimed that any peace deal must prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons, which Tehran denies it is pursuing. Iran's negotiators have sought the lifting of international sanctions, unfreezing of frozen assets, and control over the Strait of Hormuz.

One of Iran's top negotiators warned that "wrong strategies and impulsive decisions will reset the entire board for the worse, explode energy infrastructure and markets and create an endless quagmire."

The cancelation drew criticism from Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, who accused the Trump administration of misleading the public about the status of the conflict. "Saying this war is over is an insult to the brave American servicemen who are risking their lives every day to fight it," Schumer said on the Senate floor. He called on Senate Republicans to support a war powers resolution to end the conflict.

Trump has claimed multiple times in recent weeks that an agreement with Iran was close, only to see negotiations stall. Both sides have continued limited strikes during the ceasefire period. The involvement of Qatari mediators indicates that indirect diplomacy between Washington and Tehran continues despite the recent military escalation and ongoing disputes over fundamental issues.
