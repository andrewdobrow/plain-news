House votes to halt Iran military operations in rebuke to Trump

The U.S. House of Representatives voted 215 to 208 to pass a war powers resolution directing President Trump to end military hostilities with Iran. Four Republicans joined Democrats to approve the measure, which had failed in three previous attempts. The vote marks a rare bipartisan rebuke of the ongoing military conflict.

The resolution is largely symbolic. Democrats have been unable to pass similar legislation in the Senate, where Republican support would be needed. Even if the measure cleared both chambers of Congress, Trump would likely veto it. War powers resolutions require congressional authorization for sustained military action, but presidents have historically resisted such constraints on their role as commander in chief.

The vote reflects growing unease among some Republicans about the scope and duration of military engagement with Iran. The four Republicans who crossed party lines to support the resolution represent a shift in GOP sentiment on the conflict, though the vast majority of the party remains aligned with the administration's approach. Democrats have repeatedly sought to limit Trump's authority to conduct military operations against Iran without explicit congressional approval.

The measure comes after months of escalating tensions and military action between the United States and Iran. Congressional Democrats have argued that the president exceeded his constitutional authority by ordering sustained military operations without a formal declaration of war or explicit authorization from Congress. The administration has maintained that existing authorizations and the president's constitutional powers justify the military response.

The House vote sets up a potential showdown over war powers and executive authority, even as the practical impact of the resolution remains limited. Without Senate passage and the ability to override a presidential veto, the measure serves primarily as a statement of congressional opposition to the current military posture toward Iran.
