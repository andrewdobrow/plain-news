House votes to block further military action against Iran as Trump attacks Republicans who backed measure

The House of Representatives passed a measure seeking to halt additional military action against Iran, delivering a rebuke to President Trump. The vote took place after the United States conducted strikes on Iranian targets, though the resolution is considered largely symbolic and faces an uncertain path in the Senate.

Trump responded by calling Republicans who voted for the measure "bad" and "unpatriotic." The president's public attacks on members of his own party highlight growing tension within the Republican caucus over the administration's handling of military operations in Iran. The lower chamber's vote represents a rare instance of congressional pushback against presidential war powers, even as many observers question whether the measure will lead to any practical constraints on military action.

The United States announced a ceasefire agreement with Lebanon on Wednesday night following talks aimed at reducing tensions in the region. However, Hezbollah rejected the renewed ceasefire that Israel and Lebanon had agreed to, complicating diplomatic efforts. The U.N. peacekeeping mission for Lebanon reported that one peacekeeper was killed and others were wounded when they came under mortar fire in southeastern Lebanon, underscoring the volatile security situation.

The House vote follows a pattern of congressional efforts to reassert authority over decisions about military force. War powers resolutions typically require the president to withdraw forces from hostilities unless Congress authorizes continued military action. However, presidents of both parties have historically challenged the constitutionality of such measures and often continued operations despite congressional objections.

The split within the Republican Party over Iran policy could complicate Trump's ability to maintain unified support for his foreign policy agenda. Republicans who broke ranks faced immediate criticism from the president, a tactic Trump has used throughout his presidency to pressure party members into alignment. The measure's symbolic nature means military operations can continue in the near term, but the vote signals that some lawmakers are willing to challenge the administration on national security decisions despite potential political consequences.
