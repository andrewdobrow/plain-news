Republican lawmakers push Trump toward major military action against Iran

Republican defense hawks in Congress are urging President Trump to authorize major combat operations against Iran as the administration weighs a large-scale military strike. The push from GOP lawmakers comes as Trump has increased his public criticism of Iran, with diplomatic negotiations at a standstill and regional tensions mounting.

The calls for expanded military action coincide with attacks by Iran-aligned Houthi forces in Yemen on commercial vessels in the Red Sea, raising concerns about a broader regional conflict. The strikes on shipping lanes have added urgency to discussions within the administration about how to respond to Iranian activities in the region.

Trump has used increasingly aggressive language when discussing Iran in recent public appearances. Republican supporters in Congress are now openly advocating for the president to pursue significant military operations rather than limited strikes, arguing that a more substantial response is necessary to address what they describe as escalating threats from Tehran and its regional partners.

The situation represents a potential turning point in U.S. policy toward Iran during Trump's second term. With negotiations between the two countries completely stalled, the administration faces pressure from within its own party to take decisive military action. The debate over how to respond to Iranian activities is playing out as commercial shipping faces direct threats in critical waterways.

Several prominent Republican foreign policy figures have expressed strong views on the appropriate course of action. Senator Roger Wicker, who chairs the Senate Armed Services Committee, described a proposed ceasefire agreement as a "disaster" that would undo gains from military operations. "Everything accomplished by Operation Epic Fury would be for naught," Wicker stated.

Senator Lindsey Graham, a close Trump ally, warned that accepting a deal allowing Iran to control shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz would represent a major shift in regional power dynamics. Graham argued that if Iran maintains the capability to disrupt global trade and damage Gulf oil infrastructure, it would create "a nightmare for Israel" and questioned why the conflict began if such concessions were inevitable.

Senator Ted Cruz expressed deep concern about reported agreement terms, stating that allowing Iran to enrich uranium and develop nuclear weapons while receiving billions of dollars would constitute "a disastrous mistake." Cruz urged Trump to "continue to hold the line" and enforce previously stated red lines.

Former Secretary of State Mike Pompeo criticized the emerging deal as "not remotely America First," comparing it unfavorably to the 2015 nuclear agreement that Trump previously abandoned. Pompeo called for opening shipping lanes while denying Iran access to funds and military capabilities that could threaten regional allies.

The discussions in Washington reflect broader concerns about Iranian influence across the Middle East and the activities of groups aligned with Tehran. Trump's current Secretary of State Marco Rubio reported "significant" progress in negotiations on Sunday.
