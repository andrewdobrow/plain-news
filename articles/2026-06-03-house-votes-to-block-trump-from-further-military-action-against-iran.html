House votes to halt Iran war in bipartisan rebuke to Trump

The House of Representatives voted 215-208 to pass a war powers resolution aimed at limiting President Trump's military actions in Iran, marking a rare bipartisan rebuke of the administration. Four Republicans joined Democrats in supporting the measure, which had failed in three previous attempts before succeeding.

The resolution represents a significant assertion of congressional authority over war-making powers and caps what multiple sources described as a losing streak for Trump in congressional votes. The measure seeks to withdraw U.S. troops from the Iran conflict, though the specific mechanisms and timeline were not detailed in available reports.

The vote reflects growing unease among some lawmakers about the scope and duration of U.S. military engagement with Iran. The bipartisan support, though narrow, signals that concerns about executive war powers extend beyond partisan lines. The four Republicans who broke with their party to support the measure demonstrated willingness to challenge the president on national security matters.

Trump had opposed the resolution, making the House vote a notable defeat for the administration. The measure now moves forward in the legislative process, though its ultimate impact on U.S. military operations remains to be determined. War powers resolutions have historically faced challenges in implementation, as they often clash with executive branch claims of constitutional authority over military deployments.

The passage follows sustained debate over the proper balance between executive and legislative authority in decisions about military force. Congress has sought to reclaim its constitutional war powers in recent years, particularly regarding open-ended conflicts in the Middle East. The Iran vote represents the latest effort by lawmakers to impose checks on presidential military action, testing the boundaries of executive power in foreign policy.
