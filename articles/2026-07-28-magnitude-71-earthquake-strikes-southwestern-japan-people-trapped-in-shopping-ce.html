Magnitude 7.1 earthquake strikes southwestern Japan, people trapped in shopping center

A magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck the southwestern prefecture of Kumamoto, Japan, trapping people inside a shopping center and causing fires and injuries across the region. Japan's state broadcaster reported that some people remained trapped inside the Aeon Mall in Kumamoto, with some reports indicating an explosion occurred at the shopping center following the quake.

Authorities issued a tsunami warning immediately after the earthquake, though it was later lifted. Fires broke out in multiple locations across Kumamoto prefecture, and injuries were reported, though specific casualty numbers have not been confirmed. Emergency response teams were dispatched to affected areas.

The earthquake struck a region that has experienced significant seismic activity in the past. Kumamoto prefecture, located on the island of Kyushu, sits in one of Japan's most earthquake-prone zones. The magnitude 7.1 quake represents a major seismic event capable of causing widespread structural damage and triggering secondary disasters like fires and building collapses.

Japan maintains one of the world's most advanced earthquake early warning and response systems, designed to alert residents seconds before shaking begins and coordinate rapid emergency response. The country's building codes require structures to withstand significant seismic activity, though older buildings and infrastructure remain vulnerable to major quakes.

Rescue operations were underway at the Aeon Mall and other affected sites across Kumamoto. The full extent of casualties and structural damage was still being assessed as emergency crews worked to reach trapped individuals and extinguish fires. Japanese authorities typically conduct thorough building inspections following major earthquakes to identify structures unsafe for occupancy and coordinate evacuation and relief efforts for affected residents.
