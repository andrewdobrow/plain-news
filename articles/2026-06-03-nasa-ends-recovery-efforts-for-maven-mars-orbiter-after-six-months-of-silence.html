NASA Ends MAVEN Mission After Mars Orbiter Goes Silent

NASA has officially ended its MAVEN Mars orbiter mission after losing contact with the spacecraft that had orbited the red planet for more than a decade. The Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution mission stopped communicating with ground stations over the weekend and has not reestablished contact despite recovery efforts.

According to NASA, the spacecraft was functioning normally before passing behind Mars. "Telemetry showed all subsystems working normally before it orbited behind Mars," the agency stated. When MAVEN reappeared from behind the planet, ground controllers received only silence. The spacecraft and operations teams investigated the anomaly but were unable to restore communications.

MAVEN launched in 2013 and began its scientific work in 2014 studying the upper Martian atmosphere and its interaction with solar wind. The mission's research revealed how the sun played a role in Mars losing most of its atmosphere to space over billions of years. This atmospheric loss transformed Mars from a warmer, wetter planet into the cold, dry world seen today. The spacecraft far exceeded its original expected operational lifespan, continuing to deliver valuable scientific data throughout its extended mission.

Beyond its primary research objectives, MAVEN served as a communication relay for NASA's two Mars rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance. The loss of this relay capability removes an important tool for maintaining contact with the rovers exploring the Martian surface.

The exact nature of the orbital anomaly that disabled MAVEN has not been detailed. After attempts to restore contact failed, mission controllers decided to formally declare the mission over. NASA emphasized that MAVEN had substantially exceeded its expected operational life and produced significant scientific returns.

MAVEN's scientific contributions include mapping the structure of the Martian upper atmosphere, measuring rates of atmospheric escape, and identifying the processes responsible for stripping Mars of its once-thicker atmosphere. The data collected throughout the mission will continue to inform research on Mars habitability and planetary atmospheric evolution for years to come.

NASA maintains two other active spacecraft in orbit around Mars. The Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter launched in 2005, and Mars Odyssey launched in 2001. Both continue to function and provide NASA with orbital assets for Mars exploration.

The loss of MAVEN represents the end of a highly successful mission that provided crucial insights into Mars' climate history and atmospheric processes. Although the spacecraft now falls silent in Martian orbit, the scientific legacy of over a decade of observations will remain valuable to researchers studying planetary science and the potential for habitability on Mars.
