Qween Jean becomes first openly trans person to win Tony Award

Costume designer Qween Jean made history at the Tony Awards as the first openly trans person to win the prestigious award. Jean won recognition for her costume design work on the musical Cats, marking a significant milestone for transgender representation in theater's most prominent awards ceremony.

During her acceptance speech, Jean addressed the broader cultural moment and societal challenges facing the nation. "The world right now is deeply, deeply combating so many ailments, and we know as a society that when we come together, we can make real, permanent change," she said to the audience.

The Tony Awards ceremony took place at Radio City Music Hall and featured several historic achievements across multiple categories. The evening opened with a large-scale production number featuring the song Lady Marmalade, with dozens of performers promoting themes of unity and celebration.

Jean's work on Cats involved creating distinctive costume designs to bring the feline characters to life on stage. The musical, based on T.S. Eliot's Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber, has remained a staple of theatrical production since its original 1981 premiere. The costume design for the current production competed among other notable entries in the technical categories.

Jean's historic win came alongside other significant achievements throughout the evening. Actor John Lithgow set a record as the oldest male performer to win a competitive acting Tony at age 80 for his role in Giant. This broke a previous record that had stood since 2000.

The revival of Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman emerged as the night's biggest winner overall, taking home six awards across various categories. The musical Schmigadoon! won the award for best musical, continuing the night's theme of celebrating theatrical excellence and innovation.

The ceremony emphasized inclusivity and cultural recognition, with the opening production number setting the tone for an evening dedicated to honoring theatrical achievement. Jean's historic recognition as the first openly trans Tony winner represents an important moment for representation in one of theater's most visible and celebrated events.

Her win comes at a time of heightened national conversation surrounding transgender rights, visibility, and representation across various sectors of society and culture. The Tony Awards have long served as a major platform for recognizing excellence in theater while also reflecting broader conversations about diversity and inclusion in the arts.
