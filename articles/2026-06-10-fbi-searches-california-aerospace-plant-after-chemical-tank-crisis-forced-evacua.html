FBI searches California aerospace plant after chemical tank crisis forced evacuations

Federal agents served a search warrant at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, California, following a hazardous chemical incident that forced the evacuation of 50,000 residents last month. The FBI conducted the search after a federal judge approved the warrant, which authorized the seizure of records related to the storage, use and disposal of methyl methacrylate.

The chemical tank overheated in late May, prompting immediate evacuation concerns. The tank contained between 6,000 and 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable liquid used in manufacturing plastics and coatings such as Plexiglas and dental prosthetics. Authorities feared the overheating tank could explode. Most evacuees were able to return home over the Memorial Day weekend after a crack formed in the tank, relieving internal pressure and averting a potential disaster.

The Orange County district attorney's office is conducting a separate criminal investigation into the incident and has ordered the company not to modify or destroy any evidence. The search warrant also approved the seizure of samples of the chemical from any tank, container or vessel suspected of containing it, as well as records related to cooling equipment or other devices used to control the tank's temperature.

Methyl methacrylate poses significant health risks. Exposure to the chemical can cause serious respiratory problems, neurological issues and irritation to the skin, eyes and throat, according to the Environmental Protection Agency. Some evacuated residents reported strong odors, respiratory irritation, headaches and dizziness following the incident. About a dozen evacuated people and businesses have filed lawsuits against GKN.

At a community meeting held Tuesday, GKN senior vice-president Steve Carlin apologized to the Garden Grove community. "I'm sorry that this event and this incident occurred. I understand what a disruptive event it was and how unsettling it is to the greater community," he said. He thanked firefighters and local leaders who responded to the May crisis.

City leaders and residents pressed GKN to relocate the methyl methacrylate tanks, but Carlin said it was premature to make such a decision while the investigation remained ongoing. Orange County health officials reported that no contamination or fumes were released and said they would monitor air quality for several months and check sewer and storm drains.

GKN Aerospace Transparency Systems, a UK-based company, manufactures cockpit windows, canopies and windshields at the Garden Grove facility. A company spokesperson said they are cooperating with authorities and will continue to do so.

The incident was one of two major hazardous chemical emergencies on the west coast within a week. Five days after the GKN incident, a tank containing a corrosive chemical at a paper mill in Longview, Washington, ruptured and imploded, killing 11 people.
