FBI searches California aerospace plant after chemical tank nearly exploded

Federal agents executed a search warrant at a Garden Grove aerospace facility following a chemical emergency that forced 50,000 residents to evacuate in May. The FBI searched GKN Aerospace Transparency Systems, a UK-based company that manufactures cockpit windows and aircraft canopies, after an overheated chemical tank threatened a potential explosion.

The warrant, signed by a federal judge, authorized the seizure of records related to the storage, use and disposal of methyl methacrylate, the highly flammable chemical contained in the malfunctioning tank. The tank held 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of the substance, which is used in manufacturing plastics and coatings including Plexiglas and dental prosthetics. According to the Environmental Protection Agency, exposure to methyl methacrylate can cause serious respiratory problems, neurological issues and irritation to skin, eyes and throat.

The Orange County District Attorney's office is conducting a separate criminal investigation into the aerospace plant and has ordered the company not to modify or destroy evidence. Federal agents also sought to seize samples of the chemical from any tanks, containers or vessels suspected of holding it, as well as records related to cooling equipment used to regulate the chemical's temperature.

The emergency began in late May when the tank overheated. Residents were evacuated the following day as hazardous materials teams worked to stabilize the situation. A crack eventually formed in the tank, relieving pressure and preventing an explosion. Most evacuees were able to return home over the Memorial Day weekend.

At a community meeting, GKN senior vice-president Steve Carlin apologized to the Garden Grove community. "I want to say that I'm sorry that this event and this incident occurred. I understand and I realized sitting here tonight what a disruptive event it was and how unsettling it is to the greater community," Carlin said. He thanked firefighters and local leaders who responded to the incident.

When city leaders and residents asked GKN to consider relocating the methyl methacrylate tanks, Carlin said it was too early in the investigation for the company to make such determinations. A GKN spokesperson said the company is cooperating with authorities.

About a dozen evacuated people and businesses have filed lawsuits against GKN since the incident. Some locals reported strong odors, respiratory irritation, headaches and dizziness. Orange County health officials said no contamination or fumes were released but would monitor air quality for several months and check sewer and storm drains.

The incident occurred days before a second major chemical emergency on the west coast. Five days later, a tank containing corrosive chemicals at a Longview, Washington paper mill ruptured and imploded, killing 11 people.
