U.S. Strikes Iran for Second Consecutive Night as Trump Says Negotiations Taking Too Long

The United States launched airstrikes against Iran for a second straight night following the downing of a U.S. Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump blamed Tehran for the incident and said the military action would continue until a deal is reached.

The helicopter went down early Tuesday off the coast of Oman while on patrol. A U.S. official told the Associated Press the aircraft collided with an Iranian drone, though it remains unclear whether the collision was intentional. The two crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat after spending about two hours in the water. Trump later downplayed the incident, telling the Wall Street Journal that the helicopter being downed "wasn't a big deal" and that the "pilot is fine."

The strikes came after Iran launched retaliatory attacks Wednesday morning, targeting U.S. assets in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan. Tehran said it used long-range missiles and drones to strike American bases, including the al-Azraq base in Jordan. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed they targeted F-35 fighter jet hangars and a command-and-control center. However, U.S. officials told Reuters that nearly all missiles and drones launched by Iran were intercepted. Jordan's armed forces said they shot down five missiles before impact.

U.S. Central Command announced that Trump directed the military to launch strikes beginning at 10 p.m. UK time Tuesday in response to the helicopter downing. Media reports citing White House officials indicate the strikes targeted radar and air defense facilities along the Strait of Hormuz. Centcom described the response as "proportional" to what it called "unjustified Iranian aggression."

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded on social media, warning that Iran "will leave no attack or threat unanswered. Leave our region if you want to be safe." His statement suggested Iran prefers "the language of diplomacy," but added that Iran speaks "other languages far more fluently."

The escalating military action threatens a ceasefire announced in April between the two countries. Before the helicopter incident, Trump had expressed optimism about negotiations, saying there was "a good chance" of signing a deal within "two or three days." He also warned about the costs of continued bombing, saying "a lot of people are going to be killed."

The conflict has already strained the global economy and pushed up energy prices worldwide. Since fighting between the U.S., Israel and Iran began in February, many essential goods including food have become more expensive. Officials have struggled to convert the April ceasefire into a permanent agreement, particularly as Israel expands military operations against the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militia in Lebanon.
