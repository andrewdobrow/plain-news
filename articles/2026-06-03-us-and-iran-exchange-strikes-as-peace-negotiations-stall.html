Oil Prices Jump After U.S. Launches Second Day of Strikes on Iran

Oil prices surged following a second consecutive day of military strikes between the United States and Iran, intensifying concerns about a potential escalation in the Middle East. The renewed conflict has rattled global energy markets already dealing with multiple sources of uncertainty.

The escalation began after a U.S. Army Apache helicopter went down near the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday morning. President Trump stated that Iran was responsible for downing the aircraft, though U.S. officials told Reuters the helicopter collided with an Iranian drone and the circumstances remain under investigation. Trump directed the military to launch strikes beginning at 10pm UK time on Tuesday in response to the incident. U.S. Central Command said the strikes targeted radar and air defense facilities along the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran responded with its own barrage of attacks on Wednesday morning. The country's Revolutionary Guards struck the U.S. al-Azraq base in Jordan with long-range missiles, according to Iranian media reports. Iran also said it had launched drone attacks at bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, targeting F-35 fighter jet hangars and command-and-control centers. U.S. officials stated that nearly all missiles and drones launched by Iran were intercepted. Jordan's armed forces said they shot down five missiles before impact.

Despite the military exchanges, U.S. officials signaled they were not seeking full-scale war with Iran. Centcom called the operation "a proportional response to unjustified Iranian aggression." Trump himself downplayed the helicopter incident in a Wall Street Journal interview, saying it "wasn't a big deal" and that the pilot was fine. The helicopter's two crew members were rescued in an unprecedented operation using an unmanned drone boat.

The Strait of Hormuz remains critical to global energy supplies, with roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum passing through the waterway. Any sustained military engagement threatens shipping routes and could constrain global supply, directly pushing prices higher.

The conflict erupts as diplomatic negotiations appear stalled. Trump had earlier expressed optimism about reaching a deal with Iran within days, but those hopes dimmed after the helicopter incident. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned on social media that Iran "will leave no attack or threat unanswered" and told the U.S. to "leave our region if you want to be safe."

This latest exchange follows months of tension since the U.S. and Iran agreed to an initial ceasefire in April. The broader U.S.-Israel campaign against Iran since late February has already shaken global markets and pushed up energy prices and food costs worldwide.

Markets will likely focus on whether military exchanges continue, whether diplomatic channels reopen, and whether other nations become involved in the escalating conflict.
