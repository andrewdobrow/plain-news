Iran war reaches six-month mark with no clear exit strategy

The war with Iran entered its seventh month with President Trump's path to ending the conflict remaining uncertain. Trump had originally projected a four to five week military campaign when the war began in late February, but the operation has evolved into an extended engagement centered on economic pressure and control of the Strait of Hormuz.

The war began with goals of degrading Iran's missile capabilities and destroying what remained of its nuclear program. Over six months, the focus has shifted to an open-ended fight over the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial waterway for global energy transport. U.S. officials say they have steadily eroded Iran's leverage over the strait in recent months, with the American military gaining what officials describe as the upper hand in the battle for control.

Trump's proposed exits from the conflict have ranged from military victory to a negotiated peace deal tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The war now centers on an economic squeeze of Iran, while the U.S. moves increasing amounts of oil out of the strait to reduce Iranian leverage. Iran and Oman are engaged in some form of negotiations, though the status and prospects of those talks remain in flux.

The extended conflict has become a major feature of Trump's second term, affecting his standing with voters domestically and the U.S. position in the Middle East. Trump is scheduled to visit NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston, where his ambitious space priorities are also facing challenges from a divided Republican Congress.

What began as a targeted campaign to address Iran's nuclear and missile programs has transformed into a protracted struggle over regional control and energy security, with no clear timeline for resolution. The war has now lasted more than twelve times longer than the president's initial estimate.
