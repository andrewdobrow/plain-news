Venice Film Festival Announces 2026 Lineup With New Films From McDonagh, Zeller, and Boyle

The Venice Film Festival has revealed its 2026 lineup, showcasing new works from prominent international directors including Martin McDonagh, Florian Zeller, Danny Boyle, Werner Herzog, Lance Oppenheim, Nanni Moretti, and Hirokazu Kore-eda. The festival will also debut a documentary about the Oasis reunion, blending acclaimed cinema with mainstream entertainment at the prestigious Italian event.

The 82nd edition runs from August 14 to 21 and features 21 world premieres, six international premieres, one European premiere, and 23 regional premieres across its competition sections. Robert Pattinson will star in a film titled Primetime, while McDonagh will present Wild Horse Nine.

One significant entry in the Horizons competition is "The Difficult Bride," a supernatural feminist drama directed by Rubaiyat Hossain. The film marks a historic milestone as the first Bangladeshi feature film to screen at Venice. The cast features three Bangladesh National Film Award winners: Azmeri Haque Badhon, Rikita Nondine Shimu, and Sunerah Kamal. Films Boutique has secured international sales rights for the project.

Another Horizons selection, "Oasis" by writer-director Jannis Lenz, has been picked up by Totem for international sales ahead of its Venice premiere. The film is centered on a school setting where a viral video triggers a narrative exploring themes of fear, exclusion, and violence. Totem added the film to its sales slate as the company transitions its primary focus toward production.

The Sarajevo Film Festival, which specializes in South-East European cinema, simultaneously announced its competition programs during the same period. The festival revealed lineups across four competition sections, featuring 21 world premieres, six international premieres, one European premiere, and 23 regional premieres. The Sarajevo festival will run from August 14 to 21, with nine narrative features from South-East European filmmakers competing in the main section.

This year's Venice lineup represents a diverse range of storytelling approaches. Beyond the major names attached to various projects, the festival continues its tradition of discovering emerging voices and supporting cinema from underrepresented regions. The inclusion of "The Difficult Bride" demonstrates Venice's commitment to expanding global representation in its programming, particularly in highlighting cinema from South Asian countries.

The parallel timing of Venice and Sarajevo festivals underscores the importance of late summer as a significant period for festival cinema. These events serve as launching pads for films that will compete throughout the upcoming awards season and establish themselves within the international film market. The combination of established filmmakers and fresh talent announced for Venice's 2026 edition suggests another compelling year for the festival.
