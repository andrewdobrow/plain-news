England beats Mexico 3-2 in World Cup round of 16

England progressed to the World Cup quarter-finals with a dramatic 3-2 victory over Mexico at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City. Jude Bellingham scored twice and Harry Kane converted a penalty kick to secure the win despite England playing with 10 men for much of the second half.

The match was played in front of 80,824 passionate Mexico fans at one of football's most intimidating venues. Mexico scored twice but could not equalize, ending their World Cup campaign on home soil. The victory represents one of England's most memorable away wins in tournament history.

Bellingham's two-goal performance highlighted his continued strong showing in the tournament. Goalkeeper Jordan Pickford made crucial saves that proved vital in preserving England's narrow advantage. Head coach Thomas Tuchel praised his team's resilience in difficult circumstances, acknowledging that while the performance was not aesthetically pleasing, it demonstrated character under pressure.

The match came at a cost for England. Midfielder Jordan Henderson suffered what Tuchel described as a "really bad" wrist injury during post-match celebrations. The extent of the injury and Henderson's availability for the quarter-final remain uncertain as England prepares for their next fixture against Norway.

Mexico entered the match riding high after winning all four group stage matches without conceding a goal. The team's defensive strength and cohesive play had generated considerable excitement among Mexican supporters. However, they were unable to maintain that defensive record against England's attacking prowess.

The victory sets up England's quarter-final matchup with Norway. Other Round of 16 matches are continuing, with the United States facing Belgium and Portugal taking on Spain in marquee matchups.

Pickford acknowledged the team's composure after the match, noting that England managed to hold on despite the numerical disadvantage and the challenging environment. The goalkeeper's contributions were essential to maintaining the team's advantage throughout the second half.

England's performance at the Azteca adds to the team's legacy of significant away victories in World Cup history. The combination of clinical finishing from Bellingham and Kane, along with solid defending from the England backline, proved sufficient to overcome Mexico's determination and home-field advantage.

The injury to Henderson may force Tuchel to consider tactical adjustments as England advances deeper into the tournament. The England camp will assess Henderson's condition before the quarter-final, hoping the midfielder recovers in time to contribute to their campaign. England's ability to win under pressure, despite playing with reduced numbers, suggests they have the mental strength to progress further in the competition.
