Everton ordered to pay Burnley £35 million over financial rule violations

Burnley have won a landmark legal case against Everton for breaching Premier League financial rules, with an independent disciplinary commission ordering the club to pay compensation of nearly £40 million.

The verdict comes from the same three-person panel that previously deducted Everton 10 points in November 2023 for the same breach. The commission concluded that Everton's profit and sustainability rule violations gave them a sporting advantage that contributed to Burnley's relegation from the Premier League at the end of the 2021-22 season.

Burnley's successful claim has significant implications for English football and is likely to encourage other relegated clubs to pursue similar legal action against rivals they believe gained unfair advantages through financial rule breaches.

Everton were found to have breached Premier League regulations during the four-year period up to June 2022. The 10-point deduction was later reduced to six points on appeal. Under Premier League rules, clubs that breach financial regulations can face points deductions, fines, and compensation claims from rival clubs affected by the violations.

The Premier League's profit and sustainability framework limits clubs to losses of £105 million over three seasons. Those exceeding this threshold face various penalties depending on the severity of the breach.

Everton expressed strong disagreement with the decision. In a statement, the club said it was "surprised and angered" by the ruling and announced plans to appeal. The club disputed the commission's finding that Burnley's relegation was caused by a sporting advantage gained through Everton's breach, arguing that the club had already received a substantive sporting sanction through the points deduction.

Everton also challenged the legal basis of the decision, claiming the panel's reasoning "sets a dangerous and unworkable precedent for English football." The club argued the ruling is flawed in both law and fact, and contended that the evidence presented by its legal representatives was misrepresented. It expressed confidence that an appeal would be successful.

The compensation payment reflects the significant financial impact of Premier League relegation, including lost broadcasting revenue and sponsorship income that Burnley would have earned had they remained in the top flight.

This ruling adds to Everton's ongoing financial challenges. The Merseyside club continues to operate under substantial constraints as it manages debts and funds stadium development while striving to comply with Premier League financial regulations. The decision increases scrutiny on how English football's financial governance is enforced and demonstrates the potential cost of regulatory breaches to clubs found in violation.
