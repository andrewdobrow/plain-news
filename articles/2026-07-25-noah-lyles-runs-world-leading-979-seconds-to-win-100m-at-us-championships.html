Noah Lyles runs world-leading 9.79 seconds to win 100m at US championships

Noah Lyles produced the fastest 100-meter time in the world this year, running 9.79 seconds to claim victory at the US athletics championships. The Olympic champion's performance marked a dominant showing at the national competition.

Lyles entered the championships as one of the favorites following his success on the international stage. His winning time establishes him as the fastest sprinter globally in 2026 and puts him in strong position heading into the remainder of the season. The US championships serve as a key selection event for American track and field athletes seeking spots on national teams for major international competitions.

The 9.79-second clocking represents elite-level sprinting. Only a handful of athletes have run faster times in competitive history. Lyles' ability to produce such a mark at the national championships demonstrates his form and readiness for high-stakes competition. The performance also adds to his growing legacy as one of America's premier sprinters.

The victory continues Lyles' rise in track and field. He has established himself as a consistent performer at major championships and a regular presence on podiums at the highest levels of the sport. His combination of speed and competitive reliability has made him a central figure in American sprinting.

The US championships draw the nation's top track and field talent, making victories particularly meaningful. Lyles' world-leading time ensures he will enter upcoming international meets as a marked man, with competitors seeking to match or surpass his benchmark. The result sets the stage for what could be a compelling season of men's sprinting competition.
