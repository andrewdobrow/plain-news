Iran launches missiles and drones toward Strait of Hormuz as peace talks stall

Iran fired ballistic missiles and drones at Kuwait and Bahrain on Saturday, escalating tensions after the US military shot down Iranian drones and struck radar installations in the region. The attacks represent the latest in a series of tit-for-tat exchanges that threaten to unravel an already fragile ceasefire agreement between Washington and Tehran.

Air raid sirens sounded across Bahrain as residents were instructed to move to safe locations. Kuwait's military confirmed it was intercepting drones and missiles launched at the country. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps said it was targeting the Ali al-Salem airbase in Bahrain, where the US Navy's 5th Fleet is headquartered.

The escalation began when US Central Command announced it had shot down four Iranian attack drones headed toward the Strait of Hormuz, describing them as posing "an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic." The US military also struck Iranian coastal radar sites on Sirik and Qeshm Island in what it characterized as a defensive measure "to defend against further attacks."

Hours later, Iran responded with its own strikes. The Iranian military said it fired seven ballistic missiles toward Kuwait and Bahrain. Central Command reported that six were intercepted and one failed to reach its target. The command denied Iranian claims that the strikes damaged the 5th Fleet headquarters, stating there were no reports of harm to US personnel.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical global chokepoint through which roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supplies transit. The ongoing military confrontations in the region have sent energy prices spiking and disrupted global supply chains. The US military is enforcing a blockade on Iranian ports in response to Iran's restrictions on shipping through the strait.

The World Food Programme warned that millions of people face increased hunger due to the conflict's effects, primarily from soaring energy and food prices. Analysts say the war has extended regional instability and thrown the global economy into disarray.

President Trump told reporters on Friday that "the situation with Iran seems to be going quite well," despite the continued military exchanges. He predicted the administration would "come out of Iran very quickly" and promised that fertilizer prices would decline. Trump attributed the slow progress in negotiations to the difficulty of the situation for Iran, saying the country faced demands "they never thought they'd be doing."

US and Iranian negotiators have spent weeks attempting to extend the ceasefire by 60 days and initiate new discussions on Iran's nuclear program. However, both sides continue demanding changes to any agreement, with neither appearing willing to compromise significantly. The repeated cycles of military strikes undermine diplomatic efforts to achieve a lasting resolution to the conflict.
