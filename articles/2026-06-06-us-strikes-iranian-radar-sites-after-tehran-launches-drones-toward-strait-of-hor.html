US Shoots Down Iranian Drones Over Strait of Hormuz After Strike on Radar Sites

The United States military shot down an Iranian drone that approached the aircraft carrier Abraham Lincoln in the Arabian Sea, according to US Central Command. An F-35C fighter jet fired on the Iranian Shahed-139 unmanned aircraft after it flew toward the carrier "with unclear intent," the military said on Tuesday.

"An F-35C fighter jet from Abraham Lincoln shot down the Iranian drone in self-defence and to protect the aircraft carrier and personnel on board," said Captain Tim Hawkins, a navy spokesperson at Central Command. No American service members were injured and no equipment was damaged in the incident.

Iran's UN mission did not comment on the US account. Iran's Tasnim news agency reported that connection had been lost with a drone in international waters but stated the reason was unknown. The semi-official Fars news agency claimed an Iranian drone had completed a surveillance mission in international waters.

On the same day, the Iranian Revolutionary Guards harassed a US-flagged merchant vessel in the Strait of Hormuz. Two IRGC boats and an Iranian Mohajer drone approached the tanker M/V Stena Imperative at high speeds and threatened to board and seize the ship, according to Central Command.

The military escalation comes as diplomats work to arrange nuclear negotiations between the countries. Donald Trump said that "bad things" would probably happen unless a deal could be reached, with US warships heading toward Iran. However, Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian instructed the country's foreign minister to "pursue fair and equitable negotiations" with the US, signaling Tehran's willingness to engage.

The White House confirmed that Trump envoy Steve Witkoff plans to hold talks with Iranian officials later in the week despite the drone incident, according to press secretary Karoline Leavitt. Iran's foreign ministry said consultations were still underway on the meeting location, with Turkey and Oman both expressing readiness to host discussions focused on nuclear issues.

Pezeshkian's announcement represented a significant shift for the reformist president, who had previously said the situation in Iran had gone beyond his control. His stance appeared to have support from Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who had previously dismissed talks.

The Lincoln carrier strike group represents the most visible element of a US military buildup in the Middle East following a violent crackdown against anti-government demonstrations in Iran last month. The buildup came after Trump demanded Tehran make nuclear concessions and sent additional military forces to the region. Trump previously stated Iran was "seriously talking" about a potential deal.
