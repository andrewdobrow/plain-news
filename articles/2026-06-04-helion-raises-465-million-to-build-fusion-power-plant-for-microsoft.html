Fusion startup Helion raises $465 million to build power plant for Microsoft

Helion, a fusion energy company supported by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, has secured $465 million in funding to construct a power plant for Microsoft. The company is targeting 2028 for completion, working to fulfill a commercial agreement with the technology company to supply electricity generated through fusion.

Fusion energy remains largely theoretical in commercial applications, though it has been pursued as a clean energy solution for decades. Helion's approach involves heating plasma to extreme temperatures and using magnetic fields to compress it until fusion reactions occur. Unlike traditional nuclear fission, which splits atoms and generates long-lived radioactive waste, fusion combines atoms and produces no greenhouse gas emissions or hazardous byproducts.

The investment reflects growing interest in fusion technology from major tech companies seeking reliable clean energy sources. Data centers and artificial intelligence infrastructure require enormous amounts of electricity, driving companies to explore alternatives to conventional power generation. Microsoft's commitment to purchase power from Helion represents one of the first major commercial agreements for fusion-generated electricity.

The 2028 deadline is aggressive for an industry that has experienced repeated setbacks. Helion has built several prototype machines and announced achieving key technical milestones, but has not yet demonstrated net energy gain, the point at which a fusion reaction produces more energy than is consumed to create it. Other fusion companies and government-funded projects worldwide are pursuing different technological approaches on varying timelines.

The funding will support the power plant's construction and further technology development. Altman has been instrumental in backing Helion, viewing fusion as essential infrastructure for future technological progress. The company's ability to meet its 2028 deadline will serve as a crucial test case for whether private fusion ventures can deliver on their promises to transition the technology from research laboratories to commercial power generation.

Helion operates within a broader landscape of fusion energy investment. Multiple private companies and government programs are racing to commercialize the technology, recognizing its potential to address energy demands while producing minimal environmental impact. The sector has attracted billions in funding from technology leaders and investors confident that fusion will play a central role in powering next-generation infrastructure.

Success would represent a major breakthrough for the energy industry and could reshape how data centers and other power-intensive operations obtain electricity. However, the field faces significant technical challenges, and skeptics question whether fusion can be produced at commercial scale within the timelines companies have announced. Helion's progress toward its 2028 goal will provide important evidence about whether the private fusion sector can deliver on its ambitious promises.
