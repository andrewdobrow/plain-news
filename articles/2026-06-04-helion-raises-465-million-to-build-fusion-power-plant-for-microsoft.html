Fusion startup Helion raises $465 million to build power plant for Microsoft

Helion Energy, a fusion company backed by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, has secured $465 million in new funding as it works toward completing a power plant for Microsoft by 2028. The capital injection represents a major milestone for the startup as it pursues its goal of delivering electricity through nuclear fusion technology.

Under a power purchase agreement with Microsoft, Helion has committed to building and operating a fusion power plant that will supply the tech giant with clean energy. Fusion energy, which replicates the nuclear reactions that power the sun, has long been pursued as a potentially limitless source of clean electricity. Unlike nuclear fission reactors currently in operation, fusion reactions produce far less radioactive waste and cannot experience a meltdown.

Helion's technology uses magnetic fields to compress plasma until fusion reactions occur, releasing energy that can then be converted into electricity. While the concept shows promise, no fusion company has yet achieved sustained net energy production at a commercial scale. The 2028 deadline represents an ambitious timeline, giving Helion roughly two years to construct the facility and begin generating power for the grid.

Microsoft's participation reflects a broader trend among major technology companies seeking dedicated clean energy sources. As data centers and artificial intelligence systems consume ever-increasing amounts of electricity, tech firms have grown more aggressive in pursuing unconventional energy solutions. The partnership represents a significant wager that fusion technology can reach commercial viability sooner than many industry observers expect.

The funding will support Helion's construction phase and ongoing research and development efforts. Altman has been a vocal proponent of fusion energy, arguing that it represents essential infrastructure for powering future artificial intelligence systems. His backing underscores the growing convergence between the tech and energy sectors.

The investment comes as multiple fusion startups race to achieve commercial success, with billions of dollars flowing into the sector from private investors, venture capital firms, and government funding initiatives. Competition to become the first company to generate grid-scale fusion power has intensified as major technology companies and investors recognize both the potential rewards and the strategic importance of the technology.

The deal highlights how tech companies are increasingly willing to make substantial bets on emerging energy technologies to solve their power challenges. As computing demands continue to surge, securing reliable and abundant clean energy has become a critical strategic priority for the industry. Helion's partnership with Microsoft demonstrates confidence that fusion energy could play a significant role in meeting these growing power requirements in the coming years.
