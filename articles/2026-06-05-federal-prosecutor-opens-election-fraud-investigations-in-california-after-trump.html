U.S. 2026-06-06

Federal prosecutors open investigations into California election administration

The United States Attorney's Office has launched investigations into California's election processes and deployed a prosecutor to a Los Angeles vote center. The announcement came as millions of ballots remained uncounted in the state's primary election.

The decision to send federal prosecutors to observe election administration represents an unusual level of federal involvement in state election operations. The investigations target unspecified aspects of how California conducted its primary voting, which concluded this week.

California uses a top-two primary system in which all candidates appear on a single ballot regardless of party, with the top two vote-getters advancing to the November general election. The state allows mail-in ballots postmarked by election day to be counted for days afterward, a practice that typically means results take longer to finalize than in states with different ballot-counting rules.

The U.S. Attorney's Office has not publicly detailed what prompted the investigations or what specific election procedures are under scrutiny. Federal prosecutors have authority to investigate potential violations of federal voting rights laws, but state and local officials typically manage election administration with minimal federal intervention.

The deployment of a prosecutor to a Los Angeles vote center suggests federal officials are monitoring ongoing ballot processing. Los Angeles County, the nation's most populous county, processes millions of ballots and has historically taken weeks to complete its count. The presence of federal observers at vote-counting locations is rare outside of areas with histories of voting rights violations.
