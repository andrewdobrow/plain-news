Dolly Parton buried in Nashville following private family funeral

Country music legend Dolly Parton was laid to rest in Nashville alongside her husband following a private family funeral. The singer died on Tuesday at the age of 80 after suffering from cancer.

Parton's death marks the loss of one of the most celebrated and influential figures in American music history. Over a career spanning more than six decades, she became known for her distinctive voice and songwriting, but also for her humanitarian work and business ventures.

The private ceremony reflects the family's desire for an intimate farewell to the entertainer who had touched millions of lives. Parton had been married to Carl Dean, who remained largely out of the public eye throughout their decades-long marriage.

Parton possessed a distinctive soprano with a remarkable vibrato and east Tennessee twang that made her voice among the most recognizable in music. Her wit was equally famous, with self-deprecating humor that endeared her to audiences worldwide. Her maxim "It takes a lot of money to look this cheap" captured her appetite for glitz and mischief that defined her public persona.

She was a pioneering figure in taking country music into the mainstream, becoming one of the first country stars to achieve significant crossover success. At the 2014 Glastonbury festival, she attracted what was reportedly the largest crowd in the event's history, performing while wearing a crystal-studded white suit and tooting a rhinestone saxophone.

Her songwriting prowess earned her recognition across the industry, with 10 Grammy awards, 11 from the Academy of Country Music, and numerous other honors. Her composition "I Will Always Love You" became one of the best-selling singles of all time after Whitney Houston recorded it for "The Bodyguard" soundtrack in 1992. The song earned Parton enough money, she said, "to buy Graceland" and proved that her refusal to share publishing rights with Elvis Presley when he wanted to record it had been the right decision.

Beyond music, Parton's charitable work reflected her commitment to helping others. From 1988, she provided support to local schoolchildren, and from 1995, she ran an international book reading programme. She also provided aid for people affected by wildfires and hurricanes and supported medical research. Her Imagination Library became known worldwide for distributing free books to millions of children, inspired in part by her father's inability to read.

Though known for her glamorous exterior and larger-than-life persona, Parton grounded her public image with deep intelligence and honesty. She remained active in her career and charitable work until recent years, leaving behind a lasting impact on generations of musicians who followed in her footsteps and a legacy that extended far beyond music into film, television, and popular culture.
