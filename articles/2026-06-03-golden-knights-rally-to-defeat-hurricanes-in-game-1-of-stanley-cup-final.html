Marner scores Stanley Cup Final hat trick in second period as Hurricanes fall in double overtime

Mitch Marner made NHL history by recording the fastest hat trick ever in a Stanley Cup Final game, but it was not enough to prevent the Vegas Golden Knights from escaping with a 5-4 double-overtime victory over the Carolina Hurricanes on Saturday night.

Shea Theodore scored the game-winner at 5:38 of the second overtime period, his goal deflecting off goalie Brandon Bussi's skate to secure Vegas a 2-1 series lead. The Golden Knights had squandered a four-goal advantage before Theodore's heroics ended the marathon contest.

Marner's performance in the second period was the highlight of an otherwise dramatic game for Vegas. His three goals in a single period marked the fastest hat trick in Stanley Cup Final history, giving the Golden Knights a commanding advantage early in the match. The pace and efficiency of Marner's scoring set a new benchmark for the championship series.

However, the Hurricanes mounted an impressive comeback that nearly rewrote the outcome. Carolina systematically erased Vegas's four-goal lead and forced the game into overtime, refusing to concede despite facing a historic individual performance from their opponent. The first overtime period failed to produce a winner, pushing the game into a second extra frame where Theodore eventually ended the contest.

The loss puts Carolina in a difficult position as the series shifts focus. The Hurricanes now trail 2-1 with the remainder of the series returning to other venues. They must regroup quickly to avoid falling deeper into a deficit against a Vegas team that has proven capable of closing out tight contests.

Both teams demonstrated the intensity expected in championship hockey throughout the evening. The game featured multiple lead changes and strong performances on both sides, making it one of the most dramatic contests in recent Stanley Cup Final history. The Hurricanes showed resilience by fighting back from such a significant deficit, but ultimately came up short in the extra periods.

Vegas's ability to hold on despite losing a substantial lead will likely boost confidence heading forward, though the team's vulnerability in that final stretch may concern coach Bruce Cassidy. For Carolina, the comeback effort showed the team has the character to compete with Vegas, but they will need to find a way to eliminate the early deficits that have plagued them through the first three games.

Marner's historic achievement will be remembered as a significant moment in Cup Final lore regardless of the series outcome. The feat of scoring three goals in a single Final period had never been accomplished before, making his performance historically significant even in a losing effort for Vegas.
