Iran attacks Kuwait airport, killing one in strike near US military base

Iran launched a deadly drone attack on Kuwait International Airport, killing at least one person and wounding 63 others, according to Kuwait's military. The strike damaged a terminal at the airport and marked the first deadly attack in the Gulf region since a ceasefire took effect on April 8.

The assault came as part of an escalating exchange between US and Iranian forces. US Central Command said it had targeted an unladen tanker, the M/T Lexie, in international waters near Kuwait as the vessel moved toward Iran's Kharg Island. An American aircraft fired a missile to disable the tanker's engine after the crew ignored repeated warnings over 24 hours. Iran responded with the drone strike on Kuwait's passenger terminal.

The incident intensified further exchanges of fire. US forces said they had shot down three attack drones launched by Iran toward civilian ships in regional waters. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it had attacked the US Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain with missiles and drones in response to the strike on Qeshm Island, though US Central Command denied repelling such an attack. The US military also said it had attacked sites on Qeshm.

Kuwait's defence ministry described the attack as "criminal Iranian aggression which resulted in significant material damage to the building and injuries." Civil aviation authorities suspended traffic and transferred incoming flights to alternative airports. Kuwait's deputy foreign minister summoned Iran's chargé d'affaires to formally protest the attacks and ordered a reduction in Iranian embassy staff, declaring two diplomats persona non grata with 24 hours to leave.

Iran's foreign ministry blamed Kuwait and Bahrain for the strikes, stating they had allowed the United States to use their territory for aggressive plans against Iran. The statement emphasized the responsibility of Kuwait and Bahrain's leaders for "last night's acts of aggression."

The latest escalation jeopardizes ongoing ceasefire negotiations between Washington and Tehran. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio claimed during congressional testimony that war in Iran "is over" and suggested a deal was within reach. However, Trump said in an interview that talks were continuing and he was not seeking escalation, adding that Iran appeared "pretty close" to signing a peace agreement and "it could happen over the weekend."

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned that Iran was "playing with fire" and that Trump could order a "full-scale return to military action" if negotiations fail. A military adviser to Iran's supreme leader threatened more missile and drone strikes if the US renewed its attacks, posting that "every shot fired and every attack will be met with a deluge of missiles and drones."

The exchange of strikes demonstrates the fragile state of regional security and the difficulty of achieving diplomatic progress amid active military hostilities.
