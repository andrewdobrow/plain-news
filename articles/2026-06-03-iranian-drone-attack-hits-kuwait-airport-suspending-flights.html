Iran attacks Kuwait airport, killing one and injuring dozens

Iranian drones struck Kuwait's international airport, killing at least one person and wounding 63 others. The attack damaged a passenger terminal and forced the airport to suspend traffic and redirect incoming flights to alternative airports.

The strike represents a significant escalation in the Gulf region and marks the first deadly attack since a ceasefire took effect on April 8. Iran said the assault was retaliation for earlier US military action, including a strike on an Iranian oil tanker near the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on Iran's Qeshm Island.

The sequence of events began when US Central Command targeted an unladen tanker, the M/T Lexie, in international waters as it moved toward Iran's Kharg Island near Kuwait. US forces fired a Hellfire missile to disable the vessel's engine after the crew ignored repeated warnings over 24 hours. In response, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps attacked the US Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain with missiles and drones, a claim the US military denied. The Iranian military also launched three attack drones toward civilian ships in regional waters, which US forces said they shot down.

Kuwait's defense ministry called the airport attack "criminal Iranian aggression" causing significant material damage. The country summoned Iran's diplomatic representative to formally protest and ordered a reduction in Iranian embassy staff. Kuwait declared two Iranian diplomats persona non grata and gave them 24 hours to leave.

Iran's foreign ministry blamed Kuwait and Bahrain for the attacks, condemning what it described as the "colonialist use" of their territory and infrastructure by the United States to advance military plans against Iran. The statement held Kuwait and Bahrain's leaders "directly and unmistakably responsible" for the earlier strikes.

The attacks undermine ongoing diplomatic efforts between the United States and Iran. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio claimed during congressional testimony that a nuclear deal could be imminent, suggesting progress on aspects of Iran's nuclear program previously off limits for negotiation. However, Iran has indicated it would suspend peace talks in protest of the US actions.

Donald Trump said he is not seeking to escalate tensions and that talks with Iran continue. He claimed Iran is "pretty close" to signing a peace agreement and suggested it "could happen over the weekend." Trump also expressed interest in meeting Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned that Iran is "playing with fire" and suggested Trump could order a "full-scale return to military action" if negotiations fail. Netanyahu asserted that Israel and US forces remain ready for military operations.

The latest exchange of strikes highlights the fragility of ceasefire arrangements and the difficulty of achieving diplomatic breakthroughs amid continued military confrontation in the Middle East.
