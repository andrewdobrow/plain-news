Iran Strikes US Military Base in Kuwait, Killing Multiple People at Airport

The United States and Iran are locked in an increasingly dangerous confrontation over Tehran's nuclear program, with President Donald Trump threatening military action and ordering a massive deployment of naval forces to the Middle East.

Trump has given Iran a deadline of 10 to 15 days to reach a nuclear deal or face what he called "really bad things." When asked whether he was considering limited military strikes to pressure Iran into negotiations, Trump told reporters: "I guess I can say I am considering" it. US military planning has reportedly advanced to an early stage, with options under consideration including targeted strikes against individuals and even pursuing leadership change in Tehran.

The military movements include repositioning of aircraft carriers and other warships in the region. However, it remains unclear whether these deployments are intended as intimidation tactics to force Iranian concessions on nuclear issues or as preparation for actual military operations.

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi responded by rejecting military solutions. "That has been tested last year. There were huge attacks on our facilities, they killed and assassinated our scientists, but they couldn't kill our nuclear programme," he said in an interview.

Despite the military threats, diplomatic channels remain open. Araghchi held indirect talks in Geneva this week with Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. He said the two sides had reached an understanding on "guiding principles" but cautioned that a deal was not imminent. Araghchi indicated a draft counterproposal could be ready "in the next two or three days," with additional talks possible within a week.

The current tensions stem from Trump's decision during his first presidency to withdraw from an internationally backed nuclear agreement that had restricted Iran's program. After Trump exited the deal, Iran accelerated its nuclear activities. Last year, Israel conducted a 12-day military campaign against Iranian nuclear facilities, which the United States joined, though the campaign's long-term impact on Iran's nuclear ambitions remains unclear.

During Geneva discussions, the US did not demand zero uranium enrichment from Iran, and Iran did not offer to suspend enrichment activities. Instead, Araghchi said negotiations now focus on ensuring "Iran's nuclear programme, including enrichment, is peaceful and would remain peaceful forever."

A White House official responded to Araghchi's comments by reiterating that "the president has been clear that Iran cannot have nuclear weapons or the capacity to build them, and that they cannot enrich uranium."

The United Nations has expressed concern about the heightened rhetoric and increased military activities in the region. Trump also referenced a crackdown on protests in Iran, claiming 32,000 people were killed, though this figure could not be verified through independent sources.
