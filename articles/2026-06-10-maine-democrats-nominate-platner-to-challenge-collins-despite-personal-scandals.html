Maine Democrats nominate Platner to challenge Collins despite personal scandals

Graham Platner, a Marine veteran and oyster farmer, won Maine's Democratic primary for U.S. Senate on Tuesday, securing the party's nomination to challenge Republican incumbent Susan Collins. The victory sets up a competitive race in a state Democrats view as critical to their efforts to gain control of the Senate.

Platner's path to the nomination was unusual for a candidate with no prior elected office experience. His campaign gained momentum through social media and resonated with progressive voters, earning endorsements from prominent Democratic senators including Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren, and Ruben Gallego. His fundraising proved substantial, and he eventually sidelined the campaign of Maine Governor Janet Mills, who had initially sought to challenge Collins.

The nomination comes despite a series of personal controversies that have shadowed Platner's campaign. In recent weeks, reports emerged that Platner exchanged sexually explicit text messages with women outside his marriage, according to information his wife shared with the campaign. The New York Times, Wall Street Journal, and The Guardian confirmed these details after learning of the messages from the campaign's former political director.

Earlier, Reddit posts from 2013 to 2021 resurfaced in which Platner used offensive language, made derogatory comments about various groups, and questioned the accounts of sexual assault survivors. Additionally, Platner revealed he had received a tattoo while stationed abroad that resembled a Nazi emblem, though he later had it covered and said he only recently understood its significance.

Throughout these controversies, Platner has offered explanations framing his past conduct as products of post-traumatic stress disorder from military service in Iraq and Afghanistan. He has presented himself as imperfect and spoken publicly about his mistakes, describing his comments and actions as mistakes stemming from his wartime experiences.

Despite these issues, Democratic leaders have continued backing Platner. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer met with Platner in Washington and reaffirmed support, stating "We're going to beat Susan Collins and take back the Senate." Bernie Sanders defended the nomination, telling reporters that voters should focus on economic issues affecting working families rather than personal matters, and confirmed his continued endorsement.

Rep. Ro Khanna characterized voters' decision as giving Platner "a chance at redemption," noting that Platner had spoken about Maine providing him grace and a second chance.

Collins is seeking a sixth Senate term. Democrats need to gain four seats to control the Senate, and Collins represents the only Republican running for reelection in a state won by Democrat Kamala Harris in the previous presidential election, making Maine a top Democratic target.
