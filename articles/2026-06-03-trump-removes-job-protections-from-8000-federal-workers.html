Trump removes job protections from 8,000 senior federal workers

President Trump signed an executive order removing civil service protections from approximately 8,000 senior federal employees, converting their positions to at-will appointments that can be terminated without cause. The directive affects workers earning up to roughly $200,000 annually across multiple agencies, including senior officials and grants staff at the National Institutes of Health.

The change reclassifies these positions into a new employment category called Schedule Policy/Career, which eliminates the procedural safeguards that have traditionally protected career civil servants from political dismissal. These protections have historically required that terminations follow specific procedures and be based on performance rather than political considerations.

The affected positions represent a fraction of the federal workforce but include senior policymakers and administrators deemed to be "influencing" government policy. By reclassifying these roles, the executive order gives political leadership greater control over personnel decisions in key government agencies. This marks a significant shift in federal employment structure, where most career positions have historically been insulated from political turnover.

The move is part of a broader federal workforce overhaul by the Trump administration. More than 300,000 federal workers have been laid off or pushed to resign or retire since Trump's second term began, with over 25,000 workers terminated during their probationary periods.

A survey of fired probationary employees conducted by 27UNITED, a network of former NIH employees, found significant personal costs from the terminations. Among more than 300 respondents across 43 states and 15 agencies, 95 percent reported ongoing mental health effects, with nearly half experiencing PTSD-like symptoms. A quarter are taking new medications to manage these symptoms.

Many terminated workers struggle to find comparable employment. One in five respondents reported being unemployed as of January 31, while 49 percent of those who found new jobs reported earning significantly less. Only 11 percent found positions elsewhere in the federal government.

A federal judge ruled in September that the firing of probationary employees was unlawful, though the government was not required to reinstate terminated employees. Court cases related to these terminations remain ongoing, with workers filing appeals with the merit systems protection board.

The departures have included highly trained scientific experts. According to an analysis by Science, more than 10,000 doctoral-trained experts in science and related fields have left the country since Trump's second term began.

The executive order takes effect immediately, though implementation across different agencies will unfold over coming weeks. Federal employee unions and advocacy groups are expected to challenge the directive, arguing it violates existing civil service laws. The Office of Personnel Management did not respond to requests for comment.
